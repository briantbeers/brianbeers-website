HR just got a random-pool hit for six CDL drivers.  
A staffing desk needs twenty pre-employment screens before Monday’s warehouse start.  
A clinic’s occupational-health patient showed up for a post-accident urine — and the DOT clock is already ticking.

Somebody needs a **local collection site that won’t botch chain of custody**, turn results fast enough for the hiring manager, invoice the company (not the nervous applicant who “forgot their wallet”), and still answer the after-hours post-accident call.

That’s a **drug testing / screening lab** business — usually a **collection + employer-program operator** first, not a full clinical laboratory with analyzers in the back.

Not [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (same *B2B employer sales + compliance + collections* neighborhood — you sell *people on client payrolls*, not *screens that clear them to work*).  
Not [home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical) (same *regulated people / clinical-adjacent* brain — totally different Medicare, nursing leadership, and survey stack).  
Not [tax-preparation](/models/tax-preparation) (same *compliance throughput + seasonal spikes + storefront lease* feel — different buyer, different calendar).  
Not “I’ll hang a CLIA certificate on a closet and wait for Google Local” as the whole *employer-account* story.

You’re running a **compliance throughput + B2B account** machine: tests / collections × price − lab / MRO pass-through − collector labor − occupancy − marketing − royalty/fees (if any) − the cash hole when **employers pay Net-30/45** while your lab and payroll want cash sooner — plus the attach stack (backgrounds, BAT, physicals, DNA, wellness panels, TPA / consortium admin) that separates a real book from a walk-in novelty.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **account concentration**, **CLIA / DOT / chain-of-custody discipline**, **collections**, **collector labor**, **national-lab / PSC competition**, and **location / lease gravity** show up before the model looks like “everybody needs a drug test.”

Any Lab Test Now / Fastest Labs / ARCpoint–class franchise systems, indie collection sites, occupational-health clinics with a testing desk, and mobile collector + TPA shops sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The employer book, the compliance stack, and the lab contract are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local screening / collection operator**: a visible suite (or clinic desk / mobile-first route) that sells **workplace drug & alcohol testing**, often **DOT-compliant collections**, **breath alcohol**, **hair / oral fluid** where offered, **background screening**, and frequently **employer program administration** (random pools, consortium / TPA-style services) — then partners with **SAMHSA-certified labs** and an **MRO** for confirmation and review rather than trying to be Quest in a strip center.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **B2B employer / clinic channel (primary spine)** | **Retail / consumer walk-in attach** |
--|---------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, return-to-duty screens; company accounts; often backgrounds + BAT + physicals | Cash / card walk-ins: wellness panels, DNA / paternity, individual screens, “doctor’s-order-optional” consumer labs (brand-dependent) |
| **Primary constraint** | **Accounts × tests per account × attach − lab pass-through − collector hours − DSO** | **Foot traffic × ticket mix − rent − staffing for open hours** |
| **Cash shape** | Recurring / scheduled volume; Net-30/45 AR; sticky when you own the random program | Lumpier; price-shoppable; easier to lose to national PSC or another strip site |
| **Risk feel** | Account concentration, DOT error, slow-pay HR, national bid pressure | Lease burn, idle hours, consumer marketing spend, panel mix that isn’t your lab strength |
| **Owner job early** | Outbound B2B sales to HR / safety / staffing / clinics | Google Local, clinic partnerships, hours coverage |

Many real shops run **both**. Underwrite the mix honestly — a Fastest Labs–class **employer-first** book is a different company than an Any Lab Test Now–class **retail wellness + employer** storefront. Dual-brand / employer-enhancement stories (e.g. Cresso-class ALTN + ARCpoint adjacency in public franchise news) are **category proof that B2B attach matters** — not a pitch for any logo.

Typical menu:

1. **Urine / oral fluid / hair drug screens** — DOT and non-DOT panels; rapid vs lab confirmation  
2. **Breath alcohol testing (BAT)** — often paired with post-accident / reasonable-suspicion  
3. **Background screening / eVerify-adjacent packages** — classic multi-service attach next to pre-employment  
4. **DOT physicals / occupational health** (where credentialed / partnered) — clinic-channel glue  
5. **Random pool / consortium / TPA administration** — recurring fees that outlast one-off collections  
6. **Mobile / on-site / after-hours collections** — premium dispatch for fleets and construction  
7. **DNA / paternity / wellness / consumer panels** — retail attach; brand and lab-menu dependent  
8. **Clinic / staffing / attorney / court referral channel** — B2B2C volume without owning the end patient

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — cold call / door knock to HR and safety, staffing-agency partnerships, clinic / urgent-care referral, trucking associations, Google Local for walk-ins, franchise lead systems (if any)  
2. **Qualify** — DOT vs non-DOT, volume, random-pool needs, creditworthiness, who signs the PO, after-hours expectations  
3. **Price** — collection + lab + MRO + BAT + background + mobile fee — from **pass-through up**, not a round “$50 screen” fantasy  
4. **Collect / document** — collector qualification, chain of custody, eCCF where required, privacy / shy-bladder protocols  
5. **Ship / lab / MRO** — turnaround and rejection rates *are* the product  
6. **Report / invoice / collect** — portal for employers; Net terms; don’t confuse “tested today” with “paid today”  
7. **Retain / expand** — more departments, sister sites, backgrounds, physicals, consortium seats — or lose the account when a national lab undercuts or one custody error ends trust

**Scope lock:** this page is the **local drug-testing / screening collection business** — B2B employer + clinic channel, compliance, multi-service attach — selling screens and program admin, not inventing new assays.  
It is **not** [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (you clear candidates; you don’t employ them for clients).  
It is **not** [home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical) (skilled intermittent clinical care under Medicare rules).  
It is **not** [tax-preparation](/models/tax-preparation) (seasonal filing crush — related only as *compliance + storefront + labor bench*).  
It is **not** a high-complexity reference laboratory, a hospital lab, or a pure tele-impairment app.  
It is **not** “Quest / LabCorp PSC only” as your differentiation if you plan to win on price alone against their network.

Treat named systems as **proof the category professionalizes** — not as a pitch.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, billable collections and employer program seats** (plus attach) — not “we’re open and the restroom is clean” and not a LinkedIn post about workplace safety.

Education pricing / spread shape (national operator ranges; metros and after-hours sit higher; commodity pre-employment urine sits tighter):

- Common education talk puts a **DOT urine collection + lab + MRO** package often roughly in the **~$50–$90+** employer-billed band depending on panel, market, and whether you’re the TPA or just the site — **verify your lab contract**  
- Non-DOT rapid / oral fluid and BAT add-ons create mix; **after-hours / on-site dispatch** can materially lift ticket (sometimes **~2×** scheduled rates in operator education)  
- Background packages and consortium / random-admin fees are often where **recurring** lives — one 50-driver fleet with monthly seats beats a dozen one-off walk-ins  
- Franchise Item 19 education (category proof, **not** your forecast): Fastest Labs–class 2026 FDD public summaries cite franchised-location average gross revenue near **~$156k** (median ~**$127k**; wide low–high) for locations open **12+ months** in the disclosed group; Any Lab Test Now–class public summaries often cite average unit gross nearer **~$245k–$280k** bands depending on FDD year / cohort — **gross sales, not owner profit**; always pull the current Item 19 basis and call franchisees

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Employer accounts × tests per month** — empty “territory” with no HR relationships is a lease with a bathroom  
2. **Attach rate** — backgrounds, BAT, physicals, DNA, wellness, TPA seats vs naked urine only  
3. **Lab / MRO pass-through discipline** — fee creep erases contribution margin across every test  
4. **Collector utilization** — idle open hours vs mobile windshield time vs peak morning pre-employment rushes  
5. **Client credit quality × DSO** — growth that outruns collections is how small labs look busy and die  
6. **DOT / non-DOT mix** — regulated work pays for process discipline; non-DOT is volume and price pressure  
7. **Sales capacity** — owners who won’t outbound to employers don’t have a screening company; they have a waiting room

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Collection-first shops often talk **mid-to-high contribution** on the collection fee itself — then **lab pass-through**, royalties (~**7%** + ~**2%** brand fund in common franchise education), rent, and collector wages eat the dream  
- Gross profit % on “a test” is meaningless if you ignore rejected specimens, recollections, and free make-goods after a custody error  
- Net on a single suite is often a **tighter** band than “compliance is recession-proof” headlines — volume rises; labor, rent, and lab invoices rise with it

You’re not a bathroom with a logo.  
You’re an **account book + compliance process + pass-through margin** company wearing scrubs-optional business casual.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Employers still hire. CDL and safety-sensitive roles still sit under **DOT / Part 40** rules. Workers’-comp carriers and insurers still reward documented drug-free workplace programs. Staffing agencies still need volume screens before Monday. Clinics still need a reliable occupational-health partner. Those needs don’t disappear because a national lab has a nicer app.

Good times → more pre-employment volume, fleet growth, construction starts, staffing fills.  
Tight times → hiring slows (pre-employment dips) — but **random pools, post-accident, return-to-duty, and regulated fleets** keep a floor under operators who own programs rather than only walk-ins.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) rides **wage markup + outbound B2B sales + payroll float + WC**.  
[Home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical) rides **clinical episodes + Medicare timing + nurse supply**.  
[Tax-preparation](/models/tax-preparation) rides **seasonal filing crush + preparer bench + DIY competition**.  
Drug-testing labs ride **employer compliance demand + collection process + lab pass-through + account concentration risk**.

Same “I need someone cleared / compliant by Monday” demand emotion. Different invoice, different lawsuit, different cash gap.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “we’re the Uber of pee cups” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one location

A single screening suite lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Employer account book (the career-defining line)**  
This is a **B2B sales business that happens to collect specimens**. Match the staffing angle: outbound to HR, safety directors, fleet managers, staffing desks, construction GCs, and clinic partners. Retail walk-ins help — they rarely carry the P&L alone. If you won’t prospect, the model doesn’t care about your CLIA certificate.

**Account concentration**  
One staffing agency, one trucking fleet, or one clinic system at **30–50%+ of sales** feels great until their volume manager switches to a national PSC network or an in-house collector. Diversification is ops, not a slide deck.

**CLIA / DOT / chain-of-custody discipline**  
Most local operators are **collection sites** (often **CLIA-waived** rapid testing where applicable) partnered to certified labs — not full moderate/high-complexity labs. DOT collectors need training and documented procedures under **49 CFR Part 40**. A custody error, shy-bladder mishandle, or wrong panel can lose the account *and* create legal exposure. Compliance is the product, not a binder on a shelf.

**Lab / MRO contracts & fee creep**  
Your contribution margin lives inside someone else’s price list and turnaround. Rebid labs; track rejection rates; don’t lock “cheap” if it creates employer complaints. National labs are both **partners and competitors** (their PSC networks take walk-ins and contracted volume).

**Collections & credit**  
Employers and staffing agencies love Net-30/45. Your lab may want faster money. DSO by client beats blended vanity. Early-pay discounts, credit checks, and the courage to fire a slow-pay account matter as much as the collector’s smile.

**Collector / tech labor**  
Certified, reliable collectors who show up for 6 a.m. pre-employment rushes and 11 p.m. post-accident dispatches are scarce. Turnover means missed appointments and burned accounts. Cross-train BAT and intake; don’t make the owner the only person who can run a DOT collection correctly.

**Location & hours**  
Strip visibility helps retail and last-minute walk-ins. Employer accounts care more about **proximity to their sites**, parking, privacy, and **hours that match shift start**. A cheap lease far from industrial / trucking density is a hobby. Mobile attach expands reach and complicates utilization.

**Multi-service attach**  
Backgrounds, physicals, DNA, wellness panels, consortium seats — the backlog’s “multi-service attach” line is the difference between a commodity urine desk and a sticky employer partner. Underwrite what *your* unit actually sells vs what the brochure lists.

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only hunter *and* the only DOT-qualified collector *and* the only person who understands the aging report, you own a stressful B2B sales job with biohazard side effects. Scale starts when sales + collectors + billing can run Tuesday without you on every cup.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD Item 19 writeups, franchise comparison sites, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing sales by account, DSO, lab contracts, CLIA / DOT credentials, and lease terms.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Fastest Labs–class 2026 Item 19 public education: franchised locations open **≥12 months** in the disclosed group — average gross revenue ~**$156k**, median ~**$127k**, with a very wide low–high (education chatter also cites affiliate / company examples higher — **don’t underwrite the affiliate row as franchisee reality**)  
- Any Lab Test Now–class public Item 19 education often lands average unit gross roughly **~$250k–$280k** (median often a bit lower) depending on FDD year and cohort — retail + employer mix differs by brand era  
- Operator education sometimes frames “path to ~$1M sales” as a multi-year sales-culture target for dense territories — treat as **aspiration / system marketing**, not a base case  
- Top-quartile / multi-unit stories exist; bottoms of Item 19 distributions remind you that **open ≠ profitable**

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Lean indie mobile collector + home office: operator education often **~$10k–$50k+** before real working capital and insurance — then you still need accounts  
- Franchise Fastest Labs–class Item 7 education (2026 public summaries): roughly **~$130k–$200k** all-in (fee, suite, equipment, training, working capital) by territory size — **verify actual Item 7**  
- Franchise Any Lab Test Now–class Item 7 education (2026 public summaries): roughly **~$180k–$320k** — more retail build-out gravity  
- Indie strip suite with CLIA-waived setup, furniture, IT, deposits: commonly discussed **~$75k–$200k+** before sales ramp  
- Working capital for AR (employer Net terms) and lab payables matters more than the cute waiting-room chairs

**Margin / AR education shape:**

- Contribution after lab/MRO often healthier on **collection + mobile + admin fees** than on pure pass-through panels  
- Franchise royalty + brand fund commonly educated near **~9% of gross** plus monthly tech fees — price it into every quote  
- DSO education often **~30–60+ days** on employer books; staffing-agency clients can be slower  
- Confusing **test price** with **contribution after lab + royalty + labor** is the classic pricing mistake

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **sales vs gross profit vs owner cash** (don’t stop at top-line)?  
- Mix: DOT vs non-DOT; employer vs retail; backgrounds / TPA / physicals attach %?  
- Client concentration (top 5 / top 1 as % of sales)?  
- DSO by client; bad-debt history; credit policy?  
- Lab / MRO contracts, fee schedule, rejection / recollection rates, turnaround SLAs?  
- CLIA status (waived vs higher), DOT collector roster, Part 40 SOP audit trail?  
- Collector count, certifications, after-hours coverage, owner hours still on every hard collection?  
- Lease: rent vs density of employers / staffing / clinics; hours; bathroom / privacy layout?  
- Competition: Quest / LabCorp PSC density, other franchise strips, occupational clinics, mobile TPAs?  
- If franchised: royalty base, required vendors, territory reality, Item 19 cohort you actually resemble — or only a logo?

Same brain you’d use buying any thin-spread B2B compliance shop: credit quality, process risk, sales bench, and whether *you* want outbound prospecting and regulated paperwork in your week.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Account concentration (the silent killer)**  
One fleet, one staffing firm, or one clinic system funds the lease — until they don’t. National RFPs, PE roll-ups of occupational health, and “we’ll bring collections in-house” wipe books overnight. Diversify by industry and by buyer; watch top-account %.

**2. CLIA / DOT / chain-of-custody / regs**  
You’re in regulated work even when you’re “just a collection site.” Wrong paperwork, untrained collectors, privacy failures, and panel mistakes create employer liability and your liability. State rules, HIPAA-adjacent handling, and record retention sit next to Part 40. Regs are ops, not a weekend PDF.

**3. Collections & employer AR**  
Winning volume from a slow-pay staffing desk or distressed contractor is how you fund *their* hiring spree. Invoice disputes on “who ordered the test” are common. Credit checks and deposits feel mean until the aging report teaches manners.

**4. Collector / tech labor**  
Certified collectors who will do mornings, weekends, and post-accident calls are hard to recruit and harder to keep. One call-out on a DOT morning loses the account faster than a competitor’s brochure. Cross-training and backup coverage cost money you must price.

**5. Competition from national labs & PSC networks**  
Quest / LabCorp patient service centers, large occupational-health chains, and preferred-provider networks already sit in most metros. They win on brand, portal integration, and national contracts. Your edge is **local responsiveness, after-hours, relationship, and program admin** — not beating their catalog price on commodity urine.

**6. Location & lease gravity**  
Wrong side of town relative to industrial parks, trucking, staffing, and clinics = empty chairs. Too much retail rent for a B2B book that never needed the pretty facade. Hours that don’t match shift starts waste payroll. Mobile helps — it doesn’t erase a bad site choice if you promised walk-in convenience.

**7. Lab fee creep & pass-through margin erosion**  
A small increase in lab/MRO costs across thousands of tests is a quiet profit funeral. Operators who never rebidding labs or who sell at “market” without loaded cost get busy and broke.

**8. Commodity price pressure / RFP culture**  
Employers shop screens like office supplies. Reverse auctions and national TPA mandates compress fees. Competing only on price trains clients to treat you as interchangeable — then punish you when commodity quality arrives.

**9. Sales dependency / outbound culture**  
This is not “open the door and compliance walks in.” Like staffing, owners who won’t hunt HR and safety don’t last. Franchise lead systems help; they don’t replace a weekly outreach cadence.

**10. Multi-service attach failure**  
Brochure lists backgrounds, DNA, physicals, consortium — unit only sells cheap urine. Attach is how you justify the lease and survive a hiring slowdown. Selling attach requires different partnerships (clinics, MRO, background vendors) and different sales scripts.

**11. After-hours / post-accident readiness**  
The call that builds the relationship is often the 2 a.m. reasonable-suspicion or crash collection. If you can’t staff it, someone else owns the fleet. On-call pay is a real cost.

**12. Specimens, rejections, and make-goods**  
Rejected cups, temperature failures, and “donor left” events create free labor and angry HR. Process discipline and collector coaching matter as much as marketing.

**13. Retail vs B2B identity confusion**  
Chasing wellness-panel Instagram while ignoring employer outbound (or vice versa) splits the P&L. Pick a primary spine; let the other be attach. Brand systems skew differently — underwrite *your* mix.

**14. Owner-as-hero collector / hunter**  
If only you can open accounts *and* only you can run a clean DOT collection *and* only you understand AR, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a high-stress compliance job with personal guarantees.

**15. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties on gross, brand funds, required vendors, and tech fees help only if they shorten sales ramp, improve lab terms, and protect compliance. A logo does not make 50 cold calls to fleets.

**16. Confusing “drug testing is required forever” headlines with “your collectible contribution margin after lab fees, rent, collectors, and slow-pay accounts”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday no-show collector and your aging AR.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means screening labs are a **sales + compliance + pass-through** business where empty account books, custody errors, and national PSC competition decide whether the suite survives the next hiring slowdown.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re wired for **outbound B2B sales** to HR / safety / fleets / staffing / clinics (or will hire and hold a hunter accountable) — and you won’t romanticize “healthcare vibes” as a substitute  
- Chain-of-custody detail, DSO dashboards, and lab invoices energize you more than they nauseate you  
- You can recruit and schedule reliable collectors — including after-hours coverage  
- You’ve compared this door to [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (float + WC), [home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical) (clinical survey stack), and [tax-preparation](/models/tax-preparation) (seasonal compliance crush) — and still want the screening stack  
- You’re honest about concentration risk and national-lab competition in *your* trade area  
- You’re willing to own process risk in a regulated niche without needing to be a physician

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a passive “compliance is mandatory” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to cold calls, rejection, collections conversations, or 2 a.m. post-accident logistics  
- You assumed a franchise brochure erased Quest / LabCorp from your zip code  
- You actually want **staffing**, **home health**, **tax prep**, or a **true high-complexity lab** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll add DNA and IV therapy next quarter” was a margin patch, not new compliance, vendors, and sales motion  
- You need the trendiest wellness vertical more than timeless account-and-custody discipline

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: accounts, collections, compliance, pass-through, labor. A brand is only worth the fee if it improves lab terms, training, and sales ramp — not because the website has stock photos of specimen cups.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** B2B services door or a non-biohazard vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Top-account loss / concentration → lease with no volume  
- DOT / chain-of-custody / CLIA event → account loss + legal exposure  
- Slow-pay / bankrupt employer or staffing client → you funded their screens  
- Collector walk-off or no-show → missed regulated collections  
- Lab fee spike or bad partner SLA → margin and reputation hit  
- National PSC / occupational-health bid → busy hours, thin price  
- Wrong location / hours → retail rent without B2B density  
- After-hours gap → fleets choose someone who answers  
- Owner (or only hunter) stops outbound → pipeline death  
- Attach never materializes → commodity urine desk forever  
- Royalty / brand drag without sales or lab leverage  
- Buying a “book” that was one salesperson + one fleet + soft AR  
- Confusing industry “required testing” headlines with collectible GM after pass-through

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “companies have to drug-test forever.”

You’re buying the right to run a **local employer-compliance + collection machine** — if you can sell outbound without apology, price from lab pass-through up, keep custody and DOT process boringly perfect, collect before hope becomes policy, staff collectors who show up at ugly hours, survive national-lab competition on relationship and speed, and not confuse top-line test counts for free money when one account leaves.

Some people should buy a tired suite and fix sales process, credit policy, and attach mix (resale path as *category* inventory — diligence still required).  
Some people should open lean with a franchise or indie collection model and prove outbound employer accounts before a second territory.  
Some people should choose [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting), [home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical), or [tax-preparation](/models/tax-preparation) and never take on specimen custody and after-hours dispatch.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do employers still need drug screens?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful B2B sales job with CLIA paperwork, aging AR, and a personal guarantee when the only collector who can run a clean DOT collection texts “I’m done” the morning your biggest fleet’s randoms hit?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other business-services vehicles vs senior-care or tax-compliance paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
