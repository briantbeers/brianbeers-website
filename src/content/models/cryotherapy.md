She’s got DOMS from a Hyrox Saturday and a membership draft that hits whether she books or not.  
He’s got a marathon in six weeks and a group chat that won’t shut up about “the chamber.”  
Somebody wants red light + compression + a cold hit — or a $65 walk-in that only works if the next twelve visits ride a prepaid pass.

Someone needs a **chamber (or plunge) that actually turns**, a **membership book that gets *used* (not just sold)**, add-ons that densify the visit without inventing a second regulated business overnight, intake that screens contraindications before frostbite becomes a Google review, and an owner who can survive equipment CapEx, rent, staffing, soft Tuesdays, and the moment the neighborhood decides cold exposure was last year’s Reel.

That’s a **cryotherapy / recovery wellness** studio.

Not [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) dues for access to iron (same *membership + facility fixed cost* brain — different product, different trend shape).  
Not [med-spa](/models/med-spa) injectables under a medical director (adjacent “optimize me” dollars — totally different clinical and CAC stack; IV drip attach can *blur* into that world).  
Not [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) table hours and LMT recruiting (same *membership utilization + room* emotion — different labor ceiling and modality).  
Not “I’ll buy a chamber for the gym lobby and call it a company” as the whole *studio* story.  
Not “passive income with a nitrogen tank in the logo.”

You’re running a **membership + modality + add-on** business: treatment capacity × utilized member visits × ticket / stack mix − equipment debt / lease − rent − front desk / tech labor − marketing − consumables (nitrogen, IV bags, linens) − the empty chamber when members draft but don’t show and the novelty cohort churns to the next biohack.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **equipment CapEx** is real money that depreciates whether you book or not, **membership utilization** is the whole engine, **trend / novelty risk** can rewrite demand faster than your lease, **liability** (cold injury, contraindication misses, IV reactions if you attach clinical) is not optional wallpaper, **rent** clears on soft weeks, and **staffing** still exists even when Instagram calls it “self-serve recovery.”

Restore Hyper Wellness / CryoOne / iCryo–class branded recovery shops, indie cryo bars, cold-plunge + sauna boutiques, gym-attached recovery rooms, and multi-modality “hyper wellness” stacks sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The utilized chamber, the retained member habit, honest CapEx math, and the waiver that actually means something are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local recovery / cold-exposure wellness operator**: a retail box (often roughly **~1,200–3,500+ sq ft** depending on modality count; lean cryo-only shops and full hyper-wellness stacks sit outside that band) with whole-body cryotherapy chambers and/or cold plunges, often infrared sauna, red light, compression boots, sometimes localized cryo, float adjacency, hyperbaric, and — in heavier concepts — **IV / clinical attach** under medical oversight. Booking + membership tech, front desk, intake / waiver discipline, and a plant that keeps equipment online.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Cryo / cold-led recovery studio (primary spine on this page)** | **Multi-modality “hyper wellness” stack** |
|--|------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | WBC / plunge sessions + memberships + light add-ons (red light, compression, sauna) | Membership credits across cryo + heat + light + compression **plus** higher-ticket clinical (IV, biomarkers, HBOT, aesthetics) |
| **Primary constraint** | **Chamber / plunge utilization × membership utilization × CapEx service** | **Same + clinical labor + medical-director / MSO complexity + consumable COGS** |
| **CapEx feel** | Mid-heavy: chamber(s) often **~$40k–$150k+** each (electric vs nitrogen changes opex); TI + HVAC / power | Heavy: multi-platform equipment + clinical TI; branded Item 7 bands often mid/high-six to low-seven figures |
| **Cash shape** | Memberships + packs; walk-ins for acquisition; high session margin *if* utilization holds | Recurring dues + IV/package upside; compliance and staffing tax rise with the menu |
| **Failure mode** | Novelty churn; dark chamber with debt; at-home plunge substitution | Clinical attach complexity; membership without utilization; CapEx wall of toys |

Many real shops run **both** — cold as the door, stack as the wallet share. Underwrite the mix honestly. A cryo-only membership box with one electric chamber is a different company than a Restore–class studio with IV nurses, HBOT, and a medical stack.

Typical menu:

1. **Whole-body cryotherapy (WBC)** — 2–4 minute chamber sessions; the category namesake  
2. **Cold plunge / immersion** — longer immersion; lower CapEx; rising **at-home competition**  
3. **Infrared sauna / heat** — longer dwell; pairs with cold for “contrast” marketing  
4. **Red light / photobiomodulation** — short sessions; low variable cost; attach densifier  
5. **Compression (Normatec–class boots, etc.)** — recovery ritual attach  
6. **Localized cryo / facial cryo** — incremental ticket; technique / training required  
7. **Memberships / credits / unlimited cold** — the cash-engine *design*; utilization decides whether it’s LTV or a discount club  
8. **Packages / series** — cash up front; deferred liability until redeemed  
9. **IV drips / shots / biomarkers / HBOT / aesthetics** (heavier concepts) — high ticket; **different clinical, pharmacy, and regulatory stack** — don’t treat as a free margin patch  
10. **Retail / take-home** — recovery tools, topicals, merch; coaching matters  
11. **Not the same company:** [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) access dues; [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) LMT tables; clinical [med-spa](/models/med-spa) as the *primary* spine; pure IV bar without recovery plant; home plunge as a substitute P&L

Typical loop:

1. **Demand** — Google Local, Instagram / TikTok, athlete / CrossFit / run-club COIs, gym partnerships, employer wellness, prior-member winback, strip co-tenancy next to fitness  
2. **Tour / convert** — first session, membership pitch, contraindications *before* the chamber, card-on-file  
3. **Intake / service** — waiver, vitals / screening where required, session protocol, oxygen / nitrogen / plunge safety discipline  
4. **Stack / rebook** — red light + compression + heat before they leave; next visit booked  
5. **Collect / review** — draft clearing, failed-card recovery, review ask, freeze / cancel policy that actually fires  
6. **Keep plant online + stay safe** — chamber service contracts, nitrogen logistics (if any), staff coverage, claim readiness — or watch CapEx sit dark while rent clears

**Scope lock:** this page is the **operating cryotherapy / recovery wellness studio** — membership + add-ons, equipment CapEx, utilization, trend/novelty risk, liability, rent, staffing.  
It is **not** [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) as the primary thesis (even when you partner with one).  
It is **not** [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) (cousin membership labor).  
It is **not** [med-spa](/models/med-spa) injectables-first aesthetics (even when IV / longevity attach lives next door).  
It is **not** “I’ll franchise a biohack Reel and the market educates itself for free.”

Named proof points exist across the category: **Restore Hyper Wellness**, **CryoOne**, **iCryo** (Brian’s 250-ideas Cryotherapy row), indie cryo bars, plunge + sauna boutiques, gym-attached recovery rooms, and Perspire / DRIPBaR–class adjacent heat or IV specialists. Independents, franchisees, and multi-unit operators can all sit here. Same umbrella. Wildly different CapEx, clinical depth, and trend scars.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, paid recovery sessions** (and membership dollars that convert into those sessions, plus add-ons that ride them) — not a nitrogen fog Reel and not a lobby full of chrome.

Education ticket / mix bands (national category chatter; metros and lifestyle centers sit higher; discount / gym-attach sit lower — **verify your trade area**):

- Single WBC sessions often discussed roughly **~$40–$75+** walk-in  
- Cold plunge drop-ins often lower (**~$15–$40+**) — price power is weaker as home plunges proliferate  
- Monthly memberships / unlimited cold often discussed roughly **~$99–$299+/month** depending on credits, stack access, and brand positioning  
- Packages (5 / 10 / 20 sessions) common for trial commitment; unused packs are deferred liability, not free profit  
- IV drips / clinical attach (where offered) often **~$100–$350+** per session — different COGS and labor  
- Branded multi-modality Item 19–class public summaries (Restore–class secondary writeups) have shown mature-studio gross averages in roughly the **~$0.9M–$1.1M+** conversation with wide quartile spreads — *shape, not your forecast*; pull current FDD

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Staffed / available treatment capacity × utilization** — empty chambers with rent, debt service, and service contracts still due  
2. **Membership mix × *utilization*** — prepaid / monthly plans guests **actually use** (and renew) vs sold-and-forgotten vanity  
3. **Average ticket × stack depth** — cold-only vs cold + red light + compression + heat vs IV attach  
4. **Show-up rate** — booked slots that become paid sessions (member no-shows waste capacity you staffed and heated/cooled for)  
5. **At-desk / in-bay rebook & stack (the unit of truth)** — next visit + second modality before they leave; studios that only “sell memberships at the desk” without utilization ops usually have a retention problem, not a “need more Meta ads” problem  
6. **CAC vs LTV** — education spend is expensive in pioneer categories (Brian’s published cryo caution); referrals and gym COIs cheapen the bottom  
7. **Equipment uptime** — a dark chamber is CapEx theater; nitrogen stockouts, compressor failures, and plunge chemistry kill the day  
8. **Labor model** — front desk + recovery techs vs clinical nurses (if IV) changes *who* you must recruit and *what* hits payroll  
9. **Consumables** — nitrogen (if LN2 chambers), IV bags, linens, plunge chemistry — electric WBC can look “high margin” until utilization and rent are honest

Gross / cost intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Session contribution on electric WBC / red light / compression can look excellent on a napkin (low variable cost) — then rent, labor, marketing, debt, and soft utilization eat the dream  
- Nitrogen WBC adds ongoing gas cost (operator chatter sometimes cites thousands per month at volume) and logistics risk  
- Labor commonly discussed roughly **~25–40%+** of revenue depending on clinical depth (cryo-only front desk vs IV nurse stack)  
- Rent / occupancy often discussed roughly **~10–16%+** of revenue as a stress band in recovery retail; lifestyle-center rent that only works at fantasy membership counts is a trap  
- Marketing / education CAC can rival rent in markets that still need to learn what cryotherapy *is*  
- Net on a multi-modality studio is often discussed in a **tighter** band than Instagram recovery myths — volume rises; CapEx service, people, and churn rise with it

Gross memberships *look* simple on a napkin.  
Then a soft January, a compressor failure, a landlord bump, a week of unused credits, a frostbite scare, an at-home plunge content wave, and a competing studio three miles away with a louder Reel show up.

You’re not a biohacker with a fog machine.  
You’re a **membership-utilization + equipment-utilization operator** whose inventory is *time on expensive machines* — in a category Brian has openly contrasted with “people already buy plumbing / haircuts / tires” demand.

**Light contrast (same wellness corridor, different check):**

| Model | Who pays you | What you “sell” | Overhead feel |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Cryo / recovery studio (this page)** | Members + tickets + optional clinical attach | Cold / heat / light capacity + membership utilization | CapEx, trend risk, liability, rent, utilization |

| **IV-only bar** | Patients / members | Drips under clinical rules | Clinical labor, pharmacy, compliance — thinner recovery plant |

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Athletes recover. Desk bodies hurt. Social media normalizes cold exposure, red light, and “longevity stacks.” Cash-pay consumers will pay for *feeling* better when the ritual sticks — and churn when it doesn’t. The category is still **fragmented**, still **educating** large parts of the public, and full of indie shops and branded systems rather than one 800-lb gorilla you have to kill in every trade area — which is why Brian’s 250-ideas sheet shelves **Cryotherapy** next to other health & personal-care doors (Restore / CryoOne / iCryo as examples), and why his published framing puts cold plunge / cryotherapy / red light / IV in the **Emerging Industries / pioneer** bucket: high retention *if* people try it and feel a difference — and cash-flow strain if consumer education takes longer than your runway.

Good times → membership upgrades, stack attach, second chambers, employer wellness contracts, Instagram as a free billboard.  
Tight times → deferred visits, “I’ll just plunge at home,” novelty fatigue, staff shopping hours, landlords who still want last year’s rent, and **trend risk** when the next modality (or the next gym with a cheap plunge) rewrites the Google ads.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) rides **habit dues for movement access** — Brian has published deep 24/7 / boutique membership storytelling *and* trend-fragility caution (Orangetheory–class).  
Cryo / recovery rides **habit dues for modality minutes** — often lighter coach labor than boutique fitness, heavier **equipment CapEx and novelty education**.  
Same “I want to feel better” emotion. Different machine, different pioneer tax.

[Massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) sits nearby as **membership + room utilization + licensed hands**. [Med-spa](/models/med-spa) sits nearby when IV / longevity / aesthetics attach pulls you into clinical oversight. Useful for framing *adjacency*. Dangerous if you underwrite a cryo box like an injectables flywheel or an LMT book.

That’s the engine. Not one celebrity chamber Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one studio

**Membership utilization (the unit of truth)**  
You’re not scaling “cold is cool.” You’re scaling **paid visits on a habit clock that memberships were designed to lock**. Sold passes that sit unused, freeze policies that never get enforced, unlimited plans that train guests to ghost for six weeks then cancel, and credit liability that looks like cash in month one decide whether the calendar is vanity or EBITDA. Education chatter often treats early visit frequency (first 30–60 days) as the leading churn indicator — intervene before the cancel, not after. A studio that acquires members but fails to drive utilization and stack activation is a marketing treadmill with expensive chrome.

**Equipment CapEx & uptime**  
Whole-body chambers commonly discussed roughly **~$40k–$150k+** depending on electric vs nitrogen, capacity, and vendor. Multi-modality stacks (sauna, red light, compression, HBOT) compound. Branded hyper-wellness Item 7 bands (Restore–class secondary summaries) often land roughly **~$0.75M–$1.3M+** all-in including TI — **verify current FDD**. Leasing lowers cash-up-front and raises monthly break-even. Service contracts, nitrogen logistics, three-phase power, HVAC, and spare parts decide whether CapEx *prints* or depreciates in a locked room. Buying the “full suite” for the lobby tour is how working capital dies.

**Trend / novelty / education risk (Brian’s explicit cryo caution)**  
In Drive/published “5 Questions To Find Deals Others Miss,” Brian’s cryotherapy franchise pitch story is the **education-demand** filter: if ten people on the street don’t know what it is or why they need it, you’re funding a marketing school with rent attached. His recession-proof / emerging-industry newsletter lists cold plunge, cryotherapy, red light, IV as pioneer upside *and* cash-flow strain while you educate. At-home plunges, cheap Amazon tubs, gym-attached recovery corners, and the next biohack modality compete for the same discretionary wallet. Your defense is habit, stack breadth, coaching, location convenience, and outcomes people *feel* — not racing every Groupon or assuming Instagram permanently educated your trade area.

**Liability & contraindication discipline**  
Uncontrolled hypertension, pregnancy, Raynaud’s, recent surgery, certain cardiac conditions, claustrophobia, cold intolerance, and skipped intake create frostbite, cold injury, panic events, and brand-killing reviews. FDA messaging has been explicit that WBC is **not** approved to treat medical conditions — marketing claims matter. Plunge adds slip / cold-shock / supervision questions. IV attach adds reaction, pharmacy, and clinical oversight risk. Waivers, pre-visit screens, oxygen monitoring (where required), staff training, and insurance that *actually covers cryotherapy* cost money; skipping them costs the brand and possibly the policy. Several carriers treat cryo as a special exposure — gaps show up after the claim.

**Rent / occupancy cost**  
Fixed plant clears whether you do eight sessions or eighty. Site selection (parking, visibility, trade-area income / athlete density, co-tenancy with gyms, competitor density) matters more than black-tile aesthetic. Recovery retail that only works at fantasy utilization is a classic killer.

**Staffing (even when the brochure says self-serve)**  
Front desk, session coaching, cleaning / turn protocols, clinical staff if IV, and a GM who can sell memberships without lying about outcomes. “Hands-free chamber” marketing does not erase open/close, intake, claim response, or the Tuesday the compressor alarms. Owner-as-hero opener is still a job with a fog machine.

**Add-on / stack activation**  
Members who only ever use one modality churn when a cheaper single-modality option opens. Cross-service activation (cold + heat + light + compression) is a retention lever most studios underwrite and then forget to coach. IV / biomarker attach can raise ticket — and import [med-spa](/models/med-spa)-adjacent compliance overnight.

**Owner involvement (honest version)**  
Early: hiring, floor presence, membership sales, equipment vendors, landlord, local partnerships (gyms, PTs, run clubs), compliance, claim readiness.  
Later: multi-unit is possible when GMs and utilization systems exist — semi-absentee myths die when the chamber is down, the nitrogen truck is late, and the membership list is full of unused credits.

**Adjacent stack**  
Chamber vendor / service, nitrogen supplier (if any), booking / membership software, insurance (GL + specialty), medical director / nursing (if clinical), landlord / power / HVAC, waste / sharps (if IV), marketing agency that understands education CAC — you need them *fast* when a platform goes dark or a guest reports an injury.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Equipment CapEx that sits dark**  
Chambers, plunges, saunas, red light, compression, HBOT — bought or leased for the lobby tour — still want debt service, service contracts, power, and trained operators. Underutilized platforms are how working capital dies. “Near-zero COGS” on an electric session does not pay a compressor rebuild or a landlord.

**2. Membership utilization failure**  
Sold unlimiteds and credit packs that don’t get used, freezes that never expire, and “membership” that’s really a permanent discount train guests to underpay. Cash collected ≠ capacity filled. Utilization dashboards, early-visit intervention, and honest package design are the business — not optional CRM polish.

**3. Trend / novelty / pioneer education risk**  
Brian’s published cryo caution is blunt: if the street doesn’t already understand the product, you fund education with rent attached. Novelty cohorts churn when the next Reel modality arrives. At-home plunges and gym recovery corners compete for the same discretionary cold. Underwriting cryo like “everyone already needs this” is how runway disappears.

**4. Liability & contraindication misses**  
Frostbite, cold injury, panic in the chamber, plunge cold-shock, slip-and-fall, IV reactions (if attached). Skipped pre-screens, weak waivers, and insurance gaps become refunds, reviews, and claims. FDA “not approved to treat” messaging makes overclaim advertising an ops risk, not a slogan debate.

**5. Rent / occupancy cost**  
The lease clears on soft weeks. Overbuilt TI in a B-minus center, CAM surprises, power upgrades you didn’t model, and rent that only works at fantasy membership counts are classic killers.

**6. Staffing & coverage**  
Self-serve marketing dies when intake needs a human, the chamber alarms, the nitrogen truck is late, or an IV chair needs a nurse. Recruiting a GM who can sell without medical overclaim — and clinical talent if you attach drips — is a ceiling most Instagram decks skip.

**7. Nitrogen / consumable / uptime logistics**  
LN2 stockouts, tank leases, oxygen monitoring, plunge chemistry, and vendor response times turn “high margin sessions” into closed doors. Electric chambers trade gas cost for higher CapEx and power dependency — pick your scar honestly.

**8. Stack / add-on failure**  
Shelves of red light and compression sit unused while members only book cryo. Single-modality members churn to the cheaper competitor. Coaching scripts and itinerary design matter; “we’re not salesy” is how you leave retention on the table.

**9. IV / clinical attach complexity**  
Weight-loss, drips, biomarkers, and longevity lines can grow fast and wobble fast (supply, compounding rules, medical-director quality, board attention). Treating them as “easy recurring” without separate compliance is how a recovery studio inherits a second regulated business overnight — [med-spa](/models/med-spa)-adjacent scars included.

**10. CAC & partnership dependency**  
Meta and TikTok will take the money. Creative fatigue and education-heavy funnels push CAC up while discounting destroys LTV. Gym / PT / run-club partnerships help — until the gym installs its own plunge. Shops that can’t see LTV:CAC by channel are flying blind.

**11. No-shows & late cancels on CapEx slots**  
A missed chamber block burns amortized CapEx and staffed hours. Deposits, reminders, and waitlists are ops — not niceties. Member no-shows still waste the slot you reserved capacity for.

**12. Price competition / race to the bottom**  
Screenshot pricing from a home plunge, a gym add-on, or a national intro special will always undercut a compliant, well-insured studio if you play their game. Discount wars destroy ticket, culture, and the ability to fund service contracts and claims readiness.

**13. Competition from gyms, IV bars, sauna specialists, and home equipment**  
[Fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) recovery corners, Perspire–class heat specialists, DRIPBaR–class IV concepts, indie plunge clubs, and Amazon tubs all nibble the same wallet. Consistent stack experience + retained habit + safety trust beat racing every Reel coupon.

**14. Deferred-revenue / membership accounting blindness**  
Cash from annual passes feels like profit in month one. Auditors, buyers, and your February payroll disagree. Underwrite utilization and liability, not just deposits.

**15. Insurance that doesn’t match the modalities**  
GL policies that exclude cryotherapy, missing professional liability for clinical attach, weak cyber for health intake data, underinsured equipment. Gaps show up after the claim.

**16. Owner-as-hero opener / repair-caller**  
If only you can close memberships, calm a contraindication conversation, and get the vendor on the phone at 6 a.m., you bought a high-stress recovery job with chrome — not ownership leverage.

**17. Seasonality & daypart soft spots**  
Athlete season spikes vs soft midweeks; New Year resolution waves vs summer travel. Cash planning that assumes every week is race week fails in February — especially if membership freezes pile up.

**18. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties and brand funds help only if they fill chambers, shorten education CAC, support compliance, and improve membership ops. A logo does not utilize a ghosting member or fix a dark compressor.

**19. Overbuilding the modality wall**  
Buying every platform for “the full recovery experience” before utilization on the first two is proven is how CapEx becomes a museum. Sequence like an operator: prove cold habit, then stack, then clinical — or admit you’re raising a different company.

**20. Confusing “athletes love cold” with “your utilized, collectible chamber hours after rent, CapEx service, membership utilization, and liability math”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday no-show / utilization report.

**21. Confusing Brian’s 250-ideas / pioneer adjacency with *your* unit economics**  
Category proof and education-risk framing in Brian’s content are real. They do **not** prove your chambers will print — especially when branded Item 19 tables (where they exist) show wide quartile spreads and some locations at the bottom of the pack.

**22. Thinking “self-serve chamber + membership app” means zero people work**  
Labor-light slogans die when the opener calls out, the waiver was unsigned, and the Google review says “frostbite.” Active ownership, not passive fog.

**23. Buying a “membership book” that was a novelty spike + one Meta account — or a plant with deferred equipment leases and claim history**  
Diligence that stops at trailing revenue without utilization reality, CapEx remaining life, and safety history is how you purchase a hollow calendar with a compressor attached.

**24. Marketing medical outcomes you can’t legally claim**  
“Cures inflammation / treats disease” language invites regulatory and platform risk. Wellness positioning with honest expectations is slower marketing — and safer ops.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means cryotherapy / recovery wellness is a **membership-utilization + equipment-CapEx** business where dark chambers, unused passes, pioneer education spend, rent, liability, and trend churn decide whether the chrome survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can sell and *operate* memberships — utilization dashboards energize you more than buying another modality  
- You’re willing to underwrite **equipment CapEx / leases / service** honestly and sequence platforms instead of decorating the lobby  
- Contraindication intake, waivers, insurance, and claim readiness don’t scare you off  
- You’re capitalized for TI + chambers + working capital through a soft education ramp — or you’re buying a shop with a real, transferable membership habit and a plant that stays online  
- You can hold a price and safety standard without racing the cheapest plunge Groupon or home-tub screenshot  
- You’re honest about **trend / pioneer risk** (Brian’s cryo education caution) and still want this door  
- You’ve compared this door to [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym), [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio), [med-spa](/models/med-spa), and pure IV / sauna specialists and still want **cold / recovery membership + equipment utilization** as the product

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a semi-absentee “cryo memberships print money” story from month one of dark chambers and unused credits  
- You’re allergic to people coverage, enforcing policies, or liability paperwork  
- You’re undercapitalized for CapEx + rent + education CAC while the habit builds  
- You thought “we’ll add IV and HBOT next quarter” was a margin patch, not a clinical compliance project  
- You need the street to already understand the product with zero marketing spend — Brian’s filter says look elsewhere  
- You actually want [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) dues, [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) tables, or [med-spa](/models/med-spa) injectables as the spine — different underwriting  
- You plan to win solely by matching the lowest walk-in cryo price within three miles  
- You can’t stomach trend risk, at-home plunge competition, or cold-injury reputation risk

Franchise vs independent vs buying a tired indie studio is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: chambers, members, show-ups, CapEx service, rent, liability. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens education, demand, equipment support, and systems — not because the nitrogen fog photographs well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** wellness door (massage membership, boutique fitness with known demand, or a different CapEx shape) first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition — and it matches Brian’s own published preference for built-in demand when comparing doors.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everyone wants to biohack.”

You’re buying the right to run a **membership-utilization + equipment-utilization machine** — if you can fill and *hold* sessions, design packages guests actually use and renew, underwrite CapEx only when utilization is real, fund consumer education without lying to yourself about pioneer risk, run contraindication intake like it matters, hold a price against home plunges and gym add-ons, survive trend churn without panic-buying every new modality, and choose a labor / clinical depth you can defend when a guest — or an insurer — asks hard questions.

Some people should start cryo-lean (one chamber + light stack) and prove habit before a hyper-wellness wall.  
Some people should buy a tired studio and fix utilization, membership design, uptime, and safety plant.  
Some people should choose [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) or [massage-studio](/models/massage-studio) and never take equipment novelty risk.  
Some people should stay on [med-spa](/models/med-spa) clinical aesthetics and never fund street education for cold.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely (Brian’s own cryo deal-filter story exists for a reason).

The question isn’t “do athletes like cold?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a CapEx-heavy membership job with rent that doesn’t care, memberships that sit unused, a chamber that can go dark on a Saturday, a novelty cohort that churns when the next Reel arrives, and a liability event that can turn overnight?**
