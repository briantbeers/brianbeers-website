Lunch rush hits at 11:47.  
The slicer is down. Two call-outs texted “sorry.”  
DoorDash pings while the line is out the door — and the commission doesn’t care that your food cost just crept past thirty.

Somebody wants a **sandwich in under ten minutes**, fresh, consistent, parked without a death spiral — and a local box that can still pay rent when Tuesday is soft and January is softer.

That’s a **fast casual / QSR / sandwich unit**.

Not a full-service dinner restaurant with a wine list and a host stand.  
Not [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (same *labor recruiting* brain — you sell *filled wage hours to employers*, not *tickets to hungry strangers*).  
Not [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (same *restaurant door* — you sell *night compliance contracts to kitchens*, not *lunch to guests*).  
Not “I’ll open a cool concept and Instagram my way to freedom” as the whole *prime cost + lease + manager* story.

You’re running a **throughput machine**: tickets × average check − **food & paper** − **crew labor** − occupancy − royalties/fees (if any) − delivery leakage − the cash hole when the **GM walks** and you become the closer, the scheduler, and the person who opens at 9 a.m. on a Saturday.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **labor**, **food cost**, **site / rent**, **manager dependency**, **franchise fee drag**, and **delivery platforms** show up before the model looks like “everybody eats lunch.”

Subway / Jersey Mike’s / Firehouse–class sandwich systems, indie fast-casual bowls and wraps, and strip QSR boxes sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The prime-cost math and the parking lot are not.

---

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local foodservice operator**: a fixed site (inline strip, end-cap, pad, non-traditional kiosk / C-store adjacency), a limited menu engineered for speed, a crew that can run a rush, a POS / kitchen display stack, and — if you’re honest — a **general manager** who actually owns open-to-close when you’re not there.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **QSR / sandwich throughput (primary spine)** | **Broader fast-casual (bowls / wraps / build-your-own)** |
|--|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | High-velocity tickets — subs, sides, drinks — speed + consistency | Slightly higher check, often more prep complexity / ingredient SKUs |
| **Primary constraint** | **Transactions × check × speed − food − labor − occupancy − fees** | Same equation; menu complexity can raise waste, training time, and ticket time |
| **Cash shape** | Daypart-driven (lunch spine; dinner / late depends on trade area) | Similar; catering / office lunch can thicken midweek |
| **Risk feel** | Labor no-shows, food waste, wrong site, delivery commissions | Same + more SKU / prep failure modes |
| **Owner job early** | Hire/train crew + protect food & labor % + live at the site until a real GM exists | Same — plus menu discipline so “chef feelings” don’t kill throughput |

Many real operators run **one box first**, then multi-unit. Underwrite whether *your* deal is owner-operator theater or a manager-led system with proof.

Typical menu / revenue mix:

1. **Core sandwiches / bowls / wraps** — the spine  
2. **Sides + drinks + cookies / chips** — attach that lifts check without much labor  
3. **Catering / office trays / party platters** — weekday density when walk-in softens  
4. **Delivery / marketplace** — volume that looks like sales and can behave like a tax  
5. **Loyalty / app / gift cards** (brand-dependent) — retention tool, not magic  
6. **Non-traditional sites** (airports, colleges, hospitals, C-stores) — different rent, hours, and traffic — different underwriting

Typical loop:

1. **Lead / traffic** — site visibility, drive-by habit, brand search (if any), Google Local, office lunch patterns, catering outbound, marketplace placement  
2. **Order** — counter, kiosk, app, phone, delivery aggregator  
3. **Make** — mise en place, line speed, portion control, ticket time  
4. **Collect** — cashless fees, tips (policy), delivery settlement timing  
5. **Retain** — consistent product, clean lobby, catering reorders — or lose them to the next strip door  
6. **Close / reset** — food count, waste log, prep list, labor schedule for tomorrow’s rush

**Scope lock:** this page is the **local fast-casual / QSR / sandwich unit** — labor + food cost + site + manager economics — selling prepared food to guests (and catering accounts).  
It is **not** [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (B2B wage markup + float).  
It is **not** [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (B2B exhaust compliance for the kitchens that *compete with* or *sit next to* you).  
It is **not** [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) (coffee / snack daypart specialty — different check and morning spine; same location-retail food family).  
It is **not** full-service casual dining, ghost-kitchen-only, or food-truck-only as the whole company story.  
It is **not** “passive franchise income” without a manager who can run Tuesday lunch without you.

Brian’s published corpus treats restaurants as a real **ownership door** — and as a **warning door**. Ownership-frame and calendar teaching put **restaurants** next to RE / SMB buy / startups / passive as something to *compare* for control, hours, and risk — including a “food hours trap” vs service leverage. CORE model teaching places **food** under **location-based retail**: site selection, lease, buildout, hours, local awareness, manager/team, scale by adding boxes. His buyer checklist is blunt: people **won’t go out of their way for a sandwich**; A+ vs B site can decide the box. His Jersey Mike’s YouTube uses a founder / PE-sale story to teach **systems, simplicity, and selective operators** — including a failed second location before franchising, systemwide AUV education near **~$1.3M/store** in that video’s framing, and **12-week** franchisee training as a systems tell — **category proof**, not a recommendation. Concept notes on “1-star” franchises (franchisee *financial* stress, not guest stars) call out **Subway density / cannibalization** and **Jimmy John’s–class thin sandwich margins + royalties**. Fast-food shorts drafts walk an illustrative P&L shape (~**30%** food, ~**32%** payroll, ~**8%** royalty+marketing, ~**16%** overhead). Treat named systems (Subway, Jersey Mike’s, Firehouse, indie fast casual) as **proof the category professionalizes** — not as a pitch.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed tickets** (and catering invoices) — not “we’re open” and not a brand logo on a napkin as the whole P&L.

Education pricing / check shape (national operator ranges; metros and protein-heavy menus sit higher; commodity sub shops sit tighter):

- Sandwich / fast-casual average checks commonly discussed roughly **~$12–$18+** walk-in; catering tickets are a different animal  
- Volume wins when **ticket time** stays short and **portioning** stays honest  
- Delivery platforms can lift *sales* while cutting *contribution* — underwrite net after commission, packaging, and remakes  
- Franchise / brand shops often live on **AUV × controllable costs**; indies live on **local habit + owner presence** without a national ad fund — different marketing tax, same food/labor physics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Transactions × average check** — empty lobby with a beautiful buildout still burns rent  
2. **Food & paper %** — waste, theft, over-portioning, commodity spikes  
3. **Labor %** — schedule discipline, overtime, GM wage, no-show coverage  
4. **Occupancy** — rent + CAM + utilities as % of sales (site quality vs rent greed)  
5. **Royalty + brand / marketing funds (if franchised)** — paid on **gross**, not on what’s left  
6. **Delivery mix** — commission + packaging + “we remade it” leakage  
7. **Catering attach** — weekday ballast when walk-in softens  
8. **Manager quality** — the silent line item that decides whether *you* own a company or a second job

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- **Prime cost** (food + labor) is the career-defining pair — sandwich / QSR education often puts food roughly **~28–32%** and labor roughly **~25–32%** of sales depending on wage market and owner-operator hours; combined prime often discussed in a **~55–65%** band when healthy  
- Brian’s Fast Food Shorts education framing walks ~**30%** food / ~**32%** payroll as a memorable QSR shape — illustrative shorts math, not your Item 19  
- Ongoing franchise fees in sandwich systems are commonly discussed in a combined royalty + ad band roughly **~9–12.5%+ of gross** depending on brand/year — **verify Item 6**  
- Net on a **single** unit is often a **tighter** owner-income story than “the brand did billions systemwide” headlines — multi-unit + real GMs is how many operators chase a fuller living

You’re not a sandwich with a logo.  
You’re a **prime-cost + site + manager** company wearing lunch rush.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People still eat away from home. Offices still need trays. Road trips still need a bathroom and a footlong. Wage pressure and delivery apps change the *math*; they don’t erase the *habit*.

Good times → more catering, higher check, willingness to pay for “fresh / fast.”  
Tight times → guests trade down, skip drinks, hunt coupons; delivery still shows up with a commission; food inflation and wage floors don’t ask permission.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) rides **wage markup + B2B sales + payroll float + WC**.  
[Commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) rides **NFPA-driven night contracts** for the kitchens next door.  
QSR / sandwich rides **tickets × check − food − labor − rent − fees − delivery** — **location retail** with a perishable inventory and a GM.

Same “someone needs something done today” demand emotion. Different invoice, different lawsuit, different Saturday.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “I made $200k on sandwiches” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one unit

A single sandwich / fast-casual box lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Labor (the career-defining line)**  
Crew that shows up, can run a rush, and doesn’t bleed overtime. Scheduling is the product. Brian’s shorts framing puts payroll near **~32%** in a memorable QSR example; industry education for sandwich often sits roughly **~25–32%** depending on market and whether the owner is unpaid labor on the line. One no-show opener on a Monday is a Google review and a dead lunch. Same dual-market pain as [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) — you sell guests *and* sell a job to people who have other options — without a bill/pay spread to hide behind.

**Food cost & waste**  
Portion cups, slicer calibration, prep lists, theft, spoilage, “just make it again” delivery remakes. Education targets often live roughly **~28–32%** food & paper. A point of food cost on a million-dollar box is real money. Commodity spikes (protein, cheese, oils) don’t wait for your royalty statement to feel fair.

**Site selection & rent**  
Brian’s retail checklist: people **won’t go out of their way for a sandwich**. Visibility, ingress/egress, parking at noon, office density, school/hospital adjacency, and whether you’re the *convenient* door or the *extra turn*. His Little Caesars anecdote (pickup traffic dying without 5 p.m. parking / egress) is the same lesson in a different category. Wrong rent on a B site is how AUV fantasies die. Lease personal guarantees turn soft Tuesday into a family problem.

**Manager dependency**  
If only *you* can open, close, schedule, order food, and calm a catering account, you bought a job with mayo. Multi-unit starts when a GM can protect food % and labor % without you in the building. Key-person GM walk-off is not “one FTE” — it’s your life returning to the slicer.

**Franchise fees & brand drag (if franchised)**  
Royalties and ad funds on **gross** arrive whether food cost spiked or DoorDash took a cut. Fees help if they buy training, supply chain, marketing that raises net *after* the fee — not because the logo photographs well. Brian’s Jersey Mike’s teaching emphasizes **systems + selective operators** and long training as a quality filter; density-heavy systems (Subway called out in his 1-star franchisee-performance concept notes) can create **cannibalization** where two logos split one trade area while rent and royalties don’t shrink.

**Delivery platforms**  
Volume vanity vs contribution reality. Commissions, packaging, remakes, review risk when a bag sits on a porch. Underwrite **mix %** and net after fees — or watch “growth” hollow out the P&L.

**Dayparts & catering**  
Lunch is often the spine. Dinner, late night, and weekends depend on trade area. Catering is how many boxes thicken Wednesday when walk-in is soft — and how food prep mistakes become a 40-person disappointment.

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many units is **owner on the floor**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until labor %, food %, and a GM scorecard prove it. Family-flexibility buyers: read the hours honestly before the grand opening balloon arch.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD Item 19 writeups, Brian’s published AUV / systems teaching, shorts P&L frames, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing weekly sales, food %, labor %, rent, delivery mix, and whether a real GM exists.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Brian’s Jersey Mike’s video education: systemwide sales framing and **~$1.3M average revenue per store** in that teaching — *system average, not your draw*  
- Broader sandwich-chain education often shows a **wide AUV band** (commodity density brands can sit far lower; premium sandwich concepts higher) — verify *current* Item 19 for any brand you diligence  
- Subway traditional Item 7 education bands from public FDD summaries in Brian’s Drive inventory: roughly **~$263k–$630k** initial investment (non-traditional often lower) — **verify the actual Item 7 year**  
- Multi-unit Subway resales appear in FranBizNetwork-style CIMs in that inventory (six-figure asking prices for clusters) — **price ≠ quality**; underwrite leases, AUV by store, and manager depth  
- Single-unit owner cash after debt, GM wage, and fees is often **much tighter** than systemwide “billions” headlines

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Brian’s retail-location teaching: many retail franchises discussed roughly **~$300k–$600k** per location (some concepts much higher) — food can sit in or above that band depending on brand and buildout  
- Lean indie strip shop: lower logo cost, higher marketing and systems burden; hood / grease trap / health permits still real  
- Budget **working capital** for soft opening, training wages, food spoil on ramp, and the months before catering densifies  
- Resales: buy the P&L and the lease, not the lobby paint

**P&L shape education (illustrative — not a promise):**

| Line (education shape) | Common operator chatter |
|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Food & paper | ~28–32% of sales |
| Labor (incl. payroll tax; GM) | ~25–32% (shorts framing sometimes ~32%) |
| Occupancy | often discussed ~8–12%+ (site-dependent) |
| Royalty + ad (if franchised) | often ~9–12.5%+ of *gross* |
| Delivery commissions | mix-dependent; can erase contribution on those tickets |
| Controllable “what’s left” | thin if prime + fees + rent stack without volume |

Brian-adjacent notes (for stack context, not your store forecast):

- Jersey Mike’s YT: **simplicity scales** (limited toppings / breads), **12-week training**, failed second company store before franchising, selective admissions / employee-path franchisees as culture tells  
- 1-star franchisee-performance concept: Subway **over-density**; sandwich category **thin margins + royalties**  
- Fast Food Shorts: memorable % waterfall — use as teaching shape, not underwriting  
- CORE 04: food = **location retail** life (lease, hours, manager, multi-box scale)  
- No Brian YouTube **full unit-econ deep-dive for owning a random Subway** as a recommendation turned up — don’t invent one; use FDD + local P&Ls

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **weekly sales** (24 months) by daypart — how ugly is January?  
- **Food %** and **labor %** by week — who owns the variance?  
- Delivery / marketplace **mix %** and net after commission?  
- Catering volume and who sells it?  
- Rent + CAM + % of sales; lease term; personal guarantee; assignment / franchisor approval?  
- GM tenure, wage, bonus on food/labor; who opens when they quit?  
- Crew turnover; wage reality in *this* metro; overtime habits?  
- If franchised: royalty + ad + tech — what do they actually buy? Territory / encroachment history? Item 20 transfers and closures?  
- Health / hood / grease compliance cadence — who pays when the fire marshal cares ([commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) is the vendor side of that world)?  
- Owner hours today vs “semi-absentee” claim?

Same brain you’d use buying any location-retail box: site, labor, controllable costs, and whether *you* want lunch rush forever.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Labor cost & show-up culture (the silent killer)**  
Wages rose. Guests still want $12 lunches. Scheduling errors, overtime, and no-shows erase “good sales weeks.” You’re always recruiting — often the same dual-market stress as [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting), paid in tickets instead of bill rates. Owner-on-the-line “savings” is unpaid labor with a back injury waiting.

**2. Food cost, waste & portion drift**  
A generous scoop feels like hospitality until it is a point of COGS. Theft, spoilage, remakes, and “just throw it away” culture compound. Commodity spikes hit before you can raise menu prices without losing the office crowd.

**3. Site selection & rent / real estate**  
Hyper-local demand. Bad parking, weak lunch density, or a rent that assumes AUV you don’t have. Personal guarantees. Franchisor site approval that still leaves *you* holding the lease. Brian’s teaching: sandwich traffic doesn’t travel — the site *is* the strategy.

**4. Manager dependency**  
The model scales on GMs who protect food %, labor %, and guest experience. One hero GM leaving returns you to opener shifts. “Semi-absentee” without a bench is a LinkedIn lie.

**5. Franchise fees on gross (if franchised)**  
Royalty + ad fund don’t care that DoorDash took 25% of that ticket or that cheese went up. High fee burden only works with volume and discipline. A national logo does not schedule your closers or negotiate your CAM.

**6. Delivery platform leakage**  
Marketplaces sell convenience to guests and a commission to you. Packaging, remakes, cold-food reviews, and settlement timing are real. Growing delivery mix without raising prices or cutting other costs is how busy stores stay broke.

**7. Cannibalization / density (brand-dependent)**  
Too many same-logo doors in one trade area split the lunch pie; rent and royalties don’t split with it. Brian’s Subway caution in franchisee-performance concept notes is about *this* physics — category diligence, not a brand roast for sport.

**8. Thin sandwich margins as a category**  
Brian’s Jimmy John’s–class note in those same concept materials: competing against Subway / Jersey Mike’s / Firehouse / every local deli with thin sandwich margins *before* royalties. Indie delis without fees still face food + labor + rent. The category is not “easy food money.”

**9. Daypart concentration**  
Lunch prints; evenings and weekends may not. Catering soft weeks and school holidays show up in the bank account. Underwrite the calendar, not the Saturday soft-open line.

**10. Buildout, permits & equipment**  
Hoods, grease traps, health department, fire suppression, slicers, POS. Delays burn rent before the first ticket. CapEx surprises are how “Item 7 midpoints” become personal loans.

**11. Training time vs turnover**  
Brian highlights long franchisee training (Jersey Mike’s **12-week** education example) as a systems strength — and crew turnover still resets the line every month. Training cost is recurring, not a one-time orientation.

**12. Quality variance across shifts**  
Rush vs dead hour; opener vs closer; weekday vs Saturday crew. Guests don’t care which shift “usually” portions correctly. Reviews are inventory.

**13. Cash & working-capital traps**  
Sales look fine while food invoices, payroll, and sales tax come due. Delivery settlement lag. Soft opening burn. Owners who spend week-one sales like salary discover week-four invoices.

**14. Multi-unit pressure without a replacement engine**  
Second box before the first has a real GM is how you own two jobs. Brian’s Jersey Mike’s story includes a **failed second location** before the founder chose franchising / local owners — a humility beat, not a trivia fact.

**15. Owner-as-hero closer**  
If only you can run the rush, only you can order food, and only you can sell catering, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a lunch-shift career with a lease and a logo.

**16. Confusing “people always eat” headlines with “your collectible net after food, labor, rent, fees, and delivery”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Tuesday labor % and your CAM bill.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means fast casual / QSR / sandwich is a **location-retail food** business where empty shifts, drifting portions, a B site, a missing GM, fee drag, and delivery mix decide whether the box survives the next wage hike.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can live inside **lunch rush + nights/weekends** until a real GM owns the floor — or you’ve already capitalized a manager-led plan with proof  
- Food % and labor % dashboards energize you more than they nauseate you  
- Site walks, lease redlines, and parking counts feel like diligence, not boredom  
- Recruiting and retaining hourly crews (and a GM) is a job you’re willing to own  
- You’ve compared this door to [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (B2B labor float), [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (B2B restaurant compliance), and lighter truck/route or home-service models — and still want the food box  
- You’re honest that restaurants are an **ownership door with an hours tax** (Brian’s frame) — not passive income with pickles

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted passive “everybody eats lunch” income from month one  
- You’re allergic to hourly turnover, food waste conversations, or Saturday opens  
- You’re undercapitalized for buildout + working capital and assumed the franchise brochure erased soft months  
- You actually want a **service business with leverage**, a **staffing agency**, or a **B2B restaurant vendor** play — different underwriting  
- You thought delivery apps were free volume without contribution math  
- You need the trendiest vertical more than timeless mise en place + schedule discipline in *your* trade area

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: tickets, food, labor, site, manager. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens training, supplies chain consistency, and improves guest acquisition — not because a PE headline made the category look easy.

Many operators will correctly choose a **smoother-hours** service vehicle or a non-food door first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Labor spike / no-shows → rush failure + review spiral  
- Food cost drift / commodity spike → margin wipeout  
- Wrong site / rent too high → AUV never arrives  
- GM walk-off → owner returns to the line  
- Royalty + ad on gross while delivery mix rises → busy-broke  
- Cannibalization / encroachment (brand-dependent)  
- Lease personal guarantee on a soft P&L  
- Health / fire / hood compliance event → shutdown days  
- Catering mistake → account loss  
- Buildout delay / CapEx overrun → capital stress before opening  
- Multi-unit fantasy funded by one soft box  
- Buying a “book” that was one hero GM + one A lunch corridor + one tired lease  
- Confusing systemwide sales headlines with collectible net after prime cost and fees

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everybody has to eat.”

You’re buying the right to run a **throughput food box** — if you can protect **food and labor**, pick a site guests will actually use, install a **manager** who owns the shift, treat royalties and delivery commissions as real taxes on gross, build catering without drowning prep, and not confuse a lunch line for a twelve-month salary with Saturdays off.

Some people should buy a tired unit with real sales and fix food %, labor %, and GM process (resale path — diligence still required; Subway-cluster CIMs in Brian’s Drive inventory prove the *marketplace* exists, not that any CIM is a deal).  
Some people should open one box, prove a manager, and only then add a second site.  
Some people should choose [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting), [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning), or a truck/route service business and never take on lunch rush plus a grease trap.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people buy sandwiches?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful food job with a strip lease, a labor spreadsheet, a delivery tablet, and a personal guarantee when your only GM texts “I’m done” and the lunch line is already forming?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other food-hospitality vehicles vs business-services or home-service paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
