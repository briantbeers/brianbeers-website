Saturday night.  
Four friends want a *story they finish together* — not another dinner they forget, not a movie they half-watch, not “we’ll just do a board game at someone’s apartment.”

Corporate HR still needs a Tuesday team-builder that isn’t Zoom icebreakers or another ropes course.  
Birthday groups still want a private room buyout that looks good on Instagram.  
Tourists still Google “things to do near me” and pick the puzzle that isn’t axe-throwing or [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint).

Someone needs a **facility of timed puzzle rooms**: CapEx buildout that survives kids and corporate shoes, a booking calendar that turns rooms on the clock, game masters who can hint without spoiling, a **refresh cycle** before TripAdvisor ages out, and a marketing machine that replaces one-and-done players at a CAC the ticket can repay.

That’s an **escape rooms** business.

Not [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) (easels + instructor + optional wine — shared *experience-night + facility fixed cost*; different game design, different reset, alcohol optional).  
Not [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (you don’t haul vinyl to their yard; they come to *your* rooms for a ticketed slot — same *celebration / weekend spike* emotion, different plant).  
Not [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) (recurring class packs + birthday attach — shared party demand and rent; different enrichment spine).  
Not [mobile-gaming-trucks](/models/mobile-gaming-trucks) (when published — experiential entertainment that rolls; different CapEx and throughput).  
Not a basement DIY “locks and envelopes” hobby with a Facebook page (related idea, different ceiling, different liability and fire-marshal story).  
Not VR arcade / axe-throwing / rage room as the whole company (adjacent experiential night-out — different CapEx and lawsuit feel).

You’re running a **local immersive entertainment / puzzle-room operator**: leased (or owned) square footage divided into themed rooms + lobby + reset/storage, staffed by game masters, sold in timed slots online, with corporate and party packages supposed to blunt **weekday softness** — and a capital plan that assumes rooms go stale.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **CapEx buildout**, **throughput / utilization per room**, **room refresh cycles**, **weekday softness**, **marketing CAC**, **game-master staffing**, **liability / life-safety**, and **IP / theme longevity** show up before the lobby looks like a brand Reel of screaming groups escaping on the buzzer.

The Escape Game / Breakout Games / Escape the Room–class systems and indie neighborhood puzzle venues sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The filled slots, reset discipline, refresh reserve, and CAC that doesn’t eat contribution are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local escape-room / immersive puzzle venue operator**: a set of themed rooms where groups of typically **~4–8 players** solve puzzles against a clock (often ~60 minutes of play + brief + reset), monitored by game masters, booked mostly online, with optional party and corporate packages.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — this is the CapEx + throughput fork):**

| | **Multi-room facility venue (primary spine for this page)** | **Mobile / pop-up / single-room adjacency** |
--|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Timed tickets across several permanent rooms; private buyouts; corporate packages | Games that travel *or* one thin room in someone else’s footprint |
| **Primary constraint** | **Paid room-slots × ticket − GM labor − rent − marketing − refresh CapEx** | Same slot math with **weaker room density** and travel / temp-site friction |
| **Labor feel** | Game masters + front desk; Fri–Sun peaks; Tue–Thu corporate hunt | Leaner GM bench; logistics tax if mobile |
| **CapEx feel** | Heavy: TI, soundproofing, props, electronics, cameras, ADA / life-safety; multi-room often mid–high six figures+ | Leaner per game; truck / trailer / partner-site costs if mobile |
| **Cash shape** | Weekend public spikes + gift cards + corporate deposits; soft weekdays unless pipeline works | Same calendar problem; harder to stack simultaneous slots |

Many real venues sit in a hybrid — 3–6 rooms *plus* private parties *plus* thin corporate *plus* merch / F&B *plus* occasional mobile offsite. Underwrite the mix honestly — a soft weekday book with a loud Saturday Instagram is a different company than a dense corporate pipeline with public nights as brand frosting.

Typical menu:

1. **Public timed tickets** — the awareness spine (standard / premium / horror / family-friendly dayparts)  
2. **Private room buyouts / birthday / bachelor(ette)** — higher ticket or room minimums; deposit discipline  
3. **Corporate / team-building / nonprofit** — weekday fill *or* the sales fantasy that never closes  
4. **Gift cards / vouchers** — cash today, undelivered slot liability tomorrow  
5. **Merchandise / photos / F&B / bar attach** (if any) — attach; license + labor tax if alcohol  
6. **Premium / VIP / multi-room packages** — yield management when peak slots sell out  
7. **Offsite / mobile / pop-up** — pipeline gold *or* logistics and safety tax  
8. **Add-on experiential** (axe, VR, arcade — scope note) — different CapEx factory sharing a lobby  
9. **Venue rental without games** (scope note) — don’t confuse with the ticket spine

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google Local / Maps, Meta “things to do,” TripAdvisor / Google reviews, Groupon-class deals (carefully), bridal / corporate outreach, franchise lead systems (if any), UGC escape photos  
2. **Book** — online slot; often **full prepay** or heavy deposit; waiver / age / medical / claustrophobia policy clear  
3. **Staff the slot** — game master who can brief, monitor cameras/mics, hint without spoiling, and reset fast  
4. **Stage the room** — props placed, locks set, electronics armed, cameras live, emergency egress checked  
5. **Deliver** — ~60 minutes of play + photo / debrief; upsell next room / gift card / corporate  
6. **Reset** — the unpaid factory between slots; broken prop = dead capacity  
7. **Replace / refresh** — because **one-and-done local markets**, spoiler blogs, aging reviews, and theme fatigue never sleep

## How money comes in

You sell **paid room-slots on a clock** (and private/corporate blocks) — not “people love puzzles” and not a Reel about passive Saturday cash.

Education price / slot bands (national consumer ranges; metros sit higher; secondary markets sit lower; corporate packages change yield):

- **Public tickets** commonly discussed roughly **~$30–$45+/person**; premium / urban / horror lists often climb into the **high $40s–$50s+**  
- **Average group** education often **~4–6 players**; private buyouts price for the *room*, not the lonely duo  
- **Corporate / team-building** education often **~$500–$2,500+** per booking (or **~$40–$60+/person** packages) — *if* you can sell and staff them  
- **Per-room annual revenue** education chatter often **~$50k–$150k+** depending on utilization, market, and ticket — *shape*, not your forecast  
- Indie multi-room venues: follow local ticket × booked slots × group size; don’t invent national AUV  
- Franchise Item 7 / Item 19: Escapology–class secondary education often cites total investment roughly **~$626k–$2.3M+** (fee often **~$45k**; royalty / brand / tech / local advertising stacks can be **heavy** — verify current FDD). Brian sheet brands (The Escape Game / Breakout Games / Escape the Room) are **category proof**; underwrite *their* current disclosure (or company-owned economics) separately — don’t paste Escapology math onto every logo

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Booked room-slots per week** — drafts clearing *this week*, not “followers who liked the escape Reel”  
2. **Average ticket × group size** — yield management on peak vs off-peak  
3. **Utilization / throughput per room** — the hotel RevPAR cousin: revenue per available room-hour (RevPARH-style) beats vanity “sold out Saturday” screenshots  
4. **Private-party and corporate mix** — the weekday-softness antidote *or* the fantasy pipeline  
5. **Game-master coverage** — who can run Fri 7 p.m. *and* Tue corporate without canceling, and **reset** between slots  
6. **Marketing CAC** — Google/Meta/OTA cost to acquire a first booking vs repeat / gift-card / referral rate  
7. **Room uptime** — broken electronics and slow resets are capacity death  
8. **Refresh / reroom CapEx** — sustaining capital, not a surprise in year three  
9. **Deal / coupon addiction** — Groupon-class seats that train discount habits and burn GM nights

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Ticket gross can look healthy after consumables — then **GM labor + rent + marketing + royalties + refresh reserves** eat the dream  
- Labor is often discussed as a **hard % of revenue** (secondary guides often watch GM + floor near **~28–40%** bands depending on wage market and automation) — owner-as-only-GM masks true labor  
- Occupancy / rent on strip / lifestyle / industrial-flex venues is fixed whether January Tuesday is empty  
- Marketing for new venues is often discussed as a **meaningful % of early gross** (launch education commonly **~10–15%**; mature operators try to compress with reviews and corporate) — CAC without LTV is a treadmill  
- Refresh / sustaining CapEx education often **~8–12% of room build cost annually** *or* a lumpy full reroom every **~12–30 months** — operators who skip the reserve enter the stale-room death spiral  
- Royalty + brand + tech + required marketing on branded systems can stack into **high-single to teens %+** of gross on some models (e.g. Escapology–class education often cites **~6% royalty + ~2% innovation + local ad minimums + tech fees** — verify current Item 6)  
- Gift cards / prepaid vouchers: cash today, undelivered slot liability tomorrow  
- Net on a single unit is often a **tighter** band than “every Saturday is sold out forever” lobby myths — volume rises; GM wages, rent, ads, and refresh CapEx rise with it

You’re not a puzzle influencer with a lease.  
You’re a **room-slot yield + CapEx plant + GM roster + acquisition machine** company wearing themes and cameras.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Adults still buy **experiences over things** for birthdays, dates, and team culture. Social media still turns a “we escaped with 4:12 left” photo into free marketing when the night lands. Dual-income households still outsource “we should do something fun” to a calendar widget. Corporate budgets still fund team-building that feels lighter than another offsite with flip charts.

Good times → premium themes, multi-room packages, corporate density, gift-card seasons, willingness to pay for private buyouts.  
Tight times → experiential is often among the **first discretionary cancels** after the nice dinner dies — *unless* the private party is already prepaid and the HR budget is already approved. Underwrite which buyer you actually have: public drop-in romance vs deposited private vs corporate PO.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) rides **event seats + instructor quality + optional wine** on a facility that peaks Fri–Sat.  
[Party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) rides **weekend event-day utilization** on assets that leave the building.  
[Kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) rides **class fill + birthday attach** on a kids enrichment lease.  
Escape rooms ride **ticketed room-slots + reset labor + CapEx that must refresh** — on rooms that must **turn on the clock** and markets that **spoil**.

Same “pay for a night we won’t forget” emotion. Different plant, different lawsuit story, different empty Tuesday.

That’s the engine. Not one viral escape Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one venue

A single escape-room venue lives or dies on a few boring things:

**CapEx buildout (the career-defining balance sheet)**  
Walls, soundproofing, HVAC for dense occupancy, cameras/mics, locks/electronics, custom props, lobby, ADA paths, fire egress that fire marshals actually accept (players are **not** truly locked in — quick-release / emergency exit is product and law). Per-room build education often **~$15k–$60k+** for serious indie rooms (props + electronics); full venue TI + multi-room packages commonly land **~$135k–$365k+** for lean 3-room education bands — and **franchise Item 7** for premium systems can climb into **mid-six to low-seven figures**. The trap: Instagram lobby cosplay before working capital and a refresh reserve exist. Racing Grand Opening before Certificate of Occupancy / fire approval is how amateurs burn capital.

**Throughput / utilization per room (the real KPI)**  
A room that sits empty is stranded CapEx — cousin to [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) vinyl in the trailer and [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) empty easels on Tuesday. Education planning zones often discuss **booked-slot rates** roughly **~55–65%** as a workable base when price and group size are healthy; **<40%** is fragile; weekend peaks can look like **70–90%** while the month average is much uglier. Track **revenue per available room-hour**, average group size, and win/completion rates (often discussed ~**30–50%** escape rates as a design band — too easy kills challenge; too hard kills reviews). Max theoretical starts/day (often **~6–10+** depending on slot length + reset) is not your forecast — utilization is.

**Room refresh cycle (sustaining CapEx nobody budgets)**  
Local markets spoil. Spoiler blogs, repeat locals, and aging TripAdvisor photos kill fourth-year demand. Operator education often cites **full theme refresh / replacement every ~12–30 months**, with minor mid-life refreshes (**~$5k–$15k**) vs full rerooms (**~$25k–$80k+**). Annual sustaining CapEx education often **~8–12% of initial room build**. Treat refresh as **customer retention and review-velocity product**, not a vanity remodel. Operators who skip the reserve enter the stale-room death spiral with no cash to rebuild.

**Weekday softness (the silent rent tax)**  
Public demand piles onto **Friday night–Sunday**. Tuesday–Thursday without a private/corporate machine is how rent looks like a boat payment with puzzles. Family-flexibility buyers: read this as **your weekends are inventory** — cousin to [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) Saturday concentration and [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) weekday softness, different plant. If you needed every Friday forever for your own life, underwrite GM coverage *and* a real weekday pipeline before you sign the lease.

**Marketing CAC (the one-and-done tax)**  
Many guests come once per room and never return until you refresh. Google Local, Meta, TripAdvisor, OTAs, and coupon marketplaces fill slots — and train price sensitivity. Education chatter often aims for **CAC well under contribution per first booking** (some guides cite **<$15/booking** as a launch target — *aspirational*; your metro’s CPC and conversion will argue). Measure retained full-price, private conversion, and corporate pipeline — not just headcount on a $22 deal night that barely clears GM labor. Digital ads often dominate new acquisition; reviews and referrals are the only durable CAC compression.

**Game-master staffing (people are the product)**  
GMs brief, monitor, hint, reset, and de-escalate. A star GM who can hold a corporate group and a tipsy birthday without spoiling converts gift cards and reviews. When that GM leaves — or the bench is thin at peak — resets slip, slots cancel, and Google reviews wobble. Owner-as-hero GM looks great on Instagram and terrible on a succession plan. Schedule to **demand by daypart**, not fixed overstaffed shifts that destroy labor %.

**Liability / life-safety**  
Claustrophobia, slips, prop injuries, panic, accessibility complaints, and “we were locked in” myths. Waivers, cameras, emergency egress, fire alarms/sprinklers, ADA paths, staff training, and **$1M+** GL education bands are operating costs — not paranoia. One severity claim or fire-marshal shutdown dwarfs a season of pretty Saturdays.

**IP / theme longevity**  
Licensed IP and recognizable themes sell tickets and attract license fees / cease-and-desist energy. Original themes need marketing muscle. Franchise systems often constrain themes for a reason. Indie freelancing “tonight we escape the mouse” is a legal hobby you didn’t budget. Theme longevity is both creative and legal — underwrite both.

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many venues is **owner GMs + books corporate + resets rooms + answers “can we bring six kids under eight?” texts**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until a GM bench, a GM manager, and a booking SOP exist. Existing-owner-scale buyers: the bottleneck is usually **peak-slot GM coverage + weekday pipeline + refresh capital**, not another Meta ad.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD secondary writeups, consumer ticket pages, and “owner income per escape room” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing calendars by room and daypart, corporate mix, GM schedules, lease abstracts, refresh CapEx history, marketing spend by channel, and insurance / incident logs.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Booked-slot rate education: fragile **<~40%**; base planning often **~55–65%**; peak weekends can look much higher — underwrite the *month*, not the Instagram Saturday  
- Starts per room per day: theoretical often **~6–10+** depending on game + reset length; utilized starts are what pay rent  
- Ticket shape: many consumer tickets **~$30–$45+/person**; corporate packages climb

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. CapEx buildout & code reality (the silent capital tax)**  
TI, soundproofing, electronics, props, cameras, ADA, and fire egress eat cash before the first ticket. Item 7 / blog “startup” bands are **shapes**, not your city’s electrician and fire marshal. Overbuilding a glam lobby for Instagram is how working capital dies. Underbuilding cheap consumer props that fail on night three is also how capacity dies. Same class of honesty as facility TI on [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) — different puzzles.

**2. Throughput / utilization death (rooms that don’t turn)**  
Empty slots are stranded CapEx. Peak Saturday Instagram hides a soft month. Discounting to fill utilization can destroy RevPARH while the dashboard looks “busy.” Cull or refresh underperforming themes; don’t protect ego rooms.

**3. Room refresh cycle (the year-three cliff)**  
Spoilers, local saturation, and aging reviews kill demand. Without a **refresh reserve** and a reroom calendar, fourth-year revenue can fall hard while rent and GM wages don’t. Minor refresh vs full replacement is a diligence fork — underwrite which rooms are on which clock.

**4. Weekday softness (the silent rent tax)**  
Demand piles onto weekends. Your “fun lifestyle business” may be a **non-negotiable Friday–Saturday factory** with soft Tue–Thu unless private/corporate is real. Hiring GMs for peaks doesn’t fill empty rooms midweek. Family calendars collide with birthday calendars — underwrite that first. Same honesty as [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) and [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals).

**5. Marketing CAC & one-and-done churn**  
Guests often clear one room and leave. Without gift cards, multi-room packages, email/SMS, corporate accounts, and scheduled refreshes, you are always hunting new strangers. Google/Meta CPC doesn’t care that your theme is clever. Deal sites fill rooms and train $22 habits. CAC without LTV is a treadmill.

**6. Game-master staffing & reset discipline**  
GMs *are* the product and the bottleneck. Thin benches cancel peak slots. Slow resets destroy theoretical throughput. Star-GM walk-off with “their” corporate relationships hurts. Gig calendars and day jobs fight your Friday 8 p.m. Owner forever on cameras is not semi-absentee.

**7. Liability, life-safety & insurance**  
Panic, injury, accessibility, fire, and “locked in” myths. Waivers without training are theater. Fire marshals, sprinklers, egress hardware, and GL limits are product. One claim or shutdown dwarfs a year of pretty escapes.

**8. IP / theme longevity & creative fatigue**  
Licensed themes cost money and constrain ops. Original themes need acquisition muscle. Themes go stale. Franchise brand rules help *or* trap you. Copying movie IP without a license is a legal hobby. Creative excellence without a refresh budget is still a CapEx problem.

**9. Competition density & experiential substitution**  
Other escape venues, [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint), axe-throwing, VR, bowling, [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) birthday rooms, [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) yards, and the next “things to do” fad three miles over. Differentiating on “we have locks and a timer too” is not a moat.

**10. Deposit / gift-card liability**  
Prepaid tickets and gift cards feel like revenue and aren’t fully earned. Owners who spend December gift-card cash like salary discover March redemptions and empty working capital. Same deposit honesty as [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) and [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint).

**11. Seasonality & event-calendar cliffs**  
Holiday / birthday / tourist seasons lift. January, late summer in some markets, and bad weather softens. Fixed rent and a venue manager don’t care. Corporate fiscal calendars create their own feast/famine.

**12. Labor cost & classification**  
GM wages, payroll taxes, employee vs contractor games, minors as helpers, split shifts. “We’re a fun escape family” doesn’t impress wage-hour enforcement. Owner GM hours can mask a labor problem until you get sick.

**13. Technology / prop failure at the worst minute**  
Sensors, locks, AV, and custom electronics fail when the room is full. Spare parts, GM troubleshooting, and maintenance reserves are ops — not optional. Downtime is refunded capacity.

**14. Key-person / owner-as-hero GM**  
Lose the person who knows every reset, every corporate script, every camera quirk, and the booking admin login and the “semi-absentee” story collapses into your Friday nights.

**15. Royalty / brand / tech fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, licensed-game per-player fees, tech fees, and local ad minimums help only if they fill slots and shorten GM ramp. A logo does not recruit your Friday GM or fund your year-three reroom.

**16. Noise, neighbors, parking, and landlord politics**  
Screaming groups, late exits, parking fights at 9:40 p.m., industrial neighbors who hate bass. Written house rules and landlord relationships are product.

**17. Multi-unit fantasy before one-unit mastery**  
Roll-up stories exist. They’re also how people buy a second lease while the first still needs them every reset and every refresh.

**18. Confusing “experiences are booming” headlines with “your collectible net after CapEx, utilization, weekday softness, CAC, GM labor, liability, and refresh”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your empty Tuesday and your CAM invoice.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means escape rooms are a **room-slot yield + CapEx plant + GM roster + acquisition** business where empty weeknights, soft utilization, rising CAC, deferred refresh CapEx, and life-safety gravity decide whether the themes survive payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit and retain game masters *and* respect that hosts *are* the product  
- Weekday pipeline work (private/corporate outbound) energizes you more than it nauseates you  
- Utilization dashboards, RevPARH-style yield, and refresh CapEx reserves feel like diligence, not boredom  
- You’re capitalized for TI + permit lag + months of calendar ramp + **a real refresh reserve** — and sober about personal guarantees  
- You’ve compared this door to [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint), [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), and [mobile-gaming-trucks](/models/mobile-gaming-trucks) — and still want the puzzle-room stack  
- You’re honest about semi-absentee: GM bench + manager cost hits EBITDA hard at one unit; math improves at multi-unit *after* systems exist

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a passive “everyone loves escape rooms” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to Friday nights, camera monitoring, GM drama, or explaining fire-marshal delays  
- You’re undercapitalized for buildout + working capital through soft weekdays and a floated grand-opening season — and assumed “one room for $25k” erased refresh and insurance  
- You actually want **wine-paint**, a **party-rental fleet**, **kids fitness**, or **mobile gaming trucks** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll add three more rooms next quarter” was a margin patch, not new CapEx and reset complexity  
- You need the trendiest experiential fad more than a timeless puzzle product in *your* trade area  
- You wanted bar / FEC / VR arcade economics and are pretending it’s the same diligence as a leased puzzle venue

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: paid slots, rent, GM bench, acquisition CAC, refresh CapEx. A brand (The Escape Game / Breakout Games / Escape the Room / Escapology / peers) is only worth the fee if it shortens game design, booking systems, GM training, and ramp — not because the walls are branded.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** experiential door (mobile games, paint nights, other night-out concepts) or a non-facility vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “people love escape rooms.”

You’re buying the right to run a **puzzle-venue machine** — if you can fill paid room-slots on the nights that matter, treat **CapEx buildout as a utilization problem** (not free, and not a coffee-shop remodel), hold **throughput honesty** when Instagram Saturdays lie, fund a **refresh cycle** before spoilers kill year three, blunt **weekday softness** with a private/corporate pipeline that actually closes, acquire guests at a **CAC** your gift-card/repeat/refresh math can repay, staff **game masters** who can hint and reset without you on every camera, carry **liability and life-safety** without lying to yourself, protect **theme IP longevity** without legal cosplay, and not confuse prepaid tickets for free money.

Some people should buy a tired venue and fix calendar mix, GM bench, CAC discipline, and refresh age.  
Some people should open lean indie (fewer rooms, honest CapEx) and prove slot fill before a franchise fee stack.  
Some people should choose [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint), [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), or [mobile-gaming-trucks](/models/mobile-gaming-trucks) and never take on puzzle CapEx and Friday peak labor.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

Brian’s thin direct corpus here is useful as **honesty**, not as a template: the category shows up on the **250 Franchise Ideas** sheet (row **85**); the deep teaching lives in **sister models** — experience tickets + weekday softness (wine-paint), weekend event concentration (party rentals), facility + party attach (kids fitness), rolling experiential (mobile gaming trucks). Don’t invent a Brian escape-room sermon that doesn’t exist.

The question isn’t “do people like escaping rooms?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful hospitality job with financed props, a soft Tuesday, a fire-marshal clock, a Friday GM no-show, a Meta CAC that won’t quit, and a text that says “can we move the corporate to next month?” when the gift-card cash was already spent like salary, the rooms are spoiled, and the rent draft still clears?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs wine-paint / other experiential facility vehicles vs party-asset fleets vs something outside nightlife-adjacent ops), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
