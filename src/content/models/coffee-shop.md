6:12 a.m. The grinders are screaming.  
One barista called out. The drive-thru line is already wrapping the pad.  
A pastry case looks full — until you count what will still be sellable at 2 p.m. — and rent doesn’t care that the afternoon will be soft.

Somebody wants a **hot drink and a snack before work**, fast, consistent, without leaving the car if they can help it — and a local box that can still cover labor and the lease when Wednesday rain kills the morning and Saturday brunch never quite replaces weekday commute.

That’s a **coffee / snack shop**.

Not a full-service cafe with a wine list and a third-place lounge as the whole company story.  
Not [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (same *location-retail food* physics — you sell *lunch tickets and sandwiches*, not *morning beverages + pastry attach*).  
Not [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (same *restaurant door* — you sell *night compliance contracts to kitchens*, not *cups to guests*).  
Not “I’ll open a cute specialty shop and vibe my way to freedom” as the whole *daypart + labor + rent* story.

You’re running a **morning throughput machine**: tickets × average check − **beverage & food cost** − **crew labor** − occupancy − royalties/fees (if any) − waste on the pastry case − the cash hole when the **opener walk-off** turns you into the 4:45 a.m. closer, the scheduler, and the person who remakes drinks when the espresso machine hiccups mid-rush.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **dayparts**, **labor**, **site / rent**, **throughput**, **equipment**, **competition**, and **waste** show up before the model looks like “everybody buys coffee.”

Dunkin-class coffee-and-snack systems, drive-thru specialty stands (7 Brew / Scooter’s / Dutch Brothers–class as category examples), and indie specialty cafes sell overlapping physics with different checks and CapEx. The logo is optional. The morning spine and the parking lot are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local beverage / snack operator**: a fixed site (drive-thru stand, inline strip, end-cap, freestanding pad, non-traditional kiosk / C-store / airport adjacency), a menu engineered for **speed in the morning**, a crew that can open early and run a rush, espresso / brew / ice / pastry equipment that must work at 6 a.m., and — if you’re honest — a **shift lead or GM** who actually owns open-to-close when you’re not there.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Drive-thru / high-throughput coffee (primary spine for many deals)** | **Cafe / specialty sit-down (indie or brand)** |
|--|---------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Hot/iced drinks + snack attach — cars per hour, ticket time, window discipline | Higher sit time, often higher check, more seating / ambiance CapEx |
| **Primary constraint** | **Transactions × check × speed − COGS − labor − occupancy − fees** | Same equation; linger culture can kill peak throughput if you under-size labor or over-size “third place” fantasies |
| **Cash shape** | **Morning-heavy dayparts**; afternoon/evening depend on trade area + menu | Similar morning spine; evenings may thicken with students/remote workers — or stay dead |
| **Risk feel** | Opener no-shows, machine downtime, wrong site, rent vs cars/hour | Same + seating under-utilization, longer ticket times, barista craft variance |
| **Owner job early** | Hire/train openers + protect labor % + live the 5 a.m. until a real lead exists | Same — plus menu discipline so “latte art feelings” don’t kill cars or line speed |

Many real operators run **one box first**, then multi-unit. Underwrite whether *your* deal is owner-barista theater or a manager-led system with proof.

Typical menu / revenue mix:

1. **Core hot & iced beverages** — the spine (brewed, espresso, blended — brand-dependent)  
2. **Pastries / donuts / breakfast sandwiches / snacks** — attach that lifts check; also the **waste** line  
3. **Merchandise / beans / retail** (concept-dependent) — secondary  
4. **Catering / office coffee / box lunches** — weekday ballast when walk-in softens  
5. **App / loyalty / mobile order** (brand-dependent) — throughput tool and fee stack, not magic  
6. **Non-traditional sites** (airports, hospitals, campuses, C-stores) — different rent, hours, and traffic — different underwriting

Typical loop:

1. **Lead / traffic** — commute path, drive-thru visibility, pad stacking, brand search (if any), Google Local, office density, school drop-off patterns  
2. **Order** — window, counter, kiosk, app, delivery aggregator (usually a smaller mix than sandwich QSR — still real)  
3. **Make** — brew recipes, espresso calibration, ice/drink speed, pastry pull, ticket time  
4. **Collect** — cashless fees, tips (policy), loyalty discounts, delivery settlement timing  
5. **Retain** — consistent drink, clean lot, morning habit — or lose them to the next pad / gas station / indie  
6. **Close / reset** — waste log, milk/pastry count, equipment clean, labor schedule for tomorrow’s open

**Scope lock:** this page is the **local coffee / snack shop** — daypart + labor + site rent + throughput economics — selling beverages and snack attach to guests (and light catering).  
It is **not** [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (sandwich / lunch-spine QSR).  
It is **not** [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (B2B exhaust compliance for the kitchens that *compete with* or *sit next to* you).  
It is **not** [smoothie-juice](/models/smoothie-juice) (produce cost + blender throughput — different daypart and COGS spine; same location-retail beverage family).  
It is **not** full-service restaurant, roastery wholesale-only, or “Instagram third place” as the whole company story.  
It is **not** “passive franchise income” without openers who can run Tuesday 6:30 a.m. without you.

Brian’s published corpus treats restaurants as a real **ownership door** — and as a **warning door**. Ownership-frame and calendar teaching put **restaurants** next to RE / SMB buy / startups / passive as something to *compare* for control, hours, and risk — including a “food hours trap” vs service leverage. CORE model teaching places **food** under **location-based retail**: site selection, lease, buildout, hours, local awareness, manager/team, scale by adding boxes. His buyer checklist is blunt: people **won’t go out of their way for a sandwich, coffee, haircut, or tires**; A+ vs B site can decide the box. His Dunkin YouTube *concept* (“Can I Get Rich Selling Donuts and Coffee?”) treats Dunkin as **beverage-led category proof** — coffee volume over donut nostalgia, system scale, CapEx bands, and ongoing fees near **~5.9% royalty + ~5% ad** (~**11% of gross** in that teaching) plus a **no exclusive territory** diligence flag — **not** a recommendation. The 250 Franchise Ideas sheet lists Coffee & Snack Shops with **7 Brew / Scooter’s / Dutch Brothers** as category examples. Treat named systems and indie specialty peers as **proof the category professionalizes** — not as a pitch.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed tickets** (and light catering invoices) — not “we brew great coffee” and not a logo on a cup as the whole P&L.

Education pricing / check shape (national operator ranges; metros and specialty menus sit higher; gas-station adjacency sits tighter):

- Coffee / snack average checks commonly discussed roughly **~$6–$12+** for drive-thru / QSR-beverage; specialty cafes often higher on espresso + food attach  
- Volume wins when **ticket time** stays short and **attach** (pastry / sandwich / upsize) stays honest without killing waste  
- Morning dayparts often carry **half or more** of the day’s sales in many coffee boxes — underwrite the calendar, not the Saturday soft-open line  
- Franchise / brand shops often live on **AUV × controllable costs**; indies live on **local habit + owner presence** without a national ad fund — different marketing tax, same labor/site physics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Transactions × average check** — empty pad with a beautiful buildout still burns rent  
2. **Beverage & food / paper %** — milk waste, pastry spoilage, cup/lid cost, bean commodity spikes  
3. **Labor %** — early opens, overtime, opener coverage, barista skill variance  
4. **Occupancy** — rent + CAM + utilities as % of sales (site quality vs rent greed; drive-thru pads cost more and can print more)  
5. **Royalty + brand / marketing funds (if franchised)** — paid on **gross**, not on what’s left  
6. **Throughput** — cars per hour, window discipline, mobile-order integration without chaos  
7. **Daypart mix** — morning prints; afternoon/evening may not  
8. **Manager / opener quality** — the silent line item that decides whether *you* own a company or a 4:45 a.m. second job

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Beverage-led boxes often show **lower COGS** than sandwich QSR (coffee product cost is friendly; milk, cups, and pastry pull it back) — education chatter often puts food/beverage & paper roughly **~22–30%** depending on pastry mix; Dunkin-class Item 19 education in Brian’s Drive inventory sits near **~28%** COGS for single-branded units in that framing  
- **Labor** is the career-defining pair with dayparts — coffee/beverage education often puts labor roughly **~25–35%** of sales depending on wage market, tips policy, and whether FDD “direct labor” excludes taxes/benefits; Brian’s Fast Food Shorts framing (~**32%** payroll) is adjacent QSR teaching shape, not your Item 19  
- Ongoing franchise fees in major coffee systems are commonly discussed in a combined royalty + ad band roughly **~8–12%+ of gross** (Dunkin teaching ~**10.9%** royalty+ad in Brian’s concept / Drive notes) — **verify Item 6**; loyalty / tech / mandated platforms can stack on top  
- Net on a **single** unit is often a **tighter** owner-income story than “the brand sold billions of cups” headlines — multi-unit + real openers/GMs is how many operators chase a fuller living

You’re not a latte with a logo.  
You’re a **daypart + labor + site + throughput** company wearing a morning rush.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People still need caffeine before work. Commutes still create windows. Offices still want a tray of cups. Gas stations and grocery cold brew change the *competition*; they don’t erase the *habit* of a dedicated morning stop when the site is right.

Good times → more attach, higher specialty check, willingness to wait two cars for a branded drink.  
Tight times → guests trade down, skip pastry, hunt app deals; bean and dairy inflation don’t ask permission; wage floors still hit the opener shift.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) rides **lunch tickets × check − food − labor − rent − fees**.  
[Commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) rides **NFPA-driven night contracts** for the kitchens next door.  
Coffee / snack rides **morning tickets × check − COGS − labor − rent − fees − waste** — **location retail** with a perishable pastry case and an espresso machine that must work at dawn.

Same “someone needs something done today” demand emotion. Different invoice, different lawsuit, different Saturday — and a much earlier alarm.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “I made $200k on lattes” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one unit

A single coffee / snack box lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Dayparts (the career-defining shape)**  
Morning is often the spine — many coffee operators discuss **~50–65%+ of daily sales** landing in the early window (commute + school drop-off). Rain, remote-work Mondays, holidays, and summer school-out change the bank account. Afternoon and evening are *earned*, not assumed. Underwrite **weekly sales by hour**, not Instagram peak lines.

**Labor (the other career-defining line)**  
Openers who show up at 4:45 a.m., can run a rush, and don’t bleed overtime. Scheduling is the product. Barista skill variance shows up in ticket time and remakes. Same dual-market pain as sandwich QSR and [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) — you sell guests *and* sell a job to people who have other options — without a bill/pay spread to hide behind. Owner-on-the-bar “savings” is unpaid labor with a burned hand waiting.

**Site selection & rent**  
Brian’s retail checklist: people **won’t go out of their way for coffee**. Ingress/egress, stack length for drive-thru, visibility from the commute path, office density, school adjacency, and whether you’re the *convenient* stop or the *extra left turn*. His Little Caesars anecdote (pickup traffic dying without 5 p.m. parking / egress) is the same lesson in a different category. Wrong rent on a B pad is how AUV fantasies die. Lease personal guarantees turn soft Tuesdays into a family problem. Drive-thru pads can print more *and* cost more — underwrite cars/hour vs CAM, not the rendering.

**Throughput**  
Cars per hour, drink complexity, mobile-order integration, window coaching. A beautiful specialty menu that adds 45 seconds per ticket can erase the A site. Speed is a product feature, not a personality trait.

**Equipment reliability & CapEx**  
Espresso machines, grinders, ice, cold brew, ovens, POS. Downtime at 7:10 a.m. is not “call the tech tomorrow.” CapEx and maintenance are recurring. Brian’s Dunkin concept notes flag **equipment/signage and building costs** as the big-ticket CapEx reality next to the franchise fee — category teaching, not a brochure.

**Food / pastry waste & COGS drift**  
Milk foam waste, pastry pull that doesn’t sell by 2 p.m., over-portioned cold foam, cup/lid inflation. Beverage COGS looks friendly until dairy + pastry + packaging tell the truth. Commodity bean spikes still matter — Brian’s Drive Dunkin P&L notes treat coffee-bean inflation and menu-price elasticity as real diligence, not trivia.

**Competition density**  
National coffee logos, indie specialty, grocery cold brew, gas-station coffee, and the next pad’s drive-thru. Brian’s Dunkin concept note that franchisees **don’t get an exclusive territory** (in that teaching) is a diligence flag for *any* dense coffee trade area — category caution, not a brand roast for sport. Two logos splitting one commute corridor while rent doesn’t split is classic location-retail pain.

**Franchise fees & brand drag (if franchised)**  
Royalties and ad funds on **gross** arrive whether dairy spiked or the afternoon died. Fees help if they buy training, supply chain, marketing that raises net *after* the fee — not because the cup photographs well. High fee burden (Dunkin-class ~**11%** royalty+ad education in Brian’s materials) only works with volume and discipline.

**Manager / opener dependency**  
If only *you* can open, calibrate espresso, schedule, and calm a catering tray order, you bought a job with oat milk. Multi-unit starts when a lead can protect labor %, waste, and speed without you on the bar. Key-person opener walk-off is not “one FTE” — it’s your alarm clock returning to 4:30 a.m.

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many units is **owner on the open**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until labor %, waste logs, and a lead scorecard prove it. Family-flexibility buyers: read the **hours** honestly before the soft-open balloons — coffee is an early-shift career before it is a lifestyle brand.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD Item 19 writeups, Brian’s Dunkin concept / Drive P&L teaching, Fast Food Shorts frames, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing weekly sales by daypart/hour, COGS %, labor %, rent, waste, drive-thru mix, and whether a real opener/GM bench exists.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Brian’s Drive Dunkin Item 19 / P&L education (2025 FDD framing, 2024 full-year franchised cohort): system **mean AUV ~$1.30M**, **median ~$1.24M**; top-quartile education near **~$2.07M** vs bottom-quartile near **~$0.65M** — *spread matters more than the mean*  
- Same inventory: freestanding drive-thru education higher than non-drive-thru (order-of-magnitude **~30–50%** sales lift discussed for DT vs non-DT in that analysis) — format is strategy  
- Drive-thru specialty cohorts in Brian’s 7 Brew–class Drive inventory show **higher AUV bands and higher CapEx** than many cafe boxes — different underwriting, same daypart physics  
- Broader coffee/beverage education often shows cafe / specialty AUV bands **wider and often lower** than top drive-thru specialty — verify *current* Item 19 for any brand you diligence  
- Single-unit owner cash after debt, GM wage, and fees is often **much tighter** than “billions of cups” headlines

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Brian’s retail-location teaching: many retail franchises discussed roughly **~$300k–$600k** per location (some concepts much higher) — coffee can sit in, above, or well above that band depending on drive-thru pad vs kiosk  
- Brian’s Dunkin concept notes: franchise fee education bands (**~$10k–$90k** in that draft), equipment/signage as a big line, ground-up building costs that can approach **high six figures** — **verify current Item 7**  
- Drive-thru specialty (7 Brew–class education in Drive inventory): Item 7 / total investment commonly discussed from **high six figures into ~$2M+** with multi-unit development pressure on some systems — capital is the gate  
- Lean indie strip cafe: lower logo cost, higher marketing and systems burden; espresso CapEx, health permits, and buildout still real  
- Budget **working capital** for soft opening, training wages, pastry spoil on ramp, and the months before afternoon densifies (if it ever does)  
- Resales: buy the P&L, the lease, and the equipment condition — not the mural

**P&L shape education (illustrative — not a promise):**

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Daypart concentration (the silent killer)**  
Morning prints; afternoons and evenings may not. Rain, holidays, remote work, and school calendars move the bank account. Underwrite hourly sales, not the Saturday soft-open line. A “great August week” does not pay January’s rent if your trade area dies when offices empty.

**2. Labor cost & show-up culture at dawn**  
Wages rose. Guests still want a $5–$7 drink. Opener no-shows, overtime, and remakes erase “good sales weeks.” You’re always recruiting for people willing to work before sunrise — often the same dual-market stress as sandwich QSR, paid in cups instead of footlongs. Owner-on-the-bar “savings” is unpaid labor with a back injury and a broken sleep schedule waiting.

**3. Site selection & rent / real estate**  
Hyper-local demand. Bad stacking, weak commute visibility, or a rent that assumes AUV you don’t have. Personal guarantees. Franchisor site approval that still leaves *you* holding the lease. Brian’s teaching: coffee traffic doesn’t travel — the site *is* the strategy. Drive-thru pads that can’t stack cars are expensive billboards.

**4. Throughput vs menu ego**  
Every extra syrup step, every “custom” that adds 30 seconds, every mobile-order pile-up that blocks the window — peak cars/hour dies quietly. Specialty craft is a product *only if* the line still moves. Confusing Instagram latte art with throughput is how A sites underperform.

**5. Equipment downtime & CapEx surprises**  
Espresso, ice, grinders, POS. A dead machine at 7:05 a.m. is lost sales *and* Google reviews. CapEx bands (fee + equipment + buildout) in Brian’s Dunkin teaching exist because the shiny cup is not the expensive part. Service contracts and spare capacity are operating costs, not nice-to-haves.

**6. Competition density (brand + indie + gas station)**  
National logos, indie specialty, grocery cold brew, and C-store coffee all compete for the same morning habit. Dense trade areas split cars; rent doesn’t split. Brian’s Dunkin concept diligence flag on **no exclusive territory** is a category reminder: underwrite encroachment and competitive pads, not the brand brochure’s map pin.

**7. Waste — pastry, dairy, prep**  
A full pastry case looks like hospitality until it’s a dumpster at 3 p.m. Milk waste, over-pulled shots, and “just remake it” culture compound. Beverage COGS looks pretty until attach and waste tell the truth.

**8. Franchise fees on gross (if franchised)**  
Royalty + ad fund don’t care that dairy went up or that Tuesday afternoon died. High fee burden (Dunkin-class ~**11%** education) only works with volume and discipline. A national logo does not open your store or negotiate your CAM.

**9. Manager / opener dependency**  
The model scales on leads who protect labor %, waste, speed, and guest experience. One hero opener leaving returns you to 4:45 a.m. “Semi-absentee” without a bench is a LinkedIn lie.

**10. Thin net after “everyone drinks coffee” headlines**  
Systemwide cup counts and PE headlines are not your collectible net after COGS, labor, rent, fees, and debt. Bottom-quartile AUV education (Dunkin Drive notes near **~$0.65M** in that framing) is how busy-looking boxes stay broke.

**11. Buildout, permits & format mismatch**  
Drive-thru pads, stacking lanes, health permits, grease traps if you cook, signage fights with the landlord. Delays burn rent before the first ticket. CapEx surprises are how “Item 7 midpoints” become personal loans. Buying a cafe when your underwriting needed a drive-thru (or the reverse) is a strategy error, not bad luck.

**12. Training time vs turnover**  
Barista skill and drink consistency take time; turnover resets the bar every month. Training cost is recurring, not a one-time orientation. Craft brands feel this harder; QSR-beverage brands feel it as speed variance.

**13. Quality variance across shifts**  
Rush vs dead hour; opener vs midshift; weekday vs Saturday crew. Guests don’t care which shift “usually” calibrates correctly. Reviews are inventory — and morning guests are creatures of habit who leave fast.

**14. Cash & working-capital traps**  
Sales look fine while dairy invoices, payroll, and sales tax come due. Soft opening burn. Owners who spend week-one sales like salary discover week-four invoices. Seasonal dips hit coffee when the commute changes.

**15. Multi-unit pressure without a replacement engine**  
Second box before the first has a real opener/GM is how you own two alarms. Development agreements that force unit #3–#5 before unit #1 has a bench are capital and labor stress — underwrite the *people plan*, not the map of dots.

**16. Owner-as-hero opener**  
If only you can open, only you can fix the machine, and only you can schedule the dawn crew, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a morning-shift career with a lease and a logo.

**17. Confusing “people always need coffee” headlines with “your collectible net after dayparts, labor, rent, fees, waste, and equipment”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Tuesday labor % and your CAM bill.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means coffee / snack is a **location-retail beverage** business where empty opener shifts, a soft afternoon, a B site, drifting waste, a missing lead, fee drag, and a dead espresso machine decide whether the box survives the next wage hike.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can live inside **early opens + morning rush** until a real lead owns the shift — or you’ve already capitalized a manager-led plan with proof  
- Labor % dashboards, waste logs, and cars-per-hour energize you more than they nauseate you  
- Site walks, lease redlines, stack counts, and commute maps feel like diligence, not boredom  
- Recruiting and retaining dawn crews (and a GM/opener bench) is a job you’re willing to own  
- You’ve compared this door to [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (lunch sandwich physics), [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) (B2B restaurant compliance), and lighter truck/route or home-service models — and still want the morning box  
- You’re honest that restaurants are an **ownership door with an hours tax** (Brian’s frame) — not passive income with oat milk

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted passive “everybody drinks coffee” income from month one  
- You’re allergic to hourly turnover, 5 a.m. opens, food waste conversations, or Saturday mornings  
- You’re undercapitalized for buildout + working capital and assumed the franchise brochure erased soft months  
- You actually want a **service business with leverage**, a **lunch QSR sandwich box**, or a **B2B restaurant vendor** play — different underwriting  
- You thought specialty vibes erase site selection and throughput math  
- You need the trendiest vertical more than timeless open discipline + schedule discipline in *your* trade area

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: tickets, COGS, labor, site, dayparts, throughput. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens training, supplies chain consistency, and improves guest acquisition — not because a PE headline made the category look easy.

Many operators will correctly choose a **smoother-hours** service vehicle or a non-food door first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Opener no-show / labor spike → rush failure + review spiral  
- Soft dayparts / remote-work commute shift → sales cliff with fixed rent  
- Wrong site / rent too high / can’t stack cars → AUV never arrives  
- Equipment downtime at peak → lost sales + brand damage  
- Waste / dairy / pastry drift → margin wipeout  
- GM / opener walk-off → owner returns to 4:45 a.m.  
- Royalty + ad on gross while afternoon dies → busy-broke  
- Encroachment / density (brand-dependent) + indie + gas-station competition  
- Lease personal guarantee on a soft P&L  
- Buildout delay / CapEx overrun → capital stress before opening  
- Multi-unit / development fantasy funded by one soft box  
- Buying a “book” that was one hero opener + one A commute corridor + one tired lease  
- Confusing systemwide cup headlines with collectible net after dayparts, labor, rent, fees, and waste

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everybody needs coffee.”

You’re buying the right to run a **morning throughput box** — if you can protect **labor and waste**, pick a site guests will actually use on a rainy Tuesday, install **openers** who own the dawn, treat royalties and rent as real taxes on gross, keep equipment alive at 7 a.m., and not confuse a drive-thru line for a twelve-month salary with Saturdays off and sleep intact.

Some people should buy a tired unit with real morning sales and fix labor %, waste, and opener process (resale path — diligence still required; coffee marketplace touches like Break Coffee in Brian’s leads baseline prove *deal flow exists*, not that any deal is good).  
Some people should open one box, prove a lead, and only then add a second site.  
Some people should choose [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual), [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning), or a truck/route service business and never take on 5 a.m. plus a pastry case.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people buy coffee?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful food job with a strip lease, a labor spreadsheet, an espresso machine, and a personal guarantee when your only opener texts “I’m done” and the drive-thru line is already wrapping the pad?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other food-hospitality vehicles vs business-services or home-service paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
