Saturday in June.  
Three trucks. Two no-shows. One piano nobody mentioned on the survey.  
The binding quote said six hours. The elevator reservation said 10–12.  
Fuel was up again. The HOA wants a COI faxed *now*. Someone’s grandmother’s mirror just cracked — and the review draft is already writing itself in the customer’s head.

That’s **residential moving & storage**.

Not [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) as the **facility / downsizing niche** (pace, packing, placement partners, emotional room-setup — same truck physics, different book mix).  
Not [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) (commission on what sells).  
Not [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) (soft orchestration / sort-decide-pack coordination).  
Not [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal) (haul-to-landfill economics — adjacent overflow, different tip-fee P&L).

Labor + trucks.  
Local apartments and whole houses. Long carries, stairs, long-distance when you choose that beast. Optional storage that eats warehouse and utilization math. Quotes that lie if the survey was soft. Claims that erase a “good” week.

Simple on a whiteboard: people move → charge for crew + truck → scale fleets.  
Hard in real life — because **labor**, **truck CapEx**, **seasonality**, **damage claims**, **estimates vs actuals**, **fuel**, **insurance**, and **lead gen that doesn’t just buy price shoppers** all show up before the “movers always make money in summer” brochure does.

National logos, indie wrap trucks, dual junk+moving brands, and “experience” movers all sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The **crew that shows**, the **survey that holds**, and the **claim file that stays quiet** are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **residential move operator** (local first; long-distance and storage as deliberate attach or separate factories): estimate the job, staff the crew, protect and haul the goods, collect the check, keep utilization and claims from eating the year.

This page is the **general residential / local + long-distance ops** book — apartment churn, house-to-house, office-light, storage-in / storage-out — the demand Google, realtors, property managers, and word-of-mouth all feed.

The senior / facility / downsizing specialty lives on [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving). You may *do* those jobs; don’t underwrite this page as if every lead is Grandma’s AL move-in.

Typical menu:

1. **Local residential moves** — studio to whole house; hourly or flat / binding packages  
2. **Packing / unpacking** — materials + labor; margin and damage control when done right  
3. **Loading / unloading only** — DIY rental trucks, PODS-style containers, customer-owned trailers  
4. **Long-distance / interstate** (if offered) — weight, space, or container pricing; authority, broker games, claim stack change  
5. **Small commercial / office** — nights and weekends; elevator politics and COI theater  
6. **Storage attach** — warehouse or partner facility; in/out fees, monthly rent, abandoned-goods risk  
7. **Junk / haul-away attach** (some dual brands) — disposal economics; see [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal); don’t pretend landfill tip fees are free margin  
8. **Not the same company identity:** [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) niche as the *whole* thesis; [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) sell-through; [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) orchestration fees; pure van-line agent complexity as your only story

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / LSA, brand call center, realtor / PM referrals, prior customers, aggregators, truck wraps, partner lists  
2. **Survey** — in-home or video; volume, stairs, long carries, specialty items, parking, building rules, “we’ll pack ourselves” lies  
3. **Quote / deposit** — hourly vs binding / not-to-exceed; what’s *not* included; valuation / coverage options; fuel or travel adders where disclosed  
4. **Dispatch** — trucks + crew sized to the job; peak-season math gets ugly  
5. **Move day** — protect, load, transport, unload, basic placement  
6. **Collect / follow-up** — card on site; claim window; review ask  
7. **Optional attach** — packing, short-term storage, junk partner for leftovers, or handoff to [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) / [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) when the job is really a household transition

**Scope lock:** this is **moving & storage as the company** — trucks, crews, utilization, estimates that survive contact with stairs, claims, fuel, insurance, lead cost.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed moves (and storage months) that survive the claim window** — not a calendar full of optimistic quotes and not “hours on a timesheet” the customer romanticizes.

Pricing / economics shapes you’ll see (education — **verify your metro**):

- **Hourly local** — crew size × hours + travel / materials; transparent until the clock blows past the verbal “about four hours”  
- **Flat / binding / not-to-exceed** for surveyed local jobs — your survey accuracy becomes the P&L  
- **Packing** as separate line items (materials + labor)  
- **Valuation / coverage** upgrades — and the claim culture that follows whatever you offered  
- **Long-distance / interstate** — weight, cubic, or container; deposits, transit times, and claim rules that are not “local hourly with a highway”  
- **Storage** — first month + in/out labor, then recurring rent; utilization and abandoned inventory are the quiet story  
- **Fuel / travel / stair / long-carry adders** — disclosed or baked; either way someone pays when diesel and fourth-floor walk-ups are real  
- **What you generally do *not* own:** the customer’s home-sale proceeds, the realtor’s commission, the estate-sale sell-through, the landfill tip ticket unless you also run junk

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Booked jobs** — phone answered, survey done, deposit taken, calendar filled with jobs you can *staff*  
2. **Average ticket / mix** — studio vs whole house; packing %; storage attach; LD share  
3. **Crew + truck utilization** — billable move hours vs empty windshield, elevator waits, and “truck B in the shop”  
4. **Labor % (burdened)** — wages, OT, workers’ comp, uniforms; the silent margin killer  
5. **Estimates vs actuals** — under-scoped surveys create OT, second trips, damage, and refunds; over-scoped quotes lose the job to amateurs  
6. **Claims cost** — repairs, replacements, goodwill; reputation is a marketing line item too  
7. **Fuel + maintenance** — diesel/gas, tires, Downtime; summer volume with winter fuel spikes is a real combo  
8. **Insurance stack cost** — commercial auto, cargo, GL, workers’ comp mod; wrong limits are existential  
9. **Lead gen efficiency** — CAC by source; LSA/Google vs realtor/PM vs brand call center vs partners  
10. **Seasonality hedge** — commercial, storage, packing, junk attach, winter promotions — or you pray for May–August forever

Gross margin lives in **price − crew labor − fuel − materials − claims (− commissions / lead fees)**.  
Then overhead: truck leases / notes, insurance, dispatch / call handling, warehouse if you store, marketing, software, and (if franchised) royalty + brand/MAP + tech.

This is a **utilization + trust + claims + working-capital** business.  
Not a demographic lottery ticket. Not “people always move” as a forecast.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People move. Always.

Job change. Lease ends. Upsizing. Downsizing. Divorce. New construction. Military PCS. Apartment churn. Office resets.  
Housing turnover feeds local movers even when national long-haul headlines cool. Storage demand follows “not ready to decide” and “gap between close dates.”

Demand for *someone trustworthy with a truck and a crew* is structural.

The constraint isn’t “are there moves?”

It’s **labor you can schedule + trucks you can afford + surveys that match reality + claims you can survive + leads that aren’t pure price shoppers + cash that survives January** in *your* market.

National brands exist. Independents exist. Dual junk+moving brands exist. Storage-heavy locals exist.  
Same category. Different operating reality.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Lead gen that isn’t just auctioned panic**  
Google / LSA, aggregators, brand call centers, realtor and PM lists, prior customers, truck wraps, partnerships into [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) / [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) / [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) niche partners.  
No diversified engine → feast on peak CPC and famine when school starts. Price-shopper leads win the race to a claim.

**Survey honesty (estimates vs actuals)**  
Stairs, long carries, pianos, marble tops, HOA / dock rules, packing fiction, “we decluttered” that didn’t. Under-scoped jobs eat labor, fuel, and create damage. Over-scoped quotes lose to the uninsured pickup. Binding quotes without video/in-home truth are how you donate OT.

**Crew supply**  
Reliable movers who show up, lift safely, and don’t treat the house like a demolition site. Peak summer hiring is a meat grinder. Training shortcuts show up as August claims.

**Truck CapEx / utilization**  
Box trucks are the factory. Under-fleet → you sell days you can’t run. Over-fleet → notes eat January. Maintenance, wraps, insurance, and downtime are real. Storage fleets and shuttle vans add another utilization puzzle.

**Dispatch discipline**  
Two small jobs across town with a third “quick add” is how you miss evening cutoffs and generate bad reviews. Density beats heroics. Fuel and windshield time are not free.

**Claims culture**  
Document condition. Train padding. Resolve fast. Operators who fight every scratch buy lawyer hours and one-star reviews. Operators who over-pay every soft claim train customers to file.

**Fuel and route reality**  
Diesel spikes don’t care about your binding quote from March. Idle time at elevators burns money. Long empty deadheads between booked jobs are a pricing problem dressed as geography.

**Insurance as operating system**  
Commercial auto, cargo limits, valuation offerings, workers’ comp experience mod, certificates for buildings. Miss a COI and the crew sits. Underinsure cargo and one piano ends the company story.

**Storage (if you offer it)**  
Warehouse lease or partner space, climate myths, access hours, abandoned goods, and the temptation to become a cheap mini-storage with moving labor attached. Utilization and shrinkage matter; “we’ll store it for free until they call” is not a strategy.

**Long-distance temptation**  
Bigger tickets, uglier claims, authority / broker complexity, transit damage windows. Local density businesses die chasing glamorous highway revenue without systems. Treat LD as a deliberate product — or refer it.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you *are* estimating, hiring, riding jobs, handling angry texts at 9pm.  
Semi-absentee fairy tales are how people buy a truck and a Facebook page.  
Brian’s modern-moving newsletter framed franchisee-style ownership as **culture + driving the business + managing the manager** — not as “never touch a truck” from day one. Earn that.

**Adjacent stack**  
[Senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) niche skill, [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales), [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing), [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal), realtors, PMs. You don’t need to own them. You do need to know who you send overflow to — and who sends *you* keepers when the calendar softens.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

**Operating intuition (education — not forecasts):**

- Brian’s newsletter education pass put a **~$2k average ticket** as a category feel for modern local moving — mix and metro move this hard  
- Category operator benchmarks often push **crew labor under ~30% of revenue** as a healthy target; many books run higher when OT and poor scoping creep in  
- **Claims** discipline conversations often aim **under ~1% of revenue** — spikes usually mean training / survey failure, not bad luck alone  
- **Seasonality:** industry chatter commonly frames **~40–60% of annual volume** in the late-spring / summer window — winter tests working capital and partner / commercial channels  
- Dual junk+moving public P&L shapes (education): truck labor often lands high-**20s to ~30%** of revenue in mature disclosure tables — useful for intuition, not your budget  
- Fuel as a % of revenue swings with diesel and dispatch density — books that ignore it in binding quotes learn in July

**Startup / investment shape (education — verify current FDD Item 7 / indie quotes):**

- Brian’s *A Modern Moving Company* (2023-era teaching) cited one system’s single-territory band roughly **~$140k–$197k** and two-territory roughly **~$165k–$282k** all-in — office in an industrial area, truck financing packages exist; **verify current docs**  
- His Notion franchisor card for that same modern brand later showed investment roughly **~$131k–$191k** (card fields drift; never memorize one screenshot)  
- Legacy brand public aggregator reads for a large national mover often land roughly **mid-five to low-mid six figures** Item 7 depending on fleet size (e.g. bands discussed ~**$90k–$250k+**) — system-specific  
- Dual junk+moving brands can sit higher all-in once multiple trucks and dual marketing are real  
- Independents can start leaner (one truck, insurance, crew, wrap) and still get crushed by claims, fuel, or seasonality without reserves  
- Storage-capable builds add warehouse deposit / lease — different CapEx than a pure truck-out-of-a-yard story

**Illustrative disclosure / teaching shapes (not your forecast):**

- Brian cited one corporate proof unit’s **first 12 months**: roughly **$678k** revenue, **$116k** operating income, **~17%** net margin — *their* launch, *their* market, *their* year  
- Same newsletter: metro units at the largest legacy brand averaged about **$3.2M** sales (2022 teaching) — top-end category shape, wide spread underneath  
- Dual-brand public Item 19-style tables show **wide** mature vs early-unit spreads — same logo, different execution

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Jobs per week in peak vs off-peak? Average ticket? Packing % of revenue? Storage %?  
- Labor % (burdened) and OT pattern last 24 months?  
- Estimate accuracy — quoted hours vs actual hours by lead source? Binding vs hourly mix?  
- Claims $ and % of revenue — and average days to close a claim?  
- Fuel $ / mile and deadhead %?  
- Insurance: cargo limits, valuation offerings, workers’ comp mod, commercial auto losses?  
- Lead CAC and close rate by source (Google / LSA / aggregator / realtor / brand / partner)?  
- Truck count, age, lease vs own, revenue per truck per week? Downtime days?  
- If storage: occupancy, in/out labor cost, abandoned inventory policy?  
- If LD: authority, broker % of book, claim rate vs local?  
- If franchised: royalty + brand fund + call-center / tech fees — do they actually win jobs and protect brand trust?  
- Owner hours on truck vs on hiring / estimating — is this a transferable company?

Same brain you’d use buying any field-service asset business: utilization, labor, claims, lead quality, working capital through the soft season, and whether *you* want to live inside other people’s move-day stress for years.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Labor is the product — and the bottleneck**  
Trucks don’t move sofas. People do. No-shows, turnover, and summer wage wars turn a full calendar into refunds and one-star reviews. Recruiting is unpaid until the day the backup crew saves the binding quote.

**2. Truck CapEx and maintenance**  
Notes, wraps, tires, downtime, shop weeks in peak season. Under-trucked you can’t scale; over-trucked quiet Tuesdays hurt. This is not “laptop and a phone” overhead. Storage space — if you chase it — is another fixed cost that laughs at January.

**3. Seasonality**  
May–August can carry the year. January can starve you while leases, insurance, and truck payments still clear. If your book is 100% residential peak, you’re a weather + school-calendar business with a logo.

**4. Damage claims**  
One broken antique, one scratched floor, one “missing” box can erase the job’s margin and the referral partner who sent you. Documentation and training beat charm. Soft claims culture trains serial filers; hard culture trains one-star reviews. Pick a process before peak.

**5. Estimates vs actuals**  
“Should be a 4-hour job” becomes 9 hours with OT, a second trip, a tired crew making mistakes, and a customer who thought they bought certainty. Survey lies (yours or theirs) are the structural tax of the category. Video surveys that skip the basement are how binding quotes become donations.

**6. Fuel**  
Diesel and gas move with the world, not your quote sheet. Long carries, traffic, elevator idle, and empty miles between jobs are fuel problems dressed as “ops.” Binding quotes written in March get paid in July’s pump prices unless you have policy for it.

**7. Insurance (cost + gaps)**  
Commercial auto, cargo, GL, workers’ comp — expensive when you’re compliant; existential when you’re not. Buildings demand COIs. Customers assume “you’re insured” means “my heirloom is fully covered.” Valuation education is sales *and* claims prevention. One underinsured load is a career event.

**8. Lead gen quality and cost**  
Google / LSA CPC spikes in peak season exactly when every truck in town is bidding. Aggregators sell the same shopper to five movers. Cheap leads that only close on price become expensive claims. Realtor / PM / partner channels take years and die if you screw one move. Brand call centers help until the royalty math and lead quality diverge.

**9. Peak-season quality collapse**  
You hire fast in April. August claims spike. Training time disappears exactly when volume peaks — the structural trap of the category. Calendar fullness is not the same as profitable fullness.

**10. Price war with uninsured amateurs**  
Craigslist crews and “two guys + a Budget truck” undercut. You carry insurance, payroll tax, real claim exposure, and fuel. Racing them is how professionals go broke while looking busy.

**11. Cash timing vs deposits**  
Deposits help; big jobs still front labor and fuel. Chargebacks, “Dad said he’d pay,” and card declines on move day happen. Storage customers who ghost still occupy your square footage.

**12. Building / HOA / facility friction**  
Elevator reservations, certificates of insurance, loading docks, quiet hours, parking permits. Miss one form and the crew sits unpaid while the quote clock… doesn’t.

**13. Long-distance / interstate complexity**  
Bigger tickets, uglier claims, licensing / authority, broker games, transit damage windows, and customer expectations trained by national ads. Local density businesses that “just try LD” without systems buy a different company without admitting it.

**14. Storage utilization myths**  
Empty warehouse months, abandoned goods drama, climate assumptions, and “free storage to win the move” that never converts to paid rent. Storage is a product with its own ops — or a silent margin leak.

**15. Scope creep into estate / junk / senior orchestration**  
Families want the whole transition. Pure movers who fake liquidation, landfill economics, or [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) project management inherit problems they didn’t underwrite — partner into [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales), [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal), and [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) instead of cosplaying them.

**16. Workers’ comp and injury**  
Stairs, wet driveways, heavy lifts, rushed peak crews. Injury rates in physical move work are not theoretical. Safety culture is a P&L line and a hiring brand.

**17. Owner stuck on the truck**  
No estimators, no dispatcher, no recruiting → you bought a physically exhausting job with a wrap. Brian’s ownership frame cares whether you can eventually **manage the manager** — not whether you can deadlift a dresser forever.

**18. Franchise / brand stack (if you go that route)**  
Royalty + brand fund + required marketing vs call center, playbook, and hiring help. Worth it only if the system wins jobs *and* protects trust — not because the truck wrap looks friendly on Instagram.

**19. Confusing “people always move” with “your trucks are full and paid”**  
Macro demand is the backdrop. Your company is surveys that hold, crews that show, utilization, fuel discipline, claims that stay quiet, leads that aren’t pure auctions, and a winter you can afford.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means residential moving & storage is a **real labor + CapEx + claims + working-capital business**, not a summer side hustle with a logo.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re okay with field-service ops — hiring, dispatch, ride-alongs, angry Saturday texts  
- You can obsess over survey accuracy, claims process, and labor % weekly — not annually  
- You’re capitalized for trucks, insurance, fuel swings, and slow seasons — or intentional about a lean one-truck ramp with eyes open  
- Lead gen and partner development don’t scare you (realtors, PMs, prior customers, selective paid search)  
- You’re calm when elevators fail and mirrors crack — and still willing to document like an adult  
- You’ve compared this door to [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) (niche pace / facility book), [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal) (disposal P&L), [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) / [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) (softer CapEx, different checks) — and still want truck + crew physics  
- You want ownership of a real ops company, not a passive “trucks print money” myth

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want pure desk / relationship economics with no crew drama (look at placement-style or soft transition consulting)  
- You want commission-on-stuff economics without truck CapEx ([estate-sales](/models/estate-sales))  
- You actually want [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal) tip-fee density and keep calling it moving  
- You want only the senior / facility emotional niche as the whole identity ([senior-moving](/models/senior-moving))  
- You hate hiring and hate conflict  
- You’re looking for passive / “hire a manager and fish” from month one  
- You’re undercapitalized for truck notes + insurance + off-season burn + a bad claims month  
- You can’t handle damage disputes, COI paperwork, or estimate fights  
- Fuel volatility and peak CPC make you want to close the laptop forever

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same category either way: labor, trucks, utilization, estimates vs actuals, claims, fuel, insurance, lead quality.  
Franchise trades fee/royalty for playbook and brand — only if the system helps you win moves *and* staff them cleanly.

Many operators will correctly choose **junk**, **senior-niche moving**, **estate / transition**, or a different trade first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Peak volume with green crews → claims spike  
- Off-season with fixed truck / insurance / warehouse cost → cash crunch  
- Binding quotes from soft surveys → OT and refunds  
- Fuel spikes + deadhead geography → margin surprise  
- Lead gen concentrated in peak CPC auctions → expensive, claim-prone book  
- Partner / realtor concentration → revenue cliff when one office switches  
- Labor % drift / OT culture → busy and broke  
- Underinsured cargo or sloppy valuation → existential claim  
- Workers’ comp / injury event → mod and culture damage  
- LD / broker complexity without systems → claim and cash traps  
- Storage occupancy myths / abandoned goods → silent leak  
- Owner-on-truck forever → no transferable enterprise  
- Royalty drag without lead/system advantage  
- Blurring into junk / estate / transition ops you didn’t price or insure for  
- Thinking “people always move” substitutes for dispatch skill

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “Americans relocate” or a pastel promise that summer calendars print while you sleep.

You’re buying the right to stand between a family’s deadline and a pile of keepers — and get paid when the truck leaves full and arrives intact — if you can hire, survey honestly, utilize assets, price fuel and risk like an adult, settle claims without theater, generate leads that aren’t pure auctions, and survive the months when the school calendar isn’t doing your marketing.

Some people should start with one truck and grind realtor / PM / prior-customer density before fleet fantasies.  
Some people should buy a tired book with trucks and a phone number and fix labor, surveys, and claims.  
Some people should specialize into [senior-moving](/models/senior-moving) and live in the facility / downsizing partner stack.  
Some people should run [junk-removal](/models/junk-removal) or partner into [estate-sales](/models/estate-sales) / [transition-downsizing](/models/transition-downsizing) instead of owning the move P&L.  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “will there be more moves next year?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a summer of overtime, a winter of truck payments, and a claim inbox that only works when someone else’s deadline fills your calendar and your estimate survives contact with the stairs?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs senior-moving vs estate sales vs transition-downsizing vs junk vs others), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
