A baseball through the living-room pane.  
Fog between the double panes that never clears.  
A patio screen the dog shredded — again — and a storefront that needs board-up before the storm hits Friday night.

Somebody has to **measure, cut or order the right glass (or mesh), roll a stocked truck, install without wrecking the frame, and stand behind a seal that still holds when the next cold front hits**.

That’s **window / glass / screens** — residential and light commercial **service + install**.

Not [auto-glass](/models/auto-glass) (windshields, TPAs, ADAS recalibration — sibling machine, different claim world).  
Not [window-cleaning](/models/window-cleaning) (squeegee routes on *dirty* glass — sibling density muscle, different product).  
Not [blinds-window-coverings](/models/blinds-window-coverings) (design → factory fabric → install — same openings, different factory).  
Not a [handyman](/models/handyman) who “might have a piece of plexi” as the whole company.  
Not “I own a glass cutter and a Facebook ad” as the whole *route density + inventory + warranty* story.

A local **dispatch + truck-inventory + small-crew** company that turns broken panes, failed insulated glass units (IGUs), mirrors, shower doors, storefront glass, and screen repair/replace into a dense schedule — then lives or dies on whether those jobs complete **first trip**, survive insurance paperwork when it’s a claim, and don’t come back as leak callbacks in month three.

Simple on a whiteboard: break / fog / tear → measure → glass or mesh → install → collect → next stop.  
Brutal if you confuse “houses have windows” with “this runs itself.”

## What the business actually is

You’re a **field glazing / screen service** company wearing a repair-and-replace brand.

Typical menu (operators mix differently — underwrite the mix):

1. **Pane / IGU replacement** — cracked, shattered, or fogged insulated glass in existing frames (the residential workhorse in many books)  
2. **Emergency board-up / security glass** — storm, break-in, vacant unit; speed + documentation when insurance is watching  
3. **Screens** — repair mesh, re-spline, full rescreen, custom sizes; lower ticket, high density when you run a screen-forward lane  
4. **Mirrors / shower enclosures / table tops / specialty flat glass** — higher skill, higher ticket, more measure intolerance  
5. **Storefront / light commercial** — doors, sidelites, tempered replacements, property-manager punch lists  
6. **New windows / retrofit packages** (optional drift) — full unit replacement vs glass-only; different sales cycle, different CapEx story — call it out in diligence; don’t pretend every greenfield starts as a whole-home window contractor  
7. **Attach adjacency** — hardware, weatherstrip, caulk/seal, sometimes door glass; stay honest about license and skill

Typical loop:

1. **Lead / claim** — Google / LSA, prior customer, realtor / PM / builder referral, storm surge, insurer / preferred-vendor path (when you play that game), truck wrap, screen-repair inbound (if you offer it), brand lead flow (if franchised)  
2. **Quote / authorize** — photos vs eyes-on; tempered vs annealed; Low-E / argon; grid patterns; inside vs outside access; deductible vs cash; board-up now vs glass later  
3. **Order / stage** — cut on truck (single pane) vs order IGU (days); mesh rolls and spline on the van; specialty tempered lead times  
4. **Dispatch** — right tech, right truck stock, right geography, weather window for sealants and ladder work  
5. **Install + seal** — protect finishes, seat the unit, seal, clean, photo document for claims  
6. **Collect / warranty / review** — card on site or insurer invoice cycle; your name stays on every pane the homeowner looks through when it rains

**Scope lock for this page:** residential and light-commercial **architectural / flat glass + screens** — small crews (often 1–2 techs), route density as the engine, truck inventory discipline, service/install tickets (not a curtain-wall GC as the identity). B2C + light B2B as the backlog one-liner says.

**Out of scope (different models):**  
- Pure **auto glass / tint** ([auto-glass](/models/auto-glass)) — different claim networks, VIN/NAGS, ADAS CapEx  
- Pure **window cleaning** routes ([window-cleaning](/models/window-cleaning))  
- Pure **blinds / coverings** design-to-order ([blinds-window-coverings](/models/blinds-window-coverings))  
- Full **window replacement contractor** / new-construction glazing GC as the primary identity (adjacent; longer sales, different bonding/licensing in many markets)  
- Solo Craigslist glass guy as the whole *company* (craft job with a phone ≠ this page’s ownership math)  
- High-rise / rope-access / architectural curtain wall as the greenfield default

**Format forks you must name in diligence:**

| Format | Feel |
|--------|------|
| **Glass-heavy mobile / shop hybrid** | Van + small warehouse rack; IGU orders; board-up; residential + storefront mix |
| **Screen-forward mobile** | Mesh, spline, on-site rescreen density; lower average ticket; calendar can fill with “dog door” economics |
| **Insurance / storm-weighted** | Claim documentation, adjuster theater, feast after weather, famine when skies are quiet |
| **Retail cash / Google-weighted** | Deductible economics push many homeowners to cash; CAC and speed-to-quote decide |
| **Shop / cut-table heavy** | More inventory, more specialty, less pure “windshield of the house” dispatch |

## How money comes in

You sell **completed glass and screen tickets** (and sometimes board-up + follow-on glass), not “hours on a timesheet” in the abstract.

Education ticket shape (industry / consumer guides 2025–2026; metros and tempered/specialty sit higher — **verify your market**):

- **Screen repair / rescreen** — often low-to-mid hundreds per opening; density or die  
- **Single-pane / simple glass** — commonly discussed roughly **~$150–$300** installed for standard sizes when cut same-day  
- **Double-pane / IGU replace in existing frame** — often discussed roughly **~$250–$600** per typical residential opening; large patio / specialty higher  
- **Board-up + emergency** — often mid-hundreds into low thousands depending on openings and after-hours  
- **Shower / mirror / specialty** — mid-hundreds to low thousands+; measure errors get expensive  
- **Multi-opening storm / claim jobs** — ticket stacks; cash timing and documentation become the product  
- **Full window unit replacement** (if you offer it) — different band entirely (often high-hundreds to low-thousands+ per opening installed) — don’t average it into glass-only math without splitting the P&L

Revenue mix usually looks like some blend of:

| Channel | What it feels like |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Cash retail / Google** | Deductible math; speed-to-quote; review gravity |
| **Insurance / storm** | Volume spikes; adjuster scope fights; float and documentation burden |
| **Screens** | Lower ticket, higher stop count; great density filler — can also train the market to see you as “the cheap screen guy” |
| **PM / light commercial / realtor** | Steadier when won; price pressure; access and COI friction |
| **Specialty (shower / mirror / storefront)** | Margin and skill; longer cycle; fewer techs can run it |
| **Full-unit window packages** (optional) | Higher ticket, longer sell, different warranty / manufacturer world |

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Booked density** — billable stops vs windshield, supply-house runs, and “wrong Low-E” second trips  
2. **First-trip completion** — truck stock + correct order status; incomplete stops murder utilization  
3. **Average ticket / mix** — screen-only days vs IGU clusters vs specialty  
4. **Insurance literacy** (if you play claims) — covered peril vs wear (fogged seals often *aren’t* a claim), deductible counseling, photo packs, RCV/ACV timing  
5. **Tech skill you can staff** — setting glass without stress cracks, sealing that lasts, tempered handling, screen speed  
6. **Weather windows** — sealants, ladders, storm demand, summer heat on dark glass  
7. **Lead economics** — CAC vs close vs collected job; storm feast doesn’t fund January unless you saved  
8. **Warranty / callback rate** — leaks and fog returns quietly erase months of ads

Gross margin lives in **price − glass/mesh/materials − tech labor (burdened) − waste/breakage − warranty rework (− insurer write-downs)**.  
Then overhead: vans, racks/warehouse, insurance (GL + auto + workers’ comp — glass handling is not desk work), marketing, FSM/CRM, tooling, and (if franchised) royalty + brand fund + tech fees.

Industry shape (education only): healthy shops obsess over **quoted → ordered → installed → collected** lag and **stops per tech-day**, not vanity “pipeline” screenshots. A busy phone with empty trucks and late IGUs is a hobby with a wrap.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Housing stock ages. Seals fail. Kids throw balls. Dogs invent new screen exits. Storms don’t ask your calendar. Storefronts get hit. Empty-nesters fog-gaze at south windows for years — until the energy bill or the listing photos force a call.

When the economy is good, people upgrade shower glass, add custom mirrors, and replace tired units.  
When the economy is tight, **broken glass and security board-ups still happen** — and a large share of IGU calls become “is this worth filing, or do I just pay cash under my deductible?”

That’s the demand story.

The constraint isn’t “buildings have openings.”

It’s **tech skill + truck inventory + route density + claim/cash discipline** in *your* territory — on a product that is **visible, weather-exposed, and review-sensitive**.

Unlike emergency plumbing, not every fogged pane is a same-day crisis.  
Like garage-door cables, a shattered patio door *is*.  
You live in the gap between “we should fix that fog” and “glass is on the truck and the seal is curing.”

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Dispatch / answer rate**  
Missed calls after a hail cell are free marketing for the competitor who picks up. Board-up speed is part of the product when insurance and vacant-unit risk are in play.

**Route density**  
Three IGU installs in one subdivision beats one specialty mirror across town plus two supply runs. Geography and book discipline decide whether small crews print or just stay busy.

**Truck inventory (the quiet P&L)**  
Understock → second trips → density dies. Overstock → cash sitting in racks (and breakage risk). Common spline, stops, sealant, standard mesh, and a sane cut of “always break” sizes matter. Specialty tempered is a different animal than “we might need that one day.”

**Order lag vs same-day cut**  
Single pane can be same-day craft. Modern residential IGUs are often **measure → order → wait days**. Expectation management is the sale. Selling “tomorrow” on a custom Low-E grid unit is how reviews start.

**Tech skill & small-crew math**  
One strong tech can carry a screen-heavy day. Heavy IGUs, tempered, and shower doors often want two bodies and real craft. Hiring people who can set glass *and* talk a homeowner through cash-vs-claim without creating callbacks is the hard part — same hire-the-tech seat Brian teaches across home services.

**Insurance / glass claims literacy**  
Covered sudden perils (storm, vandalism, break-in) ≠ failed seals / wear. Deductibles of **$1k–$2.5k+** (and wind/hail percentages in some markets) push many single-opening jobs to cash. Claim shops that inflate scope get a reputation; shops that can’t document get stiffed. Board-up receipts and photo timelines matter.

**Weather**  
Demand spikes when glass breaks. Production dies when you can’t safely ladder or seal. Heat, freeze, wind, and rain all vote. Storm chasing without a trough plan is a cash trap.

**Lead gen (non-negotiable)**  
Storm weeks are not a marketing system. Google/LSA in competitive metros get expensive. PM relationships, realtor turnovers, prior customers, and screen-inbound funnels smooth the calendar. Track **cost per installed collected job**, not cost per lead.

**Warranty callbacks**  
A slow leak, a stress crack, a fog that returns, a screen that pops out in the first wind. Factory vs install vs “homeowner slammed the door” fights are your brand either way. Policy and photo QA — or pain.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: lead flow, pricing, recruiting, inventory turns, claim process, culture, and often backup estimate / emergency board-up hero.  
Not “I bought a logo and went fishing.”  
Same ownership muscle: **hire the people who are the experts; run the business.**

**Systems**  
FSM/CRM, photo SOPs for claims, measure double-checks, order status, route software, review engine, breakage logs. Guesswork doesn’t scale past one heroic glazier.

**Operating intuition (industry education, not forecasts):**

- Ticket ladder as above — screens fill the calendar; IGUs and specialty pay the rent; storm clusters move the year  
- Crew productivity: watch **completed stops per tech-day** and **first-trip completion %** as hard as “leads”  
- Materials: glass and mesh are real COGS; breakage and wrong-order waste are silent margin killers  
- Cash: insurer payment cycles vs card-on-site; don’t fund the homeowner’s deductible float with your payroll  
- Seasonality: storm / pollen / move seasons vs winter troughs in many markets

Diligence questions that matter more than memorizing averages:

- What’s the mix last 24 months: **screens vs IGU/pane vs board-up vs specialty vs storefront vs full-unit windows vs (if any) auto**?  
- First-trip completion rate? Supply-house / remake trip %?  
- Average ticket and stops per tech-day by season?  
- % revenue from insurance vs cash vs PM/commercial?  
- Days from measure → glass on site → installed → collected (by product type)?  
- Callback / leak / fog-return cost as % of revenue?  
- Inventory turns and shrinkage/breakage $ last year?  
- Close rate and CAC by source (Google vs LSA vs storm vs referral vs PM)?  
- Tech vacancy, time-to-hire, and which jobs require two-person crews?  
- Insurance posture: which perils do you counsel as claims vs cash? Who owns documentation quality?  
- If franchised: royalty + brand + tech + required local spend; Item 19 spreads; Item 20 churn; HB vs Auto packaging  
- Owner hours on the glass vs on recruiting/dispatch/claims — is this a transferable company?

What that means in plain English:

The *category* has durable demand and can scale truck-by-truck with small crews.  
A *specific shop* can be busy after every hailstorm and broke by February — or quiet because it can’t hire anyone who can set tempered glass without a callback.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

**1. Insurance / glass claims complexity**  
Covered peril vs wear. Deductible math that makes filing irrational on a $350 IGU. Adjuster scope gaps. ACV vs RCV timing. Preferred-vendor steering. Get claim-fluent or stay cash-retail on purpose — mixing them without process is how AR ages and reviews sour.

**2. Weather (demand and production)**  
Storms fill the phone and empty the safe ladder days. Sealant and install quality hate freezing rain and high wind. Feast/famine without a trough plan nukes payroll.

**3. Truck inventory discipline**  
Wrong stock → second trips. Dead stock → cash in racks. Breakage in the van is a special kind of expensive. Screen mesh is easy to overbuy; tempered specialty is easy to under-order.

**4. Tech skill scarcity**  
Setting glass without stress cracks, handling tempered, sealing showers, cutting clean screen jobs fast — this is craft. Brian’s hire-the-tech lens applies hard. Empty vans don’t bill; bad installs bill once and then bleed forever.

**5. Lead gen outside storm week**  
If your “marketing system” is waiting for the next hail cell, you don’t have a company — you have a weather derivative. Paid search CAC, LSA competition, and weak follow-up will humble a wrapped van.

**6. Warranty callbacks / leak purgatory**  
Water finds the bad seal. Fog returns. Stress cracks appear after a cold snap. Factory blames install; install blames order; homeowner blames you. Photo SOPs and clear warranty policy — or a one-star novel.

**7. Measure / order errors (the quiet remake)**  
Wrong Low-E orientation. Missed grid pattern. Tempered required by code and you ordered annealed. 1/8" that doesn’t matter on a screen and destroys an IGU. Remakes murder density and trust.

**8. Route density / windshield death**  
Low-ticket screens across a 40-mile radius with no clustering is a fitness program, not a P&L. Same for one-off specialty with no backhaul plan.

**9. Small-crew capacity ceiling**  
One hero glazier is a job with a logo. Vacation, injury, or resignation zeros the book. Two-person requirements for heavy glass shrink the schedule further.

**10. Cash vs claim counseling ethics (and economics)**  
Pushing claims that will raise premiums for a sub-deductible job burns trust. Leaving money on covered multi-opening storm work leaves margin on the table. Scripts and training matter.

**11. Working capital on “small tickets”**  
Glass orders, payroll, vans, insurance, and marketing burn while IGUs are in transit. Undercapitalized launches stall when the first custom tempered job is late and the credit card is hot.

**12. Easy-entry competition + big brands**  
Handymen with plexi, big-box measure programs, national glass brands, independents, “mobile screen” specialists, and Facebook warriors. Differentiation is speed, neat install, documentation, and warranty that actually shows up — not slogans.

**13. Property-manager / commercial price pressure**  
Light B2B steadies the calendar and trains your team to race to the bottom if you let it. COI, after-hours, and access rules add cost you must price.

**14. Safety / liability**  
Ladders, broken tempered, heavy lifts, shop cuts, silica/dust, finger lacerations. One bad incident is existential. Training and insurance aren’t optional paperwork.

**15. Scope creep into full window replacement / remodeling**  
Glass-only shops that accidentally become window sales companies without estimating, financing, and manufacturer discipline create warranty monsters. Adjacent can be attach; it can also be a second business you didn’t underwrite (see exterior painting / full replacement adjacency).

**16. Screen-lane positioning risk**  
Screens are a brilliant density and trust funnel. They can also brand you as the cheapest mesh guy in town. Price and script the upsell to glass — or drown in low-dollar clog.

**17. Supplier / lead-time dependency**  
Late IGU, wrong coating, damaged crate, discontinued grid. You own the homeowner’s week either way. Dual-source where you can; communicate early.

**18. Seasonality & life-event bunching**  
Spring listings, pre-holiday hosting, post-storm clusters, summer vacation soft patches. Plan inventory and labor for the shape of *your* metro.

**19. Owner still on the glass (or still the only estimator)**  
If the company only works when you’re the emergency board-up and the measure QA, you bought a stressful craft job with insurance forms.

**20. Semi-absentee fantasy**  
Manager salary on thin early revenue is how shops bleed. Mobile glass/screens still need active ownership until density, hiring, and claim process are real — same fragmented-workforce truth as Brian’s mobile teaching.

**21. Franchise / brand stack (if you go that route)**  
Royalty + brand fund + tech + required local spend + rules + territory vs playbook, supplier pricing, lead support, and training. Worth it only if the system fills trucks and protects margin — not because the van wrap looks good. Diligence Item 19 spreads and Item 20 churn.

**22. Confusing “houses have windows” with operator skill**  
Demand is real. Inventory turns, tech bench, claim literacy, and density decide whether *you* get paid.

**23. Mixing auto glass and residential without two P&Ls**  
Some systems offer both. The claim machines, inventory, and skill stacks are cousins — not twins. Blur them in your head and you’ll mis-hire, mis-stock, and mis-forecast (see [auto-glass](/models/auto-glass)).

**24. Review death from visible work**  
Crooked mirrors, silicone smears, scratched frames, screens that bulge. Guests notice. Neighborhood chats move faster than your ad budget.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means window / glass / screens is a **real service-install business** where **insurance literacy, weather, truck stock, tech craft, lead gen, and warranty discipline** *are* the product — not a weekend plexi side hustle and not a passive royalty coupon.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re wired to hire, schedule, and lead field techs (hire-the-tech lens)  
- You like operational puzzles: routing, inventory turns, mix, claim vs cash  
- Small-crew, density-driven days sound energizing — not exhausting  
- You’re willing to learn (or hire for) glass-claim documentation without becoming a shady storm chaser  
- You’re okay living in sealant weather windows and “the dog did it again” screen tickets  
- Home-services field ops excite you more than showroom design theater (blinds energy ≠ this day-to-day)  
- You’ll treat lead gen as a first-class system, not a storm entitlement

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want passive / semi-absentee from month one  
- You refuse to recruit or manage technicians  
- You’re actually looking for a craft job with a logo (you *being* the only glazier)  
- You hate insurance paperwork *and* refuse to build a pure cash-retail engine instead  
- You’re undercapitalized for vans, racks, glass orders, payroll ramp, and marketing  
- Soft-skill conflict in living rooms and with adjusters drains you dry  
- You’re careless with measures and seals — this model punishes sloppy installs in water and one-star reviews  
- You’re shopping a logo because Glass Doctor / Glass Guru / Screenmobile “feel proven” without underwriting mix, CAC, inventory, and Item 19 variance  
- You want to chase five unrelated brands at once (Brian’s two-rabbits warning applies)

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same category either way: demand → dispatch → stocked truck → skilled small crew → dense route → collect → earn the warranty-free next job.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “glass breaks.”

You’re buying the right to run a **dispatch + inventory + small-crew machine** that turns housing stock, storms, pets, and storefront friction into completed, warrantable installs — if you can stock the truck, hire craft, pack the calendar, stay honest on claims, and keep water on the outside of the pane.

Some people should lean **screen-forward** for density and trust, then attach glass.  
Some people should lean **IGU / board-up / insurance-fluent** residential service.  
Some people should lean **light commercial / PM** once residential density is real.  
Some people should buy a tired local book and install systems (resale / acquisition paths exist in the broader glass neighborhood).  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into a vehicle with cleaner recurring routes or a skill stack they can actually staff.

The question isn’t “do windows fail?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful van company that only works when you’re the estimator, the inventory clerk, the claim department, and the backup tech?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs blinds vs handyman vs exterior painting vs plumbing vs auto glass vs window cleaning), that’s a conversation.
