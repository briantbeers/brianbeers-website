The accounting firm’s email is down and payroll is tomorrow.  
A dental practice just got a ransomware scare and the doctor wants someone *accountable* on the phone — not a help-desk chatbot.  
A 40-seat office outgrew “whoever’s nephew can fix the Wi-Fi” and still can’t hire a full-time IT person who won’t leave for a bigger paycheck in six months.

Somebody needs **always-on IT** — monitoring, backups, security stack, tickets answered, vendors coordinated — billed **monthly**, not only when something’s already on fire.

That’s an **IT / MSP** (managed service provider).

Not a Best Buy Geek Squad counter as the whole company story.  
Not pure [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) night-crew contracts (same *sticky B2B account* brain — different factory).  
Not [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (you place *people* on someone else’s payroll; here you sell *outcomes* under *your* stack and SLAs).  
Not “I’ll remote in on evenings and call it a company” wearing a Google Business Profile.

You’re running a **recurring B2B tech-labor** business: MRR from managed agreements − technician / engineer cost − RMM/PSA/security vendor stack − the hole when clients churn or tickets explode while sales of *new* MRR stalls.

Simple on a whiteboard: sign clients → standardize stack → monitor → ticket → expand seats → invoice monthly.  
Hard in real life — because **tech labor scarcity**, **ticket load vs automation**, **vendor stack cost**, **churn**, **cyber liability**, and **selling MRR (not break-fix heroics)** show up before the NOC dashboard looks like a franchise Reel.

CMIT Solutions / TeamLogic IT–class franchise systems, indie neighborhood MSPs, and PE roll-up shops sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The MRR book, the tech bench, and the stack discipline are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local managed IT / MSP operator**: an office (or hybrid home + NOC) that sells **ongoing IT management and security** to small and mid-sized businesses — then staffs technicians who deliver tickets, projects, and vCIO conversations against a standardized tool stack.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Managed services / MRR (primary spine for this page)** | **Break-fix / project / hardware-heavy shop (adjacent factories)** |
|--|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Monthly agreements: monitoring, help desk, patching, backups, security stack, sometimes vCIO | Hourly firefighting, one-off projects, PC repair, commodity hardware resale |
| **Primary constraint** | **MRR book × retention × tech capacity − stack cost − sales ramp** | **Billable hours × utilization − no-shows of demand**; feast/famine |
| **Cash shape** | Recurring; predictable *if* churn stays sane | Spiky; “busy” weeks hide weak enterprise value |
| **Risk feel** | Cyber liability + SLA expectations; labor scarcity | Reputation + unpaid invoices; no compounding book |
| **Owner job early** | Outbound B2B sales of *agreements* + delivery discipline | Owner-as-hero technician forever |

Many real shops sit in a hybrid — MRR *plus* projects *plus* hardware attach. Underwrite the mix honestly — a shop that’s 70% break-fix and “we’ll convert them later” is a different company than one with 70%+ managed agreements and a seat-expansion motion.

Typical menu:

1. **Managed IT / endpoints & users** — the MRR spine (per-user or per-device packages)  
2. **Cybersecurity stack attach** — EDR, email security, MFA, awareness training, MDR (margin *and* liability)  
3. **Cloud / Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace admin** — sticky; license resale often thin margin  
4. **Backup / DR / BCDR** — table stakes; failure is existential for the client *and* you  
5. **vCIO / QBR / roadmap** — stickiness layer; soft skill most tech founders underprice  
6. **Projects / migrations / onboarding** — necessary; don’t let them eat the MRR machine  
7. **Hardware / networking install** — attach revenue; inventory and thin GM traps  
8. **Co-managed IT** (with internal IT at larger clients) — bigger logos, politics, thinner control  
9. **Consumer / residential repair** (Geeks on Site–class adjacency on Brian’s sheet) — **different** buyer, ticket economics, and brand promise — don’t confuse with SMB MSP underwriting

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — outbound to owners / office managers / professional practices; referrals; networking; Google Local; franchise lead systems (if any); prior project → MRR conversion  
2. **Discover / assess** — seats, stack chaos, security posture, who’s the economic buyer, creditworthiness  
3. **Propose / price** — per-user or per-device MRR; scope what’s *in* the agreement vs billable project  
4. **Onboard / standardize** — RMM agents, backups, MFA, documentation — the quiet CapEx of time  
5. **Deliver** — tickets, monitoring alerts, patch windows, QBRs  
6. **Collect / retain / expand** — seat growth, security upsell, sister locations — or lose the book when a cheaper MSP or an in-house hire wins

**Scope lock:** this page is the **local B2B managed IT / MSP** — recurring MRR, tech labor, ticket delivery, vendor stack, churn, cyber risk.  
It is **not** [print-signage](/models/print-signage) CapEx presses (same B2B account hunger; different factory).  
It is **not** [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) wage-markup float (related *people scarcity*; different invoice).  
It is **not** [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) night contracts (related sticky B2B; different liability and labor market).  
It is **not** pure cybersecurity consulting / audit-only shops (Brian’s backlog lists that as a *separate* row — fear-driven sales, productized audits; adjacent, not identical).  
It is **not** consumer walk-in PC repair or “Geeks on Site–only” residential as the spine.  
It is **not** a SaaS product company or a national RMM vendor.

Brian’s published corpus treats **IT Support Services** as a real Business Services row on the **250 Franchise Ideas** sheet — **TeamLogic IT**, **CMIT Solutions**, and **Geeks on Site** named as category proof — and on the model backlog as **recurring MRR; tech labor; churn**. CORE teaching’s **people / team-based** lens applies: growth = recruit + retain technicians *and* sell customers. Treat named systems (and indie MSPs / PE roll-ups) as **proof the category professionalizes** — not as a pitch. ## How money comes in

You sell **recurring managed agreements and collectible MRR** — not “we fixed a printer once” and not a LinkedIn post about ransomware fear.

Education pricing / book shape (national operator / FDD ranges; metros sit higher; secondary markets and underpriced books sit lower):

- CMIT Solutions–class 2026 FDD Item 19 education (CY2025): **single-outlet** franchisees averaging roughly **~$517k** gross (median ~**$310k**); multi-outlet franchisee portfolios averaging roughly **~$1.61M** (median ~**$1.05M**) — *portfolio* ≠ one territory promise  
- Same Item 19 education: managed-service gross margin averages often disclosed near the high-**60% / low-70%** band; hardware/software sales GM much thinner (teen to high-**20%** education shapes)  
- Technician cost as % of managed/professional services revenue: education shapes often near the high-**20% / low-30%** band in those disclosures — labor *is* COGS  
- TeamLogic IT–class Item 7 education often roughly **~$116k–$151k**; Item 19 quartile education (where disclosed) shows a **wide** spread from low-six-figure books to multi-million first-quartile portfolios — underwrite the *distribution*  
- Indie MSP operator benchmarks commonly discussed: MRR as **~60–80%+** of revenue in healthier shops; per-user SMB packages often roughly **~$150–$300+/user/mo** depending on security attach; annual logo churn education often **~8–15%** (best-in-class lower)

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Contracted MRR** — seats × package price × retention, not “we bill a lot of hours in March”  
2. **Net revenue retention** — expansions and security attach vs downgrades and logo loss  
3. **Tech capacity after ticket load** — overloaded benches create churn; idle benches kill margin  
4. **Stack cost discipline** — RMM, PSA, EDR, email security, backup, SOC — every tool is a subscription eating contribution  
5. **Sales of new MRR** — outbound / referral machine that books *agreements*, not just projects  
6. **Project / hardware mix** — useful attach; dangerous if it becomes the identity  
7. **Collections** — monthly ACH beats chasing Net-45 dental offices after a ransomware week

Gross margin intuition (operator / FDD education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Managed services: often the high-GM line *before* you count fully loaded tech cost, owner hero hours, and stack licenses  
- Hardware / license resale: busy calendars, thin contribution — don’t confuse with MSP health  
- Labor: the career-defining line — scarcity raises wages; ticket spikes raise overtime and burnout  
- Net on a single shop is often a **wider** quartile spread than “everybody needs IT” Instagram — volume rises; tech headcount, stack, cyber insurance, and sales payroll rise with it

You’re not a help-desk freelancer with a LLC.  
You’re an **MRR + tech-labor + stack** company wearing a SOC dashboard.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Businesses still run on email, cloud apps, endpoints, and regulated data. Hiring a full internal IT department is expensive. Ransomware, insurance questionnaires, and “the office can’t work if systems are down” don’t disappear because ChatGPT exists.

Good times → seat expansion, security upsells, multi-site rollouts, project migrations.  
Tight times → clients shop price, delay projects, push for in-house hires *or* cheaper MSPs — churn and ticket complaints rise together. The shop that only sold break-fix heroics without a real agreement book feels the chill first.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Print-signage](/models/print-signage) rides **project mix + equipment CapEx + B2B sales** on businesses that must be *seen*.  
[Staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) rides **wage markup + float + outbound sales** on employers that need *bodies*.  
[Commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) rides **recurring night contracts + labor %** on buildings that stay dirty.  
IT / MSP rides **recurring MRR + tech labor scarcity + ticket load + stack cost + cyber liability** on businesses that must *stay online*.

Same “I won’t DIY this forever” demand emotion. Different cancel button, different lawsuit, different Meta bill.

That’s the engine. Not one viral ransomware Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one shop

A single MSP lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Tech labor scarcity (the career-defining line)**  
Tier-1/2 techs, senior engineers, and security-capable people are expensive and mobile. When your only senior who knows the dental clients’ imaging stack leaves for a $20k bump, capacity and quality drop before Google reviews catch up. Owner-as-hero engineer looks great on LinkedIn and terrible on a succession plan. CORE’s people/team-based point applies: you sell customers *and* you recruit labor forever.

**Ticket load vs automation**  
RMM alerts, password resets, onboarding/offboarding, printer drama, “Wi-Fi is slow.” Without documentation, automation, and tiered triage, every client becomes a custom snowflake and your bench drowns. Over-automation without human escalation creates silent SLA failures. Ticket *volume per tech* is a P&L and a burnout metric.

**Vendor / tool stack cost**  
PSA, RMM, EDR, email filtering, backup, MFA, SOC/MDR, Microsoft partner tools, phone systems — each line item is recurring. Stack sprawl (“we bought every security logo for the sales deck”) is how contribution dies. Standardization wins; snowflake stacks lose.

**Churn & net retention**  
Losing three mid-size clients can erase a year of new logos. Churn drivers: price shopping, unresolved tickets, a new internal IT hire, PE roll-up of the *client*, or a security incident that destroys trust. Expansion (seats, sites, security) is how healthy books compound; logo acquisition alone is a treadmill.

**Cyber liability & incident response**  
You’re in the blast radius when a client gets hit — contract language, cyber insurance, response playbooks, and whether you *actually* enforced MFA/backups. Fear sells; liability stays. Underwrite insurance limits and exclusions like a first-class diligence item.

**Sales of MRR (not project theater)**  
Facility managers and practice owners do not buy managed agreements like a homeowner buys a new laptop. Trust, assessments, security questionnaires, and “we already have a guy” create lag. Shops that wait for inbound Maps leads while a competitor runs outbound + referral discipline quietly lose the sticky books. Selling *projects* is easier dopamine; selling *MRR* is the business.

**Onboarding debt**  
Taking a messy client “as-is” without a paid remediation project imports years of undocumented chaos into your ticket queue. Scope discipline at onboarding is retention insurance.

**Client concentration & vertical fit**  
One law firm at 25% of MRR feels great until they leave. Vertical specialization (dental, legal, accounting, nonprofits) can improve delivery *and* concentrate risk.

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only closer *and* the only escalation engineer *and* the only person who can calm a ransomware call at 2 a.m., you own a stressful NOC job with cyber insurance and a personal guarantee on the lease.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD secondary writeups, “average franchise” pages, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing MRR by client, churn, ticket volumes, stack spend, cyber insurance, and tech payroll.

**Capacity / revenue intuition:**

- CMIT–class single-outlet Item 19 education (CY2025): average ~**$517k**, median ~**$310k**, high end education near ~**$2.9M** — wide, not a promise  
- CMIT–class multi-outlet franchisee portfolio education: average ~**$1.61M**, median ~**$1.05M** — don’t confuse with one-territory AUV  
- TeamLogic–class one-location education (where disclosed): averages often discussed near mid-**$600k** with median nearer ~**$400k** and first-quartile portfolios much higher — verify the actual FDD tables in front of you  
- Indie MSP education bands often cluster **~$500k–$2M** for common 5–15 person shops; top-quartile stories run higher with strong MRR and automation

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Lean indie MSP (home + tools + working capital): often discussed **~$50k–$150k+** before “we need a second senior tech / real cyber stack”  
- CMIT Solutions–class Item 7 education (2026): roughly **~$106k–$159k**  
- TeamLogic IT–class Item 7 education (2026 secondary): roughly **~$110k–$151k** depending on filing/source — **verify the actual Item 7**  
- Working capital: months of tech payroll + stack licenses + sales ramp before MRR compounds; cyber insurance deposits and deductibles are not footnotes

**Mix / margin education shape:**

- Managed services GM education often **~50–70%+** *before* you lie to yourself about owner labor; FDD managed-service GM disclosures in the CMIT–class tables sit high — then technician % and overhead still apply  
- Hardware/software GM often **~10–30%** education shapes — volume vanity  
- Annual churn education commonly **~8–15%** logos; model dollar churn and expansion separately  
- Technician cost as % of services revenue: watch the FDD/operator bands near ~**25–35%** as a diligence question, not a target you invent

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **MRR vs project vs hardware** (12 months); MRR as % of total?  
- Logo and dollar churn; net revenue retention; top-10 client concentration?  
- Per-user / per-device pricing; what’s included vs billable; security attach rate?  
- Ticket volume per tech; after-hours load; documentation / automation maturity?  
- Tech headcount, tenure, fully loaded cost, who holds client relationships?  
- Stack list and monthly spend (RMM/PSA/EDR/backup/SOC/etc.)?  
- Cyber insurance: limits, exclusions, claims history; incident response retainer?  
- Sales: who sells MRR; pipeline of *agreements*; CAC vs LTV realism?  
- Onboarding backlog / inherited snowflake clients?  
- If franchised: royalty + brand fund + required vendors/tech fees; does the system shorten sales ramp, NOC leverage, and stack pricing — or only a logo?

Same brain you’d use buying any recurring B2B services shop: account quality, labor bench, delivery capacity, and whether *you* want tickets, cyber risk, and outbound MRR selling in your week.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Tech labor scarcity**  
Good technicians are scarce, expensive, and recruitable by bigger MSPs, internal IT, and cloud employers. Walk-offs reset capacity overnight. “We’ll just hire from Indeed” is not a staffing plan. Wage pressure never sleeps while clients resist MRR increases.

**2. Ticket load drowning the bench**  
Alert noise, password resets, unmanaged onboarding, and snowflake environments turn a “managed” book into break-fix wearing a contract. Without triage, documentation, and automation, every new logo makes delivery worse — then churn follows.

**3. Vendor / security stack sprawl**  
Too many tools, overlapping agents, rising license prices, and “we need this logo to win the deal” purchasing. Stack cost is silent COGS. Standardization is ops discipline, not a boring preference.

**4. Churn & weak net retention**  
You can out-sell a leaky bucket for a while. Then sales fatigue meets logo loss. Price shoppers, unresolved tickets, PE acquisitions of clients, and “we hired someone in-house” are structural — not bad luck.

**5. Cyber liability & incident theater**  
Ransomware, BEC, lost backups, MFA gaps you “meant to enforce.” Contracts, insurance, and response capability matter more than the fear-based sales deck. One bad incident can erase years of trust — and invite lawyers.

**6. Selling MRR (the hard sale)**  
Projects are easier to close than 36-month managed agreements. Owners who only sell migrations and hope for conversion own a project shop. Outbound, referrals, assessments, and the courage to walk from bad-fit clients are the growth engine.

**7. Break-fix / hardware mix trap**  
Busy benches, thin margins, no enterprise value. Hardware revenue flatters the top line and distracts from MRR health. Underwrite the mix or inherit a treadmill.

**8. Onboarding debt & undocumented clients**  
Cheap “lift and shift” onboarding imports chaos. Every undocumented server is a future after-hours ticket with your name on it.

**9. Scope creep & “you’re our IT department”**  
Unlimited free after-hours, “can you also manage the door system / cameras / phone vendor,” and unpaid project work destroy contribution. Change orders and clear SOWs are the product; fear of churn is how margin dies.

**10. Client concentration**  
One vertical, one big logo, one referral partner. Concentration feels efficient until the cancel email.

**11. After-hours / on-call burnout**  
Someone answers at 2 a.m. If it’s always you or your only senior, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a pager with MRR.

**12. Pricing below fully loaded cost**  
Copying a competitor’s per-user rate without modeling tech cost, stack, after-hours, and cyber insurance is how “busy” MSPs lose money. Markup theater ≠ margin.

**13. Competition density & PE roll-ups**  
Other franchise territories, indie MSPs, national remote MSPs, and roll-ups with cheaper capital. Differentiating on “we have RMM too” is not a moat. Relationship + QBR + security outcomes + response speed are.

**14. Easy-entry noise at the bottom**  
Anyone with a ConnectWise trial and a Facebook ad can *look* like a competitor by Monday. You carry insurance, SLAs, stack cost, and employment risk. Amateurs undercutting you are both competition *and* why buyers are skeptical.

**15. Owner-as-hero engineer / closer**  
If only you convert agreements *and* only you escalate tickets *and* only you run the ransomware bridge call, you own a high-stress technical sales job with cyber liability.

**16. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, required vendors, tech fees, and territory rules help only if they fill the MRR book, improve stack pricing, and shorten sales/delivery ramp. A logo does not hire a Tier-2 tech on Thursday.

**17. Confusing “every business needs IT” headlines with “your collectible MRR after churn, tech wages, stack invoices, and cyber insurance”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday ticket queue and your churn report.

**18. Multi-unit fantasy before one-unit mastery**  
Roll-up stories exist in mature MSP systems. They’re also how people buy a second territory while the first still needs them on every P1 ticket.

**19. Buying a “book” that was one engineer + one Meta account + soft contracts**  
Customer lists look like assets until post-close you discover the top clients were the seller’s personal relationships, the stack is a mess, and half the agreements are month-to-month handshake deals.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means IT / MSP is a **recurring MRR + scarce tech-labor** business where ticket overload, stack cost, churn, and cyber risk decide whether the NOC dashboard survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can sell B2B *agreements* (or hire and manage someone who will) — pipeline math energizes you  
- MRR, churn, ticket-per-tech, and stack-cost dashboards feel like the job  
- You’re capitalized for tech payroll + tool stack + sales ramp — and sober about cyber insurance  
- You respect that technicians *are* the factory and you’ll recruit/retain them in a tight labor market  
- You’ve compared this door to [print-signage](/models/print-signage) (CapEx projects), [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting) (wage markup float), and [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) (recurring night contracts) — and still want the MSP stack  
- You’re honest about semi-absentee: sales + senior tech leadership cost real EBITDA at one unit; math improves at multi-unit *after* systems and escalation paths exist

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a passive “recurring IT money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to tickets, after-hours escalations, sales cycles, or cyber risk conversations  
- You’re undercapitalized for tech wages + stack through a slow sales ramp  
- You actually want **consumer PC repair**, **pure cyber audit consulting**, **janitorial routes**, or **staffing float** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll beat the other MSP on price” was a moat  
- You need the trendiest security logo more than a timeless MRR book with documented delivery in *your* trade area

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: MRR, tech bench, ticket load, stack cost, churn, cyber liability. A brand (CMIT / TeamLogic–class peers) is only worth the fee if it shortens sales ramp, delivery playbooks, and vendor leverage — not because the website has a lock icon.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** B2B door (janitorial contracts, staffing with a funder, print accounts) or a non-cyber vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “every business needs IT support.”

You’re buying the right to run a **local managed-services machine** — if you can hold a real MRR book, staff technicians who don’t walk off with the quality, keep ticket load inside human capacity, discipline the vendor stack, sell agreements without living on break-fix dopamine, carry cyber liability with eyes open, and not confuse a busy PSA board for free money.

Some people should buy a tired MSP and fix stack standardization, ticket triage, and MRR sales.  
Some people should open lean indie and prove retention before a second senior hire.  
Some people should choose [print-signage](/models/print-signage), [staffing-recruiting](/models/staffing-recruiting), or [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) and never take on cyber blast radius.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do businesses need managed IT?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful technical sales job with cyber insurance, a bloated tool stack, a churn report, and a personal guarantee when the only senior who can clear P1 tickets texts “I’m done”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other business-services vehicles vs acquisition or home-service paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
