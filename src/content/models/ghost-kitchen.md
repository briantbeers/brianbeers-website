7:42 p.m. Three tablets light up at once — “Brand A” burrito bowl, “Brand B” wings, “Brand C” late-night smash burgers — all printing into the same 280-square-foot bay.  
One courier is already at the door. Packaging for Brand B is wrong. A one-star just dropped because fries steamed themselves into mush on a thirty-minute ride, and the shared-kitchen rent doesn’t care that your “five virtual restaurants” are really one stressed line and one tired lead cook.

Somebody wants **food that shows up hot, consistent, and searchable on a marketplace** — without walking into a dining room — and an operator who thought they bought **rent arbitrage** only to discover they also bought **platform fees**, **multi-brand chaos**, and **rating roulette**.

That’s a **ghost kitchen / delivery-only** business (virtual brands + marketplace fulfillment — not a strip QSR lobby, not event catering).

Not [catering](/models/catering) (same *kitchen + delivery* neighborhood — catering sells **booked events / corporate programs with deposits and a sales calendar**; this page is **marketplace tickets × platform settlement**, not planner RFPs).  
Not [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (same *food + labor* physics — QSR sells **walk-in / drive-thru tickets and a guest-facing site**; ghost kitchens sell **app discovery with no dining room as the spine**).  
Not [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) (same *location-food* family — you sell *morning caffeine habit*, not *evening delivery search share*).  
Not [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) (same *non-traditional food door* emotion — trucks/trailers sell **events, permits, weather, and a moving storefront**; this page is a **fixed kitchen + virtual storefronts**).  
Not “I’ll list five fake restaurants on DoorDash and Instagram my way to freedom” as the whole *rent + fees + ratings + utilization* story.

You’re running a **marketplace throughput machine**: orders × average check − **food & packaging** − **kitchen labor** − **platform commissions / ads** − bay rent − remakes / refunds − the cash hole when a **rating cliff** kills placement and your “cheap rent” still comes due on a soft Tuesday.

Simple on a whiteboard: cheaper kitchen + no servers + apps find customers.  
Hard in real life — because **platform fees**, **multi-brand operations**, **quality control off-prem**, **delivery ratings**, **rent vs utilization**, **brand confusion**, and **thin unit economics** show up before the model looks like “restaurants without the restaurant.”

Indie virtual-brand operators, restaurant kitchens running off-peak “ghost” menus, shared commissary / cloud-kitchen bay tenants, and delivery-only concepts sell overlapping physics. The logo stack is optional. The commission, the packaging, and the star rating are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **delivery-first foodservice operator**: a production kitchen (owned industrial bay, shared cloud/commissary stall, or dual-use restaurant kitchen after lobby hours), menus engineered to **survive a bag and a ride**, tablets / POS integrations for one or more marketplaces, packaging and labeling per virtual brand, and — if you’re honest — a **kitchen lead** who can run peak dinner without you remaking every wrong bag.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — and don’t blur either with catering or QSR):**

| | **Pure ghost / virtual-brand kitchen (primary spine)** | **Hybrid: existing restaurant + virtual brands (attach)** |
|--|--------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Marketplace orders only — one or many virtual brands from one bay | Incremental delivery volume from a kitchen you already pay for |
| **Primary constraint** | **Orders × check − food − packaging − labor − platform fees − bay rent** | Same equation on the *attach*; lobby still carries prime site rent |
| **Cash shape** | Dinner / late spikes; soft mid-days; settlement lag on apps | Daypart fill when walk-in softens; collision with lunch rush prep |
| **Risk feel** | Fee stack + rating death + utilization of fixed bay | Brand confusion + kitchen collision + “free” volume that isn’t free |
| **Owner job early** | Ops + menu travel quality + platform ads / SEO + remake discipline | Protect dine-in quality while delivery tablets scream |

Many real shops are **hybrids** — a [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) or pizza DELCO box with heavy off-premise mix is *adjacent*, not identical. Underwrite whether *your* deal is a true delivery-only bay or a restaurant using “ghost brands” as a second skin. [Catering](/models/catering) can share a commissary address and still be a different company: planners, deposits, and event calendars vs app search and courier handoffs.

Typical revenue / brand mix:

1. **Core virtual brand(s)** — the spine that must travel and rate well  
2. **Additional virtual brands** from the same mise — volume lever *and* confusion tax  
3. **Marketplace promotions / sponsored placement** — paid visibility that can eat contribution  
4. **Direct / first-party ordering** (website, white-label) — margin rescue when it works; CAC when it doesn’t  
5. **Pickup window / limited walk-up** (hybrid) — commission escape hatch with different trust economics  
6. **Not the same company:** full [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) lobby throughput; [catering](/models/catering) event pipeline; [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) morning spine; [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) truck/trailer events; grocery “dark store” retail

Typical loop:

1. **Discover** — marketplace search, cuisine filters, ads, past orders, map proximity  
2. **Order** — app cart, upsells, promo codes, small-order fees  
3. **Make** — ticket routing across brands, portion, travel-ready pack, label accuracy  
4. **Hand off** — courier wait times, bag integrity, “order not ready” penalties  
5. **Settle** — platform payout minus commission, refunds, chargebacks, ad spend  
6. **Rate / retain** — stars and comments decide tomorrow’s placement — or you disappear on page three

## How money comes in

You sell **settled marketplace (and direct) orders** — not “we have five brands on the app” and not a cheap bay as the whole P&L.

Education pricing / check shape (national operator / industry chatter; metros and protein-heavy menus sit higher; promo-heavy menus sit tighter — **verify your market**):

- Delivery average checks commonly discussed roughly **~$18–$35+** depending on cuisine and combo engineering; family packs lift AOV  
- Volume wins when **ticket time**, **pack accuracy**, and **travel quality** hold under multi-brand peaks  
- Platform commissions commonly discussed roughly **~15–30%** of order value depending on tier, ads, and promo participation — underwrite **net after commission**, not gross GMV  
- Delivery menus often need **~15–25%+ price uplift** vs a hypothetical dine-in parity ticket just to *attempt* fee recovery — guests still comparison-shop  
- Packaging is a **every-order COGS** (education often roughly **~4–7%** of sales or **~$1–$3+/order**) — no dine-in offset  
- Franchise / brand shops (where virtual-brand licensing or cloud-kitchen systems exist) live on **system playbooks × controllable costs**; indies live on **local platform SEO + owner presence** — different marketing tax, same fee/packaging physics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Orders × average check (net of refunds)** — empty tablets with pretty brand photos still burn bay rent  
2. **Food %** — travel-friendly recipes, overportion, waste, commodity spikes  
3. **Packaging %** — containers, liners, labels, insulated bags, eco fees  
4. **Labor %** — smaller crew than full-service, still real; multi-brand complexity raises heads-per-order  
5. **Platform commission + ads / sponsored placement** — the career-defining tax on *gross*  
6. **Bay / kitchen occupancy** — “cheap rent” only helps if utilization and contribution cover it  
7. **Remakes / refunds / “not ready” penalties** — silent margin death  
8. **Ratings / placement** — organic discovery is a product feature you can lose overnight  
9. **Brand count vs execution depth** — more logos ≠ more contribution if the line collapses  
10. **Kitchen lead quality** — the silent line item that decides whether *you* own a company or a second job with tablets

Gross / cost intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- **Food** education often roughly **~26–34%** of *gross* marketplace sales depending on cuisine and waste  
- **Platform fees** education often roughly **~15–30%** — treat as a near-prime-cost peer, not a “marketing nice-to-have”  
- **Packaging** education often roughly **~4–7%**  
- **Labor** education often roughly **~18–28%** (no FOH servers; still peak cooks and leads) — multi-brand chaos pushes you out of band  
- Occupancy can look “cheap” at **~$1.5k–$7k+/mo** shared-bay bands in many markets (standalone industrial leases sit different) — **utilization** decides whether cheap is actually cheap  
- Illustrative single-bay models in industry education sometimes show **mid-single-digit net** on healthy volume — and **negative** when orders, AOV, or fees move a few points the wrong way  
- Net on a single ghost kitchen is often a **wide** quartile spread — “no dining room” Instagram ≠ your Tuesday contribution after commissions

You’re not five restaurants with one lease.  
You’re a **fee + packaging + ratings + utilization** company wearing cuisine logos.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People still order food to the couch, the hotel, and the office desk. Wage pressure and dining-room rents change the *math*; they don’t erase the *habit* of tapping an app when cooking feels hard. Grocery kits and QSR first-party apps change the *competition*; they don’t erase demand for someone else’s kitchen tonight.

Good times → higher AOV, more late-night, willingness to pay for “new” virtual brands, denser courier networks.  
Tight times → guests hunt promos, skip add-ons, punish fees with one-stars; platforms still take a cut; packaging and wage floors don’t ask permission; placement ads get more expensive when organic dies.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Catering](/models/catering) rides **booked events × ticket − food − peak labor − kitchen − logistics − deposits**.  
[Qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) rides **tickets × check − food − labor − rent − fees − delivery** on *habit lunch with a door*.  
[Coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) rides **morning tickets − beverage COGS − labor − rent**.  
[Mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) rides **event/street tickets − permits − weather − equipment**.  
Ghost kitchen rides **marketplace orders × check − food − packaging − labor − platform fees − bay rent − ratings physics** — foodservice with a **digital storefront** instead of (or instead of relying on) a lobby.

Same “someone is hungry *now*” demand emotion. Different invoice, different co-owner named **the algorithm**, different Saturday.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “I made $20k/month with five fake restaurants” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one kitchen

A single ghost / delivery-only bay lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Platform fees (the career-defining tax)**  
Commissions, payment processing, sponsored listings, promo co-pays, and refund policies that often favor the guest. Industry education commonly puts headline commissions roughly **~15–30%**. Drive QSR P&L inventories in Brian’s world (Wingstop / McD / Dunkin / Pizza Hut teaching) treat third-party delivery as a **real variable cost** even when delivery is only a *mix* — here it is often the *channel*. Underwrite contribution **after** fees, ads, and refunds — or watch “GMV growth” hollow out the bank account.

**Rent arbitrage vs utilization**  
You skipped the A-street dining room — good. You still pay for a hooded bay, grease interceptor access, refrigeration, and peak electricity. Shared cloud kitchens look cheap until unused hours and mandatory hours stack. A “$2,500 bay” that sits soft six nights a week is not arbitrage; it’s a fixed cost with tablets. Underwrite **orders per bay-hour**, not the brochure’s rent comparison to a full-service restaurant.

**Multi-brand operations (the volume fantasy)**  
Two or three well-executed brands can raise discovery. Five-to-seven brands on one line can raise **SKU chaos, wrong bags, and one-star contagion**. Marginal logo cost is low; marginal *execution* cost is not. Backlog teaching names **brand confusion** for a reason — guests, couriers, and cooks all get lost.

**Quality control off-prem**  
You don’t control the ride, the porch time, or the guest’s microwave decision. You *do* control menu design for travel, vented vs sealed packaging, hold times, and “don’t send fries that die in eight minutes.” Off-prem QC is the product. Restaurant plating ego is how ratings die.

**Delivery ratings & placement**  
Stars and recent reviews are inventory. A brick-and-mortar can survive a bad week with drive-by habit; a pure ghost brand often cannot. Trust-gap research in industry chatter (many diners prefer restaurants with a visible physical location) is the diligence flag: your marketing tax may be permanent, not temporary.

**Thin unit economics**  
Food + packaging + fees can consume the ticket before labor and rent speak. Small drops in AOV or orders/day flip illustrative mid-single-digit nets to red. Volume is the lever — and volume without contribution is a job with more tablets.

**Kitchen labor & lead dependency**  
Fewer bodies than a dining room; still dual-market pain — you sell guests *and* sell shifts. Owner-as-packer “savings” is unpaid labor with a rating bomb waiting. If only *you* know which sauce goes with Brand B, you bought a job.

**Menu engineering for the bag**  
Bowls, burritos, wings, pizza, smash burgers often travel better than plated steakhouse theater, dressed salads, or fries without a packaging plan. Data from platforms can cut losers fast — if you actually cut them.

**Direct-order escape hatch**  
First-party ordering can rescue margin — and introduces CAC, website ops, and courier or white-label delivery cost. Bag inserts are not a strategy until repeat rate proves it.

**Health regs, permits & insurance**  
Kitchen permits, food-handler certs, cottage-food myths that die under scrutiny, aggregator onboarding, GL / WC / product liability. Shared kitchens add landlord / operator politics. Health-department reality is why “start from my apartment Instant Pot” fantasies fail outside narrow legal exceptions.

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many pure ghosts is **owner on peak tablets, packaging, and review response**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until a lead protects food %, pack accuracy, and ratings without you. Family-flexibility buyers: read the **dinner/late tax** honestly before the fifth virtual logo.

Treat public blogs, cloud-kitchen marketing decks, “average ghost kitchen profit” pages, Brian’s Fast Food Shorts / QSR delivery-fee adjacency, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull trailing weekly orders by brand/platform, AOV, food %, packaging, fully-loaded labor, commission + ad spend, refund/remake rate, bay cost, settlement timing, and whether a real kitchen lead exists.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Lean shared-bay single-brand tests and multi-brand industrial kitchens are different animals — don’t paste a $30k “test” story onto a six-figure build  
- Illustrative education models sometimes assume roughly **~60–120+ orders/day** before fixed costs feel friendly — *shape*, not a starter promise  
- Utilization of peak dinner hours matters more than logo count  
- Single-bay owner cash after debt, lead wage, fees, and soft nights is often **much tighter** than “no rent for a dining room” headlines

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Virtual brand from an **existing restaurant kitchen**: often discussed as **low hundreds to low thousands** (photos, packaging, menu setup) — prove demand before new rent

- Industry “ghost vs full restaurant” contrasts sometimes cite roughly **~$75k–$200k** vs much higher full-service bands — useful * CapEx contrast*, not your bid

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Platform fees (the career-defining cut)**  
Fifteen to thirty percent of the ticket is not “marketing.” It is a co-owner. Sponsored placement to stay visible can push effective take higher. Refunds and chargebacks often land on you. Growing GMV while contribution shrinks is how busy kitchens stay broke. Drive QSR teaching in Brian’s world already treats delivery commissions as real when delivery is only a mix — here the mix is often *everything*.

**2. Multi-brand ops complexity**  
More logos, more menus, more labels, more allergen matrices, more ways to bag the wrong sauce. Peak hour is when three brands collide. The “marginal cost of a new brand is near zero” pitch ignores the marginal cost of **errors**. Two excellent brands beat seven confused ones.

**3. Quality control off-prem**  
You own the food until the courier leaves — then you own the review anyway. Hold times, vented packaging, fries policy, temperature abuse, missing utensils. Restaurant plating that doesn’t survive the bag is a ratings machine pointed at your face. Specs for *travel* are the product.

**4. Delivery ratings & algorithmic placement**  
A rating cliff can erase organic discovery overnight. Responding to reviews is ops, not ego. Fake-looking brands and authenticity skepticism (including marketplace anecdotes about resold frozen food) raise the trust tax. No dining room means fewer ways to recover a bad first impression in person.

**5. Rent vs utilization (arbitrage that isn’t)**  
Cheap industrial or shared-kitchen rent still burns on soft nights. Mandatory hours, CAM-like fees, parking for couriers, and grease compliance don’t care that Tuesday tablets were quiet. Underwrite orders per productive hour. Dual-use restaurant kitchens collide with dine-in prep and create brand confusion for guests who thought Brand X was a real address.

**6. Brand confusion (guest, courier, cook, and Google)**  
Similar logos, similar stock photos, similar “local” stories. Guests can’t find you again. Couriers grab the wrong bag. Cooks swap recipes under pressure. Search results dilute. Backlog lists **brand confusion** beside fees and rent for a reason — clarity is strategy.

**7. Thin unit economics**  
Food + packaging + fees can leave a sliver for labor, rent, and owner income. Illustrative models that “work” at 80–100 orders/day fail when AOV drops $3 or commissions tick up. Promo addiction trains guests to never pay full ticket. Bottom-quartile ghosts look busy on dashboards and empty at the bank.

**8. Labor show-up at dinner/late peaks**  
Ghost kitchens don’t erase labor; they concentrate it. Call-outs at 5:30 p.m. don’t wait. Multi-brand training raises the cost of turnover. Owner-as-hero packer is unpaid labor with a one-star waiting.

**9. Packaging cost & waste**  
Every order needs a container system. Eco mandates and guest expectations raise cost. Wrong packaging destroys food quality and ratings. Packaging inventory ties cash.

**10. Settlement lag & cash timing**  
Sales look fine while payroll and food invoices come due before platform payouts clear. Soft-open promo burn. Owners who spend week-one GMV like salary discover week-four fee statements.

**11. Trust gap vs physical restaurants**  
Many diners prefer brands with a place they could theoretically visit. Pure ghosts pay in ads, photography, social proof, and perfect packs to compensate. Hybrids with a real door inherit different physics — and different rent.

**12. Platform dependency / rule changes**  
Tier changes, fee changes, ranking changes, category competition, and “preferred partner” pressure. You don’t control the storefront landlord named DoorDash / Uber Eats / Grubhub-class platforms. Diversifying platforms adds tablet chaos; staying mono-platform adds concentration risk.

**13. Menu / cuisine copycats**  
Low conceptual barriers: a bay, a camera, a stock logo, and a marketplace account. Competitors can clone your bestseller faster than your lease amortizes. Dense delivery zones split tickets; rent doesn’t split.

**14. Kitchen CapEx & equipment downtime**  
Hoods, refrigeration, fryers, packaging stations, tablet mounts. A dead freezer at peak is lost sales *and* ratings. Shared kitchens fight you for storage and hours. CapEx surprises are how “cheap test” stories become personal loans.

**15. Blurring into catering, QSR, or truck physics**  
Trying to be [catering](/models/catering) event pipeline + [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) lobby + [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) events + five virtual brands without separate operating systems creates sludge. Catering’s deposits and planner calendar are a *different* backlog model — don’t accidentally buy ghost-kitchen fee math while thinking you bought catering (or the reverse). Scope clarity is strategy.

**16. Promo / ad addiction**  
Boosted placement fills the tablet and trains unprofitable guests. Turning ads off collapses orders; leaving them on collapses contribution. Adults track **paid vs organic** and contribution by channel.

**17. Allergen / labeling / wrong-bag liability**  
Multi-brand lines raise mix-up risk. A wrong allergen pack is not a “remake” — it’s a crisis. Label discipline is insurance, not admin.

**18. Training time vs turnover**  
Travel specs, brand SOPs, and pack standards take time; turnover resets the line every month. Training cost is recurring. A beautiful brand deck without a trained bench is fiction.

**19. Multi-bay / second-city fantasy without a bench**  
Second bay before the first has a lead and stable contribution is how you own two fee stacks. Development pressure without utilization proof is capital stress — underwrite the *people and contribution plan*, not the map of dots.

**20. Owner-as-hero tablet babysitter**  
If only you can fix the integrations, only you can answer one-stars, and only you can pack Brand C at 8 p.m., you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a dinner-shift career with a bay lease, a commission statement, and a logo pack.

**21. Confusing “delivery is the future / no dining room rent” headlines with “your collectible net after food, packaging, platform fees, ads, labor, bay rent, remakes, ratings, and soft nights”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Tuesday contribution and your shared-kitchen invoice.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means ghost kitchens are a **fee + packaging + ratings + utilization** business where empty tablets, a soft night, a fee tier change, a multi-brand mistake, a rating cliff, and a missing lead decide whether the company survives the next wage hike — and the next Friday dinner rush.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can live inside **dinner/late peaks + contribution dashboards + review response** until a real kitchen lead owns the tablets — or you’ve already capitalized a manager-led plan with proof  
- Fee math, packaging specs, and travel-menu engineering energize you more than they nauseate you  
- Platform statements, refund disputes, and rating triage feel like diligence, not boredom  
- You’re honest about **brand count** — you’ll underwrite execution depth, not logo vanity  
- Recruiting kitchen labor (and a lead) is a job you’re willing to own  
- You’ve compared this door to [catering](/models/catering) (pipeline + deposits), [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (lobby tickets), [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) (morning beverage), and [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) (truck/events) — and still want the marketplace bay  
- You’re honest that food businesses are an **ownership door with an hours tax** (Brian’s frame) — not passive income with virtual logos

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted passive “delivery is booming” income from month one  
- You’re allergic to dinner peaks, one-star triage, fee statements, or packaging debates  
- You’re undercapitalized for bay + packaging + ad burn and assumed “no dining room” erased working capital  
- You actually want a **catering calendar**, a **QSR lobby**, a **morning coffee box**, or a **food truck** — different underwriting  
- You thought five virtual brands erased contribution math  
- You thought platform apps were a free sales team without a co-owner cut  
- You need the trendiest “cloud kitchen” aesthetic more than timeless pack quality + fee discipline in *your* zone

Franchise / licensed virtual-brand systems vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: orders, food, packaging, fees, ratings, utilization. A brand pack is only worth the fee if it shortens menu travel engineering, packaging, and acquisition — not because five logos photographed well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **smoother-hours** service vehicle, a ticket-based food box with delivery as *attach*, catering as a pipeline business, or a non-food door first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition. Some will correctly use ghost brands as a **low-CapEx test** inside an existing kitchen — underwrite that as a *test*, not as proof the permanent factory prints.

## Risks (the short list)

- Platform fee / ad stack → contribution wipeout  
- Rating cliff → discovery death  
- Multi-brand errors / wrong bags → refund + reputation spiral  
- Soft utilization + bay rent → fixed-cost crunch  
- Off-prem quality failure (fries, soggy, cold) → one-star machine  
- Labor no-shows at dinner peak → tablet backlog + penalties  
- Kitchen lead walk-off → owner returns to the pack station  
- Promo addiction → unprofitable volume habit  
- Settlement lag / cash timing → payroll stress  
- Allergen / mislabel incident → liability  
- Platform rule / fee change → overnight model break  
- Copycat density in the same delivery zone  
- Dual-use kitchen collision with dine-in  
- Blurring into catering / QSR / truck without operating clarity  
- Buying a “book” that was one hero cook + boosted ads + soft contribution  
- Confusing “no dining room” headlines with collectible net after fees, packaging, labor, and ratings

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everybody orders delivery now.”

You’re buying the right to run a **local delivery-only / virtual-brand machine** — if you can protect **contribution after platform fees**, design food that **survives the bag**, keep **ratings** as inventory, use **rent arbitrage** only when **utilization** is real, resist **brand-confusion** logo vanity, install a **kitchen lead** who owns peak tablets, and not confuse GMV dashboards for a twelve-month salary with dinners off and sleep intact.

Some people should add virtual brands inside an existing [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) or pizza kitchen as a **test** and only then lease a dedicated bay.  
Some people should run one excellent brand, prove contribution, and only then add a second logo.  
Some people should choose [catering](/models/catering) (calendar + deposits), [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop), [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food), or a non-food service vehicle and never take on marketplace fee stacks plus rating roulette.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people order delivery?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful food job with a bay lease, a commission statement, a packaging invoice, a personal guarantee, and five confused logos when your only lead texts “I’m done” and the tablets are already lighting up for Friday at seven?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other food-hospitality vehicles vs business-services or home-service paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
