She’s got a forehead that moves when she laughs.  
He’s got a jawline he saw on Instagram and a calendar that won’t clear for a plastic-surgeon consult.  
Somebody’s birthday is in three weeks and “just a little Botox” is already booked in their head.

Someone needs a **licensed clinical chair**, product in the fridge that isn’t counterfeit, an injector who can refuse a bad request, and a medical director whose name on the wall means something when a board asks questions.

That’s a **med spa / medical aesthetics** practice.

Not a day spa with hot stones and no needles.  
Not a plastic-surgery OR.  
Not [pet grooming](/models/pet-grooming) (same *appointment + skilled-hand + facility* brain — totally different clinical and liability stack).  
Not “I’ll rent a room and do lips on weekends” as the whole *company* story.

You’re running a **cash-pay clinical retail** business: treatment rooms × provider hours × ticket mix (neurotoxin / filler / energy devices / skincare) − product COGS − injector pay − medical-director oversight − rent − marketing CAC − the empty chair when memberships churn and Meta stops being cheap.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **medical-director rules vary by state**, **injector recruiting is the ceiling**, **paid-social CAC climbs**, **lasers are CapEx that can sit dark**, **compliance isn’t optional wallpaper**, and **membership churn** shows up before the lobby looks like a magazine.

Indie injectables studios, multi-room medical spas, derm-adjacent aesthetics wings, and franchise / PE roll-up concepts sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The oversight stack and the utilized chair are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local medical-aesthetics operator**: purpose-fit (or heavily TI’d) clinical space — treatment rooms, medical-grade lighting, sharps, refrigeration for biologics, device rooms, consult area — staffed by injectors (RN / NP / PA / MD depending on state) and often aestheticians, under a **physician medical director** and state medical-practice rules.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Injectables-led med spa (primary spine)** | **Device / energy-heavy med spa** |
--|---------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Neurotoxin (Botox / Dysport / Xeomin / peers), dermal fillers, biostimulators; often skincare + light peels | Laser hair, IPL, fractional resurfacing, RF microneedling, body contouring, etc. |
| **Primary constraint** | **Injector hours × chair utilization × rebook cadence** | **Device CapEx + utilization + trained operator + consumables** |
| **CapEx feel** | Lighter plant; inventory of vials/syringes; rooms + TI | Heavy: platforms often **~$50k–$200k+ each**; leases common |
| **Margin feel** | High gross on toxin/filler *if* waste and discounting are controlled | Lower variable product cost on some devices, but debt service / lease + utilization risk |
| **Cash shape** | Cash-pay tickets every ~3–4 months (toxin); filler less frequent | Packages / series; longer sales cycles; financing more common |

Many real shops run **both**. Underwrite the mix honestly — a laser that books two days a week is a different company than an injectable flywheel with three chairs full.

Typical menu:

1. **Neurotoxin** — the volume door; ~12–16 week rebook for many faces  
2. **Dermal fillers / biostimulators** — higher ticket, lower frequency, skill + complication risk  
3. **Energy devices** — hair removal, pigment, resurfacing, body contouring (CapEx + protocol discipline)  
4. **Medical-grade skincare / retail** — attach and continuity; don’t confuse with the spine  
5. **Memberships / packages** — monthly credits + discounts; retention machine *or* discount club in disguise  
6. **Wellness attach** (IV, hormone, weight-loss/GLP-1) — growth story in some markets; **different clinical, pharmacy, and regulatory stack** — don’t treat as a free margin patch  
7. **Consult / treatment planning** — the sales layer that turns a $400 toxin visit into a year of wallet share

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Instagram / Meta, Google Local, Allē / Aspire–class loyalty, derm / PCP / hair-salon COIs, referral, prior-patient winback  
2. **Consult / book** — goals, contraindications, deposit / card-on-file, honest time and units  
3. **Clinical intake** — history, consents, photos, medical-director protocols  
4. **Treat** — inject / device session; complication plan ready  
5. **Rebook / membership** — next toxin date before they leave; membership offer when LTV math is real  
6. **Retain / review** — reminder texts, loyalty points, review ask, no-show policy

**Scope lock:** this page is the **cash-pay medical aesthetics clinic** — injectables + optional energy devices under physician oversight.  
It is **not** a full plastic-surgery practice / OR.  
It is **not** a non-medical day spa / massage / wax-only shop (adjacent membership labor — different license).  
It is **not** pure weight-loss / Medi Weight Loss–class medical coaching (Brian has named that door as a *physician-oversight wellness* franchise example — related clinical feel, different P&L).  
It is **not** salon-suite landlord math ([Image Studios–class] beauty real estate where “medspa” is sometimes a tenant category — different CapEx).  
It is **not** [pet grooming](/models/pet-grooming).

Brian’s published content reviewed for this draft is **thin on dedicated med-spa unit economics**. What shows up: the **backlog one-liner** (high ticket; clinical oversight; marketing CAC); **250 Franchise Ideas** adjacency to hair-removal and cryotherapy/recovery wellness — not a med-spa P&L walkthrough; and YT/Notion pieces that name **Medi Weight Loss–class medical weight-loss**, **retail spa membership** (Hello Sugar / goGlow–class), and salon-suite beauty TAM that *mentions* medspa as a tenant type. ## How money comes in

You sell **completed, paid clinical treatments** (and memberships that convert into treatments) — not follower counts and not a lobby photo.

Education ticket / price bands (national consumer ranges; metros sit higher; discount clubs sit lower):

- Neurotoxin often roughly **~$10–$18+/unit** retail (education medians near ~**$12**/unit in 2026 operator benchmarks) — a common forehead/eye visit lands in the **~$300–$600+** conversation depending on units  
- Filler often roughly **~$500–$800+/syringe** by product and market  
- Device sessions / packages: wide — hair-removal packages and resurfacing series can run from a few hundred to several thousand  
- Memberships often discussed roughly **~$99–$299+/month** with credits + discounts  
- Blended average ticket in modern aesthetics cohorts often orbits roughly **~$350–$450+** — *your* mix will lie to that number

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Staffed clinical hours × utilization** — empty rooms with rent and medical-director retainers still due  
2. **Service mix** — injectables often **~40%+** of revenue in education cohorts; devices, skincare, wellness, packages fill the rest  
3. **Average ticket × enhancement / treatment-plan depth** — one-and-done vs planned series  
4. **Rebook / 90-day return** — toxin cadence is the flywheel; single-visit leakage is the silent killer  
5. **Membership penetration × churn** — members often spend **~2–2.5×+** non-members in education data *when the program is real*  
6. **CAC vs LTV** — paid social and Google fill the top; referrals and loyalty cheapen the bottom  
7. **Provider productivity** — revenue per injector / per service hour; losing a top injector is not “one FTE”

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Injectables: high *gross* on toxin/filler (often discussed **~60–75%** before labor) — then injector commission, waste, and discounts eat the dream  
- Devices: lower consumable COGS on some platforms; **lease / debt + dark-time** is the real tax  
- Labor commonly **~35–50%+** of revenue when pay is real  
- Marketing is a **line item that can rival rent** in paid-acquisition shops  
- Net on a multi-chair med spa is often discussed in a **tighter** band than Instagram lobby myths — volume rises; compliance, people drama, and CAC rise with it

You’re not an influencer with a syringe.  
You’re a **clinical capacity + acquisition + retention** company wearing good lighting.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Faces age. Social media normalizes “maintenance.” Cash-pay consumers skip insurance hassle for elective aesthetics. AmSpa-class industry baselines still cite mid-single-digit annual growth while well-run, tech-enabled cohorts compound faster by **wallet share** (more spend from existing patients) more than by endless new faces.

Good times → filler, devices, memberships, wellness attach.  
Tight times → toxin stretches longer, discounting creeps, memberships churn — then the “I’ll wait” patient still comes back when the forehead returns.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Pet grooming](/models/pet-grooming) rides **skilled-hand scarcity + wet facility + animal liability** on a recurring appointment cadence.  
Med spa rides **clinical oversight + injector scarcity + marketing CAC + device CapEx** on a recurring *face* cadence.  
Same “I won’t DIY this” demand emotion. Different license, different lawsuit, different Meta bill.

That’s the engine. Not one viral before/after Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one clinic

A single location lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Medical director & state structure (the career-defining line)**  
Almost every state expects a **licensed physician** overseeing physician-grade services (injectables, lasers, prescription protocols). **Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM)** states often require a physician-owned professional corporation (PC) with a non-physician **MSO** running business ops under a management agreement. Passive “name on a contract” directors are an enforcement story in multiple large states — chart review, protocols, credentialing, and adverse-event readiness have to be real. Healthcare counsel is not optional theater.

**Injector bench**  
Top RN/NP injectors with a following are the rate-limiting resource. Education chatter puts strong total comp (base + per-unit / per-syringe) into the **high-five to mid-six figures** for producers — and when they leave, the book often walks. Hybrid pay, clinical culture, and not burning people on volume vanity matter as much as the Instagram grid.

**CAC discipline**  
Blended new-patient CAC in modern aesthetics cohorts is often discussed with a **median near ~$60–$70** and a brutal spread: referral/organic injectable shops can sit under **~$35**; consult-led / body-contouring shops can sit **~$400–$700**. Brands that can’t *see* spend cluster in the weak growth stories. LTV:CAC by channel beats “we spent on Meta.”

**Membership that isn’t a discount club**  
Education benchmarks: members outspend and out-retain — median penetration often in the mid-teens % of active clients when a program exists, with wide variance. Monthly churn that looks “small” compounds: **~5–10% monthly** churn (cited in some M&A diligence frames) implies short member lives. Model cancel rates, failed cards, and credit liability — not just signup vanity.

**Laser / device CapEx honesty**  
A single quality platform often **~$50k–$200k+** (body contouring and premium RF can climb higher). Utilization, trained operators, service contracts, and consumables decide whether the box prints or depreciates in a locked room. Leasing lowers cash-up-front and raises the monthly break-even.

**Compliance stack**  
HIPAA, consents, photo storage, FDA device use within clearance, pharmacy / compounding rules (especially if wellness/GLP-1 attach), OSHA / sharps, advertising claims (no “guaranteed results”), adverse-event documentation. Board complaints and platform ad bans are ops risks, not PR footnotes.

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only injector and the only person who can close a $2k treatment plan, you own a clinical job with a beautiful lobby. Scale starts when providers + front desk + a clinical lead can run Tuesday without you on every syringe.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, AmSpa summaries, CorralData-class cohort benchmarks, FDD secondary writeups, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current legal structure advice for *your* state, insurance quotes, trailing utilization, and marketing ledgers.

**Capacity / productivity intuition:**

- Revenue per service hour in education cohorts often spans roughly **~$300–$900+** (median near **~$575** in one 2026 multi-brand set)  
- H1 revenue per active provider medians in that same class of data orbit roughly **~$200k** per half — spread is enormous  
- ~**70%** of revenue from existing clients is a common “depth over acquisition” story in strong shops

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Lean injectables-forward / 1–2 rooms: often discussed **~$150k–$350k+** all-in (TI, inventory, legal, runway)  
- Full multi-room med spa with devices: often **~$250k–$750k+**; premium buildouts climb past **$1M**  
- Equipment alone: commonly **~$50k–$300k+** depending on how many platforms you buy vs lease  
- Working capital: months of payroll + medical-director retainer + marketing before the book compounds  
- Franchise / branded systems (when they exist): Item 7 bands vary wildly — **verify the actual FDD**; royalties only help if they fill chairs and shorten recruiting

**Membership / CAC education shape:**

- Memberships ~**$99–$299+/mo**; year-one conversion of actives often modeled **~10–20%** in planning guides  
- Blended CAC medians ~**$68** in one 2026 cohort — **your** channel mix matters more than the median  
- LTV education medians in that cohort sit roughly **~$1.5k–$3k+** — still not *your* LTV

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **completed** treatments vs booked slots (no-show / late-cancel %)?  
- Mix: toxin vs filler vs devices vs retail vs wellness?  
- Injector count, tenure, pay model, units/syringes per month by provider?  
- Medical-director agreement: active oversight evidence, compensation, state CPOM / MSO-PC structure?  
- Delegation / scope-of-practice docs for NPs/PAs/RNs current for *this* state?  
- Marketing: spend by channel, CAC, lead response time, LTV by cohort?  
- Membership: penetration, monthly churn, deferred credit liability, failed-payment recovery?  
- Device list: owned vs leased, utilization hours, service contracts, remaining life?  
- Claims / board / adverse-event history (redacted pattern)?  
- If franchised or PE-affiliated: does the system shorten hiring, compliance, and ramp — or only a royalty and a lobby package?

Same brain you’d use buying any high-ticket clinical retail shop: hours, oversight, acquisition cost, and whether *you* want regulated faces in your care.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Medical director & state regs (by state)**  
CPOM, supervision proximity rules, who may inject, what must be on-site, how charts are reviewed — **none of this is national wallpaper**. A structure that works in a permissive state can be illegal next door. Passive directors, expired delegation agreements, and “we’ll fix legal later” are how clinics get closed and physicians get disciplined.

**2. Injector recruiting & retention**  
The #1 operating ceiling for most shops. Schools and “my friend took a weekend course” don’t refill chairs as fast as demand. You compete with other med spas, derm offices, and “I’ll take my Instagram following and booth somewhere else.” Comp design, clinical respect, and not treating producers like interchangeable syringes are the real recruiting budget.

**3. Marketing CAC (and vanity ROAS)**  
Meta and Google will take the money. Creative fatigue, iOS attribution fog, and race-to-the-bottom toxin ads push CAC up while discounting destroys LTV. Shops that can’t compute **LTV:CAC by channel** are flying blind. Lead response measured in hours (not minutes) quietly burns paid spend.

**4. Laser / energy CapEx that sits dark**  
Buying the “full suite” for the lobby tour is how working capital dies. Underutilized platforms still want service contracts, trained operators, and marketing verticals. Lease payments don’t care that Tuesday is soft.

**5. Compliance & clinical risk**  
Vascular occlusion, burns, pigment disasters, infection, HIPAA photo leaks, off-label marketing claims, compounded-drug rules. One bad outcome without a protocol is a reputation and insurance event. Documentation culture is the product as much as the lip.

**6. Membership churn & credit liability**  
Signup spikes look like recurring revenue. Cancel rates, failed cards, and unused credits on the balance sheet tell the truth. A membership that only discounts without raising visit frequency is a margin haircut wearing a SaaS costume.

**7. Discount culture & toxin price wars**  
Botox is close to a commodity at the unit level in many metros. Racing $8/unit specials fills the book with patients who leave when the next shop texts a code. Differentiation is outcomes, safety, experience, and retention design — not the loudest Groupon.

**8. Provider walk-off / book concentration**  
Top-decile patients and a star injector often carry a scary share of revenue. Diligence the concentration. Non-solicits help less than a real bench and a CRM that owns the relationship.

**9. Wellness / GLP-1 attach complexity**  
Weight-loss and hormone lines can grow fast and wobble fast (supply, compounding rules, payer/pharmacy shifts, clinical oversight). Treating them as “easy recurring” without separate compliance is how aesthetics shops inherit a second regulated business overnight.

**10. No-shows & late cancels on high-ticket slots**  
A missed filler or device block burns more than a missed facial. Deposits, automated confirmations, and waitlists are ops — not niceties. Education cohorts still lose meaningful booked value to no-shows; fees recover little once the hour is gone.

**11. Inventory waste & cold-chain**  
Opened toxin vials, expired filler, fridge failures, and “free touch-up” culture. COGS discipline is unsexy and decisive.

**12. Real estate & TI for clinical use**  
Medical-looking buildout, sinks, HVAC, privacy, parking, ADA, landlord comfort with clinical disposal. A pretty retail shell is not automatically a compliant treatment plant.

**13. Insurance that matches the procedures**  
Professional liability for injectors and directors, general liability, cyber (photos!), property for devices, workers’ comp. Gaps show up after the claim.

**14. Owner-as-hero injector / closer**  
If only you can inject *and* only you can sell the plan, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a high-stress clinical sales job with regulatory overhead.

**15. Franchise / brand / PE fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, required vendors, and territory rules help only if they fill chairs, train injectors, and shorten compliance ramp. A logo does not place a cannula safely on Tuesday.

**16. Confusing “aesthetics is booming” headlines with “your utilized, collectible chair hours after CAC and oversight”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday show-up rate and your medical-director invoice.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means med spa is a **clinical-oversight + skilled-provider + paid-acquisition** business where empty chairs, open claims, and rising CAC decide whether the marble lobby survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit and retain injectors (or partner with someone who can) — and respect the clinical craft  
- You’re willing to build a **real** medical-director / CPOM-compliant structure with counsel, not a PDF  
- CAC dashboards, rebook rates, and membership churn math energize you  
- You’re capitalized for TI + devices (or leases) + working capital through a soft ramp  
- You can enforce deposits, refusals, and safety protocols when patients push for “just a little more”  
- You’ve compared this door to lighter health-wellness labor ([pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) as appointment-ops cousin; wax/spray-tan membership shops; cryo/recovery CapEx) and to heavier clinical doors (skilled home health) and still want the aesthetics stack

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a semi-absentee “Botox prints money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to regulated clinical risk, board rules, or upset patients after swelling  
- You refuse to manage commission drama or provider recruiting  
- You’re undercapitalized for clinical TI + inventory + months of paid acquisition  
- You actually want **non-medical spa**, **salon-suite landlord**, pure **weight-loss clinic**, or **plastic surgery** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll add a laser and GLP-1 next quarter” was a margin patch, not two new compliance projects

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: clinical hours, oversight, CAC, rebooks, device utilization. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring, compliance, and ramp — not because the lobby photographs well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** membership beauty door or a non-clinical service business first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Can’t hire / keep injectors → waitlist vanity, revenue ceiling  
- Passive or non-compliant medical-director / CPOM structure → board action, forced closure  
- CAC spike + discount addiction → busy book, thin cash  
- Laser debt / lease with soft utilization → fixed-cost trap  
- Complication / board / HIPAA event → reputation + insurance spiral  
- Membership churn + credit liability → “recurring” that isn’t  
- Star injector walk-off with the book  
- Wellness attach regulatory whiplash  
- Owner forever on the syringe and the close  
- Royalty / brand drag without recruiting or demand leverage  
- Buying a “book” that was one producer + one Meta account  
- Confusing industry boom headlines with utilized chairs after oversight cost

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everyone wants to look younger.”

You’re buying the right to run a **clinical chair-hour machine** under state medical rules — if you can structure oversight correctly, recruit injectors, acquire patients at a CAC your LTV can repay, rebook faces on cadence, deploy device CapEx only when utilization is real, and carry compliance without confusing membership signups for free money.

Some people should start injectables-lean and prove rebooks before a laser wall.  
Some people should buy a tired clinic and fix utilization, pay design, and medical-director reality.  
Some people should choose a **lighter** beauty membership or [pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming)-class appointment business and never take on CPOM.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people get Botox?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful clinical-retail job with Meta bills, injector drama, and higher stakes when the medical board or the only NP who can fill the book texts “I’m done”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other health-wellness vehicles vs home-service or senior-care paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
