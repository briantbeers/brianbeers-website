Checkout was 11:00.  
Check-in is 15:00.  
The calendar just stacked three same-day turns on one side of town and a late checkout on the other.  
The lockbox code expired. The linens aren’t back from the laundry. Last night’s guest left a review draft about hair in the shower — and the host already forwarded it with *fix this before the next guests arrive*.

That’s **cleaning for turns** — the **STR / Airbnb / vacation-rental turnover** ops business.

Not [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) as a **recurring bi-weekly maid book** (steadier household LTV; different clock).  
Not [short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) as the **full co-host** (channels, pricing, guest inbox, owner % fees — you may *be their cleaner*; you are not automatically their manager).  
Not [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) night crews on square-footage contracts.  
Not [property-management](/models/property-management) long-term lease ops.  
Not “I’ll mop Airbnbs on the side between residential clients” as the whole *company* story.

You’re a **local turnover / hospitality-cleaning** company whose product is a **guest-ready unit delivered inside a hard window**: clean, linen reset, restock, trash out, damage photos, access that actually works — and a show-up rate that doesn’t become someone else’s 1★ review.

Simple on a whiteboard: hosts need turns → flat fee per checkout → scale doors.  
Hard in real life — because **same-day spikes**, **quality that shows up in public reviews**, **platform / calendar dependence**, **labor reliability**, **keys and lockboxes**, **seasonality**, and **thin pricing** show up before the “Airbnb cleaning is recurring gold” brochure does.

MaidThis–class remote/STR-leaning systems, Properly/Turno-style marketplace tools, indie multi-property cleaners, and STR managers who vertically own their bench all sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The **clustered turn calendar**, the **QC that survives a Saturday stack**, and the **backup cleaner who answers at 10:40 a.m.** are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **short-term rental turnover cleaning operator** — independent, branded, hybrid with residential, or vendor-of-choice for co-hosts — paid to reset someone else’s listing between guests.

Typical job: a host, remote investor, co-host / STR manager, or property company needs the unit guest-ready between checkout and check-in. You sync (or get pinged from) the calendar, dispatch a cleaner or crew, run a checklist that includes hospitality details residential maids often skip, swap linens, restock consumables, document damage, and text photo proof before the next code goes out.

Brian’s published teaching puts this demand shape next to residential cleaning — not under it. His **MaidThis** newsletter frames vacation-rental turnovers as a **higher-frequency host need** (hosts may need cleans after *every* stay — often educated as roughly **~5–10 cleans/month** on a busy listing vs a homeowner’s **~2–4**/month), with calendar sync, remote CS, and contractor benches as the operating story. His home-based franchises YT treats that remote/STR cleaning adjacency as category proof. His STR management and residential-cleaning pages deliberately **split** “hire / own a turn bench” from “be only a maid book” and from “be the co-host.”

Typical menu (mix varies by market / company):

1. **Standard turnover clean** — the spine: bathrooms, kitchen reset, floors, beds/linens, trash, guest-facing surfaces under a deadline  
2. **Same-day / rush / late-checkout recovery** — compressed windows; surcharge territory  
3. **Deep / periodic resets** — oven/fridge interiors, baseboards, behind furniture — every N turns or quarterly  
4. **Mid-stay refresh** — towel/trash/tidy for longer stays (half-ticket class, different SLA)  
5. **Linen / laundry program** — on-site wash, off-site commercial laundry, or owner-supplied swap logistics  
6. **Restock / hospitality add-ons** — TP, toiletries, coffee, welcome snacks, trash staging — margin and inventory risk  
7. **Damage / inspection documentation** — photo proof timed to host claim windows  
8. **Not the same company:** full [short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) (guest messaging + channels + owner %); pure [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) bi-weekly identity; [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) night contracts; owning the STR units yourself (landlord CapEx)

Typical loop:

1. **Win the host / co-host / portfolio** — Google, host Facebook groups, STR manager vendor lists, realtor / investor intros, marketplace apps, brand lead flow, prior residential clients who bought a cabin  
2. **Onboard the unit** — access (codes, lockboxes, smart locks, garage), supply standards, linen ownership, restock list, what “clean” means in *this* listing’s photos, who pays rush fees  
3. **Calendar truth** — iCal / PMS / host app sync; know same-day stacks *before* Tuesday  
4. **Dispatch** — density routing; backup for no-shows; buffer for lockouts and laundry delays  
5. **Produce the turn** — checklist → clean → linen → restock → trash → photo QC → “ready” ping  
6. **Collect / retain** — flat per-turn or monthly volume; rush and deep as separate line items; fire hosts who train you to race price and ignore scope  
7. **Grow doors without breaking Saturdays** — the whole game after a handful of busy listings

**Scope lock:** this page is **STR / vacation-rental / Airbnb-style turnover cleaning ops** — route density under guest clocks, quality that feeds reviews, platform/calendar dependence.

It is **not** residential recurring maid service as the identity of the company ([residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) — you may *layer* bi-weeklies; don’t underwrite a turn book as if it were one).

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, guest-ready turnovers that hit the window** — not a calendar screenshot of “booked cleans” and not the host’s guest cleaning fee as if it were all margin.

Pricing / economics shapes you’ll see (education bands — **verify your metro**):

- **Flat per-turnover fee** — the honest unit; studio/1BR often discussed roughly **~$65–$140**; 2–3BR roughly **~$90–$220**; larger / luxury **~$200–$400+** (resort and coastal premiums sit higher)  
- **Same-day / rush surcharge** — commonly educated **~+15–30%** (sometimes higher under &lt;12-hour notice) because the calendar ate your flexibility  
- **Deep / periodic** — often **~+30–50%** over standard, or a separate ticket  
- **Mid-stay refresh** — often roughly **~50–60%** of a full turn  
- **Linen / laundry add-ons** — per set or bundled; commercial laundry and lost-linen quietly redefine the job  
- **Restock / hospitality markups** — disclosed or baked; ethics and inventory live here  
- **Monthly volume / retainer + overage** — for portfolios with predictable occupancy; still price same-day and deep as exceptions  
- **What you generally do *not* own:** the guest’s booking payout, the host’s ADR, Superhost badges as *your* equity — those are host / co-host economics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Turns completed on time** — not “accepted jobs” that no-showed  
2. **Doors / hosts that stay** — portfolio concentration and host churn  
3. **Route density by checkout geography** — scattered luxury cabins destroy windshield economics  
4. **Mix** — standard vs same-day vs deep vs linen-heavy luxury  
5. **Labor realization** — cleaner show-up × minutes-per-turn × re-clean rate  
6. **Calendar truth** — occupancy spikes *and* ghost months; instant bookings that land at 9 p.m. for tomorrow  
7. **Who owns the relationship** — direct host book vs co-host vendor slot vs marketplace auction

Gross per turn *looks* simple on a napkin.  
Then same-day overtime, linen loss, supplies, software, bonding/GL, lockout time, re-cleans that eat the next guest’s window, and the host who paid the uninsured Facebook cleaner $40 less until the review hit — show up.

This is a **route + hospitality QC + calendar logistics** business.  
Not bi-weekly household LTV alone. Not co-host % of GBV. Not night-crew square footage.

**Light contrast (same housing stack, different check):**

| Model | Who pays you | What you “sell” | Overhead feel |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Residential cleaning** | Homeowner (recurring schedule) | Bi-weekly / weekly consistency | Attrition + daytime density |
| **STR co-host / management** | Owner (% of bookings) | Channel + guest CX + turn *orchestration* | Platforms, regs, inbox |
| **Commercial janitorial** | Facility / office (monthly contract) | Night scope on a building | Bid pressure + night labor |
| **Long-term property management** | Owner (lease fees) | Leasing + trust + vendors | Sticky monthly; eviction math |
| **Cleaning for turns** | Host / co-host / guest fee pass-through | Guest-ready unit inside a hard clock | Same-day spikes; review QC; calendar dependence |

Brian’s cleaning content treats vacation-rental turns as **calendar spikes with occupancy risk** — that’s the demand spine of *this* company. Don’t confuse layering residential bi-weeklies for cash-flow smoothing with *being* a maid franchise story. Don’t confuse being the preferred cleaner for a co-host with *being* the co-host.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

America (and tourism corridors worldwide) keeps converting housing into **nightly hospitality product**. Every checkout needs a reset. Remote investors, tired self-hosts, and professional co-hosts keep outsourcing the mop — because the guest calendar does not care who owns the deed.

When travel is strong, turn frequency rises and Saturday stacks get ugly (in a good-revenue, bad-ops way).  
When travel softens or a city chokes STR supply, **your book can thin overnight** even though “people still hate cleaning” as a macro slogan.

The constraint isn’t “do guests leave messes?”

It’s **enough legal, bookable listings in a geography you can densify — plus cleaners who show, QC that protects reviews, and pricing that funds rush capacity** — without growing so fast the bench collapses on the first festival weekend.

National remote/STR cleaning brands exist. Local boutiques exist. Marketplace apps exist. In-house benches inside management companies exist.  
Same category umbrella. Different who owns the host, the calendar, and the Saturday failure.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Turns under a hard clock (the factory)**  
Checkout → clean → inspect → linen → restock → ready ping → next guest. Miss the window and you eat a refund, a bad review, a lost host, or all three. Count productive turns the way a route company counts stops — then ask what happens when three land in the same zip at 11:15.

**Route density vs trophy doors**  
A downtown cluster of 1–2BR apartments is a different company than five lakefront homes forty minutes apart. Hosts love “we cover the whole metro.” Your P&L loves Tuesday geography discipline.

**Quality / review gravity**  
In residential cleaning, a soft clean often becomes a private complaint. In STR turns, it becomes a **public platform score** that cuts the host’s occupancy — and then your vendor slot. Photo QC, checklists that match listing photos, and re-clean SLAs are product, not niceness.

**Platform / calendar dependence**  
Airbnb / Vrbo / Booking calendars, PMS tools, and host apps are external bosses. Instant book, same-day reservations, algorithm changes, and co-host software that auto-assigns the lowest bidder all reshape your day. Diversify host relationships where you can; never pretend one OTA’s booking pulse is a diversified business.

**Labor reliability (the silent half of the product)**  
No-shows at 10:50 a.m. are existential. Preferred cleaners who quit take tribal knowledge (and sometimes hosts) with them. Contractor vs W-2 classification, training to hospitality checklists, and a **named backup bench** matter more than a pretty Instagram before/after.

**Keys, codes, lockboxes, smart locks**  
Access failure is unpaid labor with a guest clock running. Dual control, code hygiene, “what to do when the lockbox is empty,” and not texting codes into group chats are trust infrastructure — same family of risk as other in-home services, with worse timing.

**Linen & restock logistics**  
On-site laundry that isn’t done, off-site laundry that didn’t return, and “owner said they’d leave towels” are how on-time cleans still fail the guest. Par levels and who owns linen loss need to be contracted, not vibed.

**Seasonality & event spikes**  
Ski weeks, beach summers, graduation weekends, festivals, and shoulder-season ghosts. Peak months hide weak backup coverage. Soft months reveal whether your pricing and host mix still fund the bench.

**Thin pricing / race-to-bottom**  
Someone always undercuts on Facebook. They often skip insurance, linen truth, and Saturday backups. Competing only on host-facing cleaning fee trains everyone to treat you like a commodity — then punish you when commodity service arrives in a review.

**Host vs co-host vs marketplace customer**  
Direct hosts can be sticky and demanding. Co-hosts may give volume and replace you. Marketplaces may fill gaps and auction your margin. Underwrite concentration and switching costs.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you *are* the dispatcher, the QC, the lockout hero, and often the backup mop.  
Semi-absentee “run it from a beach with VAs” fairy tales skip the Saturday when two cleaners ghost.  
Scale means a cleaner bench, calendar ops, photo QA, and a book that transfers — not “Airbnb turns print while I sleep.”

**Adjacent stack**  
[Short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) (your biggest B2B buyer or your vertical competitor), [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (smoothing book), handyman / junk / laundry / pest, locksmiths — you don’t need to own them. You do need named humans between stays.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat blogs, FDD secondary summaries, marketplace averages, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull local wage reality, insurance quotes, trailing turn completion %, and host concentration.

**Operating intuition (education — not forecasts):**

- Money event = **paid completed turns** (+ disclosed rush / deep / linen / restock) — never confuse the *guest cleaning fee on the listing* with *your* collectible margin after labor  
- Public 2025–2026 operator education often puts standard turns roughly in the **~$65–$220+** band by bedroom count, with luxury/resort higher; US “average” chatter around the mid-**$100s–$180s** appears in category writeups — **verify metro**  
- Same-day / compressed-window surcharges commonly **~15–30%+**; deep turns often **~30–50%** over standard  
- Capacity intuition: many operators discuss roughly **~3–5 quality turns/cleaner/day** before windshield and QC break (large homes fewer; studios denser) — mix lies to averages  
- Re-clean / callback rate and on-time ready rate matter as hard as ticket price  
- Brian’s MaidThis teaching: host frequency can run **~5–10 cleans/month** on a busy listing vs homeowner **~2–4**; startup conversation in that newsletter ~**$49.5k** class; corporate Item 19 anecdote cited there — **not your guarantee**  
- Brian’s corpus is **strong** on STR turns as adjacency to residential / remote cleaning and on not collapsing this into co-host or maid identity, and **thin** on a dedicated turn-only multi-unit earnings table — do **not** invent one

**Startup / investment shape (education — verify current quotes / FDD if any):**

- Entity, GL + bonding / employee dishonesty where required, contracts, calendar/PMS or host-tool stack, supplies kit, laundry plan, marketing to hosts/co-hosts, working capital for soft seasons and no-show recovery  
- Franchise / remote-brand paths (MaidThis–class category) add fee + tech + playbook — still not a substitute for local cleaner show-up and Saturday QC  
- Buying an existing **host book** is a different check (diligence on host churn, turn volume by season, concentration, cleaner retention, whether relationships follow the buyer, and how much volume was marketplace vs direct)  
- You’re often **office-light / home-plus-software** early — not a retail buildout. You *are* buying the ability to hit 15:00 readiness when the calendar stacks

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing completed turns vs accepted / cancelled / no-show?  
- On-time ready % and re-clean %?  
- Mix: standard vs same-day vs deep vs linen-heavy? Average ticket all-in?  
- Host / co-host / marketplace concentration — top 5 accounts as % of revenue?  
- Geography: % of turns inside a tight cluster vs metro spray?  
- Cleaner count, tenure, pay model, backup coverage on Saturdays?  
- Access failure rate; linen failure rate; supply shrink?  
- Seasonal volume — peak month vs shoulder; how did pricing hold?  
- Claims / damage disputes / theft / lockout incidents — pattern?  
- If franchised or marketplace-heavy: royalty / take-rate vs who owns the host relationship?  
- If buying a book: will hosts re-sign; cleaners stay; calendar integrations portable?

Same brain you’d use buying any trust + deadline field book: density, show-ups, quality under time pressure, and whether *you* want to live inside other people’s checkouts for years.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Same-day spikes & compressed windows**  
Checkout at 11, check-in at 15, three units, one late guest still in the shower. Instant book doesn’t ask your bench. Without rush pricing, backup cleaners, and ruthless geography, “growth” is just overlapping failures. Buffer or bleed.

**2. Quality that becomes a public review**  
Hair in the drain, sticky counters, missing TP, beds that don’t match the listing photos — the host feels it as occupancy risk; you feel it as vendor death. Checklist theater without photo QC and re-clean SLAs is how you lose the portfolio that paid CAC.

**3. Platform / calendar dependence**  
Your demand is downstream of OTAs, PMS tools, and co-host software. Fee changes, algorithm shifts, listing bans, and auto-dispatch to cheaper cleaners are *external* bosses. A book that only lives inside one marketplace or one co-host’s preferred list is a concentration risk with a mop.

**4. Labor reliability / no-shows**  
The product is people in someone else’s unit on a clock. Call-outs, ghosting, and “I only work residential Tuesdays” destroy Saturday stacks. Hiring is unpaid until the day the backup saves the turn. Brian’s broader cleaning teaching applies harder here: **people are the #1 challenge** — culture and reliability beat “we’ll pay a dollar more” until a competitor does both *and* shows up at 10:45.

**5. Keys, lockboxes, codes, smart locks**  
Expired codes, dead batteries, missing keys, garage apps that only work on the host’s phone. Access drama is unpaid time with a guest ETA. Soft access protocols create hard refunds.

**6. Seasonality & occupancy cliffs**  
Peak weeks hide thin benches. Shoulder season empties the calendar and tests whether hosts still pay professional rates — or revert to the cousin with a vacuum. Annualize before you hire to July’s fantasy.

**7. Thin pricing / race-to-bottom**  
Uninsured solos and marketplace low bids train hosts to shop cleaning fees like airline seats. Underpricing same-day risk and linen truth fills the week and empties the margin. Fee honesty in the host agreement beats winning volume you’ll resent.

**8. Linen & laundry failure modes**  
Clean unit, wrong sheets, damp towels, laundry not returned. Guests don’t leave 5★ for “floors were fine.” Linen ownership, par levels, and who pays loss need ink, not hope.

**9. Host / co-host expectation theater**  
Hosts want hotel resets at motel prices with zero communication. Co-hosts want SLA photos in twelve minutes. Education at onboarding is cheaper than churn at turn forty. Fire accounts that need a miracle more than a cleaner.

**10. Damage, claims, and “who broke it” ambiguity**  
Guests lie; hosts panic; cleaners get blamed. Timestamped photo protocols and stay-in-your-lane documentation matter. Playing adjuster without process is how you inherit drama.

**11. Portfolio concentration**  
One 30-door co-host feels like winning until they verticalize cleaning in-house or switch vendors. Whale risk is real. So is the power imbalance when they demand free heroics every festival weekend.

**12. Hybrid complexity tax**  
Mixing residential bi-weeklies + STR turns + light commercial in one brand sounds synergistic until dispatch, pricing, training, and brand promise fight. Underwrite as **different factories** sharing a logo until proven otherwise — sister pages exist for a reason.

**13. Classification, insurance, and trust claims**  
Contractor vs employee law, bonding, employee dishonesty coverage, GL — wrong structure is worse than no structure after a theft allegation or injury. Get quotes before the LOI fantasy.

**14. Tech / sync failures**  
Broken iCal, double-booked turns, host apps that don’t ping, photo uploads that fail on bad wifi. You own the guest-ready outcome even when the software “should have” prevented the miss.

**15. Semi-absentee myth**  
“Hire offshore CS and watch Airbnb cleans compound.” Early reality is host sales *and* cleaner drama *and* Saturday fires. Earn leverage with systems and leaders later — don’t buy the myth with a territory map and a beach photo.

**16. Confusing this with co-host fees or landlord STR investing**  
[Short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) sells channel + guest CX for a % of bookings. Landlord STR investing owns CapEx and NOI. This page sells **completed turns**. Steal muscles from neighbors; don’t steal their P&L story — Brian’s ownership frame keeps **business vs real estate vs investing** as separate doors.

**17. Confusing “tourism is forever” with “your utilized Saturday bench”**  
Macro travel demand can be real. Your company is turns won, turns completed on time, hosts kept, cleaners who show, access that works, and a price that still funds rush capacity when the calendar gets ugly.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means cleaning for turns is a **real hospitality logistics business**, not a passive Airbnb side hustle with a logo.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re calm under same-day turn pressure and still willing to hold quality when the calendar is stacked  
- You can obsess over density maps, dispatch, and photo QC (or partner with someone who will)  
- Host / co-host sales *and* cleaner recruiting don’t scare you off — or you honestly split the jobs  
- Keys, lockboxes, awkward access conversations, and damage documentation feel like *product*  
- You’re willing to enforce rush fees, scope, and cancel/no-show policies when hosts push back  
- You’re capitalized for soft seasons and for the week two cleaners quit before peak  
- You’ve compared this door to [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (steadier LTV), [short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) (broader hospitality ops), [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial) (night contracts), and [property-management](/models/property-management) (long-term doors) — and still want guest-clock physics

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want bi-weekly household rhythm without Saturday stacks ([residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning))  
- You actually want co-host % economics and guest inbox ownership ([short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management))  
- You want landlord / STR investing returns as the whole thesis (different door — say so)  
- You hate urgent messaging, public review pressure, or entering strangers’ units on a deadline  
- You’re looking for passive / “hire a VA and fish” from month one  
- You’re allergic to labor drama and backup coverage  
- You’re under-reserved for shoulder season and a whale co-host leaving  
- You thought “I’ll just add Airbnb turns to fill residential gaps” was free margin, not a different factory

Franchise / remote-brand vs indie boutique vs buying a host book is a separate call.  
Same category either way: turns, clocks, cleaners, access, reviews, calendars — plus a Saturday that doesn’t care about your weekend plans.

Many operators will correctly choose a **residential recurring book**, a **full STR management practice**, or a **night janitorial contract** first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “America loves Airbnb” or a pastel promise that turnover fees print while you sleep.

You’re buying the right to stand between a host’s calendar and a guest’s 15:00 reality — and get paid per completed turn — if you can densify routes, hold quality under time pressure, keep cleaners who show, survive platform and seasonality shocks, price rush risk honestly, and stay boringly excellent when the lockbox fails at 11:20.

Some people should launch lean, win a tight cluster of legal doors, and prove Saturday QC before headcount.  
Some people should buy a tired turn book and fix routing, photo QA, rush pricing, and backup coverage.  
Some people should stay on [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) and never own a same-day stack.  
Some people should build or buy [short-term-rental-management](/models/short-term-rental-management) and treat cleaning as a vendor (or a vertical).  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into [commercial-janitorial](/models/commercial-janitorial), [property-management](/models/property-management), a trade, or a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “will hosts keep needing cleans between guests?”
