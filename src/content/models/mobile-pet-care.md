The doodle is in the crate by the front door.  
The owner left a key under the mat (or in a lockbox that expires at 6).  
The radar shows thunderstorms by 2 p.m. — and your only groomer van is already booked solid on the north side while three sitting requests just pinged from the south.

Someone needs **care that comes to the pet** — a bathtub on wheels, a walker who texts a photo at the park, a sitter who knows the meds schedule — without a four-hour salon wait, a boarding kennel, or a retail aisle of kibble.

That’s **mobile pet care**: the **route + trust + weather** category that covers **mobile grooming vans**, **dog-walking routes**, **pet-sitting / drop-in / overnight in-home care**, and the hybrids that mix them.

Not [pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) as a **facility salon** (wet plant, tables, drains, multi-groomer chairs — mobile is only a light contrast there).  
Not [pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail) (inventory turns, shrink, box rent — grooming may attach; the CapEx is retail).  
Not [pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) (outdoor scooping subscriptions — same “people have dogs” macro, different dignity tax and ticket).  
Not [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (humans’ houses — same **key / trust / no-show** war, different animal liability).  
Not “I’ll walk dogs on Rover between jobs” as the whole *company* story.

You’re running a **field pet-services** business: **stops per day × ticket / visit mix × show-up rate − drive time − labor − vehicle / insurance − weather idle − the hole when the only person clients trust texts “I can’t make it.”**

Simple on a whiteboard: pets need care → owners outsource → you show up.  
Hard in real life — because **route density**, **home-trust / key access**, **weather cancellations**, **van CapEx**, **booking churn**, **insurance that matches animals in your care**, and **key-person risk** show up before the van wrap looks like a lifestyle Reel.

Aussie Pet Mobile–class grooming vans, Fetch! Pet Care–class sitting/walking systems, Woofie’s–class hybrid mobile pet brands, indie multi-sitter ops, and solo van operators sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The **clustered calendar**, the **trust protocols**, and the **vehicle that actually starts on a rainy Tuesday** are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local mobile pet-services operator** whose product is **professional care delivered at the customer’s home (or driveway)** — not a salon lease, not a kennel suite, not a supply store.

**Three product shapes (don’t blur them — and don’t underwrite a hybrid as if it were one P&L):**

| | **Mobile grooming (van / trailer)** | **Dog walking / day routes** | **Pet sitting / drop-in / overnight** |
|--|-------------------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Baths, full grooms, nails/ears add-ons — one pet (or household) at a time in a self-contained wet plant on wheels | Midday / after-school walks, pack walks (where legal/insured), potty breaks | Drop-ins, overnight house-sits, vacation coverage, meds / feeding |
| **Primary constraint** | **Van hours × groomer skill × driveway density × vehicle uptime** | **Walker hours × stop density × leash reliability** | **Sitter calendar × overnight coverage × key / alarm access** |
| **CapEx feel** | High: converted Sprinter / custom van, generator, fresh/grey water | Low–medium: reliable car, gear, software, insurance | Low–medium: same + backup sitters for peak weeks |
| **Liability feel** | Bite during groom; heat/dryer; grey-water rules; commercial auto | Escape / bite on leash; third-party injury; dog fights in packs | Care/custody/control in the *home*; property damage; meds errors |
| **Cash shape** | Premium tickets, rebook every 4–8 weeks by coat | Recurring weekly walks; lower ticket, higher stop count | Spiky (holidays / travel); retainers or packages smooth it |

Many real companies are **hybrids** — walk + sit as the recurring spine, mobile spa days as attach; or a grooming van that also sells nail-only driveway stops. Underwrite the mix. A calendar that’s 90% one-off Rover-style gigs is a different company than one with **neighborhood clusters**, **card-on-file rebooks**, and **named backup coverage**.

Typical menu:

1. **Mobile full groom / bath & tidy** — the high-ticket door; size + coat + condition drive time  
2. **Driveway / van add-ons** — nails, ears, teeth, de-shed (margin and schedule risk)  
3. **Recurring dog walks** — the density machine when clustered  
4. **Drop-in visits** — potty, feed, meds, play — mid-ticket recurring or travel-triggered  
5. **Overnight / house-sit** — high trust, high key-person, holiday peaks  
6. **Puppy / new-client intro packages** — pipeline for the book  
7. **Not the same company:** facility [pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) salon; [pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail) inventory box; [pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) scoop routes; daycare/boarding *facility* occupancy; vet clinic; pure dog-training academy as the identity

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / Maps, Instagram before/after, vet / trainer / pet-waste / cleaner COIs, apartment/HOA pet boards, prior-client referral, brand/app lead flow (if franchised)  
2. **Book / qualify** — pet notes, temperament, vaccine proof where required, access instructions, deposit / card-on-file, weather policy  
3. **Route** — cluster by neighborhood day or bleed windshield; build buffer for traffic and “one more photo” owners  
4. **Access & produce** — key/lockbox/garage code → walk / sit / groom → photo proof / report card  
5. **Collect / rebook** — next groom slot before you leave; walk packages auto-renew; sit packages for next trip  
6. **Retain / cover** — reminder texts, cancel policy, backup sitter/groomer bench, review ask

**Scope lock:** this page is **mobile / in-home / driveway pet care** — routes, trust, weather — with grooming vans, walking books, and sitting books as the hybrid category.  
It is **not** the facility salon deep-dive ([pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming)).  
It is **not** pet retail CapEx ([pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail)).  
It is **not** outdoor scooping ([pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal)).  
It is **not** a pitch for any named van or app brand.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, paid visits and grooms that actually happen** — not a waitlist screenshot and not a “pets are family” headline.

Education ticket / visit bands (national operator ranges; metros and doodle coats sit higher; pack-walk volume sits lower — **verify your market**):

- Mobile full groom often roughly **~$85–$170+** by size / coat (often cited **~20–40%+ convenience premium** over comparable salon stickers; some metros push higher)  
- Bath / tidy mobile often roughly **~$65–$120+**  
- Solo van capacity often discussed around **~4–7 dogs/day** before quality and windshield break (assistants can push higher *if* the van and process allow)  
- Dog walks often roughly **~$20–$45+** per solo walk depending on length / metro; packages discount unit price to buy density  
- Drop-ins often roughly **~$20–$40+**; overnights / house-sits often **~$75–$150+/night** class conversations (holiday premiums climb)  
- Healthy groom rebook cadence for many coats: roughly **every 4–8 weeks** (high-maintenance doodles often **4–6**)

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Stops / dogs per productive day** — after drive, setup, key fumble, and “quick chat”  
2. **Route density** — same neighborhood Tuesday vs zigzag metro heroics  
3. **Average ticket × mix** — groom vs walk vs sit; size/coat; overnight vs drop-in  
4. **Show-up / weather realization** — booked ≠ collected when storms cancel and policies are soft  
5. **Rebook / package retention** — walk packages and groom cadence beat one-off app gigs  
6. **Labor model** — owner-operator vs W-2 / 1099 sitters and groomers (recruiting *and* margin)  
7. **Vehicle / equipment uptime** — van down = grooming revenue down; carless walker = walking revenue down  
8. **Insurance + compliance yield** — grey-water legality, HOA driveway rules, apartment pet policies that let you *work*

Gross / contribution intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Mobile grooming: high ticket, high van fixed cost; labor + fuel + generator + maintenance eat “no rent” myths  
- Walking / sitting: lower CapEx, thinner tickets; density and utilization decide whether the book is a job or a company  
- Hybrids: attach looks beautiful until dispatch complexity and insurance exclusions fight each other  
- Franchise royalties / brand / local-marketing minimums (when present) are a real tax — underwrite after fees  
- Net quartile spreads are **wide** — Instagram vans ≠ collectible cash after weather weeks and one claim

You’re not a pet influencer with a leash.  
You’re a **density + trust + uptime** company wearing embroidered polos.

**Light contrast (same pets, different check):**

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Pets keep entering households. Owners keep working long days, traveling, and treating dogs like family — then outsourcing the parts that ruin carpets, calendars, and guilt.

APPA-scale pet spending stays enormous; **services** (grooming, walking, sitting, boarding) sit inside the growth story. Convenience wins when salon waitlists run weeks and boarding feels like boarding school. Mobile and in-home care sell **time back** and **lower stress for the animal** — when the operator is reliable.

Good times → spa add-ons, daily walks, vacation sits.  
Tight times → walk frequency stretches, groom intervals stretch — then the mat emergency and the destroyed shoe show up anyway.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) rides **skilled hands + wet facility**.  
[Pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail) rides **inventory turns + foot traffic**.  
[Pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) rides **outdoor recurring routes**.  
[Residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) rides **home trust without animals as the product**.  
Mobile pet care rides **field density + home access + weather + vehicle** — same demand emotion, different factory.

That’s the engine. Not one viral driveway Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

A single territory lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Route density (the career-defining line for vans *and* walkers)**  
Same street, same day beats “I’ll take any zip code.” Operator guides repeatedly show an extra dog or walk per day from clustering alone — and that delta compounds annually. Brian’s mobile teaching keeps returning to the same idea: field businesses scale on **utilization and accountability**, not truck count vanity.

**Trust / key / alarm protocol**  
You enter homes. Lost keys, tripped alarms, missing jewelry claims, and “who else has the code” drama are the silent P&L. Written access logs, dual-control for high-value homes, photo proof, and a refusal right when access is chaotic matter more than cute bandanas.

**Weather & seasonality**  
Ice, heat advisories, thunderstorms, and flooded streets cancel production you already paid to schedule. Soft cancel policies train customers to treat you like a free option. Hard policies without makeup capacity create one-star reviews. Annualize a bad February and a holiday December before you believe the model.

**Van / vehicle CapEx & uptime**  
A mobile salon is a plant with a transmission. Grey water dumping rules, generator noise, HOA driveway bans, fuel, brakes, and “the van is in the shop” days. Used conversion vs new custom is a **risk profile**, not a personality test. Walking/sitting books still die when reliable transport and backup coverage fail.

**Booking churn & platform leakage**  
App marketplaces and Facebook groups train customers to re-shop every trip. Your defense is packages, card-on-file, named caregivers, and rebook before exit — ops, not vibes.

**Insurance that matches the work**  
General liability alone is often **not** enough. Education checklists repeatedly surface **commercial auto**, **animal bailee / CCC** (care, custody, control), property damage in the home, and workers’ comp once you have employees. Pack walks and overnight sits can change the carrier conversation. PDF wallpaper ≠ claim payment.

**Groomer / sitter / walker bench (key-person)**  
If only *you* can handle the reactive doodle or the anxious overnight client, you own a stressful job with a cute logo. Scale starts when trained backups can run Tuesday without the book melting — and when recruiting doesn’t mean “post on Craigslist and pray.”

**Trade area & competitive set**  
Facility salons, big-box grooming counters, indie vans, franchise vans, marketplace sitters, neighbor kids, and daycare vans all skim the same owners. Differentiation is **reliability, safety, communication, and density discipline** — not a louder wrap.

**Owner involvement**  
Early: you’re often the brand, the groomer/sitter, and the dispatcher. That can be fine as a lifestyle. It is not the same as buying **control of the outcome** through systems.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD secondary summaries, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDDs (if franchised), local wage surveys, insurance quotes, grey-water / mobile-vendor rules, and trailing utilization.

**Capacity intuition:**

- Solo mobile groomer: often **~4–7 dogs/day** class conversations (mix and drive time lie to averages)  
- Solo walker: stop count is a **density** story — 30–45 minutes + windshield, not “infinite dogs”  
- Sitting books: overnight capacity is **calendar + backup**, not “I said yes on the app”

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Lean indie walking / sitting launch: often discussed roughly **~$1k–$10k** (insurance, gear, software, marketing) before you confuse a side hustle with a company  
- Indie multi-sitter / small walking company: often **~$10k–$40k+** working capital + recruiting runway  
- Indie mobile grooming van: used conversion conversations often **~$20k–$80k+**; new custom / turnkey often **~$80k–$140k+** all-in class ranges in operator guides  
- **Aussie Pet Mobile–class** Item 7 education (2025–2026 secondary summaries): roughly **~$167k–$209k** total initial (franchise fee ~**$20k** class; territory + van + working capital dominate) — Brian’s Notion franchisor log historically cited ~**$180k–$203k**  
- **Fetch! Pet Care–class** home-based sitting/walking Item 7 education: roughly **~$89k–$104k** (fee-heavy; royalty ~**7%** class + local marketing minimums in older FDD summaries — **verify current**)  
- **Woofie’s–class** hybrid mobile pet care Item 7 education: roughly **~$180k–$295k** band in 2025-class writeups  
- Facility salon / daycare CapEx ([pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming)) and pet-supply boxes ([pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail)) are **different capital stacks** — don’t underwrite them as “mobile without the van”

**Item 19-style gross (category proof only — not your territory):**  
Secondary FDD summaries have cited Aussie Pet Mobile–class average unit revenue conversations in the mid-**$200k–$300k+** gross band depending on vintage and cohort — **verify the actual FDD**. Gross ≠ owner cash. Royalties / brand funds only help if the system fills density and shortens recruiting.

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **completed** visits/grooms vs booked (weather cancel %, no-show %, late-cancel %)?  
- Route map: % of stops inside a tight cluster vs metro spray?  
- Rebook / package retention within 7–14 days of service?  
- Groomer/sitter/walker count, tenure, pay model, and productive stops/day?  
- Claims history (bites, escapes, property, heat) — redacted pattern?  
- Insurance: GL + **commercial auto** + **animal bailee/CCC** + workers’ comp — limits and exclusions (pack walks? overnights?)?  
- Van age, maintenance log, grey-water plan, backup vehicle?  
- Key/access SOP; dual control; lost-key incidents?  
- If franchised: does the system fill density and train backups — or only a royalty and a wrap color?

Same brain you’d use buying any field-service book with animals and house keys: density, show-ups, insurance, and whether *you* want to be the forever hero on the route.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Route density / windshield bleed**  
Scattered “yes” bookings feel like growth and pay like a second job. Without neighborhood days, you buy fuel and lose dogs-per-day. Density or die — same physics Brian stresses on other ultra-local pet routes, applied to higher-ticket vans and walking books.

**2. Trust, keys, and home access**  
You are in people’s houses with their animals *and* their stuff. Soft protocols create hard claims. Owners who “just leave the door unlocked” are a risk profile, not a convenience.

**3. Weather cancellations & seasonality**  
Storms, ice, extreme heat, and “we’ll reschedule free forever” policies destroy realization. Holiday sitting peaks hide how thin February walks look. Annualize before you hire to the December fantasy.

**4. Van / vehicle CapEx and breakdown risk**  
Van down = revenue down for grooming. Generator, plumbing, AC, and transmission don’t care about your booked calendar. Underbuying the plant to “save money” is how you buy a year of tow trucks and angry doodle owners.

**5. Booking churn & marketplace leakage**  
One-off app customers re-auction every trip. Without packages, deposits, and relationship ownership, CAC never compounds. Churn looks like “slow week” until you measure it.

**6. Insurance gaps (bailee / CCC / commercial auto)**  
Wrong policy is worse than no policy when a bite, escape, or living-room accident happens. Pack walks, overnights, and mobile wet work each change underwriting. Get quotes *before* the LOI fantasy.

**7. Key-person / owner-as-only-trusted-human**  
If clients refuse backups, you didn’t buy a company — you bought a leash with payroll tax. Illness, vacation, and burnout become existential.

**8. Groomer / sitter / walker recruiting & retention**  
Same scarcity story as facility grooming for skilled van groomers; same caregiver-market war as other in-home services for sitters. Hybrid pay, sane routes, and not burning people on reactive dogs all day are the real recruiting budget.

**9. Temperament & “difficult pet” selection**  
Saying yes to every reactive dog fills the day and empties the bench (and the insurance appetite). Saying no protects the book — and loses some Google reviews.

**10. Grey water, noise, HOA, and municipal rules**  
Dumping rules, generator complaints, and driveway bans can erase a whole subdivision you thought you’d densify. Compliance is product, not paperwork.

**11. Schedule honesty vs Instagram fantasy**  
Underscheduling large double-coats to hit dog-count vanity creates overtime and injury. Overblocking creates empty gaps. The calendar is the factory.

**12. Hybrid complexity tax**  
Walking + sitting + mobile spa in one brand sounds synergistic until dispatch, insurance, training, and brand promise fight. Underwrite as **three factories** sharing a logo until proven otherwise.

**13. Confusing facility salon math or retail box math with mobile math**  
No lease ≠ free. Inventory aisle ≠ route density. [Pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) and [pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail) are sister doors — different CapEx and different ceilings.

**14. Easy-entry / neighbor-kid competition on walks and sits**  
Barriers look low; trust and reliability are the real moat. Racing the cheapest Facebook sitter is how margins die.

**15. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties and mandatory local marketing help only if they fill clustered demand and shorten ramp. A wrap does not create density on Tuesday.

**16. Confusing “Americans love pets” headlines with “your utilized, collectible stops”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro love ≠ your Monday show-up rate in the rain.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means mobile pet care is a **routes + trust + weather + vehicle** business where empty driveways and open claims decide whether the cute brand survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can obsess over density maps and dispatch discipline (or partner with someone who will)  
- Home-trust protocols, key control, and awkward owner conversations don’t scare you off  
- You’re willing to enforce weather / cancel / deposit policies when customers push back  
- Vehicle maintenance and insurance diligence feel like *product*, not nagging  
- You can recruit and retain field people — or stay intentionally owner-operated with eyes open  
- You’re capitalized for van and/or working capital through soft weather months  
- You’ve compared this door to [pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) (facility plant), [pet-supply-retail](/models/pet-supply-retail) (inventory), [pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) (lighter outdoor route), and [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (home trust without animal CCC) — and still want mobile pet physics

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a semi-absentee “pets print money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to animal unpredictability, bites, upset owners, or entering strangers’ homes  
- You refuse to manage recruiting drama or backup coverage  
- You’re undercapitalized for a real van (if grooming) + months of marketing while density builds  
- You actually want a **salon lease**, a **retail box**, or pure **scooping routes** — different underwriting  
- You thought “I’ll just add overnight sits to fill gaps” was free margin, not a liability upgrade

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: density, show-ups, trust, weather, insurance, vehicle. A brand (mobile groom system, sitting/walking system, or hybrid) is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring and fills clustered demand — not because the van photographs well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **facility salon**, a **lighter scoop route**, or a **human cleaning** book first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “Americans love their dogs.”

You’re buying the right to run a **field pet-care machine** — if you can cluster routes, earn and protect home trust, price for time and risk, survive weather weeks, keep vehicles (and people) online, and carry insurance that matches animals in your care without confusing a cute wrap for a scalable company.

Some people should start lean on walks/sits and prove density before a six-figure van.  
Some people should buy a tired mobile book and fix routing, deposits, and backup coverage.  
Some people should choose a **facility** [pet-grooming](/models/pet-grooming) salon and never own a generator.  
Some people should stay on [pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) or [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) and never take custody of animals.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do busy owners need pet help?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful field job with keys, thunderstorms, and higher stakes when the only person clients trust texts “I’m not coming in”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs facility pet grooming vs pet-supply retail vs pet-waste routes vs residential cleaning), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
