She’s got a wedding in nine days and a membership she froze in January because “winter skin doesn’t need it.”  
He’s got a photo shoot tomorrow and a fear of walking out orange.  
Somebody wants an express airbrush before brunch — or a $45 walk-in that only works if the next twelve visits ride a prepaid pass and the booth never sits empty on a Tuesday.

Someone needs a **trained tech who can turn a low ticket fast without streaking**, a **membership book that gets *used* (not just sold)**, aftercare product that walks out the door, retail hours that match when people actually tan, and an owner who can survive rent, soft January, seasonality spikes, tip-heavy labor math, and the DIY booth / mobile gun three plazas over selling “same glow, half the price.”

That’s a **spray tan / beauty express** company.

Not [hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax) Brazilian cadence and wax-temp / skin-lift liability (same *beauty membership + suite-then-flagship* brain in Brian’s content — different skill ceiling, intimacy trust, and contraindication stack).  
Not [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio) fill-cycle beds and adhesive near the cornea (same corridor — different ticket density and eye-adjacent risk).  
Not [med-spa](/models/med-spa) clinical aesthetics under oversight (adjacent “I want to look camera-ready” dollars — totally different CapEx, protocols, and visit math).  
Not [salon-suites](/models/salon-suites) where *you* are the landlord and spray artists pay *you* rent (different product: suite occupancy, not service tickets) — though Brian’s published goGlow–class story often *starts* as a suite **tenant** before flagship.  
Not “I’ll spray friends in a booth forever” as the whole *company* story if you’re trying to own the brand and the membership book.

You’re running a **service + membership + retail** business: booths / rooms × staffed retail hours × ticket / pass mix − tech pay − solution / disposables − rent − marketing − the empty hour when members ghost until Memorial Day and your best closer texts that she’s opening a mobile route from her garage.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **throughput** is the whole engine on a low ticket (you don’t win with occasional $200 services; you win with *turns*), **membership utilization** decides whether prepaid cash is LTV or discount theater, **seasonality** rewrites the calendar, **rent** clears on soft weeks, and **tech training** is the gap between “anyone can learn” marketing and a Google review that says “streaked / orange / patchy.”

goGlow–class airbrush membership shops, indie sunless boutiques, beauty-express bars stacking tan + brow + express add-ons, suite-tenant launches building toward a flagship, mobile-only guns, and self-serve booth hybrids sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The utilized hour, the consistent finish, the membership that *gets used*, and the retail shelf that attaches aftercare are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local sunless / beauty-express operator**: a retail box or suite (often roughly **~200–1,500+ sq ft** depending on booth/room count; single-suite launches sit at the lean end; branded strip / lifestyle centers — Brian’s goGlow education often cites roughly **~1,200 sq ft** flagships — at the heavier end) with spray booths or open airbrush stations, solution inventory, HVAC / exhaust that keeps solution mist manageable, retail shelving, booking / membership tech, and techs who turn skin tone into tickets and recurring passes.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Operating spray-tan / beauty-express studio (this page)** | **Salon-suite landlord** ([salon-suites](/models/salon-suites)) |
|--|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Spray / airbrush services + memberships + retail (+ optional express beauty add-ons) | Private suite + amenities to beauty-pro *tenants* (incl. spray artists) |
| **Who pays you** | Clients / members (tickets + prepaid packages) | Beauty pros (weekly / monthly rent) |
| **Primary constraint** | **Hourly throughput × membership utilization × tech consistency × retail × seasonality × rent** | **Suite occupancy × rent collected − fixed plant** |
| **Labor feel** | Mid: trainable techs, tip-heavy pay stories, quality QA, schedule coverage across retail hours | Low: often near-zero W-2; tenants open/close |
| **CapEx feel** | Mid-light to mid: suite start can be lean; multi-booth flagship TI + booth/equipment is heavier | Heavy: wet every room, corridors, high-six to low-seven figures |
| **Cash shape** | Membership cadence; event spikes; winter soft; retail attach; payroll %; refund / redo risk | Recurring rent; lease-up cliff; amenities race |

Brian’s published framing puts **salon suites** as **real estate + amenities** — and goGlow–class spray shops as operators who often **rent a suite first**, hire and train a team, sell memberships, then (if the market proves) transition into a **flagship** while the strip buildout takes roughly **~6–9 months**. This page is the **operating** identity: **you sell spray tickets and memberships**. Same beauty corridor as the landlord model. Different check, different scars.

**Careful adjacency (do not confuse products):**

- **goGlow–class** airbrush spray-tan membership shops (Notion newsletters + YT “11 Recurring Revenue” / “14 Businesses” + Melanie Richards podcast): suite-then-flagship path, recurring beauty membership, trainable labor with tip-heavy total-comp stories, brand-booked (not tech-bonded) scheduling as *category education* — *facility-path and labor-structure education*, not a promise your P&L will match any Item 19 table.  
- **Hello Sugar–class** wax / sugar membership ([hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax)): overlapping suite-then-flagship + membership emotion in Brian’s content — higher skill ceiling, intimate-service trust, burn/skin liability.  
- **[Lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio)** fill-cycle rebooks — adjacent beauty cadence, different product and eye-adjacent risk.  
- **[Med-spa](/models/med-spa)** clinical / device aesthetics — competes for “event-ready skin” dollars with different CapEx and protocols.

Typical menu:

1. **Custom airbrush / hand spray** — full body, express, face/neck touch-ups; the high-frequency core when finish quality holds  
2. **Booth / automated spray** (in some shops) — lower labor per turn, different quality/consistency story and CapEx  
3. **Memberships / tan passes / prepaid packages** — the cash-engine *design*; utilization (visits actually taken) decides whether it’s LTV or a discount club that trains guests to wait for promos  
4. **Walk-in / à la carte / event rush** — acquisition and wedding / vacation / content spikes; dangerous if priced as a loss leader without a membership path  
5. **Add-ons / beauty-express stacks** — brows, lashes express, contour / contour-adjacent services, rapid dry, face tan — densify ticket *if* you can staff skill without killing throughput  
6. **Retail / take-home** — extenders, moisturizers, primers, mitts, gradual tans, aftercare; often the highest-margin dollars when coached (Brian’s goGlow education leans hard on post-tan product)  
7. **Mobile / offsite** (bridal parties, events) — high ticket days that wreck your in-store schedule if not sequenced  
8. **Booth rent / hybrid rooms** (inside *your* brand) — fixed rent from independent artists; less control — **not** the same as being a suite landlord with 30 private doors  
9. **Not the same company:** [salon-suites](/models/salon-suites) landlord math; [hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax) / [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio) cousins; clinical [med-spa](/models/med-spa) as the *primary* product; “I bought a gun off Amazon” as a substitute P&L

Typical loop:

1. **Demand** — Google Local, Instagram / TikTok before/after, bridal / vacation / content calendars, referrals, strip co-tenancy next to fitness / beauty, prior-member winback after freeze season  
2. **Book / convert** — appointment-heavy (or smart walk-in capacity); membership pitch on first or second visit; deposits where policy is real  
3. **Intake / service** — skin-tone consult, solution choice, prep instructions (exfoliate / shave / lotion rules), spray technique / booth protocol, dry time, quality bar that matches the “not orange” promise  
4. **Retail / rebook / membership** — extender kit + **next visit or pass utilization before they leave**  
5. **Collect / review** — card, tip flow, review ask, no-show / freeze / rollover policy that actually fires  
6. **Retain coverage + stay consistent** — schedule fairness across retail hours, training / QA, tip culture — or watch redo refunds and soft Tuesdays stack

**Scope lock:** this page is the **operating spray-tan / beauty-express studio** — hourly throughput on a low ticket, membership package design & utilization, tech training & coverage across retail hours, retail attach, rent, seasonality, quality/redo risk.  
It is **not** the [salon-suites](/models/salon-suites) landlord model (even when you launch *inside* one).  
It is **not** [hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax) or [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio) (cousins, not clones).  
It is **not** [med-spa](/models/med-spa) clinical aesthetics as the spine.  
It is **not** “passive income with a spray booth in the logo.”

Named proof points exist across the category: **goGlow** (Brian’s deepest published spray-tan storytelling — suite-then-flagship, membership, trainable labor, Item 19 corporate education shapes), indie multi-booth sunless bars, beauty-express hybrids, mobile-first operators, and strong local suite / booth hybrids. Treat logos as **category proof** — not as the point of this page, and not as a substitute for throughput math, wage/tip reality, seasonality planning, and whether *you* want to manage retail-hour coverage and finish quality for a decade.

Independents, franchisees, and multi-unit operators can all sit here. Same umbrella. Wildly different CapEx, brand rules, and labor scars.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, paid spray hours** (and membership dollars that convert into those hours, plus retail that rides them) — not lobby marble and not a Reel of golden hour skin.

Education ticket / mix bands (national category chatter; metros and lifestyle centers sit higher; discount / mobile guns sit lower — **verify your trade area**):

- Single full-body / custom airbrush tickets often discussed roughly **~$35–$75+** walk-in depending on market and brand positioning; express / face / touch-ups densify or dilute depending on how you price time  
- Membership economics often sit *under* headline à la carte by design — Brian’s goGlow education cites monthly subscriptions often discussed roughly **~$50–$100** depending on visit frequency (shape, not your menu)  
- Visit cadence for many members often discussed roughly **~weekly to monthly** when the habit holds (event-driven guests spike then ghost)  
- Branded / franchise Item 7–19–class public summaries for spray concepts vary hard by suite vs flagship and by FDD year — pull current docs; don’t napkin from Instagram or a 2024 newsletter screenshot

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Staffed hours × throughput** — low ticket means **turns per hour** matter more than one hero service; empty booths with rent and wages still due  
2. **Membership mix × *utilization*** — prepaid passes / monthly packages that guests **actually use** (and rebuy) vs sold-and-forgotten liability / discount theater  
3. **Average ticket × service mix** — full body vs express vs add-on stacks vs retail  
4. **Show-up rate** — booked hours that become paid hours (member no-shows still waste capacity you staffed for retail hours)  
5. **At-station rebook / pass renewal (the unit of truth)** — next appointment or pass top-up before they leave; studios that only “sell memberships at the desk” without utilization ops usually have a retention problem, not a “need more Meta ads” problem  
6. **Retail attach** — extenders / moisturizers / primers / mitts; education targets often discuss roughly **~5–15%+ of gross** as a coaching band when the aftercare story is real; highest-margin dollars when coached  
7. **Tech productivity** — revenue per tech / per available hour; a slow, streaky tech destroys throughput *and* reviews  
8. **Labor model** — tip-heavy wage design (Brian’s goGlow corporate education: modest base + strong tips = total-comp story that can look low on *payroll %* while still needing tip culture, training, and coverage) vs commission vs booth rent — and **misclassification** risk if labels don’t match control  
9. **Retail hours** — this is not a 10–2 B2B route book; evenings, weekends, and pre-event rushes are often when the money happens — and when you’re short-staffed

Gross / cost intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Payroll / labor commonly discussed as a *wide* band in beauty retail — Brian’s published goGlow **corporate** Item 19 education highlighted roughly **~14%** payroll *including management* as a *disclosed corporate shape* (tip-heavy total comp sits partly off that line) — **franchisee / indie shops often sit higher**; tip compliance, guarantees, and redo labor push the high end. Treat the low corporate % as **illustrative**, not your underwriting.  
- Solutions / disposables / towels / gloves often **~8–20%** depending on product positioning and waste; cheap solution that looks orange is expensive  
- Rent / occupancy often discussed roughly **~8–15%+** of revenue as a stress band; lifestyle-center rent that only works at summer fantasy utilization is a trap  
- Marketing fills the top; membership utilization and aftercare trust cheapen the bottom

Gross tickets *look* simple on a napkin.  
Then a soft January, a wedding weekend that needs three techs you don’t have, a landlord bump, a week of unused memberships, a streaky redo refund, and a mobile gun’s “half price” Reel show up.

You’re not a glamorous influencer with a glow filter.  
You’re a **throughput + membership-utilization operator** whose inventory is *staffed retail hours* — in a category where **seasonality and finish quality** travel in the same Google search as your “custom airbrush.”

**Light contrast (same beauty corridor, different check):**

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People still want color without UV damage. Weddings, vacations, content calendars, fitness photos, “I can’t leave the house looking pale,” and event seasons don’t wait for your CapEx model. The category is **fragmented**, **habitual when membership retention works**, and full of mobile guns, salon add-on chairs, and indie boutiques rather than one 800-lb gorilla you have to kill in every trade area — which is why Brian’s 250-ideas sheet shelves **Spray Tan** next to other health & personal-care doors, and why his deeper published unit-econ storytelling leans hard into **goGlow–class** airbrush membership and **suite-then-flagship** facility paths.

Good times → membership upgrades, weekly cadence, retail kits, second booth, Instagram as a free billboard, bridal month that feels like Christmas.  
Tight times → freezes, “I’m using the drugstore mitt,” DIY booths, mobile competitors, techs shopping tip pools, landlords who still want last year’s rent, and **seasonality cliffs** when the calendar stops caring about your lease.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Salon suites](/models/salon-suites) ride **independence demand from spray artists and other beauty pros** — keep 100% of tickets, pay weekly rent.  
Operating spray studios ride **client demand for a consistent finish, a membership that doesn’t feel like a scam, and retail hours that match real life** — and then fight to keep coverage, quality, and utilization discipline.  
Same beauty TAM emotion. Opposite P&L spine.

[Hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax) and [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio) sit nearby as **appointment + beauty-habit** cousins. [Med-spa](/models/med-spa) sits nearby as **event-ready / aesthetics** cousin. Hello Sugar–class concepts sit nearby as **beauty membership / suite-launch** cousins — useful for framing *cadence and facility path*, dangerous if you underwrite spray like wax skill ceilings (or wax like “train anyone / clients never bond to the tech”).

That’s the engine. Not one glow Reel with a drone shot of the plaza.

## Micro: what actually drives one studio

**Throughput on a low ticket (the unit of truth)**  
You’re not scaling “people want a tan.” You’re scaling **paid turns per staffed hour**. A $45–$65 average ticket that takes 45 minutes of chair + dry + redo time loses to a disciplined express system that turns cleanly. Over-consulting, under-training, and stacking beauty-express add-ons that kill the next appointment are how “premium” shops go broke on busy-looking calendars.

**Membership utilization**  
Sold passes that sit unused, freezes that never get enforced, rollover rules that become free-labor debt, and discount clubs that train guests to wait for promos decide whether the calendar is vanity or cash. A studio that acquires members but fails to drive utilization and renewal is a marketing treadmill with pretty extender shelves — especially when winter freezes pile up.

**Tech training & quality consistency**  
Brian’s goGlow education leans on a **trainable labor pool** and “customers book a time & whoever is on schedule” as *category contrast* to wax/lash tech bonding. That is a real structural advantage when it holds — and a liability when training, QA, and tip culture don’t. Streaks, patchiness, orange tones, and missed prep instructions become refunds and one-star reviews faster than a rent bump. “Anyone can learn” is a recruiting *story*; consistent finish under Saturday rush is an *ops system*.

**Retail hours & coverage**  
Evenings, weekends, and pre-event rushes often carry the P&L. Daytime soft hours still burn rent. Part-time tip-heavy schedules help flexibility — they also create coverage holes when three bridal parties hit Friday and your closer is at another job. Schedule design is the business.

**Seasonality**  
Wedding / prom / spring break / summer / holiday content spikes vs January–February soft weeks (and climate-dependent “pale season” length). Cash planning that assumes every week is bridal week fails in February — especially if membership freezes pile up and you staffed like July.

**Rent / occupancy cost**  
Fixed plant (lease, CAM, utilities for HVAC / exhaust that keep mist and guest comfort workable) clears whether you do eight sprays or forty. Site selection (parking, visibility, trade-area income, density of mobile guns / salon add-ons / DIY booths) matters more than rose-gold aesthetic. Suite-start paths (Brian’s goGlow / Hello Sugar adjacency stories) can lower *initial* CapEx — they do not remove utilization math or throughput discipline when you flagship.

**Retail attach**  
Extenders and aftercare are where margin often hides *and* where the next tan looks better (and rebooks). Coaching scripts, shelf discipline, and tech incentives matter; “we’re artists not salespeople” is how you leave money in the guest’s Amazon cart.

**Suite-then-flagship path (facility identity)**  
Brian’s published goGlow storytelling: flagship strip buildout can take roughly **~6–9 months** while CapEx sits in roughly mid/high-six figures *before* you know the neighborhood; suite launch can compress to roughly **~90 days** and lower CapEx while you hire, learn, and build members — then graduate to flagship if the book proves. That’s a **risk-sequencing** story. It is not automatic cash flow, and suite sales often sit below flagship sales for a reason.

**Owner involvement (honest version)**  
Early: hiring, floor presence, guest recovery, quality audits, solution inventory, landlord, local marketing, weekend coverage.  
Later: multi-unit is possible when managers and training pipelines exist — semi-absentee myths die when the only closer calls out on prom Saturday, a streak review hits Google, and the membership list is full of unused credits.  
Contrast carefully with wax/lash “clients follow the tech” categories — spray *can* be more brand-booked when training holds; it still isn’t a vending machine.

**Adjacent stack**  
Booking / membership vendor, payroll / tips compliance, solution supplier, laundry / linens, HVAC / exhaust maintenance, insurance, landlord — you need them *fast* when a booth goes dark or a guest reports a bad finish.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Throughput failure on a low ticket**  
You cannot “premium your way” out of bad turns-per-hour forever. Overlong consults, slow techs, beauty-express add-ons that wipe the next slot, and redo loops turn a busy calendar into thin cash. Low ticket volume is the backlog warning for a reason — volume discipline is the product.

**2. Membership utilization failure**  
Sold passes that don’t get used, freezes that never expire, rollovers that become free-labor debt, and “membership” that’s really a permanent discount train guests to underpay. Cash collected ≠ capacity filled. Utilization dashboards, renewal scripts, and honest package design are the business — not optional CRM polish.

**3. Seasonality cliffs**  
Bridal / prom / vacation / summer spikes vs soft winter weeks. Staffing and rent that only work in June fail in February. Freeze season that empties the book while the lease still clears is how spray shops die quietly.

**4. Rent / occupancy cost**  
The lease clears on soft weeks. Overbuilt TI in a B-minus center, CAM surprises, and rent that only works at fantasy summer utilization are classic killers. Suite-start can lower CapEx; it does not erase occupancy math when you flagship.

**5. Tech training & finish consistency**  
“Train anyone” is a recruiting advantage only if QA holds. Streaks, orange tones, patchiness, and skipped prep instructions become refunds and reputation damage. Training pipelines, shadowing hours, and redo policies cost money; skipping them costs the brand.

**6. Retail-hour coverage & tip-culture fragility**  
Evenings and weekends carry demand. Part-time schedules help — until three events hit and nobody wants the Friday night shift. Tip pools that feel unfair, base wages that don’t clear without tips on soft days, and call-outs on peak days are ops problems, not “kids these days.”

**7. No-shows & late cancels**  
Calendar green ≠ bank deposit. Without deposits, reminders, and enforced policy, you pay labor and rent for empty air — including members who “meant to come before the trip.”

**8. Booth / room utilization discipline**  
Overstaffing for a dream Saturday and understaffing for a real Tuesday both hurt. Services that run long wipe the next hour. Empty booths are pure overhead.

**9. Retail attach failure**  
Shelves sit, aftercare isn’t coached, techs apologize for selling. You leave margin on the shelf *and* worsen next-tan fade / blotch complaints.

**10. Price competition / race to the bottom**  
Screenshot pricing from a mobile gun, Groupon shop, DIY booth, or salon add-on chair will always undercut a compliant, well-staffed studio if you play their game. Discount wars destroy ticket, culture, and the ability to fund wages and training.

**11. Competition from mobile / DIY booths / salon add-ons / chains**  
Mobile sells convenience; DIY booths sell price; salons sell “while you’re here”; branded apps sell trust. Consistent finish + utilized membership + aftercare + hygiene / HVAC trust beat racing every Instagram coupon.

**12. Quality / redo / refund risk**  
Bad finish is visible in every photo. Redos steal capacity; refunds steal cash; reviews steal the next month’s bookings. Solution standards and technique audits are not optional polish.

**13. Tip / wage / compliance load**  
Payroll taxes, tip reporting, overtime, and any state beauty / cosmetology rules that touch your services aren’t optional wallpaper. Tip-heavy models that look beautiful on a corporate Item 19 slide still need counsel on *your* classification and reporting.

**14. Owner-as-hero sprayer**  
If only you can fix the corrective tans and cover every Friday night, you bought a physical job with weekend lock-in — not ownership leverage.

**15. Event / bridal chaos**  
High-ticket party days that aren’t sequenced wreck the in-store book. Underpricing offsites “for exposure” trains the wrong customers.

**16. Inventory / solution waste & product consistency**  
Expired solution, wrong shade mix, and booth maintenance downtime are silent margin killers. Cheap product that photographs orange is expensive.

**17. HVAC / exhaust / facility plant**  
Mist, temperature, and guest comfort affect finish and reviews. Flagship TI that skimps on plant to hit a pretty CapEx number creates redo loops and landlord drama.

**18. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties and brand funds help only if they fill hours, recruit/train talent, and improve membership ops. A logo does not utilize a ghosting member or fix a streaky tech.

**19. Suite-then-flagship transition risk**  
Proving the book in a suite does not guarantee the flagship lease, TI budget, or staffing plan will clear. Some operators never graduate — and that’s okay if suite math works; it’s a trap if you underwrote flagship dreams on suite CapEx.

**20. Deferred-revenue / membership accounting blindness**  
Cash from annual passes feels like profit in month one. Auditors, buyers, and your February payroll disagree. Underwrite utilization and liability, not just deposits.

**21. Confusing “people always want a glow” with “your utilized, collectible staffed hours after rent, labor, membership utilization, and seasonality math”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday no-show / utilization report.

**22. Confusing goGlow Item 19 corporate adjacency with *your* spray unit economics**  
Suite-then-flagship, trainable labor, and membership cadence lessons in Brian’s content are real. They do **not** prove your booths will print the same — especially when disclosed tables can be small corporate samples, CapEx bands shift by FDD year, and tip-heavy payroll % can look “magical” until you run *your* coverage and tip reality.

**23. Thinking “train anyone / clients book the brand” means zero people work**  
Labor-light slogans die when the closer calls out, the booth is down, and the Google review says “streaked.” Active ownership, not passive spray.

**24. Buying a “membership book” that was one weekend closer who leaves at close — or a plant that fails the next soft season**  
Diligence that stops at trailing revenue without tech concentration, membership liability, utilization reality, seasonality curve, and redo history is how you purchase a hollow calendar with a claim attached.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means spray tan / beauty express is a **throughput + membership-utilization + retail-hours** business where empty booths, unused passes, soft winters, rent, finish quality, and training decide whether the brand survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit, train, and schedule techs across **retail hours** (or partner with someone who can) — and obsess over finish consistency  
- Membership utilization dashboards, turns-per-hour, retail attach, and seasonality planning energize you more than spraying every bridal party yourself forever  
- You’re willing to pick and defend a **labor / tip model** with counsel when needed  
- Rent, no-show / freeze policy, solution CapEx / inventory discipline, redo protocols, and guest recovery don’t scare you off  
- You can hold a price and quality standard without racing the cheapest mobile gun screenshot  
- You’re capitalized for suite and/or flagship TI / working capital through a soft season — or you’re buying a shop with a real, transferable membership book and a plant that holds quality  
- You’ve compared this door to [hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax), [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio), [salon-suites](/models/salon-suites) (landlord), and [med-spa](/models/med-spa) and still want **operating spray / beauty-express tickets + memberships** as the product

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a semi-absentee “tan passes print money” story from month one of dark booths and unused credits  
- You’re allergic to people drama, recruiting, weekend coverage, enforcing policies, or QA paperwork  
- You actually want to be a **suite landlord** — different CapEx and identity ([salon-suites](/models/salon-suites))  
- You thought goGlow-class “train anyone” means zero training investment or that tip-heavy corporate payroll % transfers to your market automatically  
- You’re undercapitalized for rent + payroll while reviews and membership utilization build across a soft season  
- You refuse retail coaching or think tips / classification compliance is optional  
- You plan to win solely by matching the lowest spray price within three miles  
- You can’t stomach seasonality, redo risk, or retail-hour staffing and want a demand curve that never moves

Franchise vs independent vs buying a tired indie studio is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: hours, talent, show-ups, membership utilization, rent, retail, finish quality. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens recruiting, demand, and systems — not because the neon photographs well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** beauty door (single suite as a *tenant*, mobile-only, booth only, or a different facility model) first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everyone wants a tan.”

You’re buying the right to run a **throughput + membership-utilization machine** — if you can train and cover techs across retail hours, fill and *hold* appointments, design packages guests actually use and renew, attach retail without apology, underwrite rent that survives soft weeks and winter freezes, hold finish quality under Saturday rush, hold a price against mobile guns and DIY booths, survive seasonality without panicking, and choose a labor / tip model you can defend when a tech — or an auditor — asks hard questions.

Some people should start lean (suite as tenant or mobile) and prove membership utilization before heavy TI — the goGlow–class path Brian publishes as *risk sequencing*, not a guarantee.  
Some people should buy a tired studio and fix utilization, training, no-show / freeze policy, retail coaching, and hours coverage.  
Some people should choose [salon-suites](/models/salon-suites) and be the **landlord** instead of the spray-shop boss.  
Some people should stay on [hair-removal-wax](/models/hair-removal-wax), [lash-brow-studio](/models/lash-brow-studio), [med-spa](/models/med-spa), or another beauty door and never take low-ticket volume + weekend coverage drama.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people want color without UV?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a high-touch retail-hours job with rent that doesn’t care, memberships that sit unused, no-shows that steal the day, a streaky review that can turn overnight, a soft winter that empties the book, and a best closer who can leave Tuesday with half your Friday nights in their phone?**

If you want help comparing doors (operating spray-tan / beauty express vs salon-suite landlord vs wax / lash vs med-spa vs other health-wellness / skilled-hand vehicles), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
