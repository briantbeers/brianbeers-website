The doodle is matted.  
The nails are clicking on the hardwood.  
The owner works until 6:30 and won’t crate the dog for a four-hour salon wait if they can avoid it.

Someone needs a **table, tub, dryer, and a groomer who shows up** — coat work every four to six weeks, nails and ears that don’t become an ER story, and a shop that can handle water, hair, noise, and the occasional bite without pretending pets are soft pillows.

That’s **pet grooming** — usually a **facility salon**, sometimes with daycare/boarding attach, sometimes with a **mobile van** as a lighter adjacent door.

Not [pet waste removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) (outdoor scooping routes — same “people have dogs” macro, different P&L).  
Not [residential cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (humans’ houses — same labor + trust war, different wet plant).  
Not a pet-supply big box where grooming is an attach counter behind the kibble aisle (related adjacency — different inventory CapEx).  
Not “I’ll bathe dogs in my garage on Saturdays” as the whole *company* story.

You’re running a **labor + wet facility** business: appointment capacity × rebooks × ticket mix − groomer pay − rent/utilities − insurance − the empty chair when someone no-shows or your only stylist texts “I’m done.”

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **groomer recruiting**, **no-shows**, **bite / animal liability**, **water and plant buildout**, and **seasonality** show up before the model looks like “pets are family.”

Indie neighborhood salons, membership-style hygiene shops, big-box grooming counters, and mobile van operators sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The table hours and the drain line are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local pet-services operator** whose primary product (on this page) is **professional grooming** in a purpose-fit (or heavily TI’d) wet shop — tubs, tables, dryers, laundry, nonslip floors, hair traps — staffed by groomers and bathers who can safely handle real animals.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Facility grooming salon (primary)** | **Daycare / boarding attach (or primary elsewhere)** |
|--|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Baths, full grooms, nails/ears/teeth add-ons, breed/style cuts; sometimes membership hygiene cadence | Day play, overnight kennels/suites, pickup/drop windows |
| **Primary constraint** | **Groomer hours × schedule utilization** | **Safe capacity × supervision + facility occupancy** |
| **CapEx feel** | Wet plant: plumbing, drains, dryers, ventilation, TI | Runs/suites, fencing, HVAC redundancy, overnight staffing |
| **Liability feel** | Bite/scratch during groom; coat damage; heat-dryer incidents | Care/custody/control (CCC / animal bailee); escapes; fights; overnight illness |
| **Cash shape** | Appointment tickets + rebook interval (often ~4–8 weeks by coat) | Day fees / night fees; weekend + holiday peaks |

**Light mobile note (adjacent, not the spine of this page):**  
A **mobile grooming van** trades lease for vehicle CapEx, fresh/grey water handling, generator noise rules, and driveway density — typically fewer dogs per day, often a convenience premium over salon stickers. Franchise and indie vans exist (Aussie Pet Mobile–class systems are **category proof**, not a recommendation). Same groomer scarcity. Different plant. Different weather and breakdown risk. If the van is the whole thesis, underwrite routes + vehicle uptime — don’t pretend it’s “salon math without rent.”

Typical facility menu:

1. **Bath & brush / bath & tidy** — volume door; shorter slots  
2. **Full groom / haircut** — the spine ticket; size + coat + condition drive time and price  
3. **Add-ons** — nails, ears, teeth brush, de-shed, anal glands, flea treatments (margin and schedule risk)  
4. **Puppy intro / first grooms** — pipeline for the rebook book  
5. **Membership / hygiene clubs** (some systems) — monthly baths/nails cadence; different from classic “style cut every six weeks”  
6. **Retail / consumables** — shampoo, collars, treats (nice attach; don’t confuse with Pet Supplies Plus–scale inventory)  
7. **Daycare / boarding** (if licensed/zoned/insured for it) — occupancy machine next to the salon; **boarding liability is a different insurance conversation**

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / Maps, Instagram coat-before/after, vet / trainer / pet-waste / walker COIs, big-box overflow, prior-client referral, local Facebook pet groups  
2. **Book** — size, coat, temperament notes, deposit / card-on-file, time estimate honesty  
3. **Intake** — vaccine proof where required, temperament screen, coat condition (demat fees *before* the clippers start)  
4. **Produce** — bath → dry → cut → finish; double-handle when needed; photo / pickup  
5. **Rebook** — next slot before they leave (the real product)  
6. **Retain / review** — reminder texts, no-show policy enforcement, review ask, VIP cadence for high-maintenance coats

**Scope lock:** this page is **facility grooming primary**, with daycare/boarding as the **liability + CapEx** sibling many buyers blur into the same LOI, and **mobile as a light contrast** — not a full mobile-route deep-dive.

**Out of scope (different models):**  
- Pure [pet waste removal](/models/pet-waste-removal)  
- Pure pet retail / Pet Supplies Plus–class inventory boxes (grooming may attach; the P&L is retail)  
- Vet clinic / veterinary hospital  
- Dog training academies as the company  
- Solo booth-rental “I rent a table” lifestyle without building a multi-groomer plant (valid job; different ceiling)

## How money comes in

You sell **completed, paid grooming appointments** (and, if attached, **daycare days / boarding nights**) — not “pet love” and not a waitlist screenshot.

Education ticket bands (national salon ranges; metros and doodle coats sit higher; bath-only sits lower):

- Bath / tidy often roughly **~$30–$80+** by size  
- Full groom often roughly **~$55–$200+** by size / coat / condition (extra-large and heavy demat climb)  
- Mobile often carries a **~20–40% convenience premium** over comparable salon work (trade travel time for ticket)  
- Healthy rebook cadence for many coats: roughly **every 4–8 weeks** (high-maintenance doodles/poodles often **4–6**)

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Staffed tables × productive hours** — empty chairs with rent still due  
2. **Schedule utilization** — booked *and* present (no-shows / late cancels are stolen capacity)  
3. **Average ticket × mix** — bath vs full groom vs add-ons; size/coat mix  
4. **Rebook rate** — walk-out with next appointment vs “I’ll call you” churn  
5. **Groomer productivity pay model** — commission / hybrid / hourly floor (recruiting tool *and* margin lever)  
6. **Daycare / boarding occupancy** (if you sell it) — weekday vs weekend vs holiday; supervision minimums don’t shrink with soft census  
7. **Retail / membership** — secondary; membership can smooth cash if utilization is real

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Labor is the monster (commonly discussed **~35–55%+** of grooming revenue when pay is real and double-handling happens)  
- Supplies (shampoo, blades, towels) are real but smaller than labor + occupancy  
- Water, gas/electric for dryers, laundry, and hair-trap maintenance are the wet-plant tax  
- Net on multi-groomer salons is often discussed in a **tighter** band than solo/home myths — volume ceiling rises; fixed costs and people drama rise with it  
- Daycare/boarding can look like “easy occupancy dollars” until overnight staffing, cleaning, and claim history show up

You’re not a influencer with a tub.  
You’re a **capacity + rebook + liability** company wearing cute bandanas.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Pets keep entering households. Coats keep growing. Owners keep treating dogs like family — and outsourcing the parts that ruin floors and weekends.

APPA-scale pet spending stays enormous; services (grooming, boarding, training) sit inside the growth story. Pandemic pet acquisition wasn’t a one-year fad for high-maintenance coats — doodles and curly coats **need** professional cadence or become mat emergencies.

Good times → premium styles, spa add-ons, daycare while owners travel.  
Tight times → baths stretch longer — then the emergency demat and the “why is he chewing his paws” vet bill show up anyway.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Pet waste removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) rides **outdoor recurring routes** and dignity tax — low ticket, density or die.  
[Residential cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) rides **human home trust + cleaner retention** on a similar “recurring chore” emotion.  
Pet grooming rides **skilled hand scarcity + wet facility + animal behavior risk** — same “I won’t do it myself” demand, different plant and insurance stack.

That’s the engine. Not one viral before/after Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one shop

A single location lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Groomer bench (the career-defining line)**  
BLS-class animal-care labor stories and operator chatter agree: demand often exceeds **qualified** groomer supply. Waitlists of four to eight weeks are frequently a **staffing** problem, not a marketing problem. Hybrid pay (floor + production), predictable lunch breaks, and not burning people on 10 aggressive dogs a day matter as much as Instagram.

**Rebook machine**  
A $120 groom that returns every six weeks beats a $150 hero cut that ghosts for nine months. Reminders, deposits, and “next appointment before you leave” are ops — not niceties.

**No-show / late-cancel discipline**  
Education guides often cite raw no-show rates in the **~10–15%** conversation without reminders/deposits — and much lower when card-on-file + automated reminders are real. Every empty slot still paid the groomer’s floor and the dryer electricity.

**Wet plant / water / facility**  
Hair traps, sloped waterproof floors, oversized drains, hot water capacity, ventilation, noise, parking for drop-off. Landlords who “had a boutique before” are not the same as landlords who understand commercial pet plumbing. Mobile skips the lease — then owns grey water, generators, and a van that cannot call in sick without killing the day.

**Temperament & safety protocols**  
Muzzles, double-handling, refusal rights, “we stop when the dog says stop.” Macho “I can handle anything” culture is how bite claims and Instagram disasters start.

**Insurance that matches the animals**  
General liability alone is often **not** enough when the animal is in your care, custody, and control — especially if you board or day-care. Animal bailee / CCC, workers’ comp for bites/scratches, and property for the wet plant are diligence items, not PDF wallpaper.

**Trade area & competitive set**  
Indie salons, mobile vans, membership hygiene concepts, and big-box counters (PetSmart / Petco–class) and retail+groom attach (Pet Supplies Plus–class adjacency) all skim the same dogs. Differentiation is reliability, coat skill, safety, and rebook experience — not a louder logo.

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only person who can finish a double-coated Groodle, you own a job with a lease. Scale starts when bathers + groomers + a front desk can run Tuesday without you on every scissor pass.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, FDD secondary summaries, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDDs (if franchised), local wage surveys, insurance quotes, and trailing book utilization.

**Capacity intuition (facility):**

- Solo table: often discussed around **~5–7 dogs/day** before quality and body break  
- Multi-groomer salon: volume scales with **tables × hours × show-up rate**, not with hope  
- Average ticket planning bases in education pieces often orbit roughly **~$75–$100+** blended — *your* mix will lie to that number

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Booth rent inside someone else’s shop: roughly **~$1k–$5k** to start + monthly booth rent (lifestyle / ceiling)  
- Indie multi-groomer salon TI + equipment: often discussed **~$50k–$150k+** (plumbing and dryers do not care about your spreadsheet optimism)  
- Mobile van: roughly **~$20k** used conversion conversation up to **~$100k+** new custom  
- Franchise salon systems (Scenthound-class Item 7 secondary summaries): roughly **~$328k–$550k** all-in band cited in 2025–2026 education writeups  
- Mobile franchise systems (Aussie Pet Mobile–class Item 7 secondary summaries): roughly **~$179k–$203k** band cited in the same class of writeups  
- Pet-supply retail with grooming attach (Pet Supplies Plus–class): **much heavier** inventory + box CapEx — different model; don’t underwrite it as a salon

**Item 19-style gross (category proof only — not your store):**  
Secondary FDD summaries have cited membership-salon averages in the mid-**$400k** gross conversation and mobile-system figures that vary widely by report vintage — **verify the actual FDD**. Gross ≠ owner cash. Royalties (often cited ~**6% + brand fund**) only help if the system fills chairs and shortens recruiting.

**Daycare / boarding overlay:**  
Higher fixed plant, overnight/weekend coverage, and claim severity. Occupancy looks beautiful on Saturdays before Labor Day and cruel on a rainy Tuesday in February.

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **completed** appointments vs booked slots (no-show / late-cancel %)?  
- Rebook % within 7 days of service?  
- Groomer count, tenure, pay model, and dogs-per-day by stylist?  
- Ticket mix: bath vs full groom vs add-on; size/coat mix?  
- Claims history (bites, heat incidents, lost animals) — redacted pattern?  
- Insurance: GL + **animal bailee/CCC** + workers’ comp — limits and exclusions?  
- Plumbing / dryer / HVAC CapEx remaining life; landlord willingness for wet use?  
- If daycare/boarding: licensed capacity, supervision ratios/policies, overnight coverage, sanitation SOPs?  
- If franchised: does the system shorten hiring and ramp — or only a royalty and a wall color?

Same brain you’d use buying any skilled-labor shop with a wet plant: hours, show-ups, insurance, and whether *you* want animals in your care all day.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Groomer recruiting & retention**  
The #1 ceiling. Schools and “my cousin bathes dogs” don’t refill tables as fast as doodle demand. You compete with other salons, mobile outfits, and “I’ll just booth-rent and keep 100%.” Hybrid pay, sane schedules, and not destroying bodies on volume are the real recruiting budget.

**2. No-shows & late cancels**  
A booked day that doesn’t show still burns labor floors, dryer heat, and the dog you turned away on the waitlist. Weak deposit policy = subsidizing other people’s chaos.

**3. Bite / injury liability (animal + human)**  
Dogs bite. Cats scratch. Groomers get hurt. Owners sue over style, injury, or “he was never aggressive at home.” Protocols, refusal rights, training, and the right insurance matter more than cute lobby art.

**4. Care / custody / control — especially with daycare & boarding**  
Once the animal is in your possession, standard GL exclusions can leave holes. Escapes, kennel fights, overnight illness, heat events — boarding liability is why “we’ll add suites later” is a different company, not a menu toggle.

**5. Water, plumbing, and wet-facility CapEx**  
Hair clogs, drain backups, hot-water starvation, nonslip failures, ventilation, laundry, noise complaints. The plant is a product. Underbuilding it to “save money” is how you buy a year of emergency plumbers and angry landlords.

**6. Seasonality & coat calendar**  
Spring shed, summer “make him look cute for the wedding,” holiday boarding spikes, January soft books. Mobile feels weather and driveway ice. Daycare feels weekday vs weekend. Annualize one December before you believe the model.

**7. Schedule honesty vs Instagram fantasy**  
Underscheduling large double-coats to hit dog-count vanity metrics creates overtime, injury, and quality complaints. Overblocking creates empty gaps. The calendar is the factory.

**8. Temperament & “difficult dog” selection**  
Saying yes to every reactive dog fills the day and empties the staff. Saying no protects the bench — and loses some Google reviews from people who wanted a miracle.

**9. Commission math that bankrupts the shop**  
Aggressive commission without a floor can chase groomers away; floors without production discipline can strand you paying for empty chairs. Hybrid structures exist because both pure models fail in different ways.

**10. Heat, dryers, and safety incidents**  
Cage-dryer misuse and overheating are reputation-ending events. Training + equipment standards are non-negotiable ops, not “old school” optional lore.

**11. Competition from big-box, mobile, and booth renters**  
Price shoppers will find a cheaper bath. Your defense is skill, safety, rebook reliability, and local trust — not racing the national flyer.

**12. Pet Supplies Plus / retail adjacency confusion**  
A retail box with a grooming bank is a **different capital stack** (inventory, shrink, retail hours). Borrow their traffic ideas carefully. Don’t underwrite salon cash like a 10,000 sq ft supply store — or assume their grooming wage pressure is irrelevant to you.

**13. Mobile-specific traps (if you lean van)**  
Van down = revenue down. Grey water dumping rules, generator noise, HOA driveway bans, fuel, and one-groomer-per-van ceilings. Premium ticket ≠ unlimited scale.

**14. Owner-as-hero stylist**  
If only you can finish the book, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a physical job with animal stress and wrist pain.

**15. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties and brand funds help only if they fill chairs, train groomers, and shorten ramp. A logo does not demat a Groodle on Tuesday.

**16. Confusing “pets are family” headlines with “your utilized, collectible table hours”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro love ≠ your Monday show-up rate.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means pet grooming is a **skilled-labor + wet-facility + animal-liability** business where empty chairs and open claims decide whether the cute brand survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit and retain groomers (or partner with someone who can) — and respect the physical craft  
- Wet-plant ops (drains, dryers, laundry, landlords) don’t scare you off  
- You’re willing to enforce deposits, refusals, and safety protocols when owners push back  
- Rebook dashboards and utilization math energize you  
- You’re capitalized for TI / van / working capital through a soft ramp  
- You’ve compared this door to [pet-waste-removal](/models/pet-waste-removal) (lighter plant, dirtier dignity) and [residential-cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) (similar labor war, different animal risk) and still want the salon stack

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a semi-absentee “pets print money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to animal unpredictability, bites, or upset owners  
- You refuse to manage commission drama or bench recruiting  
- You’re undercapitalized for plumbing-grade TI (or a real van) + months of marketing while chairs fill  
- You actually want **pet retail** scale, pure **mobile-only** without understanding vehicle risk, or pure **boarding** without CCC insurance — different underwriting  
- You thought “I’ll just add overnight suites” was a margin patch, not a liability upgrade

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: table hours, show-ups, rebooks, wet plant, insurance. A brand (membership salon, mobile van system, or indie peers) is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring and ramp — not because the van wrap photographs well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** pet-adjacent route (waste removal) or a human-services labor business first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Can’t hire / keep groomers → waitlist vanity, revenue ceiling  
- No-show bleed → busy calendar, empty cash  
- Bite / heat / coat-damage claim → reputation + insurance spiral  
- Daycare/boarding claim without proper CCC / bailee coverage → existential  
- Wet-plant failure or landlord conflict → forced dark days  
- Seasonality misread → January cash shock  
- Commission structure that can’t retain or can’t margin  
- Owner forever on the table  
- Van breakdown (mobile) → zero production  
- Royalty drag without recruiting / demand leverage  
- Buying a “book” that was one star groomer who leaves at close  
- Confusing pet-humanization headlines with utilized appointments

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “Americans love their dogs.”

You’re buying the right to run a **table-hour machine** in a wet facility (or a van that acts like one) — if you can recruit groomers, fill and *hold* appointments, rebook coats on cadence, price for size and time, and carry real animal liability without confusing daycare occupancy for free money.

Some people should start with a booth or a lean indie shop and prove rebooks before heavy TI.  
Some people should buy a tired salon and fix utilization, pay design, and safety protocols.  
Some people should choose **mobile** for lower lease / higher convenience tickets — and underwrite vehicles honestly.  
Some people should stay on the [pet-waste](/models/pet-waste-removal) or [residential cleaning](/models/residential-cleaning) side of pet/home labor and never take custody of animals.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do doodles need haircuts?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful skilled-labor job with drain clogs, no-shows, and higher stakes when the only groomer who can handle large dogs texts “I’m not coming in”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs pet-waste removal vs residential cleaning vs other health/wellness or home-service vehicles), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
