Saturday 10:40 a.m. Cul-de-sac.  
The trailer needs sixty feet of straight curb. The quiet generator that “never fails” hiccups once, then holds. Twelve kids are already in party mode on the lawn while a parent asks whether the PlayStations have the *new* sports title — the one that dropped Thursday and isn’t imaged on your SSD yet.

Corporate HR wants a Tuesday team-builder that isn’t another escape room or [wine-paint](/models/wine-paint) night.  
A school carnival wants a half-day block and a COI naming the district.  
Birthday season still wants a private party that comes to *their* driveway so nobody has to clean a trampoline park.

Someone needs a **climate-controlled gaming theater on wheels**: vehicle CapEx that survives miles and kids, a booking calendar that fills weekends without lying about January, a generator that doesn’t kill the party (or the neighbor’s patience), coaches who can host without you, consoles and titles that don’t look ancient on TikTok, insurance that parks will accept, and deposit discipline so May cash isn’t already spent when October softens.

That’s **mobile gaming trucks** — rolling video-game parties / game theaters / laser-tag attach variants — in the real world.

Not [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) (they come to *your* puzzle rooms for a timed ticket; you don’t haul a theater to their curb — same *experiential Saturday* emotion, different CapEx plant).  
Not [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (you don’t stake vinyl; you sell **completed gaming hours** inside a financed trailer — same *weekend delivery + weather + deposit* brain, different merchandise and liability).  
Not [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) (same *events + truck + generator + weather* neighborhood — you serve *tickets of play time*, not perishable food under a health code).  
Not [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) (recurring class packs + in-facility birthday blocks — shared birthday demand; different enrichment spine and lease).  
Not “I’ll wrap a used box truck, buy three Switches off Marketplace, and retire on Saturdays” as the whole *vehicle CapEx + seasonal calendar + power + refresh + insurance* story.

You’re running an **event-utilization machine on a rolling plant**: booked event hours × ticket − driver/coach labor − fuel − generator − insurance − tech refresh − the cash hole when peak weekends are full, shoulder months are quiet, and the note on the trailer still clears.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **vehicle CapEx**, **event calendar seasonality**, **weather**, **generator / power**, **booking pipeline**, **game / tech refresh**, **insurance**, **driver / staff**, and **deposit discipline** show up before the wrap looks like a brand Reel of kids with headsets.

GameTruck / Rolling Video Games / Mobile Game Party–class systems and indie neighborhood trailers sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The filled calendar, power reliability, refresh reserve, insurance file, and coach who shows up are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **mobile entertainment / video-game party operator**: a climate-controlled trailer or truck outfitted with consoles, screens, seating, sound, and onboard power; booked for birthdays, school events, festivals, and corporate blocks; staffed by a driver/host (“game coach”) who sets up, runs the party, and tears down.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — and don’t blur either with indoor arcades or bounce fleets):**

| | **Mobile gaming theater / trailer (primary spine for this page)** | **Indoor arcade / trampoline / soft-play venue (different CapEx factory)** |
|--|--------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Timed private gaming parties *at their site* (plus corporate / school / festival blocks) | Tickets and parties inside *your* leased building |
| **Primary constraint** | **Booked event hours × ticket − coach labor − vehicle CapEx − power − insurance − refresh − seasonality** | Occupancy + lease + floor labor + birthday CAC |
| **Cash shape** | Spiky Fri–Sun + holiday/summer peaks; soft midweeks and shoulder months | More weekday potential; rent doesn’t care about rain (kids still might) |
| **Risk feel** | Generator death, curb space, weather holds, tech obsolescence, commercial auto | Lease + buildout + staffing + local competition |
| **Owner job early** | Book + drive + host + image games + chase COIs | Fill rooms, hire hosts, manage facility ops |

Many real operators are **hybrids** — video theater *plus* outdoor laser tag / foam / sports attach *plus* thin corporate. Underwrite the mix. A pure birthday trailer is a different company than a multi-activity festival fleet. [Party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) vinyl and [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) party rooms share *celebration demand* without sharing your vehicle note or SSD refresh tax.

Typical menu / revenue mix:

1. **Residential birthday / private party packages** — the spine in most books (often ~2-hour blocks)  
2. **Extended time / larger guest counts / multi-trailer** — yield when peak Saturdays sell out  
3. **School / church / HOA / carnival / festival blocks** — fewer events, bigger tickets, COI / additional-insured friction, permit calendars  
4. **Corporate / team-building / employee appreciation** — weekday fill *or* the sales fantasy that never closes  
5. **Laser tag / outdoor activity attach** (where offered) — higher ticket, weather and yard-space tax  
6. **Fundraiser / ticketed public events** — pipeline gold *or* logistics and liability theater  
7. **Not the same company:** [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) facility slots; [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) inflatable fleets; [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) cook-line trucks; [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) recurring enrichment; fixed arcades / trampoline parks

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google Local / Maps, Facebook / Instagram birthday searches, school vendor lists, corporate outreach, franchise lead systems (if any), referral parents  
2. **Book** — online calendar; deposit (often **~25–50%**) weeks out; curb-space / HOA / weather / age policy clear  
3. **Stage** — games imaged, controllers charged, generator fueled/tested, spare HDMI / controller kit, waivers, COI if venue requires  
4. **Mobilize** — drive / tow; park in ~60 ft of level curb (operator chatter — verify your unit); power up; host briefing  
5. **Deliver** — timed party; coach manages turns, conflicts, bathroom runs, “is Fortnite on?” diplomacy  
6. **Tear down & recover** — power down, secure gear, fuel, next drop if the calendar stacked it  
7. **Clean / refresh / insurance file** — Monday’s unpaid factory: update titles, replace sticks, patch wrap, answer the deposit refund queue

**Scope lock:** this page is **mobile gaming trucks / trailers as an event-utilization + vehicle-CapEx business** — events, seasonal calendar, generator/power, booking pipeline, tech refresh, insurance, driver/staff, deposit discipline.  
It is **not** [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) puzzle venues.  
It is **not** [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) bounce / tent fleets (sister delivery calendar; different plant).  
It is **not** [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) (rolling kitchen + health regs).  
It is **not** [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) facility class packs.  
It is **not** “passive weekend cash from a wrapped trailer” without someone who drives, hosts, images games, and answers when NOAA ruins the deposit stack.

## How money comes in

You sell **completed event hours that actually perform** (and attach packages) — not “kids love video games” and not a Reel about passive Saturdays.

Education pricing / check shape (national operator / consumer menu chatter; metros sit higher; rural books sit tighter; corporate and festival blocks change yield — **verify your market**):

- Residential birthday / private packages commonly discussed roughly **~$250–$600+** for a standard ~2-hour window; branded metro menus often cluster nearer the **~$400–$500** start for signature theater packages  
- Per-guest education illustrations sometimes land near **~$20–$30**/guest as a *story* check — *shape*, not your rate card  
- Corporate / school / festival half-days commonly climb into **four figures** when headcount, duration, and multi-activity attach show up  
- Volume wins when **weekend utilization × show-up reliability × coach quality × attach** compound — an empty trailer on a sold-out May Saturday and a dead generator at kickoff erase “great marketing”

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Peak-day / weekend utilization** — how many billed event hours the trailer earns in a week (peak seasons can look busy; full-year averages are lower)  
2. **Average ticket + attach** — extended time, second unit, laser-tag/outdoor add-ons, travel fees beyond a radius  
3. **Booking pipeline density** — inbound birthday SEO *and* outbound school/corporate; empty midweeks start as empty outreach  
4. **Coach / driver labor** — the “passive” line item; show-up, hosting skill, overtime on stacked Saturdays  
5. **Fuel + windshield** — stacking three parties across a metro feels like an empire and behaves like a part-time driving job  
6. **Generator / power reliability** — downtime is infinite COGS of zero revenue mid-party  
7. **Insurance + COI friction** — commercial auto, GL, inland marine; additional-insured certificates for schools/parks  
8. **Weather holds / reschedules** — outdoor attach and even theater parties die when storms, heat advisories, or unsafe roads hit; deposits aren’t earned until the day performs  
9. **Game / console / TV refresh CapEx** — titles age out; kids notice; TripAdvisor notices  
10. **Seasonality** — summer / holiday / spring birthday clusters fund the year — or they don’t while the trailer note still clears in February

Gross / cost intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Well-run event books often look **fat on contribution per party** once the trailer is rolling — labor, fuel, and consumables are modest vs ticket  
- Net after insurance, trailer payment, storage/yard, marketing, refresh reserve, weather refunds, and owner unpaid hours is often a **tighter** owner-income story than “$500 × every Saturday forever” fantasy math  
- Franchise fee / royalty stacks (where present) are a co-owner — underwrite net after fees, not Instagram GMV  
- Dollar utilization teaching: annual event revenue ÷ acquisition cost of the rolling plant — strong units can multiple CapEx in a good year; trailers under weak annual utilization are stranded capital with a wrap

You’re not a gamer with a CDL fantasy.  
You’re an **event-calendar + vehicle-CapEx + power + refresh** company wearing LED lights.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Kids still have birthdays. Parents still want a party that doesn’t trash the living room. Schools still run field days and fundraisers. Churches and HOAs still want a Saturday that feels like an event. Corporate still needs a team-builder that isn’t another Zoom icebreaker — and sometimes a gaming trailer beats another ropes course.

Good times → more multi-hour packages, second trailers, corporate festivals, willingness to pay for premium titles and attach activities.  
Tight times → families trade down to backyard Switch nights or bounce-house amateurs; price wars with underinsured Marketplace trailers get louder; console generations, insurance, and fuel don’t ask permission.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) rides **event-day asset utilization × ticket − delivery labor − weather − damage**.  
[Mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) rides **event/lot access × tickets − food − labor − permits − weather − equipment**.  
[Escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) rides **paid room-slots × ticket − GM labor − rent − marketing − refresh CapEx**.  
[Kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) rides **class fill × coach quality − rent − birthday attach ops**.  
Mobile gaming trucks ride **booked event hours × ticket − coach labor − vehicle CapEx − power − insurance − tech refresh − seasonality** — experiential entertainment that **rolls to the customer** instead of (or instead of relying on) a leased puzzle room or padded gym.

Same “someone wants a memorable party *here, now*” demand emotion. Different invoice, different CapEx story, different empty Tuesday — and a co-owner named **the generator** and another named **the console generation cycle**.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “I make $10k/month passive on game trucks” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one trailer

A single mobile gaming book lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Vehicle CapEx (the career-defining balance sheet)**  
Climate-controlled trailers / trucks, wraps, seating, AV, consoles, generators, tow vehicles. Indie education bands for a working start commonly land roughly **~$40k–$150k+** all-in depending on new vs used, size, and whether you already own a tow truck; branded franchise Item 7 education for GameTruck–class systems often lands roughly **~$164k–$360k** total investment including fees (verify current FDD — ranges move). Notion’s Mobile Franchises teaching is blunt for the *class*: the biggest launch bottleneck is **buying the truck/equipment** (and warehouse/yard if required). Cheap used consumer “party vans” that insurers won’t write and that overheat mid-party are CapEx in disguise.

**Event calendar seasonality (utilization upstream of the wrap)**  
Birthday and school demand piles onto **Fri–Sun**, spring–early fall peaks, and holiday clusters. Northern winters and wet springs still bill insurance, storage, and notes. Corporate / school / weekday mix is how some operators blunt the cliff — not a free lunch. Underwrite **trailing productive events per month for 12–24 months**, not the soft-open May Instagram grid.

**Weather (the co-owner you can’t fire)**  
Storms cancel outdoor attach and sometimes the whole party when roads or yards are unsafe. Extreme heat turns a poorly cooled trailer into a liability. Neighbor noise complaints and HOA generator rules are real. Same weather honesty as [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) and [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) — different product, same NOAA.

**Generator / power (mid-party death)**  
Quiet inverter generators, fuel, load management, shore-power options, spare cables. A dead generator at minute twenty is a refund, a one-star review, and a ruined referral chain. Power is not a vibe — it’s an ops checklist with a spare plan.

**Booking pipeline (inbound birthdays ≠ a company)**  
Website calendar, Google Local, Meta ads, school vendor applications, corporate outreach, referral loops. Franchise lead systems (if any) are a tax *or* a shortcut — underwrite what they actually deliver. Empty Tuesdays start as empty outbound. Same pipeline honesty as party-rentals weekends and escape-room corporate hunts.

**Game / tech refresh (the escape-room cousin on wheels)**  
Consoles, TVs, titles, controllers, headsets. Kids and parents notice last-gen hardware and missing day-one titles. Spoiler blogs don’t kill rooms the same way, but **review age and “old games” comments** kill rebooks. Budget a refresh reserve — [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) call it room refresh; you call it SSD and SKU discipline.

**Insurance (the silent gatekeeper)**  
Commercial auto, general liability, inland marine for gear in transit, workers’ comp when you hire, additional-insured COIs for schools/parks/corporates. Personal auto policies often fail the moment the trailer is commercial. One severity claim dwarfs a season of pretty birthday tickets. Underinsured Facebook competitors still show up in the price war — racing them is how professionals go broke.

**Driver / staff (the passive-income lie)**  
Someone drives, parks, hosts, mediates controller fights, cleans, and answers “are we getting refunded?” texts. Owner-as-only-coach “savings” is unpaid labor with a weather app and a stacked Saturday. Hiring before density exists burns cash; waiting too long burns the owner’s weekends and marriage. Family-flexibility buyers: read this as **your weekends are inventory**.

**Deposit discipline (cash that isn’t yours yet)**  
Booking deposits feel like revenue and aren’t. Weather refunds and no-show policies cluster. Owners who spend May deposits like salary discover August console replacements and October voids. Damage deposits on controllers are refundable liabilities until you document a claim. Same accounting honesty as [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals).

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many books is **owner books + drives + hosts**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until a coach bench, a dispatcher, and QA exist. Existing-owner-scale buyers: the bottleneck is usually **peak-day capacity + trained hosts + second unit CapEx**, not another Facebook ad.

**Curb space / HOA / venue rules**  
~60 feet of straight level space is common operator chatter for full theaters — cul-de-sacs help; tight urban streets don’t. HOAs ban generators or overnight parking. Schools want COIs days or weeks ahead. Ops friction is a sales cycle.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Vehicle CapEx vs slow cashflow**  
Trailers, wraps, AV, generators, and tow vehicles tie up cash before shoulder seasons prove out. Buying a second unit because May looked busy is how notes outrun utilization. Same trap class as [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) vinyl fleets and [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) cook-line trucks — different merchandise. Notion’s mobile teaching names equipment CapEx as the launch bottleneck for a reason.

**2. Event calendar seasonality (the silent year tax)**  
Peak months fund the year — or they don’t. Summer birthdays, spring parties, and holiday clusters can look like a business while February is a storage payment with LED lights. Without corporate/school/weekday mix or cash reserves, quiet months feel like a boat payment with headsets.

**3. Weekend concentration (the lifestyle tax)**  
Demand piles onto Fri–Sun. Your “flexible lifestyle business” may be a **non-negotiable Saturday factory**. Hiring coaches helps; training and showing up still sit on you until systems exist. Family calendars collide with birthday calendars — underwrite that first.

**4. Weather**  
Storms, heat, unsafe roads, and canceled outdoor attach don’t care about your deposit stack. Written weather policies and refund reserves are ops — not optional “nice-to-haves.” You cannot spreadsheet away NOAA.

**5. Generator / power failure**  
Mid-party blackouts, noisy units that get you banned from HOAs, fuel mismanagement, no spare inverter. Power reliability is a product feature parents feel immediately. Test before you roll; carry a spare plan.

**6. Booking pipeline gaps**  
Inbound birthday SEO is not a twelve-month P&L. Soft midweeks and soft winters expose operators who never built school/corporate outbound. Franchise lead promises that don’t convert are a fee stack without a calendar.

**7. Game / tech refresh**  
New console generations, day-one sports titles, broken controllers, dim TVs, stale libraries. Kids compare you to their living room *and* to the trampoline park down the road. Skip the refresh reserve and your reviews age out like an unrefreshed [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) theme.

**8. Insurance & COI theater**  
Commercial auto gaps, GL limits schools reject, inland marine after theft from an unlocked trailer, workers’ comp when you hire coaches. Personal policies that “cover my truck” often don’t. One claim can erase a season. Parks and districts that need certificates weeks ahead kill last-minute “easy” bookings.

**9. Driver / staff / coach dependency**  
No-shows on Saturday morning, hosts who can’t manage twelve kids, owners who never document SOPs. Misclassified “helpers,” commercial-auto gaps when someone uses a personal truck, and heat illness in an AC-failed trailer are real. You didn’t buy freedom if only you can drive *and* host *and* image games.

**10. Deposit / cash timing traps**  
Booking deposits feel like profit the week they hit. Weather refunds cluster. Console replacements don’t wait for October. Hold refund and refresh buffers; recognize revenue when the event performs. Damage deposits on controllers are liabilities until documented.

**11. Windshield / poor stacking density**  
Three parties across a metro feel like an empire and behave like a late tear-down with a furious 4 p.m. parent. Route businesses die on miles — game trucks die on **miles + hard party start times**.

**12. Curb space, HOAs, and venue rules**  
No sixty feet of curb, generator bans, noise complaints, school vendor applications. Ops friction is part of the sales cycle — not an afterthought.

**13. Easy-entry / fly-by-night price war**  
A Facebook “gaming van” with consumer TVs and no commercial insurance still pollutes Google. You carry commercial coverage, commercial-grade cooling/power, and real show-up rates. Matching their price is a gift to their lawyer and your bankruptcy.

**14. Outdoor attach & specialty risk**  
Laser tag / foam / sports packages lift ticket and weather/liability exposure. Add them because utilization and coverage support it — not because Instagram likes neon.

**15. Theft, vandalism, and gear death**  
Controllers walk off, trailers get broken into, AC dies in July. Inland marine and physical security are operating costs — not paranoia.

**16. Franchise / brand fee drag without leverage**  
A logo that doesn’t shorten bookings, insurance, SOPs, or coach hiring is a royalty on Saturdays you already earned. Indie is not automatically smarter — but fee stacks without demand leverage are.

**17. Owner-as-hero trailer tech**  
If only you know every cul-de-sac quirk, every HOA rule, every image process, and every generator quirk, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a Saturday career with HDMI cables.

**18. Confusing “kids always play video games” headlines with “your collectible net after weekend labor, insurance, weather, refresh, and idle CapEx”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your dead generator Sunday and your February note.

**19. Fantasy extrapolation math**  
Pretty per-party margins → **$X × every Saturday × N trailers** → empire screenshots — then shoulder season and one claim that don’t care. If your underwriting needs perfect weather and perfect utilization, you don’t have underwriting.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means mobile gaming trucks are an **event / vehicle / power / refresh** business where empty weekends, soft seasons, generator death, stale tech, insurance reality, coach no-shows, and the passive-income myth decide whether the trailer survives the next slow October.

---

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can live inside **weekend ops + hosting discipline** until a real coach cadence exists — or you’ve already bought a book with trailing proof  
- Utilization spreadsheets, event calendars, insurance files, and refresh budgets energize you more than they nauseate you  
- Testing generators, imaging games, and saying no to unsafe weather or impossible curb spaces feel like diligence, not boredom  
- You’re honest that “passive Saturdays” is marketing — sister energy to Brian’s mobile / vending passive myths — and you’re still willing to own the trailer early  
- You’ve compared this door to [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms) (facility experiential), [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (vinyl delivery), [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food) (rolling food), and [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) (facility enrichment) — and still want rolling gaming parties  
- You’re willing to fund tech refresh and raise prices for insurance reality instead of racing Facebook amateurs

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted passive “everybody throws gaming birthdays” income from month one  
- You’re allergic to weekend work, windshield time, kid chaos, or explaining weather cancellations to angry parents  
- You’re undercapitalized for trailer + insurance + shoulder seasons + refresh and assumed Marketplace prices erased working capital  
- You actually want an **escape-room venue**, a **bounce fleet**, a **food truck**, or a **kids gym** — different underwriting  
- You thought “kids love Fortnite” erases utilization, generator, and insurance math  
- You need the trendiest vertical more than boring calendar density + power reliability in *your* metro

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: bookings, utilization, coach labor, insurance, weather, power uptime, refresh. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens bookings, SOPs, insurance, and coach systems — not because a Reel made a wrap look easy.

Many operators will correctly choose a **weekday route/service truck** or a different celebration vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Low utilization → CapEx stranded in a wrapped trailer  
- Weekend-only cash → lifestyle and labor cliff  
- Seasonality → notes and insurance through the off-season  
- Weather refund clusters → deposit cash already spent  
- Generator / power failure → mid-party refund + review death  
- Stale games / console lag → rebook death  
- Injury / liability / auto claim → severity that dwarfs a season  
- Theft / vandalism / AC death → replacement spiral  
- Coach no-shows → late starts → referral death  
- Poor stacking density → windshield eats peak days  
- Underinsurance / wrong carrier → claim denied when it matters  
- Easy-entry price war → busy-broke  
- Buying a “fleet” that was consumer junk + verbal bookings + no loss runs  
- Confusing passive-income marketing with collectible net after labor, weather, insurance, refresh, and debt  
- Fantasy extrapolation ($X per Saturday × N) without trailing by month and by weekend  
- Royalty / brand drag without demand or hiring leverage  
- Owner forever on the calendar, the generator, and the 10 a.m. kickoff

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “kids love video games.”

You’re buying the right to run an **event-utilization machine on a rolling plant** — if you can **keep the trailer booking on the days that matter**, treat **vehicle CapEx as a utilization problem** (not free, and not a coffee-shop buildout), fund **generators that don’t fail mid-party**, staff coaches before your weekends break, price after insurance and weather reality, hold a **tech refresh reserve** before reviews age out, survive **deposit discipline** when May cash tempts you, and not confuse a Marketplace van and a pretty per-party margin for a twelve-month salary with Saturdays off.

Some people should buy a tired delivery book with real trailing-by-month and fix pricing, contracts, insurance, coaches, and refresh age (resale path — diligence still required).  
Some people should start with one honest unit, prove weekend utilization and power reliability, and only then add outdoor attach or a second trailer.  
Some people should choose [escape-rooms](/models/escape-rooms), [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals), [mobile-food](/models/mobile-food), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), or another vehicle and never chase HDMI kits.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different door entirely.

Brian’s thin direct corpus here is useful as **honesty**, not as a template: the category shows up on the **250 Franchise Ideas** sheet (row **87**); mobile-class teaching lives in the **Mobile Franchises** newsletter and CORE **truck/route** lens; the deep operating teaching lives in **sister models** — weekend event concentration (party rentals), rolling events + generator (mobile food), experiential refresh tax (escape rooms), birthday demand (kids fitness). Don’t invent a Brian game-truck sermon that doesn’t exist.

The question isn’t “do kids play video games at parties?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful trailer job with a financed wrap, a weather app, a stacked Saturday, a generator that hiccups at kickoff, a parent texting “where’s the new sports game?,” and a deposit stack already spent like salary when February’s note still clears and the next birthday is already booked behind a coach who just called out?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs escape-room facility experiential vs party-asset fleets vs mobile food vs kids enrichment vs other event/truck paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
