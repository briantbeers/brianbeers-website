The living room is a fishbowl.  
The south windows cook the sofa.  
The builder’s cheap vinyl blinds broke the first time a kid yanked the wand.

Someone has to measure every opening (inside mount vs outside, depth, obstructions), sell light control + privacy + style the couple can both live with, order custom product that doesn’t exist until it’s cut, schedule installers who don’t scar the drywall, and stand behind a shade that won’t drop evenly in month four.

That’s **blinds / window coverings**.

Not big-box stock sizes as a side hustle.  
Not a handyman hanging Amazon blinds.  
Not “I own a van and wait for Facebook leads” as the whole company.

A **design-to-install pipeline**: consult → measure → sell a scope → order from manufacturers → install → warranty the fit when humidity moves a shutter or a roller is 1/8" short.

High ticket relative to a punch-list visit.  
Sales-driven.  
Manufacturer-dependent production.  
Measure-intolerant.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Brutal if you confuse “everyone has windows” with “this runs itself.”

---

## What the business actually is

You sell **custom window treatments** for the home (and often light commercial) — blinds, shades, shutters, soft treatments — and increasingly the attach that moves ticket and differentiation: motorization, smart-home integration, exterior solar shades / awnings, and (in some models) **repairs**.

Typical product ladder in the category (operators mix differently):

1. **Entry / repair / single-room** — fix a broken cord, replace one bathroom roller, small ticket that opens the door (volume + trust lane when you offer it)  
2. **Room package — blinds / shades** — cellular, roller, Roman, wood/faux wood — mid ticket, workhorse of most books  
3. **Shutters / premium hard treatments** — higher ticket, longer measure discipline, more “forever” framing in the sale  
4. **Soft treatments / drapery** — design theater, longer cycle, attach to hard treatments  
5. **Motorization / automation** — remotes, apps, solar-powered options, whole-home scenes — margin and differentiation when sold cleanly  
6. **Exterior shade / patio / awning** — bigger outdoor tickets, weather/seasonality, different install muscle  
7. **Builder / realtor / designer / commercial** — model homes, offices, multifamily common areas (relationship channel when you earn it)

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / SEO / LSA, brand search, past customer, realtor / designer / GC referral, home show, yard signs / neighborhood, repair inbound (if you offer it), showroom walk-in (if you have one)  
2. **Consult / measure** — in-home (or showroom + site measure); photos, opening count, inside vs outside mount, depth, sill, handles, HVAC registers, tile lip, “will the door clear?”  
3. **Design & propose** — samples / boards; good / better / best; light-filtering vs room-darkening; motor vs manual; deposit in stronger shops  
4. **Close** — financing, selections locked, change-order rules written *before* the factory cuts  
5. **Order** — manufacturer / national program; freight and damage protocol; zero (or near-zero) inventory is common — beautiful for cash **if** deposits are real  
6. **Wait** — lead times (days to weeks; specialty longer) while you manage expectations  
7. **Install** — W-2 crew or vetted install specialists; protect floors and drywall; level, square, program remotes; punch list  
8. **Collect / warranty** — finals, reviews, callbacks; your name stays on every window the homeowner looks through

**Scope lock for this page:** company that **sells and delivers** residential (and light commercial) custom window-covering projects. Design + project management + manufacturer partners + measure-install orchestration. Showroom **or** mobile closer — both are real lanes. Repair attach is optional but strategically real in some systems.

**Out of scope (different models):**  
- Pure big-box / e-commerce stock blinds with no custom measure muscle  
- DIY Amazon hang as the business  
- Solo installer who *is* the company (craft job ≠ this page’s ownership math)  
- Pure soft-goods drapery atelier with no hard-treatment engine (adjacent)  
- Pure **window cleaning** (glass service, not product — see window cleaning)  
- Full interior design firm where coverings are a line item, not the machine

**Two honest lanes inside the category:**  
Some systems lean **mobile / design-to-order** (garage or small warehouse, van + laptop + manufacturer partners, heavier digital + local lead gen).  
Some lean **showroom ± design studio** (intent traffic, sample theater, higher occupancy, more “feel the shutter” closing).  
Both are “blinds.” The operating reality is not identical — diligence the mix.

Named systems exist (Budget Blinds, Bloomin’ Blinds, Gotcha Covered, independents everywhere, manufacturer-dealer networks). Treat them as proof the category is real and franchises or dealers at scale — not as the point of this page.

Brian’s teaching frame that maps onto this category (he *has* published dedicated blinds content — day-in-the-life + newsletter + podcast — so this page sits on **stack teaching + category placement + those operator/education notes**, still not a P&L promise):

- Window coverings sit in the **high-ticket, sales-driven home services** stack with closets, cabinets, flooring, painting, fencing — project tickets often framed in the **$5k / $10k / $30k+** conversation depending on whole-home vs single-room scope (single rollers drag averages; shutters + motorization + multi-room pull them up)  
- Same **sales / marketing / closing KPIs** as other project home services; execution differs (measure + factory order), accountability of sellers rhymes ([TsYBbpC5UA4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsYBbpC5UA4))

## How money comes in

You sell **projects** (and sometimes repair tickets), not hours the homeowner sees on a timesheet.

A single bathroom roller or repair can land low three to low four figures.  
A solid multi-window room package often sits mid-four.  
Whole-home hard treatments commonly climb high-four to mid-five; shutters + motorization + soft treatments and exterior work go higher.  
Industry education (not Brian forecasts): consumer and operator guides often discuss per-window custom treatments from roughly **low hundreds to low thousands** depending on product (cellular vs wood vs shutter vs motorized), with whole-home packages spanning **low-to-mid five figures** in many markets — local labor, product tier, and window count move those hard. Public Item-19 invoice/category mixes in this lane often show **average invoices in the low-to-mid four figures** on core blinds/shades lines because volume small jobs dilute the Instagram shutter package.

Revenue mix usually looks like some blend of:

| Channel | What it feels like |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Repairs / small tickets** | Faster cycle, trust gateway, high contribution when done on-site; not every brand emphasizes this. |
| **Blinds / shades packages** | Workhorse volume; price-sensitive shoppers; install density matters. |
| **Shutters / premium hard** | Bigger tickets, longer sells, unforgiving measures. |
| **Motorization / soft / exterior attach** | Differentiation and ticket lift — also more SKU complexity and callbacks. |
| **Builder / designer / commercial partner** | They bring clients; you stay niche on coverings; relationship sales. |
| **Showroom vs mobile-only** | Intent traffic + rent vs go-to-the-house + digital/local CAC. |

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Close rate on qualified consults** — design confidence + process, not “more leads” alone  
2. **Average job size / attach** — second room, shutters upgrade, motorization, drapery, exterior shade  
3. **Selection discipline** — locked fabrics/colors/lift systems before cut; change orders priced or they eat you  
4. **Measure accuracy** — 1/8" errors become remakes; remakes destroy schedule, margin, and reviews  
5. **Manufacturer reliability** — lead times you can keep; freight damage; wrong SKU; discontinued fabric  
6. **Install quality + punch speed** — crooked valances and scarred drywall tour the neighborhood group chat  
7. **Cash terms** — deposit / progress / pay-before-order culture vs financing the factory yourself  
8. **Lead economics** — CAC vs close rate vs ticket; repair inbound vs paid search vs brand  
9. **Mix** — all-premium, long-cycle book without a smaller-room / repair lane can starve the calendar

Gross margin lives in **price − product/materials − install labor − sales commission − warranty/rework**.  
Then overhead: design labor or software, showroom (if any), office/PM, insurance, local marketing, vans, sample inventory, and (if franchised) royalty + brand fund + tech / call-center fees.

Industry shape (education only): healthy shops obsess over **sold → ordered → installed → collected** lag, not vanity “pipeline” screenshots. A busy consult calendar with nothing shipping is a hobby with a wrapped van.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Housing stock ages. Builder-grade coverings fail. UV destroys furniture. Privacy and light control never go out of style — even when *which* shade is fashionable changes. New construction and remodel cycles both create openings. Empty-nesters refresh before listing. Buyers walk into a fishbowl living room and mentally subtract five figures from the offer.

When the economy is good, people motorize whole homes and build dream patio shades.  
When the economy is soft, luxury whole-home packages pause first — **repairs, single-room replacements, and “make this room livable”** often last longer than fashion-forward drapery packages (Brian’s category notes lean on repair as a soft-economy buffer in models that offer it — verify whether *your* vehicle actually does).

That’s the demand story.

The constraint isn’t “homes have windows.”

It’s **trust + design close rate + measure discipline + factory partners + install capacity** in *your* territory — on a **discretionary-to-semi-discretionary, visual, long-memory** purchase.

Unlike emergency plumbing, nobody’s roller shade “fails” at 11pm in a way that forces a same-week close every time — though a broken cord on a nursery blackout comes close.  
You live in the gap between “we should do something about these windows” and “we signed and paid.”

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Sales + design as one motion**  
This isn’t storm canvass math. It’s consultative: measure, listen, budget tool or good/better/best, overcome “it depends,” get to a number without killing trust. Same pain cabinets and closets shops face when a homeowner stares at a fog between a big-box kit and a five-figure shutter package — process that gives a fast, honest budget wins.

**Measure is the product**  
You are not selling fabric samples. You are selling *fit*. Inside mount that needed outside. Depth that couldn’t clear a handle. A window that wasn’t square. A plantation shutter ordered off a bad width. This is where margin goes to die — quietly, weeks later, when the box arrives wrong.

**Long-ish sales cycle (shorter than kitchens, longer than a drain clean)**  
Samples on the coffee table. Spouse alignment. “After we paint.” Financing. Your CRM and follow-up cadence *are* the business — especially when paid leads are expensive.

**Manufacturer / supplier partners**  
You rarely manufacture. You curate programs: price tiers, lead times, freight, defect policy, motorization platforms. Partner failure becomes *your* Google review. Inventory-light (order after sell) is common — beautiful for cash **if** deposits are real and selections are locked.

**Lead-time expectation management**  
Days to weeks from locked order to install is normal; specialty fabrics and shutters stretch. Soft dates + hard homeowner vacations = rage.

**Install orchestration**  
W-2 finish techs vs 1099 install specialists. Either way: protect floors, hit studs or use the right anchors, square the headrail, program remotes, leave the room usable. Patience is thin when afternoon sun is cooking the sofa they just reupholstered.

**Showroom vs mobile (this is a real fork)**  
Intent-based category: people search; they don’t impulse-buy shutters like a smoothie. Showroom = sample theater + rent + staffing. Mobile = lower occupancy + you bring the theater in a bag + you fund lead gen like your life depends on it (because it does). Diligence *which* machine you’re buying — Budget Blinds–scale systems historically mix mobile and showroom stories; many newer blinds concepts stay home-based / van-first. Names are category proof, not a recommendation.

**Repairs as strategy (optional, powerful)**  
Some operators use low-ticket repairs as the oil-change equivalent: get in the house, win trust, convert to new product. High contribution on true on-site fixes; also scheduling complexity and “we thought you were the cheap repair guy” positioning risk. Not every brand leans this way — ask.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: lead flow, design/sales leadership (or being the closer), measure QA, factory relationship, install scheduling, cash, reviews.  
Not “I bought a logo and went fishing.”  
Brian’s anti-fit point applies hard here: **sales-heavy, high-ticket home services** fight semi-absentee until the sales culture is real. His blinds operator story frames the capacity ceiling of “I do 100% of sales and help install” — great margins, limited scale until hiring works.

**Systems**  
Estimating / quoting tools, measure SOPs (photos + dual-check culture), digital paperwork handoff from sales → ops, production calendar, warranty SOP. Hero closers don’t scale past their calendar.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

**Startup / investment shape (education):**

- Brian’s broad home-services franchise band often lands roughly **~$100k–$200k** (painting, roofing, plumbing, flooring, restoration named together in one teaching clip) — *category* band that overlaps many mobile window-covering concepts, **not** a guarantee for every blinds FDD.  
- Public FDD aggregator reads (verify current filings yourself):  
  - **Budget Blinds–style** (category proof; large national footprint): Item 7 often cited roughly **~$100k–$211k**; royalty commonly discussed around **~3.5%** (+ brand/tech fees in some summaries) — territory packaging and vehicle/showroom choices move the check  
  - **Bloomin’ Blinds–style** (category proof; mobile / repair-capable story): Item 7 often cited roughly **~$115k–$246k** depending on filing year and territory package; royalty stacks commonly discussed around **~6%** (+ brand fund ~2%, tech/call-center fees in some filings) — **always verify current FDD**  
  - **Gotcha Covered–style** (category proof; home-based design consult): Item 7 often cited roughly **~$123k–$167k**  
- FranServe / Brian’s sheet placement of blinds brands is **category proof** someone markets these deals — not your unit economics.  
- Brian’s published operator story (one franchisee, education only): lean garage start, low headcount early, multi-year ramp disclosed in public content as roughly **~$700k then ~$1.2M** calendar years on that path — **not a forecast, not typical, not transferable**. Use it to understand *shape* (lean overhead, sales-led, capacity tied to owner), not as a target.

**Operating intuition (industry + public Item-19 education, not forecasts):**

- Ticket ladder: repairs / single openings often **three to low four figures**; room packages **mid-four**; whole-home / shutters / motorization **high-four to five+**  
- Some mature franchise cohorts in public FDD summaries show **average unit sales from low-mid six figures into higher**, with **very wide** spreads (low hundreds of thousands to multi-million outliers) — proof of operator + market variance, **not** a starter forecast  
- Product mix matters: blinds/shades can dominate invoice count while shutters / motorization / exterior pull revenue share; materials/COGS often discussed near **~50%+ of sales** in some disclosed P&L shapes before labor and overhead — your mix will disagree  
- Cycle time: days for simple replacements vs **weeks of factory lag** for custom / shutters — capacity planning is a queue of sold-but-not-installed jobs  
- Lead spend: local advertising minimums and paid search CAC are real line items; ask for **lead → consult → sold → installed** conversion, not vanity lead counts

Diligence questions that matter more than memorizing averages:

- What’s the **repair vs blinds/shades vs shutters vs motorization vs soft vs exterior** mix last 24 months?  
- Median days from first consult → signed contract → ordered → install complete?  
- Deposit / pay-before-order policy — and what happens when the customer changes a fabric after the factory cut?  
- **Measure error / remake rate** — who eats it (you, factory, installer)?  
- Close rate by lead source (Google vs LSA vs repair inbound vs referral vs showroom vs realtor)?  
- What is **CAC by source** after refunds and no-shows — not just cost-per-lead?  
- Sales commission structure? Designer/closer W-2 vs commission-only?  
- % install subcontracted vs W-2? Callback / punch cost as % of revenue?  
- Top manufacturers: lead time reliability, defect rate, freight damage process, discontinued SKU policy?  
- Showroom rent vs mobile — contribution after occupancy and sample cost?  
- If franchised: royalty + brand fund + tech + call center + required local spend as % of gross; Item 20 outlet churn; territory household counts  
- Warranty length and who pays rework when the factory says “installer error” and the installer says “bad measure”

What that means in plain English:

The *category* can print attractive project margins because tickets are real and inventory can stay light if you order after sell.  
A *specific office* can drown in unpaid design time, bad measures, late factories, expensive leads that don’t close, and one viral “my windows looked worse after install” review — while still “crushing it” on Instagram.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

**1. It’s a sales + design business wearing a tape measure**  
If you can’t build a consultative close culture, the sample binder doesn’t matter.

**2. Measure mistakes → remakes (the quiet killer)**  
Wrong mount type. Ignored window crank. Out-of-square frame. Depth too shallow for the product sold. The factory cut what you ordered — and what you ordered was wrong. Remakes destroy margin, schedule, and trust. Dual-measure culture or pain.

**3. Lead gen CAC / feast-famine pipeline**  
Paid search and LSAs in competitive metros get expensive. Brand leads help until they don’t. Waiting on “the system to send leads” while the calendar is empty is a documented failure mode in Brian’s blinds operator lessons — local hustle (signs, hangers, realtors, community, referrals) still matters. Track **cost per installed job**, not cost per lead.

**4. Showroom vs mobile economics**  
Empty showroom is expensive wallpaper with pretty shutters. Mobile-only with weak digital and weak neighborhood presence is an empty calendar. Pick a model and fund it on purpose.

**5. Product warranty / callback purgatory**  
Motors fail. Cords fray. Fabrics discolor in south sun. Customer “remembered” a different opacity. Factory blames install; install blames measure; homeowner blames you. Policy or pain — write it before the five-star review turns into a one-star novel.

**6. Fashion cycles & SKU churn**  
Last year’s “everyone wants gray woven woods” becomes this year’s dead sample deck. Discontinued fabrics after you’ve sold three rooms. Trend risk is real even though *windows* aren’t going away. Train sellers to sell function (light, privacy, UV, ease) under the fashion, not only the Pinterest board.

**7. Discretionary demand (not pure emergency)**  
Broken cords create urgency; “upgrade the living room” waits. Soft economy hits whole-home fashion packages before true need replacements — and before some repair lanes (if you have them).

**8. Selection chaos / change orders**  
Unlocked colors after the factory starts = dead margin, dead schedule, dead trust.

**9. Manufacturer / lead-time dependency**  
Late truck, damaged box, wrong valance, backordered motor. You own the homeowner’s week either way.

**10. Install quality in highly visible spaces**  
Crooked headrails, light gaps, scarred casing, remotes that don’t pair. Guests notice. Reviews follow.

**11. Commission sales culture**  
High upside, high drama, high turnover — and Brian’s point that owner energy is hard to replace early. Pure closers who miss measures are expensive. Pure “helpful” designers who won’t ask for the card are also expensive.

**12. Owner dependency / anti-semi-absentee**  
Early on, the best closer (and often the best measure QA) is you. Handing “just hire a sales manager” into this model is a common expensive fantasy — especially before SOPs and hiring values are real.

**13. Cash timing traps**  
If you order before money is real — or finance the customer’s indecision — working capital vanishes in freight and custom cuts.

**14. Competition from every direction**  
National brands, independents, big-box measure programs, Amazon DIY, handymen, interior designers adding a coverings line, manufacturer referral networks. Differentiation is process + trust + fit quality + clean install, not slogans.

**15. Scope creep into “while you’re here”**  
Curtain rods, drywall repair, smart-home whole-house, painting the trim. Adjacent can be attach; it can also be a second business you didn’t underwrite (see handyman / painting adjacency — stay honest about skill).

**16. Repair positioning risk (if you offer it)**  
Gateway tickets are powerful. They can also train the market to see you as the cheap fix-it guy. Price and script the upsell path — or drown in low-dollar schedule clog.

**17. Motorization & tech support burden**  
App updates, hub incompatibilities, dead batteries on “solar” stories, customer education. Higher ticket; higher callback surface area.

**18. Seasonality & life events**  
Spring listing / move season and pre-holiday pushes; summer vacations and January freeze on decisions. Exterior shade work competes with weather. Plan the troughs.

**19. Hiring people who can sell *and* respect the measure**  
Artists who won’t close. Closers who invent dimensions. You need both muscles — or a process that separates them without dropping the baton.

**20. Franchise / brand stack (if you go that route)**  
Royalty + brand fund + tech + required local spend + rules + territory vs playbook, manufacturer pricing, lead support, and brand. Worth it only if the system wins jobs and protects margin — not because the van wrap looks good. Diligence Item 19 spreads and Item 20 churn, not Instagram aesthetics.

**21. Confusing “sold revenue” with installed, collected profit**  
A signed proposal isn’t cash. An ordered job isn’t a happy review. A big top line with remakes and refunds is a trap.

**22. “Everyone has windows” as a substitute for operator skill**  
Demand is real. Territory math, CAC discipline, measure culture, and install quality decide whether *you* get paid.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means blinds / window coverings is a **real high-ticket project business** with a short fuse on measure errors and a long memory on ugly installs — not a weekend Amazon-hang side hustle and not a passive royalty coupon.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re wired for consultative, high-ticket sales (or building that team)  
- You’re obsessive about process and QA on measurements  
- You’re patient with factory lag and still ruthless about pipeline hygiene  
- You like orchestrating design → order → install more than pure route density  
- You’re okay living in sample decks, opacity debates, and “the light gap on the left” drama  
- Project home services (same stack as closets / cabinets / painting) excite you more than recurring pest hours  
- You’ll pick **mobile vs showroom** deliberately and fund that choice  
- You’ll treat lead gen as a first-class operating system, not a franchise entitlement

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want passive / semi-absentee from month one  
- You hate selling and hate hiring sellers  
- You need weekly recurring route revenue to sleep (think pest, lawn, senior care hours)  
- You’re undercapitalized for marketing + the lag from consult to install  
- You want to *be* the installer as the whole company (craft job ≠ this page’s model)  
- Soft-skill conflict (upset homeowners in their living room) drains you dry  
- You get careless with details — this model punishes sloppy measures harder than almost any home service  
- You’re shopping a logo because Budget Blinds / Bloomin’ Blinds / Gotcha Covered “feel proven” without underwriting CAC, royalty, territory, and measure culture

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same category either way: design → close → measure twice → order → install → warranty.

## Risks (the short list)

- Pipeline full of leads that don’t close; CAC above contribution  
- Bad measure → remake spiral  
- Factory miss → your reputation  
- Change-order bleed  
- Install callback spiral / warranty drag  
- Fashion SKU obsolescence and discontinued materials  
- Discretionary demand dries in a downturn (especially whole-home fashion)  
- Sales culture collapses when you step back  
- Showroom rent with no intent traffic  
- Royalty / fee drag without lead or ops advantage  
- Thinking “everyone has windows” substitutes for operator skill

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “people need blinds.”

You’re buying the right to run a **design-to-install machine** that turns housing stock, trust, and manufacturer capacity into finished window treatments — if you can close emotional jobs, lock selections, survive lead times, **measure like a fanatic**, generate leads without praying, and install clean.

Some people should lean mobile / garage-start for lower occupancy and faster market entry.  
Some people should lean showroom and sell with sample theater.  
Some people should lean repair-plus-replace as a trust funnel.  
Some people should partner with realtors, designers, and builders and stay niche.  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into a vehicle with cleaner recurring economics or a shorter sales cycle.

The question isn’t “do windows go out of style?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful sample calendar that only works when you’re the designer, the closer, the remake department, and the apology?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs closets vs painting vs handyman vs window cleaning vs others), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
