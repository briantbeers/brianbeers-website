The tour starts with shoes off.  
A three-year-old pours water. A five-year-old lays out bead bars. Nobody is “doing circle” on a carpet square because a schedule said so.

Parents aren’t shopping “a room that watches kids until 6.”  
They’re shopping a **prepared environment** — mixed-age, materials-led, guide-trained — and a school that can explain *why* the pink tower is not a toy aisle.

Living-room “Montessori at home” ghosts when the second kid arrives.  
Big-box daycare with a Montessori *label* on the brochure is a different product when the shelves are incomplete and the lead isn’t credentialed.  
Public magnet lotteries don’t open on your preferred start date.

Someone needs a **Montessori early-education plant**: licensed (or private-school) facility, complete materials sets, AMI/AMS/MACTE-class guides, affiliation or accreditation path, and a tuition admissions cycle measured in months — not a same-week daycare soft-open fantasy.

That’s a **Montessori / early education brand** school.

Not [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) (licensed **ratios + occupancy + waitlist** math as the spine — shared facility gravity, different *method / materials / guide / accreditation* thesis).  
Not [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) (after-school subject packs — lighter CapEx, different sales emotion).  
Not [kids-music](/models/kids-music) or [kids-art](/models/kids-art) (enrichment class packs — recurring tuition neighbors, not mixed-age Children’s House as the product).  
Not a home micro-school in a living room (related idea, different ceiling, different licensing and materials bill).  
Not “edtech Montessori app” (different capital stack, different buyer).

You’re running a **method-driven early education facility**: age-banded mixed-age classrooms (Nido / Toddler / Primary / Elementary as you build), specialized materials CapEx, trained guides who *are* the product, childcare licensing *and/or* private-school rules that still apply, and an admissions motion that sells philosophy fit before autopay clears.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **facility CapEx**, **materials sets**, **guide recruiting & training**, **accreditation / affiliation physics**, **tuition sales cycle length**, **ratios/regs**, and **waitlist vanity vs cashflow timing** show up before the Instagram shelves look like a brand Reel.

Montessori Kids Universe / Guidepost Montessori / Ivybrook Academy–class systems and indie AMI/AMS schools sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The prepared environment, guide bench, and multi-year enrollment honesty are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local Montessori (or Montessori-inspired early-ed) school operator**: purpose-fit or heavily TI’d space, mixed-age classrooms, complete Montessori materials sequences, credentialed (or in-training) guides, outdoor learning / playground that passes inspection, and a tuition book — half-day preschool *or* full-day care-shaped hours, sometimes elementary extension.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — this is the method + hours fork):**

| | **Full-day Montessori childcare / early-ed center** | **Half-day / school-day Montessori preschool (sometimes + elementary)** |
|--|-----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Full-day care hours *plus* Montessori method (often 7a–6p); meals, extended day | Philosophy-led school day (half-day or school-day); extended care as attach |
| **Who must be on site** | Guides + assistants + director; **childcare license ratios** still bind in most states when care is provided | Same credential stack for guides; “school” branding rarely skips childcare rules for under-5s |
| **Typical payer** | Private tuition at a premium to commodity daycare; some subsidy seats if licensed | More private-pay philosophy buyers; half-day can mean *two* sessions per classroom (density lever) |
| **CapEx feel** | Daycare-grade plant **plus** materials sets + guide training — often **high-six to multi-million** | Still facility-heavy; Ivybrook–class half-day Montessori/Reggio hybrids still land **~$0.5M–$0.9M** Item 7 education |

Many real schools sit in a hybrid — Primary full-day *plus* Toddler *plus* aftercare *plus* a thin Elementary. Underwrite the mix honestly — a soft Primary with a full infant waitlist PDF is a different company than a dense Children’s House with elementary as the retention spine.

Typical menu:

1. **Nido / Infant** — sleep/feeding protocols *and* early sensorial; tightest ratios; highest materials + labor tax  
2. **Toddler** — practical life explosion; still ratio-heavy; materials abuse risk  
3. **Primary / Children’s House (≈2.5–6)** — the classic three-year cycle; pink tower → bead cabinet spine  
4. **Elementary (6–9 / 9–12)** — cosmic education, research, wider materials CapEx; different admissions buyer  
5. **Extended day / camps / summer** — cash bridge *or* method dilution if run as “daycare with shelves”  
6. **Parent education / observation** — the admissions and retention machine for philosophy schools  
7. **Affiliation / accreditation pathway** — AMS / AMI / Cognia / other — multi-year quality story, not a logo sticker

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / maps, pediatrician, realtor “new subdivision,” parent Facebook, open houses, franchise/system lead systems (if any)  
2. **Tour / observation** — method explanation, mixed-age fit, sticker shock, *philosophy alignment* conversation (this is not a 20-minute daycare soft-close)  
3. **Apply / deposit / contract** — application fees, enrollment deposits, annual contracts, sibling priority rules  
4. **Classroom placement** — mixed-age balance *and* licensed seat available *that term*, not “someday on the waitlist”  
5. **Staff the environment** — guide + assistant coverage; training pipeline for replacements  
6. **Deliver** — three-hour work cycle fidelity, materials complete and sequenced, observation notes parents will trust  
7. **Retain / age-up** — Toddler → Primary → Elementary; sibling enrollments; summer softness without method collapse  
8. **Replace** — because **moves, tuition fatigue, “we need full-day cheaper,” guide walk-off, and public magnet lotteries never sleep**

## How money comes in

You sell **enrolled child-years (or months) of Montessori education** — filled mixed-age seats that stay through the three-year cycle when the method works — not enrichment class packs and not “any licensed crib.”

Education price / roster bands (national consumer / operator ranges; metros sit higher; secondary markets sit lower):

- **Full-day Primary / Children’s House tuition** commonly discussed roughly **~$1,000–$2,000+/month** (**~$12k–$24k+/year**); AMS/AMI-credentialed premium and high-COL metros often **~$1,200–$2,500+/month**  
- **Toddler / Nido** often **at or above** Primary because of ratios (education bands frequently **~$1,200–$2,200+/month**)  
- **Half-day** programs often **~$500–$900+/month** — and can densify a classroom with AM/PM sessions when licensed and staffed for it  
- **Elementary** annual tuition often discussed **~$15k–$28k+** depending on metro and program depth  
- **Guidepost–class** consumer tuition education often cited roughly **~$15k–$37k+/year** by campus and level (wide spread — underwrite *your* market)  
- **Materials / enrollment / application fees** — application often **~$50–$250**; deposits **~$500–$2,000+**; annual materials fees **~$300–$800** common education chatter — secondary to tuition, real in cash timing  
- Montessori Kids Universe–class franchise Item 19 education (2025–2026 secondary): averages / medians often discussed roughly **~$1.3M–$1.5M** gross on mature full-year samples — *not* your forecast; small system, underwrite the distribution  
- Ivybrook Academy–class (Montessori/Reggio half-day hybrid) Item 19 education (2025 secondary): ~**24** mature franchised schools averaging roughly **~$858k** gross (median near **~$839k**) — different hours product than full-day MKU-class

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Paid enrolled seats by program level** — drafts clearing *this contract year*, not “interested since the open house”  
2. **Net tuition after sibling / founding-family / “we’ll discount to fill” you invented under pressure**  
3. **Age / level mix** — infant-heavy books raise labor %; Primary density is the classic spine; Elementary retention is the multi-year prize *and* CapEx  
4. **Hours product** — full-day care-shaped vs half-day school-shaped changes revenue per classroom *and* parent ICP  
5. **Admissions conversion velocity** — tour → observation → contract; Montessori sales cycles are often **weeks to months**, not “start Monday”  
6. **Length of stay / three-year cycle completion** — the method’s economic promise; early exits destroy LTV stories  
7. **Guide productivity & continuity** — who holds the room when you’re not observing  
8. **Extended day / camp attach** — bridge cash *or* method/culture tax  
9. **Waitlist → paid conversion** — sibling priority, age-band fit, and “maybe next fall” names that never enroll

Gross margin intuition (operator / FDD / foundation education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Tuition: high *gross* after variable — then **guide wages + assistants + training + rent/debt + insurance + materials refresh + royalties (if any)** eat the dream  
- Labor is still the monster (often discussed in the same **~45–60%+** of revenue conversation as other early-ed plants when ratios and credentials are real)  
- **Materials are the Montessori-specific CapEx/tax** [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) underplays: complete classroom sets often **~$12k–$25k+ per room** education (furniture another **~$15k–$35k**/room); franchise Item 7 lines for FF&E + materials for a few classrooms often land **high-five to mid-six figures**  
- Facility lease / debt service is fixed whether Primary has 18 or 28 paid children  
- Guides commonly cite roughly **~80–90%+** paid occupancy of *staffable* capacity as the healthy conversation zone — same honesty as daycare, with a longer ramp story  
- **Waitlist ≠ occupancy ≠ cash** — a 40-name Toddler waitlist while Primary has empty seats *and* August payroll due is a cash trap with better vocabulary  
- Royalty + brand + tech stacks on branded systems often land **~7%+** royalty education (MKU– / Ivybrook–class secondary often cites **~7%** royalty ± brand/tech fees — verify current Item 6)  
- Net on a single campus is often a **tighter** band than wood-shelf Instagram myths — volume rises; guide wages, training sponsorships, and materials replacement rise with it

You’re not a daycare with prettier shelves.  
You’re a **method-fidelity + guide-bench + materials-complete + multi-month admissions** company — wearing a childcare license and a personal guarantee (or a royalty floor).

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Parents still pay a premium for **differentiation** in early years — independence, concentration, mixed-age social learning, “not just worksheets.” Dual-income households still need reliable hours. Dual-*philosophy* households will drive past three commodity daycares for a school that matches their values. Public Montessori magnets and charters prove demand *and* create waitlist overflow that private schools sometimes catch.

Good times → elementary expansion, second campuses, materials upgrades, sibling enrollments, founding-family gifts.  
Tight times → philosophy tuition is often among the **first discretionary cuts** after enrichment dies — *unless* the family treats the school as non-negotiable identity; then they cut travel and keep the contract. Underwrite which parent you actually have.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) rides **licensed capacity × paid occupancy × blended tuition − ratio labor**.  
[Tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) rides **enrollment + teacher labor + school-year seasonality**.  
[Kids-music](/models/kids-music) / [kids-art](/models/kids-art) ride **class fill + instructor roster + parent churn** on enrichment calendars.  
Montessori rides **method fidelity + materials CapEx + trained guides + accreditation physics + long admissions cycles** — on top of the same facility and ratio gravity as other early-ed plants.

Same “parents pay for kids’ outcomes” emotion. Different plant, different shelf, different sales calendar.

That’s the engine. Not one viral pouring-water Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one school

A single Montessori campus lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Method fidelity (the product)**  
Parents enroll in *outcomes they can observe* — concentration, independence, grace and courtesy — not a logo that says Montessori. Incomplete materials, interrupted work cycles, and “circle time because the franchise binder said so” destroy the premium. The word “Montessori” is not trademarked. Your counter to fake shelves is **trained guides, complete sequences, and observation culture**. Drift is a product defect.

**Materials CapEx & refresh discipline**  
Sensorial, practical life, language, math, cultural — per-classroom sets that cost real money and break, go incomplete, or get “simplified” by untrained hands. Franchise Item 7 education often shows **FF&E + Montessori materials** as one of the fattest lines after TI. Indie founders who buy pretty Instagram shelves without a replacement budget discover the same tax in year two. This is the CapEx fork [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) mentions lightly and this page owns.

**Trained guide recruiting & retention (career-defining)**  
AMI / AMS / MACTE-class credentials take **~12–24 months** education timelines and often **~$6k–$18k+** in training cost per guide (program-dependent). Sponsoring training ties cash to people who can leave. Uncredentialed “leads” undercut authenticity and accreditation paths. When the beloved Primary guide leaves for another school or a district job, the three-year cycle wobbles — families follow people even in method schools.

**Accreditation / affiliation physics**  
AMS school accreditation education: self-study, onsite peer review, standards across governance/curriculum/materials/multi-age groupings; terms often discussed around **~7 years** once achieved — *after* you are already operating with fidelity. AMI affiliation has its own standards culture. Cognia / regional accreditors show up on some multi-campus networks. **Affiliation ≠ automatic enrollment fill.** Accreditation is a multi-year operating system and marketing asset — and a distraction if you chase plaques before paid seats and guide bench. “We’ll get accredited” is not a year-one cash plan.

**Tuition sales cycle length**  
Tours, observations, parent education nights, application windows, contract lock dates (often spring for fall starts). Sibling priority and mixed-age balance mean you don’t always take the next name on a numbered list — applicant *pools* are common. This is structurally slower than daycare “we have an infant seat Tuesday.” Underwrite marketing CAC against **months** of nurture and a fall-weighted conversion calendar.

**Facility CapEx / lease (shared gravity with daycare — still real)**  
Child-height environments, sinks, egress, fire/life safety, **outdoor learning / playground**, square-footage rules. Montessori Foundation–class education often puts leased early-childhood starts roughly **~$200k–$400k** (40–60 students), preschool+elementary leased roughly **~$400k–$800k**, purchased/renovated K–8 roughly **~$0.7M–$1.2M**, full campuses higher. Franchise Item 7 education: Montessori Kids Universe–class often **~$0.78M–$1.8M+**; Ivybrook–class half-day often **~$0.54M–$0.87M**. Purpose-built or heavy TI is normal. The “cheap strip with wood shelves” fantasy dies when playground, materials, and licensing punch lists show up.

**Ratios / regs (the daycare sister constraint — still binds)**  
If you provide care for under-5s, **state childcare ratios and director credentials** usually still apply — Montessori mixed-age ideals don’t repeal infant 1:3–1:4 education bands. Call-outs = compliance event. Read [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) for the occupancy spine; this page assumes you already respect that law and then adds method cost on top.

**Waitlist vs cashflow timing**  
Long waitlists feel like product-market fit. Unconverted names, wrong age bands, sibling blocks, and “we’re waiting for Primary” don’t pay August guide payroll. Soft-open reality: carrying full facility + materials debt on a half census for **12–24+ months** is the cash trap foundation guides warn about repeatedly. Deposits help; they are not a substitute for a ramp reserve.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you live in admissions calendars, guide training sponsorships, licensing binders, materials inventories, and “the lead guide quit mid-cycle.” Semi-absentee fairy tales collide with observation mornings and 6:15 pickup. Earn a real head of school / director bench before you disappear.

Brian’s pad-site point still applies as **site selection intuition**: early-ed plants show up where new housing and dual-income density are landing. That helps **demand**. It does not credential your Primary guide or buy your bead cabinet.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Facility CapEx & landlord / TI risk**  
Playground, sinks, egress, sprinklers, square-footage math, outdoor learning environments. Lease too short for TI + materials payback. HVAC failure in July closes the only plant you have. Montessori shelves don’t make strip-center physics kinder than [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool).

**2. Materials CapEx & incompleteness**  
The Montessori-specific tax. **~$12k–$25k+** sets per room, plus furniture, plus replacement forever. Incomplete sequences destroy method fidelity and parent observation tours. Cheap knockoffs that break weekly are a false economy. Franchise FF&E lines look huge because they *are*.

**3. Accreditation / affiliation physics (multi-year, not a sticker)**  
AMS/AMI paths demand multi-age groupings, fundamental materials, credentialed instructors, self-study, peer visits. Accreditation often lands **years** into healthy operation — not as a pre-opening marketing miracle. Chasing plaques before paid seats burns leadership attention. Losing affiliation for fidelity failures is a reputation event.

**4. Guide recruiting, training cost & retention**  
Credential pipelines are slow and expensive. Sponsoring MACTE-class training ties cash to people who can walk. District jobs, other Montessori schools, and burnout fight you. When the Primary guide leaves mid-cycle, renewals and observations wobble. Owner-as-hero guide looks great on Instagram and terrible on a succession plan.

**5. Tuition sales cycle length**  
Philosophy fit tours, observations, spring contract locks, sibling priority, applicant pools — not “start Monday.” CAC spends months in nurture. Founders who budget daycare-speed enrollment underwrite a fantasy. Long cycle + soft summer = working-capital stress.

**6. Ratios, licensing & regs (still bind)**  
Montessori mixed-age ideals do not repeal infant/toddler ratio law or director credentials. Call-outs close rooms or break compliance. Private-school pathways exist in some states — don’t assume; verify. Read the daycare page’s ratio spine; add method cost on top.

**7. Waitlist theater vs cashflow timing**  
Long waitlists feel like PMF. Wrong age bands, unconverted names, and “maybe next fall” don’t clear payroll. Soft census against full facility + guide wages is quiet death — often for **12–24+ months**. Deposits help timing; they don’t fix empty Primary seats.

**8. Method fidelity drift**  
Untrained assistants “helping too much,” interrupted work cycles, worksheet creep, extended-day that becomes screen-and-snack. Parents who bought Montessori notice. Online “Montessori toys” raise the “why pay you?” bar every year. Your counter is supervised independence and a complete environment — not a prettier logo.

**9. Age-mix & three-year cycle economics**  
Infants drive tuition *and* labor. Primary drives the classic density story. Elementary is retention *and* CapEx. Overselling Nido without a guide pipeline drowns. Avoiding infants to “keep it easy” competes in crowded preschool bands. Early exits from the three-year cycle destroy the LTV story decks sell.

**10. Labor cost & classification**  
Guide wages, assistants, payroll taxes, training tuition reimbursement clawbacks, employee vs contractor games. “We’re a school family” doesn’t impress the Department of Labor. Owner teaching hours mask labor problems until you get sick.

**11. Parent expectations & philosophy conflict**  
“Why isn’t there more homework,” screen-time debates, discipline philosophy clashes, observation anxiety, custody drama. You’re in the emotion *and* ideology business with a facility shell.

**12. Competition & substitutes**  
Other Montessori schools, “Montessori-inspired” daycares with incomplete shelves, Goddard/Primrose–class premium early-ed, home daycares, nannies, public magnets/charters, [tutoring](/models/tutoring-learning-center) and enrichment when budgets tighten. Winning on “we’re slightly cheaper” fills the book with price shoppers who exit early.

**13. Cash timing & delinquency**  
Payroll weekly/biweekly; some families pay late; subsidy seats (if any) lag; materials vendors want COD. Annual contracts help; summer soft spots and August ramp still punish thin balance sheets.

**14. Key-person / head-of-school risk**  
Lose the person who knows every guide’s training status, every admissions pool nuance, and the accreditation self-study binder and the “semi-absentee” story collapses into your evenings.

**15. Royalty / brand / network fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, required vendors, tech fees, territory rules help only if they shorten guide ramp, materials procurement, admissions playbooks, and fill. A logo does not credential your Toddler guide or pass your licensing visit.

**16. Owner-as-hero admissions closer / substitute guide / materials buyer**  
If only you convert observations *and* only you cover call-outs *and* only you own the accreditation binder, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a high-stakes school job with a personal guarantee or a royalty floor.

**17. Confusing “parents love Montessori” headlines with “your filled, collectible child-months after materials, guide wages, and ramp”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday show-up rate and your materials invoice.

**18. Multi-campus fantasy before one-school mastery**  
Roll-up and network stories exist. They’re also how people buy a second site while the first still needs them every observation morning and every licensing week.

**19. CapEx creep toward K–8 campus before Primary is dense**  
You opened to own Children’s House — then overbuilt elementary labs, under-reserved for ramp, and discovered materials + guides for 6–12 are a second company. Phase growth on purpose.

**20. Fake Montessori risk (your brand or your competitors)**  
If *you* sell the name without fidelity, reviews and affiliation risk follow. If *competitors* do, you still fight price confusion. Diligence the method — not the word on the awning.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means Montessori early education is a **facility + method-fidelity + guide-bench + materials-CapEx + long-admissions** business — where empty prepared environments, incomplete shelves, and rising guide dependency decide whether the three-year cycle survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re willing to own a **physical licensed (or private-school) site** — inspections, playgrounds, landlords — *and* a method you will not dilute for short-term fill  
- You can recruit, train, and retain Montessori guides *and* a real head of school / director (or partner with someone who can)  
- Materials inventories, admissions funnels measured in months, and accreditation binders energize you rather than insult you  
- You’re capitalized for TI + materials + **12–24+ months** of soft census against trained-guide payroll  
- Serving families who want a prepared environment feels like meaningful work, not a marketing slogan  
- You’ve compared this door to [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool), [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center), [kids-music](/models/kids-music), and [kids-art](/models/kids-art) — and still want the Montessori facility stack  
- You’re honest about semi-absentee: leadership bench cost hits EBITDA hard at one campus; math improves at multi-campus *after* systems exist

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted Brian’s **lower-overhead** doors and thought “Montessori” was daycare lightness with prettier shelves  
- You hate licensing, parent ideology conflict, or multi-month sales cycles  
- You need passive income or a semi-absentee story from day one  
- Thin stomach for infant/toddler care risk, allegations, or custody drama  
- You’re undercapitalized for playground-grade TI + materials sets + months of guide payroll before census  
- You’re actually shopping **daycare occupancy math only**, **tutoring packs**, or **enrichment class books** — different pages  
- You’re shopping **home micro-Montessori** capacity and calling it a campus — different ceiling, different rules  
- You thought “we’ll get AMS accredited in year one” was a cash plan, not a multi-year operating system

Franchise / network vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: paid seats, guides, materials, facility, admissions cycle. A brand (Montessori Kids Universe / Ivybrook / peers) or network (Guidepost–class) is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring, training, materials procurement, admissions, and ramp — not because the shelves are branded.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** kids-adjacent door (tutoring, enrichment, kids resale) or a non-facility vehicle first — or a standard [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) plant without the materials/accreditation tax if that’s the life they want. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Soft occupancy + full facility + guide wages → structural losses  
- Materials incompleteness / refresh spike → thin contribution and failed observations  
- Guide walk-off mid-cycle → enrollment and accreditation risk  
- Accreditation chase before census → leadership distraction  
- Long sales cycle + thin nurture → empty fall seats  
- Ratio / license action → existential  
- Injury or allegation → liability + enrollment death spiral  
- Waitlist vanity without conversion → false confidence and August cash hole  
- Method drift → premium collapse vs “Montessori-inspired” competitors  
- Wage inflation without tuition room → busy and broke  
- Lease / TI / materials mismatch → trapped capital  
- Royalty / network fee drag without hiring or admissions leverage  
- Owner forever on admissions, subbing, and the binder  
- Buying a “school” that was one guide + incomplete shelves + a pretty waitlist PDF  
- Confusing early-ed demand headlines with utilized child-months after materials and ramp  
- CapEx creep to elementary before Primary density  
- Labor misclassification / training clawback disputes

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “parents love Montessori.”

You’re buying the right to run a **method-driven early education facility** — if you can fund the plant and the materials, hold guide quality through training pipelines, respect ratio law, convert a multi-month admissions cycle into paid contracts, pursue affiliation/accreditation as an operating system rather than a sticker, and not confuse a long waitlist PDF (or a viral pouring-water Reel) for free money.

Some people should buy a tired Montessori campus and fix guide bench, materials completeness, and admissions discipline.  
Some people should open a phased Children’s House and earn elementary later.  
Some people should choose [daycare-preschool](/models/daycare-preschool) without the accreditation tax — or [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) / [kids-music](/models/kids-music) / [kids-art](/models/kids-art) and never take on mixed-age prepared environments.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do parents want Montessori?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a regulated school job with materials invoices, guide-training sponsorships, multi-month sales cycles, accreditation binders, ratio anxiety, CapEx gravity, and a personal guarantee or royalty floor when the only Primary guide who can hold observations texts “I’m done”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs daycare, tutoring, enrichment, or something outside child services), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
