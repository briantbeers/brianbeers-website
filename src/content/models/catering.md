Saturday 4:40 p.m. Two weddings, one corporate drop, and a last-minute headcount change that “shouldn’t affect the buffet.”  
The refrigerated van is late leaving the commissary. One server called out. The venue just texted that load-in is now through the loading dock with a COI on file — and the deposit you spent on protein last Tuesday doesn’t care that Monday’s RFP still hasn’t paid.

Somebody needs **food that shows up on time, tastes consistent, survives the room, and doesn’t embarrass the planner** — office lunches, wedding buffets, funeral trays, conference breaks, stadium suites, school galas — produced in a kitchen they never see and delivered by people who must hit a clock that doesn’t flex.

That’s **event / corporate catering** (commissary + delivery / on-site service — not a full QSR lobby, not a ghost-kitchen marketplace stack).

Not [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) as the whole company story (same *food + labor* neighborhood — QSR sells **walk-in tickets and a strip lease**; this page is the **pipeline + event calendar + kitchen + logistics** factory, with trays as the product).  
Not [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) (same *office weekday* emotion — you sell *caffeine + pastry attach*, not *full meal production for rooms of strangers*).  
Not [bakery](/models/bakery) (same *celebration calendar* — you sell *bake-centric sweets + cakes*, not *savory/event meal programs as the spine*).  
Not [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (same *event-day delivery* brain — you rent *bounce houses and tables*, not *perishable food under health code*).  
Not ghost-kitchen / delivery-only marketplace theater (backlog sister: rent arbitrage + platform fees + brand confusion — different CapEx story, different customer).  
Not “I’ll cook for friends and Instagram my way to freedom” as the whole *sales pipeline + deposit + peak labor + commissary* story.

You’re running a **sales-and-fulfillment machine**: booked events × collectible invoice − **food & packaging** − **peak labor (prep + delivery + service)** − commissary occupancy − vehicles − rentals pass-through − the cash hole when a cancellation hits after you’ve bought protein, or when three Saturday events collide and your only lead chef is already on a wedding.

Simple on a whiteboard: sell → deposit → produce → deliver/serve → collect balance → review → reorder.  
Hard in real life — because **labor peaks**, **food cost**, **deposit/cancellation discipline**, **kitchen CapEx**, **seasonality**, **corporate RFPs**, **delivery logistics**, and **health regs** show up before the model looks like “everybody needs catering.”

Indie commissary caterers, hotel banquet kitchens spun out as off-premise programs, restaurant catering departments, and brand-system meal/event programs sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The calendar, the kitchen, and the contract are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local (or regional) catering operator**: a production kitchen (owned commissary, shared commercial kitchen, or restaurant kitchen with off-premise volume), a sales pipeline that books events weeks or months out, a delivery / setup / service crew that can hit load-in windows, menus engineered for travel and room-temperature reality, and — if you’re honest — a **chef / ops lead and a salesperson** who can run without you on every tasting and every Saturday.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — and don’t blur either with QSR or ghost kitchen):**

| | **Event / social catering (wedding, celebration, gala)** | **Corporate / recurring catering (office lunch, meetings, standing orders)** |
|--|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | High-ticket, high-touch events — menus, tastings, staffing, rentals coordination | Drop-off trays, boxed lunches, break service, standing weekly programs |
| **Primary constraint** | **Calendar utilization × ticket − food − peak labor − logistics − deposits** | **Account pipeline × reorder rate − food − delivery labor − AR − RFP friction** |
| **Cash shape** | Weekend / season spikes; deposits help timing if you don’t spend them as salary | Weekday denser; net-30 corporate can starve cash even when “sales” look fine |
| **Risk feel** | Cancellation after protein buy; review death from one wedding; weather / venue fights | Account concentration; procurement / RFP theater; thin margins on volume bids |
| **Owner job early** | Sell + lead ops on peak weekends until a real bench exists | Outbound + account management + protect food % on high-volume drops |

Many real shops are **hybrids** — wedding weekends + corporate midweeks. Underwrite the mix honestly. A firm that’s 90% one-off social events is a different company than one with sticky office standing orders and a controlled social attach. Restaurant catering *departments* inside [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) or full-service boxes are often an **attach**, not this page’s primary P&L — same food math, different overhead and identity.

Typical menu / revenue mix:

1. **Corporate drop-off / boxed lunch / meeting trays** — weekday spine for many shops  
2. **Social / wedding / celebration events** — high check, high labor, high review risk  
3. **Funeral / institutional / school / nonprofit** — volume with price pressure and calendar quirks  
4. **On-site service staffing** (servers, bartenders, chefs) — margin helper or margin killer depending on wage and tips policy  
5. **Rentals / linens / disposables pass-through** — can lift ticket; inventory and breakage risk  
6. **Beverage / bar programs** (where licensed) — separate liability and permitting world  
7. **Standing corporate programs / cafeteria-lite** — recurring; RFP and SLA reality  
8. **Not the same company:** full [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) lobby throughput; ghost-kitchen marketplace brands; [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) equipment books; pure [bakery](/models/bakery) celebration sweets; Taste Buds–class cooking-entertainment studios (Brian has platformed that as *event food adjacency* — kids parties / corporate team-building — different factory)

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — planner referral, Google Local, wedding marketplace, corporate outbound, venue preferred lists, past-guest reorder  
2. **Propose / taste** — menu, per-person, staffing, rentals, dietary matrix, site visit  
3. **Contract + deposit** — guest count rules, cancellation tiers, balance due timing, COI / venue requirements  
4. **Produce** — prep lists, purchasing, commissary throughput, cold-chain discipline  
5. **Deliver / set / serve** — vans, timing, venue load-in, service recovery when the room changes  
6. **Collect / review / reorder** — balance, tip-out, survey, standing-order ask — or watch them ghost after one soft event

## How money comes in

You sell **collectible event invoices and corporate programs** — not a pretty tasting photo and not “we’re the preferred caterer” without a signed contract and a deposit that actually clears.

Education pricing / ticket shape (national operator / industry chatter; metros and plated service sit higher; drop-off tray wars sit tighter — **verify your market**):

- Corporate drop-off / boxed lunch education commonly discussed roughly **~$12–$25+/person** depending on market and protein; meeting break packages vary  
- Buffet / social event per-person education often roughly **~$25–$75+/person** before staffing and rentals; plated / premium climbs harder  
- On-site service staffing billed as line items or built into per-person — underwrite loaded wage, not hope  
- Volume wins when **quoting is honest** (food + labor + logistics + overhead + margin) and **deposits stick**  
- Corporate net-30 can lift *sales* while cutting *cash timing* — underwrite AR days, not logo count  
- Franchise / brand shops (where they exist in adjacent meal/event categories) live on **system playbooks × controllable costs**; indies live on **local planner relationships + owner presence** — different marketing tax, same food/labor/logistics physics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Booked, collectible events × average ticket** — empty Saturdays with a beautiful kitchen still burn rent  
2. **Food & packaging %** — overportion, waste, last-minute guest-count cuts, dietary remakes, commodity spikes  
3. **Labor % (prep + delivery + service peaks)** — the career-defining spike when three events land on one weekend  
4. **Commissary / kitchen occupancy** — rent + utilities + hood/grease as % of sales (shared kitchen vs owned)  
5. **Delivery logistics** — vans, fuel, timing failures, remakes, parking / load-in fees  
6. **Deposit & cancellation discipline** — cash timing *and* whether you ate the protein on a cancel  
7. **Corporate reorder / program retention** — the stickiness layer most “wedding-only” shops never build  
8. **Rentals / beverage attach** — ticket lift with inventory and liability tails  
9. **Sales capacity** — pipeline coverage; tastings that don’t convert; RFP win rate  
10. **Ops lead / chef quality** — the silent line item that decides whether *you* own a company or a second job with a van

Gross / cost intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- **Food + packaging** education often discussed roughly **~28–40%** of catering revenue depending on menu intensity, waste, and whether rentals/disposables sit in COGS  
- **Event labor** (prep + delivery + service, fully loaded) often discussed roughly **~20–30%+**; peaks and no-shows push you out of the band fast  
- Delivery / logistics often a few points — until a late van creates remakes and refunds  
- Overhead (commissary, insurance, sales wages, software, vehicles) is the quiet eater when utilization is soft  
- Net on a single catering company is often a **wide** quartile spread — “everybody needs catering” Instagram ≠ your January calendar and your deposit ledger

You’re not a chef with a van.  
You’re a **pipeline + peak-labor + food-cost + contract** company wearing someone else’s event.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

People still gather. Offices still feed meetings. Weddings, funerals, fundraisers, conferences, and “we need lunch for forty by noon” don’t disappear because a meal kit app exists. Grocery trays and QSR catering desks change the *competition*; they don’t erase the *need* for a reliable operator who can hit a room.

Good times → more social spend, higher per-person, corporate appreciation events, willingness to pay for staffing and better protein.  
Tight times → planners cut headcount, trade plated for drop-off, hunt grocery trays, stretch payment terms; protein and wage inflation don’t ask permission; RFP season gets nastier on price.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) rides **tickets × check − food − labor − rent − fees − delivery** on *habit lunch*.  
[Coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) rides **morning tickets − beverage COGS − labor − rent**.  
[Bakery](/models/bakery) rides **production schedule − flour/butter − early labor − waste − celebration calendar**.  
[Party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) rides **event-day asset utilization − delivery labor − weather − damage**.  
Catering rides **booked events × ticket − food − peak labor − kitchen − logistics − deposit/cancel physics** — foodservice with a **sales calendar** instead of (or in addition to) a lobby.

Same “someone needs something done on a date certain” demand emotion. Different invoice, different lawsuit, different Saturday — and a different co-owner named **the pipeline**.

That’s the engine. Not one viral “I made $200k catering from my house” Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

A single catering company lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Sales pipeline (the career-defining calendar upstream of the kitchen)**  
Empty prep lists start as empty CRM stages. Tastings that don’t convert, proposals that die in email, venue preferred-vendor politics, and “we’ll decide after we tour three more caterers” are the real top of funnel. Wedding marketplaces and Google Local help; they don’t replace outbound to offices, planners, and venues. Backlog teaching names **sales pipeline** first for a reason — busy kitchens with empty Saturdays next month are a cash problem, not a branding problem.

**Labor peaks (the silent killer)**  
Catering doesn’t need a perfect average headcount — it needs **bodies that show up for the spike**. Three events on one Saturday, zero on Tuesday. Prep overtime, server call-outs, driver no-shows, chef walk-offs mid-season. Same dual-market pain as [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) — you sell clients *and* sell shifts to people who have other options — without a steady lunch line to smooth the hours. Owner-on-the-line “savings” is unpaid labor with a bad wedding review waiting.

**Food cost & menu discipline**  
Portion specs, batch recipes, dietary remakes, “just add 10 more guests” at the dock, protein spikes, and buffet waste. Education food bands assume honesty; hospitality overproduction is how busy weekends stay broke. Last-minute vegan / allergy swaps without a change order are how contribution dies quietly.

**Deposit / cancellation discipline**  
Industry education commonly puts booking deposits roughly **~25–50%**, with balance due before the event and **tiered cancellation** schedules. Deposits are not free cash — they are obligations tied to future food and labor. Spending next month’s wedding deposit on this week’s payroll is how caterers go dark. Weak contracts turn a cancel into protein in the trash and a van still leased.

**Kitchen / commissary CapEx & utilization**  
Hood, grease interceptor, refrigeration, hot boxes, dishwash, packaging station, parking for vans. Owned commissary = fixed rent through soft January. Shared commercial kitchen = hourly fights and scheduling politics. Restaurant kitchen dual-use = lunch rush vs catering prep collision. A dead walk-in before a Saturday triple-header is lost events *and* reviews. CapEx surprises are how brochure dreams become personal loans.

**Seasonality & calendar concentration**  
Wedding season, holiday parties, graduation weeks, September corporate restart — then soft stretches. [Party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) feel the same weekend/season physics without the food. Underwrite **trailing monthly sales for 24+ months**, not the soft-open June wedding. Frios teaching names seasons even on a *lighter* event/dessert sister — full catering just adds kitchen rent and peak staffing to that valley.

**Corporate RFP / procurement theater**  
Big accounts want COIs, vendor onboarding portals, diversity questionnaires, price matrices, and “three bids.” Win on price and lose on margin; refuse theater and lose logos. Standing orders are gold when SLAs and AR are adult; toxic when Net-45 and last-minute headcount cuts are the culture.

**Delivery logistics**  
Vans, cold chain, routing, load-in windows, elevator politics, parking tickets, “the room isn’t ready.” Late food is a review. Remakes destroy food %. Multi-stop corporate mornings look efficient until traffic and a missing dock contact stack. Logistics is the product as much as the chicken.

**Health regs, permits & insurance**  
Food handler certs, kitchen permits, cottage-food myths that die under scrutiny, temporary event permits, alcohol licensing where relevant, auto liability, general liability, workers’ comp, sometimes liquor liability. Venue COIs and additional-insured requests are ops, not vibes. Health-department reality is why “start from my home kitchen” fantasies fail outside narrow legal exceptions.

**Manager / chef / sales dependency**  
If only *you* can sell, only *you* can run the line, and only *you* can calm the planner at load-in, you bought a job with chafing dishes. Scale starts when a salesperson books clean contracts and an ops lead protects food % and timing without you in every van.

**Owner involvement**  
Year-one reality for many caterers is **owner on tastings, peak weekends, and collections**. Semi-absentee is a *claim* until pipeline coverage, food %, labor on peak days, and a bench scorecard prove it. Family-flexibility buyers: read the **Saturday tax and the deposit ledger** honestly before the branded apron order.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, “average catering profit” pages, franchise adjacent Item 19 writeups, Brian’s Frios catering-attach teaching, Fast Food Shorts / QSR P&L frames, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull trailing revenue by channel (corporate vs social), food %, fully-loaded labor on peak weeks, deposit liability, cancellation history, kitchen cost, vehicle cost, AR aging, and whether a real sales + ops bench exists.

**Capacity / book intuition (category education — not your forecast):**

- Lean drop-off–heavy shops and full-service event caterers are different animals — don’t paste wedding AUV onto boxed-lunch factories  
- Many small-to-mid catering firms are discussed in rough **mid-six to low-seven-figure** revenue bands depending on market and service intensity — *shape*, not a starter promise  
- Utilization of kitchen hours and peak weekends matters more than “menu beauty”  
- Single-company owner cash after debt, chef wages, van notes, and soft months is often **much tighter** than “I catered a celebrity wedding once” headlines

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Shared commercial kitchen + van + deposits working capital: often discussed **~$50k–$150k+** before “we need our own hood and a second truck”  
- Owned / leased commissary buildout with hood, grease, refrigeration, packaging, and first van: commonly **mid-six figures** depending on metro and whether you’re taking over an existing kitchen — **verify local bids**  
- Restaurant catering arm: lower *incremental* CapEx if the kitchen exists — higher collision cost with dine-in  
- Frios–class mobile dessert catering attach (Brian newsletter / Drive): **~$40k–$100k**-class education for a *treats event* sister — useful CapEx contrast, **not** full-service catering underwriting  
- Budget **working capital** for food buys ahead of deposits clearing the right way, payroll on peak weeks, insurance, and the soft season after holiday parties  
- Resales: buy the P&L, the contract templates, the deposit ledger, the kitchen condition, and the client concentration — not the Instagram grazing table

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Labor peaks (the career-defining spike)**  
Average weekly labor lies. Three weddings and a Sunday brunch on one weekend — then a dead Tuesday — is the real P&L. Call-outs at 6 a.m. on event day don’t wait. Temp staffing agencies, overtime, and “I’ll just serve it myself” erase good sales weeks. You’re always recruiting peak labor — often harder than finding a steady QSR closer. Owner-as-hero on every Saturday is unpaid labor with a review bomb waiting.

**2. Food cost, waste & last-minute menu chaos**  
Overportion feels like hospitality until it is a point of COGS. Guest-count cuts after purchasing, dietary remakes at the dock, buffet waste, “can you just add shrimp,” commodity spikes. Specs and change orders are the product; fear of the planner is how margin dies.

**3. Deposit / cancellation / cash-timing traps**  
Weak deposits, refunds that ignore purchased protein, balances collected *after* the event, and spending deferred revenue as salary. Industry education’s **~25–50%** deposit + tiered cancel schedules exist because caterers who skip them eat inventory and labor. Contract discipline is not “being difficult” — it’s solvency.

**4. Kitchen CapEx & soft-month occupancy**  
Hoods, grease traps, refrigeration, hot boxes, dishwashing, packaging lines, van parking. Shared kitchens fight you for Saturday prep slots. Owned commissaries burn rent in January. Dual-use restaurant kitchens collide with lunch rush. CapEx and utilization are co-owners — Instagram grazing tables are not.

**5. Seasonality & calendar concentration**  
Wedding season prints; shoulder months may not. Holiday corporate parties cluster; summer can be feast or famine by market. Underwrite the soft quarter and the cash reserve — not the June soft-open. Same seasonal honesty Brian’s Frios teaching names on a lighter event sister; full catering just adds kitchen fixed cost.

**6. Corporate RFP & procurement friction**  
Portals, COIs, three-bid theater, price-only awards, Net-45, and “make it nicer for the same price.” Volume without margin is a job with a larger van. Know when to walk. Account concentration in two office parks feels efficient until the cancel email.

**7. Delivery logistics (the product you can’t plate)**  
Late vans, broken cold chain, wrong dock, elevator outages, parking tickets, multi-stop routing fantasies. Remakes destroy food %. A perfect menu that arrives warm-wrong is a one-star story. Logistics SOPs beat chef ego.

**8. Health regs, permits & venue compliance**  
Permits, inspections, allergen protocols, temp logs, temporary event rules, alcohol licensing where relevant. “Home kitchen hustle” collisions with law. Venue additional-insured demands and load-in rules. One failed inspection can wipe a weekend’s book. This is why [commercial-hood-cleaning](/models/commercial-hood-cleaning) exists next door to kitchens — grease and fire code don’t care about your tasting menu.

**9. Sales pipeline starvation**  
Feast weekends don’t refill themselves. Owners who only cook stop selling; owners who only sell underdeliver. Wedding marketplace fees and lead quality vary. Preferred-vendor lists take years and politics. Empty CRM stages are a future empty kitchen — backlog lists pipeline first for a reason.

**10. Blurring into QSR, ghost kitchen, or “we also rent tables”**  
Trying to be [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) lobby + full catering + marketplace virtual brands + [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) without separate operating systems creates sludge. Ghost-kitchen platform fees and brand confusion are a *different* backlog model — don’t accidentally buy them while thinking you bought catering. Scope clarity is strategy.

**11. Quote theater vs true job costing**  
Per-person numbers copied from a competitor’s Instagram without building food + labor + logistics + overhead + margin. Winning the job and losing the weekend. Adults quote from recipes and labor hours; amateurs quote from fear of losing the lead.

**12. Review / reputation volatility**  
One late wedding buffet outweighs twenty quiet corporate Tuesdays. Planners talk. Photo evidence lives forever. Service recovery protocols matter as much as recipes. Reputation is inventory.

**13. Rentals, beverage & third-party coordination chaos**  
Linens late, glassware short, bartender no-shows, florist blocking load-in, DJ volume vs service timing. You get blamed for the room even when you don’t control it. Clear scope boundaries in the contract — or inherit everyone’s failure.

**14. AR & corporate collections**  
“Sales” on net-30 that become net-60. Finance departments that dispute headcount. Credit risk on first-time planners. Underwrite days sales outstanding like a lender would — bakery wholesale AR teaching rhymes here.

**15. Training time vs peak turnover**  
Portion discipline, allergen handling, and service standards take time; Saturday crews reset every season. Training cost is recurring. A beautiful recipe book without a trained bench is fiction.

**16. Insurance & liability tails**  
Foodborne illness claims, auto accidents between venues, liquor liability, workers’ comp on server slips, damage to venue property. Limits and exclusions are diligence — not a binder in a drawer. Fear sells events; liability stays.

**17. Thin net after “everybody needs catering” headlines**  
Busy calendars are not collectible net after food, peak labor, kitchen rent, vans, remakes, refunds, and soft months. Bottom-quartile shops look full on Instagram and empty at the bank.

**18. Multi-event / second-kitchen fantasy without a bench**  
Second van and a bigger commissary before sales coverage and an ops lead exist is how you own two disasters. Development pressure (if any brand/system) without utilization proof is capital stress — underwrite the *people plan*, not the map of dots.

**19. Owner-as-hero chef-salesperson-driver**  
If only you can sell, only you can plate, and only you can hit load-in, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a peak-shift career with a commissary lease, a deposit ledger, and a logo on a van wrap.

**20. Confusing “people always gather / offices always eat” headlines with “your collectible net after food, peak labor, kitchen, logistics, deposits, RFPs, and seasonality”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Tuesday prep list and your January rent.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means catering is a **pipeline + peak-labor + contract + kitchen** business where empty CRM stages, a soft season, a weak deposit policy, a late van, a missing chef, and a nasty RFP decide whether the company survives the next wage hike — and the next Saturday triple-header.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can live inside **sales conversations + peak weekends + deposit discipline** until a real sales + ops bench exists — or you’ve already capitalized a manager-led plan with proof  
- Job costing, food % dashboards, and logistics SOPs energize you more than they nauseate you  
- Contract redlines, cancellation schedules, COIs, and AR aging feel like diligence, not boredom  
- Recruiting peak labor (and a chef/ops lead) is a job you’re willing to own  
- You’ve compared this door to [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual) (lobby tickets), [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop) (morning beverage), [bakery](/models/bakery) (bake production), [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (event assets), and ghost-kitchen marketplace physics — and still want the commissary + calendar  
- You’re honest that food businesses are an **ownership door with an hours tax** (Brian’s frame) — not passive income with chafing dishes

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted passive “everybody needs catering” income from month one  
- You’re allergic to Saturday peaks, cancellation conversations, sales outreach, or chasing corporate AP  
- You’re undercapitalized for kitchen + vans + working capital and assumed deposits were free salary  
- You actually want a **QSR lobby**, a **morning coffee box**, a **bakery production sheet**, an **event rental fleet**, or a **platform ghost-kitchen stack** — different underwriting  
- You thought “I’m a good cook” erased pipeline math and contracts  
- You need the trendiest grazing-table aesthetic more than timeless quoting + show-up labor in *your* market

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: pipeline, food, peak labor, kitchen, logistics, contracts. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens sales ramp, purchasing, and training — not because a tasting table photographed well.

Many operators will correctly choose a **smoother-hours** service vehicle, a ticket-based food box with catering as *attach*, or a non-food door first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition. Some will correctly choose a **lighter event sister** (Frios–class dessert drop / Sweet Ride hustle) for CapEx and complexity reasons — underwrite that calendar honestly too, and don’t confuse it with full-service catering.

## Risks (the short list)

- Peak-labor no-shows / chef walk-off → event failure + review spiral  
- Food cost drift / last-minute guest chaos → margin wipeout  
- Weak deposits / cancellation after purchasing → cash + inventory hit  
- Soft season + commissary rent → fixed-cost crunch  
- Late delivery / cold-chain failure → remakes + reputation death  
- Corporate RFP race-to-zero / Net-60 AR → busy poverty  
- Health / permit / COI failure → lost venue access or shutdown  
- Account concentration → single-threaded revenue  
- Vehicle accident / liability claim → insurance and cash spiral  
- Quote under-costing → won jobs that lose money  
- Owner forever on every tasting and every Saturday  
- Ghost-kitchen / QSR blur without operating clarity  
- Buying a “book” that was one hero chef + one planner relationship + soft contracts  
- Confusing “people gather” headlines with collectible net after food, peaks, kitchen, and logistics

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everybody needs food at events.”

You’re buying the right to run a **local catering machine** — if you can fill a **pipeline**, protect **food and peak labor**, enforce **deposits and cancellations**, keep a **kitchen and vans** alive through soft months, survive **RFPs and load-in logistics**, stay clean on **health and insurance**, and not confuse a busy June for a twelve-month salary with Saturdays off.

Some people should buy a tired catering book with real trailing months and fix quoting, deposits, and bench process (resale path — diligence still required; sister food/event deal flow in Brian’s world proves *marketplaces and demand exist*, not that any listing is a deal).  
Some people should start lean in a shared kitchen, prove corporate reorder + one clean social channel, and only then lease a bigger commissary.  
Some people should run catering as an **attach** inside [qsr-fast-casual](/models/qsr-fast-casual), [coffee-shop](/models/coffee-shop), or [bakery](/models/bakery) instead of a standalone firm.  
Some people should choose [party-rentals](/models/party-rentals) (event logistics without food liability) or a lighter Frios–class dessert-event sister — or a non-food service vehicle — and never take on commissary rent plus Saturday triple-headers.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people hire caterers?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful food job with a commissary lease, a deposit ledger you’re afraid to enforce, a labor spreadsheet that only works on paper, a van that must hit every load-in, and a personal guarantee when your only chef texts “I’m done” and the wedding is already promised for Saturday at five?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other food-hospitality vehicles vs event-services or home-service paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
