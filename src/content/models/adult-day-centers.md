Mom can’t be left alone all day.

Kids still have jobs.  
A full-time aide at the house is $30+/hour and burns through the bank.  
A nursing home feels like surrender.  
Someone needs a safe place *during the day* — meals, activities, eyes on Dad — then home at night so the spouse can sleep and the daughter can keep her career.

That’s an **adult day** / **companion day** center.

Not in-home caregivers on a schedule (that’s the agency page).  
Not skilled intermittent nursing visits (that’s medical home health).  
Not matching a family to assisted living (that’s placement).  
Not owning a residential group home with beds overnight.

You’re running a **day facility**: participants arrive (often by van), spend the day supervised, then leave. Families buy **respite**. Payers buy **participant-days**. Your P&L lives in **occupancy × rate − ratio staffing − facility − transport**.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because licensing, fixed labor ratios, empty seats, Medicaid timing, and wheelchair vans all show up before the model looks “demographic.”

---

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local adult day services operator**: licensed program space, structured day, meals, activities, personal-care help, often nursing oversight (health model), and usually door-to-door transportation.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **Social / companion day** | **Adult day health (ADHC / health-model ADS)** |
|--|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Supervision, activities, meals, light personal care, socialization | Same + nursing assessments, meds, chronic-condition management, often therapy attach |
| **Who must be on site** | Program director + direct-care staff (ratios vary) | Often **RN (or LPN under RN rules)** during hours + Activity Director + ratio staff |
| **Typical payer stack** | More private pay / limited social funding | Medicaid **HCBS waivers**, VA adult day health, private pay, LTC insurance |
| **License feel** | Lighter clinical layer; still state-licensed in most markets | Health-department / higher acuity rules; survey-ready charts |

NADSA-scale industry education puts the category at roughly **~5,000** U.S. centers serving on the order of **~260k** participants daily — real category, not a TikTok idea. Demand sits with **working family caregivers** who need daytime cover so Mom ages in place nights and weekends.

Typical service menu:

1. **Daytime supervision & safety** — falls watch, wandering prevention, dignity  
2. **Activities / cognitive engagement** — calendar that survives a survey *and* a bored participant  
3. **Meals / snacks** — often commercial kitchen + (optionally) USDA CACFP meal reimbursement  
4. **Personal care** — toileting, transfers, eating help within license scope  
5. **Health monitoring / meds** (health model) — RN assessments, care plans, med administration  
6. **Transportation** — morning pickup / afternoon return; wheelchair securement; route density  
7. **Respite packages** — full days, half days, a few days/week; crisis fill-ins when the spouse is in the hospital

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — hospital / SNF discharge, home-care agency, placement advisor, geriatrician, Area Agency on Aging, VA / VSO, prior family, Google  
2. **Assessment / admit** — acuity fit, funding path, schedule days, transport feasibility  
3. **Enrollment** — care plan, consents, payer auth (Medicaid/VA/MA), family communication norms  
4. **Daily ops** — vans run; ratios held; activities + meals; incident logs; charting  
5. **Bill / collect** — per diem or half-day; private pay weekly/monthly; Medicaid/MCO lag  
6. **Retain / escalate** — needs rise → more days, or transition to in-home hours, placement, or residential care (refer out cleanly)

**Scope lock:** this page is the **facility day program** — seats, ratios, vans, license capacity.  
It is **not** in-home non-medical agencies ([in-home-non-medical-senior-care](/models/in-home-non-medical-senior-care)).  
It is **not** Medicare skilled home health episodes ([home-care-medical](/models/home-care-medical)).  
It is **not** senior placement success fees ([senior-placement-referral](/models/senior-placement-referral)).  
It is **not** Brian’s **residential assisted living / neighborhood group home** idea (overnight beds, 24/7 staff, monthly room rates) — adjacent senior facility economics, different CapEx and risk.  
It is **not** child daycare (different license, different ratios, different culture).

Brian’s public senior stack (newsletter / X thread) hammers age-in-place, caregiver scarcity in **home** care, placement brokers, senior transport, mobility, and small residential homes. **Adult day is rarely named in that stack** — treat it as the missing daytime facility door between “aide in the living room” and “move into a building.” Same Silver Tsunami. Different machine.

## How money comes in

You sell **attended participant-days** (and half-days), not billable home-care hours and not overnight room-months.

CareScout / Genworth Cost of Care education (national medians, private-pay provider surveys): adult day health **~\$100/day** median in 2024 → **~\$95/day** in 2025 (~**\$24.7k/year** at 5 days/week). Markets and acuity move that hard. Medicaid waiver per-diems in public rate writeups often land roughly **~\$65–\$150+/day** by state and tier — sometimes *below* private-pay sticker, which is why mix matters.

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Census / occupancy** — licensed seats × average attendance (no-shows still cost staff)  
2. **Days per week per participant** — 2-day vs 5-day books change revenue density  
3. **Rate × payer mix** — private pay / LTC insurance vs Medicaid HCBS / MCO vs VA  
4. **Half-day vs full-day mix** — half days often price ~55–65% of full day in operator guides, not 50% of cost  
5. **Transportation attach** — included vs fee; route cost can eat “full” census  
6. **Ancillary** — CACFP meal offsets, therapy billing (if carved out), private respite weekends (if licensed)

Gross margin intuition (education shape from operator guides — **not** a forecast):

- Labor is the monster (often discussed **~55–70%** of the P&L when ratios + RN + activity director are real)  
- Food, vans, insurance, supplies sit under that  
- Facility lease / debt service is fixed whether 12 or 30 people show up  
- Guides commonly cite **~70–80% occupancy** of licensed capacity as a break-even *shape* before overhead; thin books below that feel “busy” and still lose money

Cash reality vs in-home private-pay: families on Visa can pay fast. **Medicaid enrollment + MCO credentialing** can take **months** after licensure; remittance often **30–45+ days** after service. Undercapitalized owners confuse “we’re licensed” with “we’re collecting.”

**Light contrast:** non-medical home care sells **hours × spread** with a small office. Adult day sells **seats × attendance × per diem** with a real facility and mandated ratios. Same demographic wave. Different fixed-cost trap.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

America is getting older. Brian’s newsletter cited seniors **85+** on a path roughly **~7M → ~15M by 2040**. People want to age in place. Adult children live elsewhere — or still work. Spouses burn out.

Adult day sits in a practical gap:

- Cheaper *per day* than many assisted-living or nursing-home paths for families who can still take Mom home at night  
- More structure and socialization than a lone aide on a short shift  
- Formal **respite** so the unpaid caregiver doesn’t quit their job or land in the ER next

Demand is not the scarce resource.

**Licensed capacity you can *fill*, staff you can *ratio*, and payers who *pay on time* are.**

Policy moves the goalposts: state ADS/ADHC rules, CMS **HCBS Settings** expectations (community integration, not warehouse-in-a-room), Medicaid rate freezes, MCO prior auth. Same story as other senior verticals — demography is the backdrop; your P&L is local execution.

## Micro: what actually drives one center

**License capacity & square footage**  
States typically license max simultaneous participants off **program** space (education bands often **~40–60 sq ft/participant**, excluding halls/kitchen/offices). You can’t “market your way” past a 24-seat license without a remodel + amendment.

**Staffing ratios (the silent CapEx)**  
Common education bands: roughly **1:6** health-model direct care, **1:8** social-model — state tables vary (some dementia / high-ADL tighter). Ratio staff + **RN/LPN rules** + **Activity Director** (often *not* counted in the ratio) means labor is semi-fixed. Empty seats still need coverage if even a thin census shows up. Sick-call = compliance event, not just a schedule headache.

**Occupancy discipline**  
North-star isn’t “how many names on the roster.” It’s **average daily attendance** vs licensed capacity, no-show rate, and days/week density. A roster of 40 who each come twice a week is a different business than 28 who come five days.

**Payer mix management**  
Medicaid-heavy books fill seats and starve margin if rates lag wages. Private-pay / VA / LTC insurance can sweeten the blend — and change marketing (physicians and care managers vs waiver caseworkers). Ted’s home-care lesson translates: **busy Medicaid ≠ healthy mix**.

**Transportation**  
Many families won’t enroll without the van. Vans need commercial insurance, securement training, often **NEMT** posture, sometimes CDL/passenger endorsements at higher capacity. Routes that zigzag the county for three riders destroy economics that looked fine on a census slide.

**Facility CapEx / lease**  
ADA restrooms, fire/life safety, kitchen, accessible entries, sprinklers — the “cheap strip-center” fantasy dies in inspection. Former clinic / church / healthcare space can save you; raw retail buildouts blow the budget. Industry startup writeups for ~20–30 participant health-model centers commonly span roughly **mid-six figures** all-in (**~$125k–$400k** bands depending on renovations, vans, and payroll runway) — verify *your* state and landlord.

**Referral engine**  
Discharge planners, home-care agencies (ironic partners — they need daytime coverage too), placement advisors, VA, churches, senior centers. Trust sells. One fall or med error story travels faster than your Facebook ad.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you live in licensing binders, census calls, staffing boards, and “the van is broken.” Semi-absentee fairy tales collide with ratio law and survey reality. Earn a real director + RN bench before you disappear.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Education shape only — **not** a forecast for your market or your deal.

**Rate benchmarks (public surveys / guides):**

- CareScout national median adult day health: **~$100/day (2024)** → **~$95/day (2025)**  
- Private-pay operator bands often discussed **~$85–\$175/day** by metro and acuity  
- Medicaid HCBS per-diems: wide state spread (public education often **~$65–\$150+**; MCOs may sit under fee schedule)

**Occupancy math (whiteboard, not a promise):**

- Licensed capacity 30 → ~**70–80%** attendance ≈ **21–24** average daily census as a common break-even *conversation* starter  
- Illustrative: 25 attendees × **$90** blended × 5 days × ~52 weeks ≈ **~$1.1M** revenue *shape* before you subtract ratio labor, food, vans, lease, insurance, bad debt  
- Guides discuss direct-program cost stacks where staffing alone can land **high-six-figures** at that scale — then facility + admin compress net; well-run stabilized targets in education pieces often sit roughly **high-single to mid-teens** net — **wide variance, easy to miss**

**Startup / runway (industry education bands):**

- Rough all-in for a small licensed center: commonly discussed **~$125k–$400k** (lease deposits, ADA/fire TI, kitchen, furnishings, **1+ accessible vans**, insurance, license/legal, **3–4+ months** payroll before Medicaid cash)  
- Soft-open at **10–15** participants and ramp **6–9+ months** is normal talk — opening at full licensed capacity on day one is how people fund payroll with hope  
- Contrast: Brian’s non-medical **in-home** franchise bands (~**$90k–$150k** / other clips ~**$130k–$200k**) buy an **office + caregiver float**, not a day-facility buildout. Different check size. Different risk.

**Brian-adjacent numbers (for stack context, not adult-day P&Ls):**

- In-home bill/pay teaching bands (~**$28–$34** bill / ~**$14–$18** pay in his newsletter) — *agency* math  
- Residential AL / group-home thread: monthly room illustrations **~$3k–$6k**/room — *overnight residential*, not day seats

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- What’s licensed capacity vs **trailing average daily attendance** (not roster count)?  
- Payer mix % (private / Medicaid / MCO / VA / LTC) and denial / auth lag?  
- Days sales outstanding by payer? Working capital if remittance stalls 60–90 days?  
- Direct-care vacancy rate? RN tenure? Agency/PRN % of labor?  
- Actual staff-to-participant ratios by hour of day (not the brochure)?  
- Transport: % of census on the van, cost per rider, accident / securement incidents?  
- Last licensing survey / HCBS settings findings and open corrections?  
- Lease remaining term, TI amortization, and what happens if capacity amendment is needed?  
- No-show / hospitalization attrition — how fast do you refill?  
- If franchised or affiliated: does the system help with Medicaid enrollment, training, and referrals — or only a logo?

Same brain you’d use buying any regulated facility business: seats, labor law, cash conversion — and whether *you* want that responsibility.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Licensing & survey burden**  
State ADS/ADHC license, facility inspection, policies, background checks, renewals. “We’ll tidy the binder later” is how openings slip six months.

**2. Occupancy — empty seats with full staffing**  
Ratios and RN coverage don’t shrink just because census is soft. Fixed cost meets soft demand = quiet death.

**3. Staffing ratios as a hard constraint**  
Hit acuity or call-outs wrong and you’re over ratio (illegal) or refusing admits (brand death). Labor *is* the product *and* the regulation.

**4. Caregiver / aide / nurse recruiting**  
Same labor war as home care — plus facility culture, dementia skills, and weekend/holiday coverage patterns. Turnover resets training and trust.

**5. Medicaid / MCO funding mix**  
Rate pressure, prior auth, credentialing delays, settings-rule compliance. One policy memo can reprice your year.

**6. VA and other specialty payers**  
Great diversifier when contracted; slow to enroll; population-specific training; concentration if you lean too hard on one VAMC.

**7. Facility CapEx & landlord risk**  
ADA, fire code, kitchen, square-footage math. Lease too short for TI payback. Flood / HVAC failure closes the only plant you have.

**8. Transportation ops & liability**  
Broken lift, no driver, icy routes, securement mistakes. The van is a profit center on the brochure and a lawsuit on a bad day.

**9. Cash timing**  
Payroll weekly; Medicaid monthly-plus. Private-pay softens it; Medicaid-heavy ramps punish thin balance sheets.

**10. No-shows, hospitalizations, and sudden discharges**  
Roster ≠ attendance. Flu week empties the room and not the labor schedule.

**11. Acuity creep**  
Families push “just a little more help” past your license and staffing design. Scope creep becomes survey risk.

**12. Family dynamics & communication**  
Guilt, siblings, “why does she come home exhausted,” unpaid invoices. You’re in the emotion business with a facility shell.

**13. Food service & special diets**  
Health permits, texture-modified meals, dietitian review, CACFP paperwork if you chase reimbursement. Not a side hustle for the activities aide.

**14. Competition & substitutes**  
Other day centers, in-home hours, senior centers, PACE/institutional options, family quitting work. Differentiation is safety + reliability + transport — not murals.

**15. Owner intensity + key-person risk**  
Lose the director or the RN and the license stack wobbles. Absentee ownership fantasies collide with ratio law.

**16. Confusing “aging demographics” with “your filled, collectible seats”**  
Same trap as home care’s “market size” vs fillable hours. Demand ≠ operable census.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means adult day is a **regulated facility + staffing business** where occupancy math and payer mix decide whether the mission survives contact with payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re willing to own a **physical site** — inspections, landlords, vans — not just a CRM  
- You can recruit and retain direct-care *and* clinical leadership (or partner with someone who can)  
- Occupancy dashboards and ratio compliance energize you  
- You’re capitalized for slow Medicaid cash and a soft-open ramp  
- Respite for family caregivers feels like meaningful work, not a marketing slogan  
- You’ve compared this door to lighter senior models (placement, in-home agency) and still want the facility stack

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted Brian’s **low-overhead** senior doors (placement, estate/relocation) and thought “day center” was the same lightness with more heart  
- You hate licensing, surveys, or food-service rules  
- You need passive income or a semi-absentee story from day one  
- Thin stomach for dementia behaviors, falls, or family conflict  
- You’re undercapitalized for TI + vans + months of payroll before collections  
- You’re actually shopping **in-home hours**, **skilled home health**, or **placement fees** — different pages  
- You wanted **overnight residential** group homes / RAL — related senior facility thesis, different page someday

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: seats, ratios, payers, transport. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens licensing, staffing, and census — not because the lobby smells like lavender.

Many operators will correctly choose **in-home non-medical** first (Brian’s stated simplicity bias) and never buy a day facility. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Soft occupancy + fixed ratios → structural losses  
- Wage inflation without rate relief → busy and broke  
- Failed survey / lost Medicaid billing → existential  
- Van accident or med error → liability + referral death spiral  
- Payer mix shift (MCO rates, waiver caps) → margin compression overnight  
- Lease / TI mismatch → trapped capital  
- Key RN / director departure → compliance and census risk  
- Distracted ownership → same mission, empty chairs  
- Franchise/affiliate stack (if any) → fees must buy real enrollment and ops leverage  
- Confusing demographic tailwinds with your collectible participant-days

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “aging population.”

You’re buying the right to fill **licensed daytime seats** — if you can pass surveys, hold ratios, run vans, mix payers, and wait for Medicaid without breaking payroll — while giving family caregivers a real workday of relief.

Some people should start (or buy) **in-home care** and stay asset-light on facilities.  
Some people should buy a tired day center and fix census + transport + payer mix.  
Some people should build health-model ADHC where waiver rates and demographics support it.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into placement, mobility, a trade, or a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do exhausted caregivers need daytime help?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a regulated facility job with occupancy anxiety, ratio law, and higher stakes when the van doesn’t start?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs in-home care, medical home health, placement, or something outside senior care), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
