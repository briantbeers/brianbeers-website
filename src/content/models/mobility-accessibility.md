Dad fell on the stairs again.

The ER discharge papers say “home with walker.”  
The house still has four steps at the front door and a bathtub he can’t climb over.  
Kids live two states away. Someone Googles “stairlift near me” at 11pm.

That’s **mobility / accessibility installs**.

Not in-home caregiving (you’re not scheduling bath aides).  
Not facility placement (you’re not matching AL / memory care).  
Not a general handyman punch list (you’re not hanging TVs for tips).

You’re selling **aging-in-place project tickets**: stairlifts, ramps, grab bars, safer baths, lifts — measure, quote, install, service when it breaks.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because referrals, insurance/VA paperwork, install skill, long sales cycles, and product liability all show up before the check clears.

---

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local accessibility / mobility project operator**: consult in the home, prescribe the right equipment or modification, install it cleanly, collect, then service the installed base.

This page covers the **install + project** side of aging-in-place — the book that falls, hospital discharges, home-care agencies, placement advisors, and Google all feed.

Typical menu:

1. **Stairlifts** — straight and curved; residential first; outdoor models exist  
2. **Ramps / modular access** — threshold, modular aluminum, permanent builds (permits change the job)  
3. **Vertical platform lifts / porch lifts** — higher ticket, more structural and code risk  
4. **Bathroom safety / aging baths** — tub-to-shower, low-threshold showers, grab bars, seats (some operators; some partner out)  
5. **Grab bars / rails / threshold work** — smaller tickets that open the door to bigger projects  
6. **Service, maintenance, rentals** — batteries, call-outs, short-term post-surgery rentals  
7. **DME / mobility equipment attach** (some books) — scooters, power chairs, hospital beds, walkers — retail + service; different inventory and payer stack than a pure install shop

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google / LSA, discharge planner, home-care agency, placement advisor, OT / PT, realtor, prior family, VA / VSO channel, facility or hospital equipment accounts  
2. **In-home consult** — stairs, doorway widths, user’s weight / cognitive state, caregiver reality, HOA / landlord rules, funding path  
3. **Quote / options** — product tiers, install scope, what’s *not* included, timeline, financing / grant / waiver paperwork  
4. **Close** — deposit; order custom rail / modular sections; schedule crew  
5. **Install** — measure twice; code / permit if required; train the user and family  
6. **Collect / follow-up** — review ask; warranty; service plan  
7. **Attach** — next barrier in the house (bath after stairs), rental for rehab, DME if you carry it, refer care / placement / movers when the house still isn’t enough

**Scope lock:** this is **mobility / accessibility as the company** — project tickets, installs, service on what you put in the home.  
It is **not** in-home non-medical caregiving (hours × caregivers — different page).  
It is **not** senior placement / referral (facility success fees — different page).  
It is **not** senior moving / estate liquidation.  
It is **not** pure handyman odd-jobs (adjacent; grab bars overlap — different utilization math).  
It is **not** a full kitchen/bath GC remodel company (bathroom *accessibility* tickets sit next door; don’t confuse with design-build).

## How money comes in

You sell **completed installs and funded projects**, not “hours on a timesheet” the family romanticizes — even though labor hours (and callbacks) are how you deliver.

Pricing shapes you’ll see:

- **Equipment + install package** (stairlift / lift quoted installed)  
- **Project quotes** for ramps and bath mods (materials + labor + permits)  
- **Rental** for short-term recovery (monthly + install/removal fees)  
- **Service plans / call-outs** on the installed base  
- **Financing** through dealer or third-party lenders (changes cash timing)  
- **Third-party pay** when it exists — VA grants, Medicaid HCBS waivers, some Medicare Advantage supplemental benefits, nonprofit / AAA programs (never assume Medicare Part B covers a stairlift)

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Closed projects** — consult done, deposit taken, install scheduled, change orders controlled  
2. **Average ticket / mix** — grab bar afternoon vs straight stairlift vs curved rail vs modular ramp vs bath conversion  
3. **Close rate × cycle time** — crisis families move fast; “someday” tire-kickers and funding waits eat calendar  
4. **Funding path skill** — private pay is clean; VA / waiver / insurance paperwork is a sales *and* ops skill  
5. **Install quality + callbacks** — one bad mount or wet-area fail becomes liability and reputation  
6. **Service / rental attach** — smooths feast-famine between big tickets  
7. **Referral partner engine** — home care, discharge, OT/PT, placement, realtors, VSOs — or you live on expensive Google leads forever

Gross margin lives in **price − equipment COGS − install labor − freight / custom rail − callbacks (− commissions / royalties)**.  
Then overhead: showroom/warehouse (many systems want one), vans, commercial liability + product liability culture, tools, marketing, software, working capital for inventory and slow-pay funding jobs, and (if franchised) royalty + brand/MAP + tech.

This is a **consultative project + install + funding navigation** business.  
Not a caregiver payroll spreadsheet. Not a facility waitlist fee.

**Light contrast (same aging stack, different check):**

| Model | Who pays you | What you “sell” | Overhead feel |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **In-home care** | Family / private pay (hours) | Caregiver time in the house | Office + caregiver army |
| **Placement** | Usually the facility (success fee) | A move-in that sticks | Lean; relationship / tours |
| **Handyman** | Homeowner (job fee) | Punch-list density | Trucks + tech utilization |
| **Mobility / accessibility** | Family / estate / (sometimes) VA–waiver–grant | Safer access in *this* house | **Consult → order → install → liability** |

Brian puts mobility next to care, placement, and senior handyman for a reason: the family trying to keep Mom home often needs *equipment and modifications* before — or instead of — a facility. Partnership across the stack, not the same P&L.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

America is getting older. Brian’s senior thread called the wave what it is: millions will need care — and many will **fight to age in place** before they accept a facility.

Falls. Arthritis. Post-surgery. Wheelchair thresholds. A spouse who can’t lift anymore.  
The house that worked at 55 becomes a trap at 78.

Demand for *someone who can make the stairs and the bathroom survivable* is structural.

The constraint isn’t “are there seniors?”

It’s **trusted consults + skilled installs + funding navigation + partners who send work** in *your* market — without betting the company on one bad product claim.

National accessibility franchises exist. Independent stairlift dealers exist. Ramp specialists exist. Bathroom remodel brands market aging showers. DME stores sell scooters down the street.  
Same demographic. Different operating reality.

## Micro: what actually drives one territory

**Lead partners**  
Home-care agencies, hospital / SNF discharge, OT/PT, placement advisors, elder-law / financial advisors, realtors doing senior transitions, VSOs / VA channels, prior families.  
No partner engine → you rent expensive keywords against every dealer in the DMA and fight price shoppers who will blame you when Medicare doesn’t pay.

**Consult honesty**  
Stairs geometry, weight limits, cognitive ability to use controls, doorway widths, outdoor freeze/thaw, landlord / HOA rules, “we’ll do the electrical” that becomes your problem on install day. Under-scoped jobs create callbacks and liability.

**Sales cycle discipline**  
Some jobs close same week after a fall. Curved rails, custom baths, and grant-funded projects can sit for weeks or months. Pipeline hygiene beats heroics.

**Funding literacy (or a person who owns it)**  
VA HISA and related grants, Medicaid HCBS waivers, Advantage supplemental benefits, state / nonprofit programs — eligibility, documentation, what is *excluded*, reimbursement vs upfront pay. Operators who “sell hope” on coverage they didn’t verify inherit angry families and dead inventory.

**Install skill**  
Rail mounting, load paths, electrical, waterproofing on bath jobs, modular ramp engineering, permits. This is not “handy cousin with a drill.” Training shortcuts show up as injuries and lawsuits.

**Product / manufacturer relationships**  
Lead times on curved rails, warranty posture, parts availability, which SKUs you can stand behind. Stockouts and orphaned brands kill service reputation.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you *are* consults, follow-ups, install QA, and the person answering “why won’t insurance pay?” at 8pm.  
Semi-absentee fairy tales are how people buy a van wrap and a showroom lease. Earn a manager after the book has process — not before.

**Adjacent stack**  
In-home care, placement, senior moving, estate sales, handyman, bathroom remodel partners. You don’t need to own them. You do need to know who you send overflow to — and who sends *you* the next barrier in the house.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

**Operating intuition (education — not forecasts):**

- Consumer education bands for **straight stairlifts** often land roughly **~$2k–$5k** installed; **curved** commonly **~$7k–$15k+** — geometry and features move this hard  
- **Ramps** span from small threshold work to multi-thousand modular / permanent projects (permits and length dominate)  
- **Bath accessibility** tickets (tub-to-shower / low-threshold) often sit in a different band than a single grab-bar call — remodel-adjacent pricing  
- **Rentals** (stairlift / ramp) can run roughly low-hundreds per month plus install/removal — useful for post-surgery, not a substitute for a sold book  
- Brian’s candidate-deck education for **Mobility Sales & Service**–type concepts used all-in cost shapes roughly **~$200k–$300k** and lean start headcount (~**3**) — *those* systems, *those* years  
- Notion **Mobility Plus** card showed investment roughly **~$197k–$460k** — verify current Item 7; multi-format brands (store vs home-access vs systems) are not one number  
- Large accessibility franchise public FDD aggregator reads (education): Item 7 bands often roughly **mid–high six figures** all-in (illustrative ~**$180k–$260k+** depending on year/system); Item 19 **per-territory sales** in one large system’s recent disclosure tables showed **wide quartile spreads** (top quartile well into seven figures; bottom quartile much thinner; system-wide averages in the mid–high six figures in those tables) — same logo, different execution; **not your forecast**  
- FranServe adjacency (Amramp, Mobility City, Green Light Mobility, etc.) proves deal flow exists in the category — placement fees ≠ unit economics

**Startup / investment shape (education — verify current FDD Item 7 / indie quotes):**

- Franchise paths: fee + warehouse/showroom + vehicle + opening inventory + launch marketing + **working capital** for long cycles and funding delays  
- Independents can start leaner (dealer agreements, one van, insurance, tools) and still get crushed by a single product claim or a custom-rail cancel  
- Inventory and deposits matter more than “laptop and a phone” senior models (placement)

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Jobs per month by SKU (straight lift / curved / ramp / bath / service)? Average ticket? Close rate by lead source?  
- Median days from consult → install → cash? % of revenue waiting on VA / waiver / financing?  
- Install labor % and callback rate last 24 months?  
- % of revenue from top 3 referral partners (home care / discharge / placement)?  
- Product liability / completed-ops insurance limits — and claims history?  
- Manufacturer concentration and lead times on curved / custom?  
- Service contract attach rate and rental utilization?  
- If franchised: royalty + brand fund + required warehouse — do they actually win jobs *and* protect install quality?  
- Owner hours on consults vs hiring installers — is this a transferable company?

Same brain you’d use buying any field-project business: close rate, cycle time, COGS, callbacks, partner concentration, and whether *you* want to live inside other families’ fall-recovery stress for years.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Referral dependency**  
Home care, discharge, OT/PT, and placement partners send beautiful aging-in-place jobs — until they switch vendors, get a competing “preferred” list, or the discharge planner you bought lunch for retires. Concentration risk is real. Google-only books compete on price with every dealer running the same stairlift ad.

**2. Insurance / VA / waiver funding complexity**  
Families hear “Medicare will cover it.” Often it won’t (classic Part B gap on stairlifts). VA HISA and related grants, Medicaid HCBS waivers, Advantage extras, and state programs each have caps, exclusions, paperwork, and reimbursement timing. Sell the wrong funding story and you own anger, cancelled orders, and AR purgatory.

**3. Install skill is the product**  
Wrong fasteners, ignored load paths, bad waterproofing on a bath job, ramp slope that fails code — these are injury and lawsuit factories. Hiring “a carpenter who can figure it out” is how brands die quietly.

**4. Sales cycle whiplash**  
Crisis closes this week. Grant-funded curved rails take a quarter. Your fixed costs don’t care which week the check arrives. Without pipeline math, you feast then starve.

**5. Product liability and completed operations**  
You’re putting devices and structures into homes with vulnerable users. A fall off a ramp you built, a stairlift failure, a grab bar that pulls out of drywall — insurance, documentation, manufacturer support, and install QA aren’t optional line items.

**6. Custom lead times and cancellations**  
Curved rails and specialty lifts aren’t shelf candy. Deposits help; families still cancel after hospital outcomes change. Dead inventory and restocking drama are real.

**7. Scope creep into full remodel / GC work**  
“While you’re here, redo the whole bathroom” sounds like margin until you’re in tile, plumbing rough-in, and permit hell you didn’t underwrite. Partner or price as a different job.

**8. Manufacturer and parts dependency**  
One vendor’s warranty posture or parts delay becomes *your* one-star reviews. Dual-source where you can; never pretend the logo on the rail is irrelevant.

**9. Cash timing vs deposits and third-party pay**  
Private-pay card-on-install is clean. VA / waiver reimbursement and lender funding create gaps. Working capital is quieter than home-care payroll — still real.

**10. Showroom / warehouse overhead (many systems)**  
Lease, utilities, display inventory. Under-showroomed you look unserious to some buyers; over-leased you bleed in slow months.

**11. Service book neglect**  
Install-and-ghost operators lose the recurring layer and the next referral. Batteries, annual checks, and rental fleets need process.

**12. Family dynamics and guilt sales**  
Adult children disagree. One sibling wants the lift; another wants Mom in AL. You’re half consultant, half referee. Soft closing beats hard close — reputation is the channel.

**13. Competition from DME stores, national lead aggregators, and bathroom remodel brands**  
Different P&Ls attacking the same fear. Differentiate on consult quality and install trust — not a clever slogan.

**14. Owner stuck on every consult**  
No estimator, no installer lead, no funding coordinator → you bought a van and a phone that never stops. Brian’s ownership frame cares whether you can eventually **manage the manager**.

**15. Franchise stack (if you go that route)**  
Royalty + brand fund + required marketing / warehouse vs playbook, buying power, and national accounts. Worth it only if the system wins jobs *and* protects install quality — not because the stairlift photo looks reassuring.

**16. Confusing “Silver Tsunami” with “your installs are booked”**  
Macro demand is the backdrop. Your company is consults, closes, funding, install QA, and liability discipline.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means mobility / accessibility is a **real project + liability business**, not a demographic lottery ticket.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re okay with consultative home sales and emotional family settings  
- You can build a local partner network (care, discharge, placement, OT/PT, VA channels)  
- You’re willing to learn funding pathways — or hire someone who lives in them  
- You respect install craft and won’t skip training to hit monthly targets  
- You’re capitalized for inventory, slow cycles, and insurance — or intentional about a lean dealer start  
- You want a senior-industry vehicle **with** field projects (contrast: placement’s light overhead)

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want pure desk / relationship economics with no install drama (look at **placement**)  
- You want recurring caregiver-hour economics (look at **in-home care**)  
- You hate paperwork and hate conflict  
- You’re looking for passive / “hire a manager and fish” from month one  
- You’re undercapitalized for custom orders + liability insurance + slow-pay jobs  
- You’re actually shopping **handyman** density or **bath remodel** as the whole identity and calling it mobility  
- You can’t sleep owning product that a fragile adult will trust with their weight

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same category either way: consults, tickets, installs, funding, liability, partners.  
Franchise trades fee/royalty for playbook, buying power, and brand — only if the system helps you win projects *and* install them cleanly.

## Risks (the short list)

- Partner concentration → revenue cliff  
- Funding mis-sold → AR and reputation damage  
- Install quality miss → injury / claim / existential  
- Long custom lead times + cancels → cash and inventory pain  
- Owner-on-every-consult forever → no transferable enterprise  
- Royalty drag without lead/system advantage  
- Thinking “aging population” substitutes for close rate and QA  
- Blurring into GC remodel or DME retail you didn’t staff or insure for

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “people want to age in place” or “stairlift market size.”

You’re buying the right to stand between a family’s fear of the next fall and a safer house — and get paid when the lift runs, the ramp slopes correctly, and the paperwork matches what you promised — if you can sell honestly, install like an adult, navigate funding without lying, and carry liability with eyes open.

Some people should start as a lean dealer with strong home-care / discharge partners.  
Some people should buy a tired accessibility book with a service base and fix install QA + funding process.  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into placement, in-home care, handyman, senior moving, or a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “will more seniors need ramps and lifts?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a calendar of consults and a warehouse of custom rails that only works when someone else’s crisis clears paperwork on your timeline?**
