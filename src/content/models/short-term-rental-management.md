Checkout is at 11:00.  
New guests want the code at 3:02.  
The cleaner’s van is stuck in traffic and the last review mentioned hair in the shower drain.  
The owner texts from another time zone: *why is the calendar empty next Tuesday?*

That’s **short-term rental co-host / management**.

Not “buy Airbnbs and be a host-investor.”  
Not long-term **property management** (monthly leases, trust deposits, eviction math — separate model).  
Not pure **residential cleaning** routes as the whole company (you may *hire* cleaners; you are not only a maid book).  
Not flipping houses or listing brokerage specialty.

You’re a **local (or multi-market) ops company** whose product is running **someone else’s** vacation / short-term listings: channel mix, pricing/calendar, guest messaging, check-in logistics, turnover cleaning QC, maintenance triage, owner reporting — and the phone that rings when the lockbox fails at midnight.

Simple on a whiteboard: more doors → % of booking revenue → scale.  
Hard in real life — because **platform dependence**, **cleaning QC under time pressure**, **local regs / HOA bans**, **seasonality**, **guest issues**, and **fee compression** all show up before the “passive STR portfolio” brochure does.

---

## What the business actually is

You’re a **short-term rental manager / co-host operator** — independent, branded, or hybrid — paid to stand between an owner’s listing and the daily reality of guests, cleaners, platforms, and city rules.

Typical job: an investor (or tired self-host) owns one or many units and doesn’t want to be the 24/7 inbox, the cleaner scheduler, or the person arguing with a guest about a missing remote. You optimize the listing, sync calendars across channels, answer guests, orchestrate turns, restock, escalate maintenance, protect reviews, and send the owner a statement that doesn’t make them fire you for “empty Tuesdays.”

Brian’s materials put this row on the **250 Franchise Ideas** sheet as **Vacation Rental Management**, with **TurnKey Vacation Rentals**, **Evolve Vacation Rental**, and **iTrip Vacations** as category-proof names — not as “this page is a brand pitch.” Separately, his cleaning / remote-ops content (e.g. MaidThis-style teaching) treats **vacation-rental turnovers** as a demand spike next to residential cleans — useful adjacency, **not** the same company identity as full STR management. His landlord-vs-business teaching keeps **owning the asset** and **operating a fee business** as different doors.

Typical menu (mix varies by market / company):

1. **Full-service management / co-host** — listing, pricing, guest CX, cleaning coordination, maintenance triage, owner reporting  
2. **Partial co-host** — messaging + calendar only; owner still owns cleaning / vendors (cheaper fee, thinner moat)  
3. **Onboarding / listing launch** — photos, copy, amenity setup, channel setup; often a one-time fee  
4. **Revenue / dynamic pricing** — channel manager, min stays, seasonal rules, OTA promotions  
5. **Turnover ops** — cleaner bench, QC photos, restock, linen, same-day failure recovery  
6. **Owner acquisition** — realtor / investor intros, Google, “tired host” outbound, franchise/brand lead flow, book acquisition  
7. **Not the same company:** owning the units yourself (landlord CapEx), pure long-term residential PM, or pure maid routes without guest/channel responsibility

Typical loop:

1. **Win the owner** — tired self-host, remote investor, realtor intro, brand lead, acquisition of a book  
2. **Onboard the listing** — agreement, access/codes, house rules, supply standards, who pays cleaning vs what guests are charged, insurance / permit status  
3. **List + distribute** — primary OTA + direct / secondary channels; calendar sync that doesn’t double-book  
4. **Price + calendar** — occupancy vs ADR; gaps kill owner trust; race-to-fill kills margins and guest quality  
5. **Operate stays** — messaging SLAs, check-in, mid-stay issues, noise / parking / HOA complaints  
6. **Turn the unit** — cleaner dispatch, QC, restock, damage documentation, re-list readiness  
7. **Report / remit** — owner statement; fees, cleaning pass-throughs, platform payouts reconciled honestly  
8. **Retain or lose** — owner churn on “you’re too expensive / calendar empty / reviews slipped”; guest churn shows up as review death  
9. **Grow listings without breaking turns** — the whole game after ~15–40 doors depending on density and systems

**Scope lock:** this is an **ops / services model** — listings under management, % of booking (or hybrid) fees, guest + cleaning orchestration.

It is **not** landlord / buy-and-hold / “I own the Airbnbs” investing (different capital, leverage, CapEx, and who owns the asset — Brian’s ownership frame keeps **business vs real estate vs investing** as separate doors).  
It is **not** “guest rent is your revenue” — that’s the *owner’s* top line (minus platform fees); yours is management / co-host fees (and whatever ancillaries you honestly disclose).  
It is **not** long-term residential property management as the identity of the company (leases, trust deposits, evictions — see **property management**).  
It is **not** residential cleaning as the whole book (see **residential cleaning** — STR turns are a demand shape you may layer or vendor).  
It is **not** “set it and forget it passive income with a logo.”

Named proof points exist: national vacation-rental management brands and strong local co-host shops. Treat logos as **category proof** — not as the point of this page, and not as a substitute for a transferable book of listings, clean agreements, and a cleaner bench that actually shows up at 11:15 a.m.

Independents, franchisees, and software-forward co-host platforms can all sit in this category. Same umbrella. Wildly different P&Ls, licensing maturity, and how much of the guest relationship they actually own.

## How money comes in

You sell **management capacity, review protection, and occupancy ops** — not the underlying booking payout (that belongs to the owner, net of platforms and taxes).

Pricing / economics shapes you’ll see:

- **% of gross booking revenue** — commonly educated as roughly **~15–25%** for meaningful co-host / management scope; full-service or tough markets sometimes chatter higher (**~25–30%+**); messaging-only can sit lower (**~10–15%**) — always verify *your* market and what “gross” means (before vs after OTA fees)  
- **% of net** — after defined deductions (platform fees, cleaning pass-throughs, taxes); fairer on paper, fight-prone in practice  
- **Flat monthly / per-door retainer** — or hybrid retainer + smaller %  
- **Onboarding / photography / setup fees** — new-listing friction coverage  
- **Cleaning coordination** — guest-facing cleaning fee often pass-through to cleaners; your margin is coordination, QC, and failure recovery — not pretending labor is free  
- **Maintenance / vendor markup** — disclosed admin or %; ethics live here  
- **Ancillary** — linen programs, restock markups, late checkout fees, damage claim admin — only where contracted and legal  
- **What you generally do *not* own:** appreciation, mortgage equity, the unit’s CapEx — those are landlord economics

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Listings under management that stay** — not doors you signed and lost after one soft season  
2. **Revenue per listing** — ADR × occupancy × your fee %; low-ADR doors can be operationally identical and economically worse  
3. **Channel mix** — concentration on one OTA is both distribution and single-point-of-failure  
4. **Turn density / geography** — scattered units destroy cleaner economics and your QC  
5. **Owner concentration** — one whale portfolio can make or destroy a month  
6. **Review velocity / rating floor** — platforms punish soft scores; your fee depends on staying bookable  
7. **Staff / vendor leverage** — listings per coordinator before the inbox and the 11 a.m. turns melt

Gross management % *looks* simple on a napkin.  
Then same-day cleaner overtime, linen loss, software stack, E&O / liability, chargebacks, “guest therapy,” and the HOA fine that landed on *your* watch show up.

This is a **platform + guest CX + cleaning logistics** business.  
Not rental NOI. Not a long-term lease book. Not a pure route van (though your cleaners live in one).

**Light contrast (same housing stack, different check):**

| Model | Who pays you | What you “sell” | Overhead feel |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Landlord / buy-hold STR** | Guests (booking revenue) → you keep NOI after debt/ops | Ownership of the asset | CapEx, leverage, occupancy risk on *your* balance sheet |
| **Long-term property management** | Owner (fees on monthly leases) | Leasing + ops + trust + vendor orchestration | Sticky monthly fees; trust accounting; eviction math |
| **Residential cleaning** | Homeowner / host (clean fee) | Recurring cleans / turn cleans | Labor attrition; route density |
| **Junk removal** | Host / owner / PM (haul ticket) | Clear-outs, furniture dumps, post-guest chaos | Truck + landfill; density math |
| **STR co-host / management** | Owner (fees on bookings you operate) | Channel + guest CX + turn orchestration | Platform policy; cleaning QC clocks; seasonality; regs/HOA |

Brian’s cleaning content treats vacation-rental turns as **calendar spikes with occupancy risk** — that’s a *vendor* pain this company must solve every checkout. Don’t confuse hiring (or owning) a cleaning bench with *being* only a maid company. Don’t confuse absorbing host pain with *being* the landlord.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

America (and tourism corridors worldwide) has a lot of housing that can be rented by the night — and a lot of owners who discover that “Airbnb side income” is actually a **hospitality ops job**.

Remote investors, tired self-hosts, second-home owners, and people who bought “cash flow” then met a 2 a.m. lockout keep the demand for a **professional middle layer** alive.

Every new listing that gets financed, inherited, or exhausted into “I can’t answer guests anymore” is a potential management agreement.

The constraint isn’t “are there short-term rentals?”

It’s **local legality + platform account health + enough quality listings at a fee that funds real turn QC and 24/7 guest response** — without growing so fast the cleaners and the inbox collapse.

National brands exist. Local boutiques exist. Software / channel managers exist.  
Same category umbrella. Different operating reality — and some cities are actively trying to shrink the umbrella.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Listings under management (the unit of scale)**  
You’re not scaling “impressions.” You’re scaling obligations. Each listing is an inbox, a cleaner slot, a lock code, a supply closet, a review score, and a regulatory file. Count doors the way a route company counts stops — then ask what service level those doors actually get between 11:00 and 16:00 on turnover day.

**Owner sales vs guest/ops (two jobs)**  
Winning listings is a B2B sales motion. Running stays is hospitality / CX / logistics. Founders who only love “investor coffees” drown in guest tickets. Founders who only love “fixing showers” never replace churned owners.

**Channel mix / platform dependence**  
Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking, direct, and whoever wins next year. Algorithms, fee changes, Superhost / rating thresholds, and account suspensions are *external* bosses. Diversify channels where you can; never pretend one OTA is a diversified business.

**Cleaning QC under a hard clock**  
Checkout → clean → inspect → restock → next guest. Miss the window and you eat a refund, a review, or both. Your product is often *someone else’s* mop — preferred cleaners, photo QC, backup benches, linen logistics. A thin cleaner bench turns every Saturday into a firefight.

**Guest issues / review defense**  
Noise, parties, parking, missing amenities, “not as pictured,” early check-in pressure, mid-stay maintenance. Fast, documented, human responses beat macros that sound like bots. One viral bad stay can cost more than a month of fees.

**Local regs / permits / HOA / short-term bans**  
Cities and HOAs change the game overnight: caps, primary-residence rules, licensing, occupancy taxes, quiet hours, parking minimums, outright bans. Operating illegal or gray inventory is how companies and owners meet fines and lawsuits. Franchise playbooks help; they don’t replace local counsel and a willingness to walk from illegal doors.

**Seasonality + gap nights**  
Tourist markets feast and famine. Shoulder seasons empty calendars and owner patience. Over-discounting to fill gaps trains guests and tanks ADR. Under-occupying “to protect quality” without owner alignment creates churn.

**Fee honesty / scope clarity**  
Owners hate surprise markups and vague “we take 20% of everything.” Race-to-10% management with silent padding — or with no cleaner QC — is how you win doors you will lose. Write what “gross” means. Write who pays cleaning labor. Write emergency definitions.

**Software + process**  
PMS / channel manager, dynamic pricing tools, cleaner apps, owner portals, lock/code systems, noise monitors where legal. Chaos scales worse than mediocre software. Migrations mid-book are painful.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you *are* the inbox, the cleaner wrangler, and the midnight lockout.  
Semi-absentee fairy tales are how people buy a territory, a logo, and twenty listings of resentment.  
Scale means coordinators, cleaner SLAs, and a book that transfers — not “STRs print while I sleep.”

**Adjacent stack**  
Residential cleaners, junk removal, handymen, locksmiths, linen vendors, landscapers, pest — you don’t need to own them. You do need named humans who show up between stays — and who don’t train your owners to fire you.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

**Operating intuition (education — not forecasts):**

- Money event = **fees on bookings / listings you keep**, plus setup and disclosed ancillaries — never confuse *owner booking payout* with *your* revenue  
- Public education bands often put meaningful co-host / STR management fees around **~15–25% of gross booking revenue** (scope-dependent; messaging-only lower; full-service sometimes higher) — verify market; don’t memorize a blog  
- Cleaning is often a **pass-through** guest fee to labor; your economics live in coordination, QC failure cost, and whether turns are dense enough to keep cleaners  
- Operators obsess over **revenue per listing**, **occupancy**, **ADR**, and **review score floors** because fee-% alone understates (or overstates) a real book  
- Scale intuition you’ll hear in the trade: a handful of doors can feel like a poorly paid hospitality job; mid books hit coordinator + cleaner walls; larger books need systems or they rot — **none of that is your guarantee**  
- Brian’s corpus is **strong** on “don’t confuse landlord investing with operating a business” and on **STR cleaning as adjacency** (MaidThis / vacation-rental turnover teaching), and **thin** on a dedicated TurnKey / Evolve / iTrip Item 19 / door-level earnings table — do **not** invent one from franchise marketing PDFs

**Startup / investment shape (education — verify current quotes / city requirements):**

- Entity, insurance, contracts, PMS / channel tools, cleaner bench (W-2 or contractor — know the law), marketing to owners, working capital for slow listing growth and soft seasons  
- Franchise / brand paths (TurnKey / Evolve / iTrip–style category) add fee + system + sometimes territory / lead math — still not a substitute for local ops competence and legal inventory  
- Buying an existing **book of listings** is a different check (diligence on owner churn, channel health, review scores, permit status, cleaner retention, and whether relationships follow the buyer)  
- You’re often **office-light / home-plus-software** early — not a retail buildout. You *are* buying the ability to answer guests and turn units correctly when the calendar stacks three checkouts on a Saturday

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Listings under management — trailing 24 months adds, losses, net?  
- Revenue per listing (all fees) vs management-%-only?  
- Channel mix — % of GBV by OTA; any account strikes / suspensions?  
- Occupancy + ADR trends by season; gap-night policy?  
- Owner concentration — top 5 owners as % of revenue?  
- Owner churn % and reasons (fee, empty calendar, reviews, self-manage, sold / banned)?  
- Average review score / response-time metrics; recent 1–3★ root causes?  
- Cleaner bench — insured, backup coverage, QC photo policy, who pays labor vs guest cleaning fee?  
- Local permits / HOA / short-term legality status per door — any gray inventory?  
- After-hours SLA — who answers, what’s “emergency,” overtime cost?  
- Fee definition — gross vs net; markup policy disclosed in writing?  
- Staffing — listings per coordinator; owner hours still glued to the inbox?  
- If buying a book: will owners re-sign; platform access transferable; cleaner relationships portable; software data exportable?

Same brain you’d use buying any trust + recurring hospitality book: concentration, compliance, service capacity, and whether *you* want to live inside other people’s guest emergencies for years.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Platform dependence**  
One OTA can be most of your distribution *and* your rating boss. Fee changes, algorithm shifts, category redesigns, and account-level enforcement can gut occupancy overnight. “We diversify channels” is strategy; “we only live on one app” is a concentration risk with a logo.

**2. Cleaning QC under time pressure**  
The brand promise is a hotel-ish unit delivered between hard timestamps. No-shows, weak cleans, missing linens, and “looked fine from the doorway” inspections become *your* reviews. Building and auditing a cleaner bench is unpaid until the day it saves the Saturday stack.

**3. Local regs / permits / HOA bans**  
Cities cap nights, require primary residence, ban non-owner-occupied STRs, or invent new licenses every council cycle. HOAs fine and foreclose leverage. Managing inventory that isn’t clearly legal is not a growth hack — it’s existential. Walking from illegal doors costs revenue and saves the company.

**4. Seasonality + occupancy cliffs**  
Peak months hide weak ops. Shoulder and off-season reveal whether your fee still covers coordinators when the calendar thins. Owners remember empty Tuesdays longer than July’s ADR.

**5. Guest issues / party risk / neighbor war**  
Unauthorized gatherings, noise complaints, parking fights, smoking, pet damage, theft. Fast response and documentation matter; so do house rules, deposits/holds where allowed, and knowing when to involve platforms vs police vs HOA. One viral stay can erase a year of owner trust.

**6. Fee compression + race-to-bottom**  
Someone always offers 10–12% “full service.” They often make it up on silent markups, unpaid founder hours, or by skipping QC. Competing only on headline % trains owners to treat you like a commodity — then punish you when commodity service arrives.

**7. Review / rating death spiral**  
Platforms surface soft scores. Soft scores cut bookings. Cut bookings anger owners. Angry owners leave. Recovering a rating floor is slower and more expensive than protecting it. Macros that don’t solve real amenity failures make it worse.

**8. Double-booking / calendar sync failures**  
Channel managers fail. Humans override. Direct bookings collide with OTA holds. You own the refund and the reputation hit even when the software “should have” prevented it.

**9. Owner expectations vs hospitality reality**  
Owners want hotel ADR with motel effort. They want 90% occupancy at peak rates with zero guest drama. Education at onboarding is cheaper than churn at month four. Fire owners who need a miracle more than a manager.

**10. Maintenance / vendor fragility between stays**  
Broken A/C in July, Wi-Fi outages, appliance death — guest stays don’t wait for “next available.” Thin vendor benches and slow owner approvals create refunds you can’t fee your way out of.

**11. Owner concentration**  
One 25-door client feels like winning until they leave or their city bans their product type. Portfolio risk is real. So is the power imbalance when a whale demands free heroics every weekend.

**12. Staffing / listings-per-human wall**  
There’s a range where adding doors without adding capable humans quietly destroys response times and QC. Burned coordinators quit; tribal knowledge walks out; Saturday turns slip.

**13. Insurance / liability / access mistakes**  
Wrong code shared publicly, missed smoke detector, unpermitted hot tub, ignored mold complaint, guest injury — E&O and worse. Stay in process; document; don’t play lawyer or building inspector.

**14. Tax / occupancy-tax / remittance confusion**  
Who collects and remits lodging tax varies by jurisdiction and channel. Getting this wrong creates owner anger and government interest. “The platform handles it” is sometimes true and sometimes dangerously incomplete — verify per market.

**15. Semi-absentee myth**  
“Hire a VA in another country and watch STR fees compound.” Early reality is owner sales *and* guest fires *and* cleaner drama. Earn leverage with systems and leaders later — don’t buy the myth with a territory map.

**16. Confusing “own Airbnbs” with “own this company”**  
Landlord / STR investing / BRRRR-into-Airbnb is a different door in Brian’s ownership frame. This page is a **services practice** that operates other people’s listings for fees. CapEx, leverage, and who owns the outcome are not the same conversation — even if both say “doors” and “occupancy.”

**17. Collapsing this into “just cleaning” or “just long-term PM”**  
Residential cleaning is a vendor/adjacency (Brian’s MaidThis / vacation-turnover teaching). Long-term PM is leases and trust accounting. STR management is **channel + guest hospitality + turn logistics + regulation**. Steal muscles from neighbors; don’t steal their P&L story.

**18. Thinking “tourism is forever” fills your calendar**  
Macro travel demand can be real. Your company is listings won, listings kept, platforms that still distribute you, cleaners that show, permits that still exist, and a fee structure that funds an inbox answered on purpose.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means short-term rental co-host / management is a **real hospitality + logistics + compliance business**, not a passive booking-fee app with a logo.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re willing to live inside platform rules, local STR regs, and owner agreements — and walk from gray inventory  
- You can sell owners **and** build a guest/ops machine (or honestly partner the half you hate)  
- You’re calm under same-day turn pressure and still willing to hold quality standards when the calendar is stacked  
- You like vendor relationships, checklists, photo QC, and documentation more than Instagram host flexing  
- You’re okay with fee revenue that moves with seasonality — not only smooth monthly lease management fees  
- You want a real-estate-adjacent vehicle **without** buying the underlying CapEx stack yourself  
- You’re willing to fire bad owners / bad doors / illegal units when they poison the book

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want **landlord / STR investing** returns and asset appreciation as the whole thesis (different door — say so)  
- You want long-term residential PM trust-accounting / eviction work as the core (**property management**)  
- You want truck-route field service without guest/channel gravity (**residential cleaning** turns may still be a *vendor* you hire; **junk removal** for clear-outs)  
- You hate conflict, urgent messaging, or public review pressure  
- You’re looking for passive / “hire a VA and fish” from month one  
- You’re under-reserved for soft seasons and a rough regulatory surprise  
- You’re allergic to compliance detail and think “the app allows the listing” equals “the city allows the listing”

Franchise brand vs indie boutique vs buying a book is a separate call.  
Same category either way: listings, fees, channels, cleaners, guests, regs — plus an inbox that doesn’t care about your weekend plans.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “America loves Airbnb” or a pastel promise that co-host fees print while you sleep.

You’re buying the right to stand between an owner’s spreadsheet and a guest’s 3:02 p.m. reality — and get paid in management fees — if you can grow listings without breaking turns, keep platforms and cities from killing the book, hold a real cleaner bench, and stay honest when the file gets ugly.

Some people should launch lean, win legal doors slowly, and build process before headcount.  
Some people should buy a tired book and fix cleaner QC + channel mix + owner reporting.  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into landlord STR investing (if that’s actually the thesis), long-term property management, residential cleaning, junk removal, a trade, or a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “will hosts keep outsourcing headaches?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a calendar full of other people’s checkouts that only pays when the listings stay, the platforms still distribute you, the cleaners show, the city still allows the product, and the fee that clears still funds an inbox answered on purpose?**

If you want help comparing doors (this practice vs landlord STR investing vs long-term property management vs residential cleaning vs junk removal vs others), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
