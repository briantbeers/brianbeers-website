Storm hit Tuesday.  
Carrier’s first check landed Friday — and it looks like a partial for a full loss.  
The roofer is already on the ladder talking “supplements.”  
The restoration truck is measuring moisture in the walls.  
Nobody in that living room works *for the policyholder* on the claim file.

That’s when a **public / independent claims adjuster** shows up — licensed (where required) to represent the *insured*, not the carrier: document the loss, build the scope, fight the lowball, chase supplements, and get paid on a **contingency** when (if) money actually moves.

Not [roofing](/models/roofing) (they *sell and install* the roof — often on the same storm).  
Not [restoration](/models/restoration) (they *mitigate and remediate* water/fire/mold — often preferred-vendor to carriers).  
Not [home-inspection](/models/home-inspection) (transaction report on a *sale*, not a property-damage claim).  
Not [real-estate-investment-buy-hold](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold) (you don’t buy the house — you advocate on someone else’s loss).  
Not a plaintiff attorney (different license, different fee rules, different product — though some files escalate there).  
Not legal advice, and not “we guarantee a bigger check.”

You’re running an **expertise brand in an insurance-dispute niche**: signed representation → documentation / estimating → negotiation with the carrier → settlement → contingency fee — with **storm cycles**, **state licensing**, **fee ethics/regs**, **carrier friction**, **marketing compliance**, **talent**, and **working capital until settle** as the real operating system.

Simple on a whiteboard: help people get what their policy owes → take a % → repeat after the next hail.  
Hard in real life — because the feast after the CAT and the famine when skies are quiet both show up before the “fight the insurance company” Reel does.

Tiger Adjusters–class systems (Brian’s **250 Franchise Ideas** sheet lists **Public Claims Adjuster** with that name as **category proof only**), indie NAPIA-adjacent firms, multi-state CAT deployers, and solo licensed practitioners sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The license that lets you touch the claim, the contract that survives DOI scrutiny, and the cash that lasts until settlement are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **public (policyholder) claims adjuster practice** — independent, association-credentialed, or branded — paid to advocate for the *insured* on property (and sometimes related) insurance claims: residential storm/water/fire, commercial losses, contents, specialty lines depending on license and appetite.

Typical job: homeowner (or business owner) believes the carrier’s estimate is thin, delayed, or wrong; you inspect and document; prepare a scope (often Xactimate-class estimating); negotiate with staff/IA/desk adjusters; manage supplements and appraisal when the process allows; collect your contingency when the check clears — months later, often.

Brian’s materials do **not** center a dedicated “how to own a public adjusting firm” episode the way they do roofing or restoration. What they *do* hammer is the **storm / insurance adjacency**: roofing as high-ticket sales + insurance navigation after hail/wind ([HxXS2P4ydbs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HxXS2P4ydbs)); restoration as carrier relationships + slow insurance AR; buy-hold teaching that names **insurance** as a landlord squeeze. Roofing and restoration drafts explicitly fence **“not a pure public adjuster.”** This page is that fenced neighbor — same weather and claim weather, different check and different regs.

Typical menu (mix varies by state / firm):

1. **Residential property claims** — wind, hail, water, fire, roof + interior — volume engine in storm corridors  
2. **Commercial / large loss** — bigger tickets, longer cycles, different expertise and capital  
3. **Contents / ALE (additional living expense) advocacy** — attach on the same loss; discipline or leave money  
4. **CAT / storm deployment** — temporary licensing, travel, surge hiring; feast revenue, compliance risk  
5. **Appraisal / dispute escalation support** (where allowed) — process literacy; not “we’re your lawyer”  
6. **Referral orchestration** — roofers, restorers, contractors, counsel — stay in your license lane  
7. **Not the same company:** installing the roof ([roofing](/models/roofing)); running mitigation trucks ([restoration](/models/restoration)); writing a buyer’s inspection report ([home-inspection](/models/home-inspection)); buying the damaged asset ([real-estate-investment-buy-hold](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold)); practicing law without a law license

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — storm aftermath, Google / reviews, prior clients, contractor / attorney / agent referrals, brand/CAT marketing (compliance-gated)  
2. **Qualify** — policy type, loss date, prior payments, coverage fights, whether a PA is even lawful / useful on *this* file  
3. **Contract** — state-approved (or statute-compliant) representation agreement; fee % and caps disclosed; cooling-off / cancel rules where required  
4. **Inspect / document** — photos, measurements, moisture / thermal where relevant, expert consults  
5. **Estimate / demand** — Xactimate (or peer) scope; line-item fights; depreciation / RCV literacy  
6. **Negotiate** — carrier staff, IAs, desk review, supplements, reinspection theater  
7. **Settle / collect fee** — contingency on *paid* recovery (structure varies by contract / state); float until then  
8. **Close file / reputation** — Google, referrals to trades you didn’t become, E&O hygiene

**Scope lock:** this is **public / independent claims adjusting as a professional-services business** — policyholder advocacy on property claims, contingency economics, state licensing, storm-cycle volume.

It is **not** legal advice, coverage opinions as counsel, or a guarantee of claim outcomes.  
It is **not** [roofing](/models/roofing) production or [restoration](/models/restoration) mitigation as the identity (you may *coordinate* with them; you don’t usually *become* them on the same entity without separate licenses and conflict rules).  
It is **not** carrier staff adjusting or independent adjuster (IA) work *for the insurer* — different client, different ethics frame.  
It is **not** a pitch for any named PA franchise or lead system.

Solo grinders, multi-adjuster firms, CAT travelers, and branded systems can all sit here. Same umbrella. Wildly different whether *you* own licensing discipline, fee compliance, and cash until settle — or the last quiet weather year and the last fee-cap statute own you.

## How money comes in

You sell **representation that converts disputed (or underpaid) claim recoveries into contingency fees** — not hours on a ladder, not dry-out days, not a buyer’s report fee.

Economics shapes you’ll see (education — **verify your state DOI / statutes / contract rules**; not advice, not forecasts):

- **Contingency % of recovery** — industry education commonly discusses roughly **~10–20%** bands on residential property, with **statutory caps** in many states (illustrative: Texas often discussed at a **~10%** cap; Florida education often cites **~20%** ordinary and **~10%** in declared-emergency windows for a period — **always verify current law**; caps and definitions move)  
- **Fee on “new money” vs total** — contracts sometimes distinguish amounts paid *before* you were hired vs incremental recovery; state forms matter  
- **Commercial / complex** — sometimes higher % or different structures where lawful; longer cycle, bigger float  
- **Hourly / flat** — rare relative to contingency in classic PA positioning; some states / situations constrain what you can charge  
- **What you generally do *not* own as “your” revenue:** the roofing job ticket ([roofing](/models/roofing)), the mitigation invoice ([restoration](/models/restoration)), the inspection fee ([home-inspection](/models/home-inspection)), equity from buying the house ([real-estate-investment-buy-hold](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold))

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Signed files that actually settle** — not “leads after a hail map”  
2. **Average settlement × lawful fee %** — thin claims at capped % stay busy and broke  
3. **Cycle time FNOL → fee collected** — months is normal; years on ugly files; overhead doesn’t pause  
4. **Win rate / coverage reality** — denied claims, wear-and-tear fights, policy exclusions kill contingency dreams  
5. **Mix** — residential CAT spike vs commercial steady vs quiet-year retail claim flow  
6. **Referral quality** — roofers/restorers who send clean files vs storm-chaser drama that becomes your E&O story  
7. **Collection mechanics** — dual checks, mortgagee clauses, fee disputes after the “win”

Gross “fee” *looks* simple on a napkin: settlement × %.  
Then unpaid months of field time, Xactimate seats, E&O, bonds, travel, marketing that must stay legal, associate adjuster draw, and the CAT year that spikes files faster than you can staff show up.

This is a **expertise + compliance + float** business.  
Not a weather lottery ticket. Not “insurance always underpays so we always win.”

**Light contrast (same storm / housing stack, different check):**

| Model | Who pays you | What you “sell” | Overhead feel |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Public claims adjusting** | Contingency from *policyholder recovery* (structure/state-specific) | Claim advocacy / scope / negotiation | License + fee caps + float until settle |
| **[Roofing](/models/roofing)** | Homeowner / insurance job proceeds | Installed roof + related exterior | Sales + crews + insurance AR |
| **[Restoration](/models/restoration)** | Insurance / cash mitigation | Emergency dry-out + remediation | 24/7 + gear + carrier programs |
| **[Home inspection](/models/home-inspection)** | Buyer (report fee) | Condition assessment on a sale | Agent book + E&O + housing cycle |
| **[Buy-hold ops](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold)** | Rent / exit / refinance | Equity outcome on *your* asset | Capital + contractors + cycle |

You will *refer into* and *receive referrals from* those neighbors. Pretending their P&L is your contingency is how diligence fails — and how conflict-of-interest landmines get stepped on.

Illustrative **shape** only (vendor marketing math — **not a forecast**, not Brian claims): some PA-software education decks show napkin rows like “~4 claims/month × ~$75k average settlement × ~15% fee → mid-six figures annualized.” Treat that as *how contingency arithmetic works on a whiteboard*, not as underwriting for your metro, your fee cap, or your settle rate.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Policies get written. Losses happen. Carriers staff for *their* cost of claims. Storms, freezes, fires, and aging housing stock create files where the first number is not the last number — or where the first number never arrives on time.

When the economy is good, people still file property claims — and argue about roofs, water, and contents.  
When the economy is bad, weather and pipes **still don’t ask the Fed for permission** — same essential-demand neighborhood Brian uses for roofing and restoration — but **your** constraint is not “do losses exist?”

It’s **state licensing that lets you practice + fee rules you can live with + pipeline when there’s no CAT + carrier friction you can navigate without becoming the villain + cash that lasts until settlement + marketing that doesn’t get you fined** in *your* DOI’s backyard.

Hail belts, hurricane corridors, freeze states, and litigious metros create different calendars.  
Same category. Different operating reality — and a very different conversation from owning the roofing trucks or the extractors on the same loss.

## Micro: what actually drives one company

**Licensing by state (non-negotiable)**  
Public adjusting is **not** a national free-for-all. Most active PA states require prelicensing education, exam, background check, bond and/or E&O, and often **contract form approval**. Reciprocity is uneven. CAT deployment may need temporary / nonresident credentials. Practicing across a state line on vibes is how you meet regulators. Verify DOI rules before you print cards.

**Contingency fee ethics and regulations**  
% caps, disclosure, cancel windows, who can solicit when, what happens if the insured already got a check, trust-account rules where they exist. “We’ll take 25%, handshake” is not a business model — it’s a compliance event. Fee structure is both your revenue engine and your license risk.

**Documentation / estimating craft**  
Photos that survive desk review. Measurements. Xactimate (or peer) fluency. Moisture science literacy when water is involved. Knowing when to bring a PE, hygienist, or contents specialist. Expertise *is* the brand — soft marketing without file quality becomes storm-chaser stigma fast.

**Carrier friction literacy**  
Staff adjusters, IAs, desk review, preferred-vendor steering on the *contractor* side, depreciation fights, matching issues, ordinance & law, ALE. You don’t control the carrier’s calendar. You control your file discipline and escalation judgment.

**Referral ecosystem (without becoming the trade)**  
Roofers and restorers live on the same storms ([roofing](/models/roofing), [restoration](/models/restoration)). Healthy referral loops fill calendars; captive kickback schemes and unlicensed “we’ll handle your claim *and* the roof” mashups are how reputations and licenses die. Stay in your lane unless you hold the other licenses and the conflict rules allow it.

**Marketing compliance**  
Storm solicitation rules, door-knock / flyer bans in some jurisdictions after declared disasters, advertising claims (“guaranteed maximum recovery”), use of “adjuster” titles, contractor lead-share arrangements. Digital ads that work in quiet months can be illegal theater after a CAT. Compliance is a product feature.

**Talent / multi-adjuster leverage**  
Solo founders hit a ceiling on inspections + negotiation + admin. Hiring licensed adjusters (or trainees toward license) means payroll or draw **before** contingency lands. QA on files becomes your E&O firewall. CAT weeks: everyone wants the same licensed people.

**Working capital until settle**  
This is the silent killer — same family of pain as insurance AR in roofing/restoration, but inverted: *you* often get paid last, on contingency, after the carrier pays the insured. Months of burn on a growing book is how “record signed files” still bounce rent. Lines of credit, reserves, and ruthless qualification of thin claims matter more than hail maps.

**Technology / pipeline**  
Claims CRM, estimating seats, document vaults, photo standards, deadline clocks. Spreadsheets break when the book leaves solo. Vendor PA platforms exist; they are tools, not a moat.

**Owner role (honest version)**  
Early stage: you *are* rainmaker, field adjuster, negotiator, compliance officer, and collections — or you’re paying someone who is. Semi-absentee fairy tales die when a desk adjuster ghosts and a client’s mortgagee holds the check. Earn leverage later with licensed bench + systems — don’t buy the myth with a storm lead list.

**Reputation / E&O**  
One overpromised recovery, one sloppy scope, one fee fight that goes public — Google and DOI both notice. Errors-and-omissions is not optional wallpaper; file standards are the deductible you control.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

**Operating intuition (education — not forecasts, not advice):**

- Contingency bands commonly educated around **~10–20%** of recovery on residential, **subject to state caps** that can sit at the low end (or lower in declared emergencies) — verify *your* statute before modeling  
- Claim cycle times often measured in **months**; complex / litigated-adjacent files longer — float planning is the P&L  
- Vendor napkin math (e.g. a few settled files/month × mid–five-figure settlements × mid-teens %) is useful only as **arithmetic illustration**; settle rate, fee caps, and overhead decide whether the napkin survives  
- Startup cash often underestimated: licensing, bond, E&O, estimating software, vehicle/field kit, website/compliance review, **and 6–12+ months of living/overhead before contingency cadence** — indie shape varies wildly; branded systems add fees (Brian’s sheet names Tiger Adjusters as **category proof** — Notion/Drive corpus reviewed for this page is **thin** on a dedicated Item 7/19 table — **do not invent one**; pull current FDD yourself if that path is on the table)  
- Contrast capital: Brian’s loose home-services franchise band (~**$100k–$200k**) in roofing/restoration teaching is about *those* trucks-and-crews models — **not** a PA startup quote

**Diligence questions that matter more than memorizing averages:**

- Which **states** are you licensed in — and what’s the **fee cap / contract form** reality there *this year*?  
- Trailing 24 months: **signed files → settled files → fee collected** (not “storm leads”)?  
- Average **days to fee** by claim type / carrier?  
- Mix: residential CAT vs quiet-year claims vs commercial?  
- % of pipeline from contractors / attorneys / digital / CAT travel?  
- E&O claims history / open complaints with DOI?  
- Marketing: what channels are **restricted after declared disasters** in your target states?  
- If multi-adjuster: who holds licenses, how are draws vs contingency splits set, who owns QA?  
- If branded/franchised: fee stack, lead quality, territory, training vs compliance burden — vs indie referral engine you can actually run?  
- Conflict policy with [roofing](/models/roofing) / [restoration](/models/restoration) partners — written, or vibes?

What that means in plain English:

The *category* can produce meaningful fees when settlements are real and cycles are managed.  
A *specific practice* can look busy after a hail map and still run out of cash — or get disciplined for a flyer that violated a solicitation rule.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Licensing by state (and temporary CAT credentials)**  
You don’t “open in 50 states” because the website says so. Exam, bond, E&O, contract approval, nonresident / temporary rules, CE — miss one and the pipeline is illegal. Multi-state growth is a compliance project, not a Canva campaign.

**2. Contingency fee ethics, caps, and contract regs**  
Your revenue *is* a regulated product. Caps, disclosures, cancel rights, “new money” definitions, trust handling. Aggressive fee theater wins a week of cash and years of DOI attention. Ethics isn’t a poster in the break room; it’s the P&L constraint.

**3. Storm feast / famine**  
CAT year = phone melting, hiring panic, travel, temporary licenses, quality risk. Quiet year = you find out whether you have referral muscle, commercial files, and non-storm claim flow — or a company that only exists when the sky cooperates. Same feast/famine physics Brian’s roofing/restoration teaching names — different balance sheet.

**4. Carrier friction**  
Lowballs, delays, reinspection loops, desk denials, preferred-contractor steering on the repair side, depreciation fights. You can be right on the scope and still wait. Adversarial files burn adjuster hours and client patience. Professional firmness ≠ becoming the problem the carrier blacklists in the market.

**5. Marketing / solicitation compliance**  
Post-storm door hangers, SMS blasts, “guaranteed” language, unlicensed lead runners, contractor pay-to-play. Some jurisdictions tighten solicitation hard after declared disasters. The lead channel that prints in May can be a fine in September. Compliance review is a cost of goods sold.

**6. Talent (licensed adjusters who can actually negotiate)**  
You can’t scale inspections and desk fights on the founder’s calendar forever. Licensed help is scarce when every PA firm and every IA shop is hiring after the same CAT. Training juniors means pay before contingency. Bad hires create E&O and Google events.

**7. Working capital until settle**  
Contingency means you finance the advocacy. Growing signed-file counts without reserves is how “best year ever” still misses payroll. Same working-capital family as insurance-heavy [roofing](/models/roofing) and [restoration](/models/restoration) — except your check often clears *after* theirs.

**8. Qualifying thin or doomed claims**  
Wear-and-tear, excluded perils, late notice, prior unrepaired damage, policies that won’t cover what the homeowner wants. Saying no is unpaid until it saves the year. Saying yes to everything fills the CRM and starves the bank account.

**9. Cycle time and client expectations**  
Policyholders hear “we fight insurance” and expect a two-week miracle. Education at intake — timelines, process, fee math — is retention. Poor expectation setting becomes chargebacks, complaints, and one-star reviews after a *successful* settlement that felt slow.

**10. Conflict and lane discipline with trades**  
Partnering with [roofing](/models/roofing) / [restoration](/models/restoration) is natural after storms. Owning or steering repairs without the right licenses, disclosures, and conflict controls is how you inherit contractor complaints *and* adjuster stigma. Expertise brand dies when you look like a claim-mill for a roof crew.

**11. E&O / file quality / overpromise**  
Missed rooms, weak photos, aggressive line items that won’t survive appraisal, verbal guarantees. Your product is judgment under scrutiny. One ugly file can cost more than a quiet quarter’s fees.

**12. Reputation / storm-chaser stigma**  
Public adjusting sits next to the same cultural landmine as storm roofing. Trust is the product. Over-solicitation and fee opacity paint the whole category — including the clean operators.

**13. Technology and admin drag**  
Estimating updates, document chaos, missed deadlines, no pipeline visibility. Solo heroes drown in admin before they drown in carriers. Systems are not optional vanity SaaS.

**14. Appraisal / litigation adjacency without being counsel**  
Some files escalate. Knowing when to hand to an attorney — and not practicing law from an estimating desk — is survival. This page is **not** a law firm model.

**15. Concentration risk**  
One contractor referral source, one metro CAT, one commercial client, one marketing vendor. Quiet weather + lost referral partner = cliff. Diversify channels or admit you’re a weather bet.

**16. Franchise / brand stack (if you go that route)**  
Fees, lead promises, branding rules, compliance burden vs playbook and training. Brian’s sheet listing **Tiger Adjusters** under Public Claims Adjuster is **category proof that the niche franchises somewhere** — not diligence that *your* unit economics work. Worth it only if the system wins compliant files and collections — not because the logo looks official on a yard sign.

**17. Confusing “carriers underpay” with “easy money”**  
Macro claim friction is the backdrop. Your company is license, fee law, documentation craft, cycle humility, and cash until settle.

**18. Semi-absentee myth**  
“Hire adjusters and collect contingency.” Early reality is your license on the wall, your name on the complaint, and your cash bridging the desk adjuster’s vacation. Active ownership ≠ passive-income hype — same ownership-frame muscle as Brian’s broader teaching.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means public / independent claims adjusting is a **real regulated professional-services + float business**, not a storm lottery with a contingency fantasy.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You’re willing to treat **state licensing and fee compliance** as the product foundation, not paperwork afterthoughts  
- You’re wired for documentation craft, negotiation patience, and adversarial-but-professional carrier work  
- You’re capitalized for **months of float** — or deliberately staying solo/small until cadence exists  
- Expertise-brand building (reviews, associations, clean referral partners) excites you more than door-knock theatrics  
- You can say **no** to thin claims and sketchy marketing channels  
- You’ve compared this door to [roofing](/models/roofing), [restoration](/models/restoration), [home-inspection](/models/home-inspection), and [buy-hold](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold) — and still want **claim advocacy** physics, not install trucks, dry-out gear, transaction reports, or leveraged housing assets  
- Storm-cycle volatility doesn’t require a guarantee

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You want [roofing](/models/roofing) / [restoration](/models/restoration) production economics (crews, materials, preferred-vendor mitigation) without PA licensing and contingency float  
- You want [home-inspection](/models/home-inspection) fee-per-report calendars tied to housing closes  
- You want [buy-hold](/models/real-estate-investment-buy-hold) asset appreciation / rental NOI  
- You hate conflict, delayed gratification, and regulated sales/marketing rules  
- You’re looking for passive / “hire a team and fish” from month one  
- You’re under-reserved for quiet weather and slow settles  
- You’re actually looking to practice law or run a contractor lead mill under an “adjusting” label  
- You refuse multi-state compliance work but want national CAT cowboy economics

Franchise-branded PA systems vs indie vs attorney-adjacent partnerships is a separate call.  
Same category either way: license, contract, document, negotiate, float, collect, stay compliant.

Many people will correctly choose the **trade** door (roof/restore), the **report** door (inspection), or a **different SMB** instead. That’s a valid read of goals and constraints — not a lack of ambition.

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “storms happen” or a pastel promise that every carrier check is easy money.

You’re buying the right to build an **expertise practice** that turns policyholder claim friction into contingency fees — if you can get licensed where you work, stay inside fee and marketing law, document like your E&O depends on it, survive carrier calendars, staff through CAT chaos, and fund the months before settlement.

Some people should start solo in one well-understood state with brutal claim qualification and reserves.  
Some people should partner referral loops with clean [roofing](/models/roofing) / [restoration](/models/restoration) operators without owning those trucks.  
Some people should buy or build the **trade** instead and stay on the install / mitigate check.  
Some people should run [home-inspection](/models/home-inspection) or another report/transaction practice.  
Some people should walk — and put the same capital into a vehicle with cleaner recurring economics or less regulatory surface area.

Brian’s published teaching gives you **storm/insurance adjacency** (roofing, restoration, insurance-as-squeeze in housing math) and a **250-ideas row** that proves the PA category exists as a named franchise idea — not a full operator playbook for this page. Use the adjacency; don’t invent a Brian endorsement of a logo.

The question isn’t “do insurance disputes happen after storms?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful contingency calendar that only works when the sky opens, carriers eventually pay, and your license survives the marketing you used to get the file?**

If you want help comparing doors (this public-adjusting model vs roofing vs restoration vs home inspection vs buy-hold ops vs other SMBs), that’s a conversation.
