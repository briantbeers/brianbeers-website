New Year resolution hits January 2.  
Someone’s jeans don’t fit.  
A coworker won’t shut up about their 5 a.m. class pack.

Somebody needs a **place with equipment that works**, a door that opens when their schedule does, a coach or front desk that isn’t hostile — and a monthly draft that survives when motivation dies in March.

That’s a **fitness gym / boutique studio**.

Not a med spa / injectables chair ([med-spa](/models/med-spa) — same *local wellness retail* neighborhood, totally different clinical and CAC stack).  
Not a tutoring / learning center ([tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) — same *enrollment + instructor labor + strip site* brain, totally different student vs sweat product).  
Not a children’s play gym / Little Gym–class kids program (adjacent backlog — birthday parties and class packs, different parent buyer).  
Not “I’ll coach out of my garage and call it a company” as the whole *facility* story.

You’re running a **membership facility**: square feet × utilized member-months − rent − labor (trainers / coaches / GM) − marketing CAC − equipment debt − the silent cancel when the card fails and nobody noticed they stopped showing up.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **membership churn**, **rent**, **instructor / manager dependency**, **CAC**, **CapEx**, and **competition** show up before the lobby looks like a brand Reel.

24-hr key-fob clubs, coach-led boutique studios (HIIT / cycling / barre / Pilates), indie neighborhood gyms, and franchise / PE concepts sell overlapping physics. The logo is optional. The paying roster and the lease are not.

---

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local fitness facility operator**: leased (or owned) square footage purpose-built or TI’d for training — rubber floors, HVAC that can handle bodies, bathrooms/lockers, access control, equipment, often a studio room or open floor — staffed by some mix of GM / front desk, personal trainers or group coaches, and cleaning / night coverage.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them):**

| | **24-hr / access club (dues-led)** | **Boutique / coach-led studio (class-led)** |
--|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Monthly / annual membership + PT / coaching attach | Class packs, unlimited memberships, intro offers; sometimes retail |
| **Primary constraint** | **Active paying members × net dues − rent − light labor** | **Class fill × coach quality × schedule density − rent − coach pay** |
| **Labor feel** | Labor-light: key fob / app access; 1–3 people for desk + sales (Brian’s published Anytime–class framing) | Labor-heavy: coaches are the product; star coach walk-off hurts |
| **CapEx feel** | Mid-heavy: ~4–6k sq ft typical strip club; full equipment package | Mid-heavy to heavy: branded tech, treadmills/rowers, reformer walls, sound/HR screens |
| **Cash shape** | Recurring dues; PT is the margin lever | Higher ticket per visit; intro discount culture; pack liability |

Many real shops sit in a hybrid — open gym + classes. Underwrite the mix honestly — a dark Tuesday class grid is a different company than a full access club with strong coaching attach.

Typical menu:

1. **Membership dues** — the spine (month-to-month or commitment; family / corporate add-ons)  
2. **Personal training / small-group coaching** — often the profit engine on access clubs  
3. **Group classes / boutique sessions** — HIIT, cycle, strength, yoga, stretch  
4. **Intro / challenge offers** — 21-day, first-month specials (acquisition tool *or* churn factory)  
5. **Retail / supplements / apparel** — attach; don’t confuse with the spine  
6. **Day passes / guest fees / corporate** — fill soft hours; watch abuse  
7. **Child care / amenity attach** (some formats) — retention play with liability and labor tax

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google Local / Maps, Meta, referral, employer wellness, drive-by / strip co-tenancy, franchise lead systems (if any)  
2. **Tour / trial** — goals, schedule fit, intro price, cancel policy honesty  
3. **Convert** — card-on-file, commitment length, e-sign  
4. **Onboard (first 90 days)** — first workouts booked, coach intro, habit before the cancel window  
5. **Retain** — attendance flags, PT attach, community, failed-payment recovery  
6. **Win back / replace** — because churn never sleeps

**Scope lock:** this page is the **facility fitness business** — boutique studio or 24-hr / access gym with membership economics, rent, and trainer/manager dependency.  
It is **not** mobile personal training / in-home only (GYMGUYZ–class adjacency — different CapEx, different route math).  
It is **not** [med-spa](/models/med-spa) medical aesthetics.  
It is **not** [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center).  
It is **not** children’s fitness / play gym as the primary thesis (separate backlog row).  
It is **not** big-box volume clubs as *your* underwriting target unless you actually have that capital stack and trade area — Planet Fitness–class physics are proof the category professionalizes at scale, not a starter template for every buyer.

Treat named systems as **proof the category professionalizes** — not as a pitch.

## How money comes in

You sell **paying member-months** (and training/class sessions that convert into retained dues) — not follower counts and not a ribbon-cutting photo.

Education price / roster bands (national consumer ranges; metros sit higher; discount clubs sit lower):

- Access / 24-hr club dues often discussed roughly **~$30–$65+/month** depending on market and coaching bundle  
- Boutique unlimited / class memberships often **~$99–$249+/month**; class packs and drop-ins fill the rest  
- Mature single 24-hr clubs in Brian’s Anytime–class education framing often orbit roughly **~600–1,000+** members (system averages cited near ~**730** in his newsletter math — *not* your forecast)  
- Boutique breakeven conversations often land roughly **~120–200+** active members depending on rent and coach cost — fill rate beats vanity capacity

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Active paying members** — draft clearing *this month*, not “signed in January”  
2. **Net dues after discounts / freezes / corporate deals you invented under pressure**  
3. **Ancillary attach** — PT, classes, retail (access clubs often live or die on coaching attach %)  
4. **Churn / retention** — monthly cancel + failed cards; annual retention industry anchor near **~66%** (HFA 2025 education) with strong boutiques aiming higher  
5. **CAC vs LTV** — Meta / Google / intro offers fill the top; referrals and onboarding cheapen the bottom  
6. **Utilization of fixed plant** — rent and equipment don’t care that 2 p.m. is empty  
7. **Manager / coach productivity** — who sells, who coaches, who owns the member relationship when you’re not there

Gross margin intuition (operator / industry education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Membership dues: high *gross* after variable — then **rent + labor + marketing + royalties** eat the dream  
- Rent targets often discussed **under ~15–20% of revenue**; occupancy that climbs past that is a trap  
- Labor: light on key-fob clubs; heavy on coach-led boutiques (class pay **~$35–$75+** per class in competitive metros shows up in operator chatter)  
- Equipment: CapEx or lease that must be replaced on a cycle  
- Net on a single unit is often a **tighter** band than Instagram lobby myths — volume rises; churn, rent escalators, and CAC rise with it

You’re not a fitness influencer with a lease.  
You’re a **roster + facility fixed-cost** company wearing good lighting.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Bodies still need training. Employers still sell wellness. Social proof still packs January. Roughly **~20%** of Americans belong to a gym/fitness club in Brian’s published TAM framing — enough demand that a small town still needs a convenient box within a **~15–20 minute** drive.

Good times → boutique packs, PT packages, corporate, premium dues.  
Tight times → memberships are often among the **first discretionary cancels** (Brian has said the quiet part out loud in franchise-critique content) — then the “I’ll come back after the wedding / after taxes / after I feel bad again” member still returns when the jeans argue.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Med spa](/models/med-spa) rides **clinical oversight + injector scarcity + paid-social CAC** on a face cadence.  
[Tutoring / learning center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) rides **enrollment + teacher labor + school-year seasonality** on a parent wallet.  
Fitness rides **membership churn + rent + coach/manager dependency + equipment CapEx** on a habit that breaks easily.

Same “I won’t DIY this forever in my living room” demand emotion. Different cancel button, different lawsuit, different Meta bill.

That’s the engine. Not one viral transformation Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one location

A single club or studio lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Membership churn (the career-defining line)**  
Industry annual retention education sits near **~66.4%** (HFA 2025). Boutique operators often treat that as a *floor*, not a trophy — strong shops aim roughly **~75–80%** annual. Monthly churn that looks “only 4–7%” compounds into a leaking bucket: if you add 12 and lose 21, marketing is a treadmill. Split **voluntary** cancels from **involuntary** (failed cards — often a large share of “churn” in vendor data). First **90 days** onboarding is where the year is won.

**Rent & lease structure**  
Biggest fixed cost on most access clubs. Small-town / secondary trade areas can win on rent and competition density (Brian’s Anytime–class small-town thesis). Escalators, CAM, HVAC capacity for gyms (often **2–3×** typical retail), and TI allowances matter as much as the sticker PSF. A pretty endcap next to Lululemon is not automatically a viable P&L.

**Instructor / coach / manager dependency**  
Boutique: the coach *is* the product. Access club: the GM who can sell memberships and run the floor is the semi-absentee hinge. Brian’s semi-absentee talking points explicitly list **F45 / Orangetheory / Anytime–class** as manager-run fitness — *if* you actually have a manager who owns outcomes, not a shift lead with a title. Star coach walk-off and weak GM are the same disease in different uniforms.

**CAC discipline**  
Intro offers and Meta will take the money. Creative fatigue and race-to-the-bottom “$0 down first month” fills the book with people who cancel when the promo ends. LTV:CAC and payback (education target often **under ~6 months** contribution payback in diligence frames) beat vanity join counts.

**Equipment CapEx & replacement cycle**  
Cardio fleets, strength, tech packages, reformer walls — buy, lease, or refinance. Soft utilization still wants maintenance contracts. Deferred replacement is how “tired gym” becomes the turnaround story Brian filmed — and also how buyers overpay for rust.

**Competition density**  
Low-cost volume clubs, boutique stacks in the same grocery-anchored center, CrossFit boxes, hotel gyms, Peloton-at-home, and the next trend modality. Differentiating on “we have treadmills too” is not a moat. Trend-hot boutiques can print — then cool (Brian’s published caution on Orange Theory–class trend risk post-Peloton/COVID).

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only closer *and* the only beloved coach *and* the night cleaner when the GM quits, you own a stressful fitness job with a lease. Scale starts when a manager + coach bench can run Tuesday without you on every tour.

## The numbers (how to read them — not a promise)

Treat public blogs, HFA / industry retention summaries, FDD secondary writeups, Brian’s published Item 7 / Item 19 education bands, and “owner income” ranges as **shape**, not your forecast. Always pull current FDD (if franchised), trailing membership ledgers, lease abstracts, and marketing spend.

**Capacity / roster intuition:**

- Access club: system averages in Brian’s Anytime–class education near **~730** members; turnaround story framed as roughly **~600 → ~1,000** members flipping a losing fixed-cost base — still not *your* book  
- Boutique: breakeven often modeled roughly **~120–170+** actives at ~**$150**-ish revenue-per-member/month conversations — rent and coach cost move the wall  
- Industry annual retention education ~**66%**; strong boutique target talk **~75–80%**

**Startup / CapEx education bands (category, wide):**

- Lean indie PT studio / micro: often discussed **~$50k–$150k+**  
- Small boutique (barre / HIIT / cycle): often **~$120k–$280k+**; franchise boutiques commonly **~$250k–$600k+** and can climb past **~$750k–$1.1M** Item 7 bands (Orangetheory–class secondary 2026 FDD education)  
- 24-hr / access franchise clubs: Brian’s published 2025 FDD education **~$458k–$907k**; secondary 2026 Item 7 bands often cited roughly **~$540k–$905k** — verify the actual FDD  
- Working capital: months of rent + payroll + marketing before the roster compounds; TI from landlords can be material (Brian cited ~**$20/sq ft** average TI in one year for that system — market-specific)  
- Equipment replacement reserve: not optional if you plan to sell or refinance later

**Membership / CAC education shape:**

- Dues bands above; PT attach can be **~25–35%+** of revenue in some access-club Item 19 education mixes  
- Model cancel rates, freezes, failed-payment recovery, and deferred pack liability — not just join vanity  
- Corporate / family discounts: volume that can quietly destroy blended dues

Your diligence questions should sound like:

- Trailing **active** members vs frozen vs pending cancel (not “all-time joins”)?  
- Monthly churn: voluntary vs involuntary; first-90-day cancel %?  
- Mix: dues vs PT/coaching vs classes vs retail?  
- Attendance frequency cohorts (0–1×/week vs 2+)?  
- Coach / trainer count, tenure, pay model, book concentration?  
- GM tenure and whether the club runs when they’re out?  
- Marketing: spend by channel, CAC, intro-offer conversion to retained month 4+?  
- Lease: PSF, escalators, CAM, HVAC, remaining term, assignment, TI clawbacks?  
- Equipment: owned vs leased, age, replacement schedule, debt/lease balances?  
- Claims / incidents / security (especially 24/7)?  
- If franchised: does the system shorten hiring, sales playbooks, and ramp — or only a royalty and a paint color?

Same brain you’d use buying any high-fixed-cost membership retail shop: roster quality, lease, labor bench, and whether *you* want sweaty customers and cancel calls in your week.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Membership churn**  
The #1 silent killer. January joins that ghost by March. “I’ll pause for the summer.” Failed cards counted as mystery attrition. A leaking bucket makes every marketing dollar a refill, not growth. Onboarding, attendance flags, and dunning are ops — not soft skills.

**2. Rent & occupancy**  
The lease doesn’t care about New Year’s resolutions. Escalators, CAM spikes, and gym-grade HVAC turn “affordable strip” into a fixed-cost trap. Overbuilding square footage for Instagram is how working capital dies.

**3. Instructor / coach dependency (boutique)**  
Members follow coaches. When the 6 a.m. star leaves for a competitor or their own garage brand, the pack walks. Schedule instability and coach drama are product defects.

**4. Manager / sales dependency (access clubs)**  
Semi-absentee only works with a real GM who sells, cleans standards, and owns the P&L feel. “Labor-light” is not “management-free.” Brian’s turnaround story still required brutal owner hours before the roster and systems supported location two.

**5. CAC & intro-offer addiction**  
Cheap first months train members to cancel and rejoin on promo. Paid social will happily burn cash while your LTV assumes a 14-month stay you don’t have. Measure retained month-4 members, not tour count.

**6. CapEx & equipment replacement**  
Financed treadmills and tech packages feel fine until utilization is soft and the balloon hits. Deferred maintenance shows up in diligence as a price cut — or a walk.

**7. Competition & price wars**  
Low-cost volume clubs, three boutiques in one center, at-home bikes, and employer gym stipends. Racing dues down fills the book with price shoppers who leave for the next $10 cut.

**8. Trend / modality risk**  
Hot formats cool. Brian has publicly used Orange Theory–class boom-then-pressure as a cautionary “timeless vs trendy” example. Buildouts and long leases outlive hashtags.

**9. Seasonality & motivation cycles**  
Q1 spike, summer soft, holiday soft. Without summer programming and corporate, cash dips while rent doesn’t.

**10. 24/7 security, cleanliness, liability**  
Unstaffed hours, theft, harassment, injury, bathroom disasters. Members have high cleanliness expectations; one viral dirty-locker Reel torches local trust. Cameras, access logs, and cleaning SOPs are product.

**11. Failed payments & freeze culture**  
Involuntary churn and generous freezes inflate “members” who aren’t paying. Collections tone vs brand tone is a real ops design problem.

**12. Pack / credit liability (boutiques)**  
Prepaid class packs sitting on the balance sheet. Busy books with deferred obligation. Cash today, service debt tomorrow.

**13. Owner-as-hero coach / closer**  
If only you can sell *and* only you can fill the 5:30 class, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a high-stress fitness job with a personal guarantee on the lease.

**14. Franchise / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, required vendors, tech fees, and territory rules help only if they fill the roster and shorten ramp. A logo does not replace a coach on Tuesday or a GM who can close.

**15. Multi-unit fantasy before one-unit mastery**  
Roll-up stories are real in mature systems (Brian highlights large multi-unit fitness franchisees). They’re also how people buy a second lease while the first still needs them every night.

**16. Confusing “fitness is booming” headlines with “your utilized, collectible member-months after rent and churn”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday show-up rate and your CAM invoice.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means fitness is a **membership + facility fixed-cost** business where empty square feet, open cancels, and rising CAC decide whether the rubber floor survives payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit and retain coaches *or* a real GM (depending on format) — and respect that people *are* the product  
- Churn dashboards, attendance cohorts, and failed-payment recovery energize you  
- You’re capitalized for TI + equipment (or leases) + months of dues ramp — and sober about personal guarantees  
- You can enforce cancel policies and intro-offer rules without becoming the villain in a Facebook group  
- You’ve compared this door to [med-spa](/models/med-spa) (clinical CAC) and [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) (enrollment labor) and to lighter mobile fitness / home-service vehicles — and still want the facility stack  
- You’re honest about semi-absentee: manager cost hits EBITDA hard at one unit; math improves at multi-unit *after* systems exist

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a passive “memberships print money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to cancel calls, sweaty bathrooms, or coach drama  
- You’re undercapitalized for buildout + equipment + working capital through a soft summer  
- You actually want **mobile PT only**, **kids play gym**, **med spa**, or **pure online coaching** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll add cryo / IV / apparel next quarter” was a margin patch, not new complexity  
- You need the trendiest modality more than a timeless habit product in *your* trade area

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: paying roster, rent, labor bench, CAC, equipment. A brand is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring, sales, and ramp — not because the paint is orange.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** service business or a non-facility vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Churn outruns joins → marketing treadmill, shrinking book  
- Rent / CAM / HVAC too heavy for the trade area  
- Star coach or GM walk-off with the relationship  
- CAC spike + promo addiction → busy tours, thin retained cash  
- Equipment debt / lease with soft utilization → fixed-cost trap  
- 24/7 incident / cleanliness / liability event → reputation spiral  
- Trend modality cools while the lease runs  
- Pack liability and freeze games mask true recurring revenue  
- Owner forever on the close and the 5:30 class  
- Royalty / brand drag without demand or hiring leverage  
- Buying a “book” that was one coach + one Meta account + one soft lease  
- Confusing industry boom headlines with utilized member-months after rent

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “everyone wants to get in shape.”

You’re buying the right to run a **membership facility machine** — if you can hold churn down, carry rent without lying to yourself, staff coaches or a GM who own outcomes, acquire members at a CAC your LTV can repay, deploy equipment CapEx only when utilization is real, and not confuse January joins for free money.

Some people should buy a tired access club and fix roster, sales process, and cleanliness (the turnaround path Brian has walked on camera).  
Some people should open boutique-lean and prove class fill before a second room.  
Some people should choose [med-spa](/models/med-spa) or [tutoring-learning-center](/models/tutoring-learning-center) or a truck-based service business and never take on gym HVAC.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do people join gyms?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful fitness job with Meta bills, coach drama, and a personal guarantee when the only GM who can close texts “I’m done”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other health-wellness vehicles vs home-service or education paths), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
