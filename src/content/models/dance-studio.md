Saturday morning lobby.  
Rec class at 9. Competition rehearsal at 10:30. Costume fittings stacked like a warehouse.  
Someone still wants a kid who can *perform* — not a living-room TikTok, not a school auditorium once a year, not “we’ll try YouTube until soccer season ends.”

Private coaches at $60–$120+/hour don’t scale to siblings, styles, and a mortgage.  
Parks-and-rec is one hour and a boombox.  
Garage “lessons with my cousin who danced in high school” ghost when the competition calendar gets real.

Someone needs a **dance studio box**: recurring tuition, a competition / recital fee machine that parents can budget (or resent), instructors who *are* the product, sprung floors that don’t destroy knees, and — often — a **retail / costume / dancewear** attach that looks like margin until inventory and returns eat the week.

That’s a **dance studio** business.

Not [kids-music](/models/kids-music) (amps, practice rooms, showcase theater — same *enrichment + instructor + parent churn* brain, different sound and different circuit).  
Not [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) (mats, birthday rooms — same *class packs + coach dependency*, different sprung-floor CapEx and costume politics).  
Not [kids-art](/models/kids-art) (materials mess / school-partner lightness — adjacent enrichment; different stage and different competition fees).  
Not [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) (adult membership utilization — shared “fixed facility + churn” gravity, different ICP and different Saturday politics).  
Not a solo teacher renting hourly room time (related idea, different ceiling, different lease and roster machine).  
Not “edtech dance app” (different capital stack, different buyer).

You’re running a **dance enrichment facility** (or a lighter mobile / partner-site kids dance program): sprung floors, mirrors, barres, sound, a schedule of recreational classes and/or competition teams and/or adult ballroom packages, front desk that sells trials and collects costume deposits, instructors who convert and retain, and a calendar that spikes around **recital season, competition weekends, and costume order chaos**.

Simple on a whiteboard.  
Hard in real life — because **instructor retention**, **competition circuit costs**, **tuition churn**, **facility rent**, **recital / costume season**, **retail distraction**, and **parent politics** show up before the lobby looks like a brand Reel of rhinestones and trophies.

Fred Astaire / Arthur Murray / Tippi Toes–class systems and indie neighborhood studios sell overlapping physics across *different* ICPs (adult ballroom packages vs kids rec/comp). The logo is optional. The filled tuition book, instructor bench, lease, and fee honesty are not.

## What the business actually is

You’re a **local dance-education operator**: leased (or owned) square footage fitted for instruction — sprung floor, mirrors, barres, sound, lobby / viewing, storage for costumes and retail — plus a roster of instructors, a tuition product (monthly / term), and seasonal fee stacks (recital, costumes, competition entries) that can dwarf base tuition for competition families.

**Two product shapes (don’t blur them — this is the ICP + fee-stack fork):**

| | **Kids rec + competition studio (primary spine for this page)** | **Adult ballroom / social / package studio (adjacent factory)** |
|--|------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What you sell** | Recurring tuition for age-banded classes; competition teams; recital; costumes; retail | Lesson packages / memberships for couples and adults; socials; sometimes comps |
| **Primary constraint** | **Active paying students × net tuition + fee attach − instructor pay − rent − circuit ops** | **Package / membership sales × instructor utilization − rent − royalties (if any)** |
| **Labor feel** | Teaching artists + competition choreographers; after-school and Saturday peaks | Pro dancers who sell packages and teach 1:1 / small group |
| **CapEx feel** | Mid–heavy: sprung floor TI, multi-room, lobby — often ~1.5–4k+ sq ft | Mid–heavy too; ballroom systems often quote franchise Item 7 in mid–high six figures |
| **Cash shape** | Tuition spine + recital/costume/comp spikes; soft summer without camps | Package cash up front *or* membership; less “June costume hell,” more sales-cycle pressure |

Many real shops sit in a hybrid — kids rec *plus* a thin competition team *plus* adult evening *plus* retail. Underwrite the mix honestly — a soft rec book with a loud competition Instagram is a different company than a dense rec roster with comps as retention frosting.

Typical menu:

1. **Ongoing recreational class tuition** — the spine (monthly by class count / unlimited tiers)  
2. **Competition / company / elite teams** — higher tuition + choreography + entry + travel; culture and cash intensity  
3. **Recital / showcase production** — retention theater *and* ops tax (venue, tech, run-of-show)  
4. **Costume fees** — wholesale → parent invoice; margin *or* trust landmine  
5. **Competition entry / convention / travel pass-throughs** — often high dollar, thin studio margin, huge parent sticker shock  
6. **Camps / intensives / summer bridge** — plug July–August when the school calendar dies  
7. **Retail / dancewear / shoes / merch** — attach; inventory distraction risk  
8. **Adult evenings / fitness dance / ballroom packages** — fill soft dayparts; different buyer, same rent  
9. **Birthday parties / studio rentals / private lessons** — attach; don’t confuse with the spine  
10. **Private coaching / college audition prep** — high ticket, key-person heavy

Typical loop:

1. **Lead** — Google Local / Maps, school night tables, Meta parent groups, recital guest tickets, competition circuit visibility, franchise lead systems (if any)  
2. **Trial / tour** — style fit, instructor chemistry, schedule, *total cost of season* conversation (not just monthly tuition)  
3. **Enroll** — monthly tuition or pack, auto-pay, cancel / freeze / makeup policy honesty  
4. **Staff the book** — instructor coverage for *that* style and *that* time slot (not “we’ll hire when full”)  
5. **Deliver** — curriculum fidelity, attendance, leveling honesty, competition readiness without injury theater  
6. **Retain / produce** — progress language, recital participation, sibling enrollments, summer offer, costume deposits collected *on time*  
7. **Replace** — because **sports conflict, age-out, instructor walk-off, competition burnout, and “we’ll restart after recital” never sleep**

**Scope lock:** this page is the **facility (or program) dance studio** — tuition + competition fees, instructor retention, recital/costume season, strip/studio rent, retail attach.  
It is **not** [kids-music](/models/kids-music) rock-school lesson books.  
It is **not** [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) birthday-room class packs.  
It is **not** [kids-art](/models/kids-art) school-partner art packs.  
It is **not** [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) adult membership utilization as the primary thesis.  
It is **not** pure online dance marketplaces or influencer choreography channels (different machine — CAC and creator risk).  
It is **not** a costume / dancewear retail store that “also does classes” as an afterthought (retail turns vs tuition book — different P&L).

## How money comes in

You sell **paying student-months of dance tuition** (and fee seasons that convert into retained families) — not daycare child-weeks and not adult gym dues alone.

Education price / roster bands (national consumer ranges; metros sit higher; secondary markets sit lower):

- **Recreational monthly tuition** commonly discussed roughly **~$65–$120+/mo** for one weekly class and **~$140–$220+/mo** for multi-class recreational loads at mid-market studios  
- **Competition / company track tuition** often **~$150–$400+/mo** before choreography, costumes, and entries — serious pre-teen/teen competitors frequently land higher  
- **Registration fees** commonly **~$25–$75+** per student / season  
- **Recital fees** commonly **~$50–$150+** per dancer; **costume fees** often **~$75–$200+** per costume (competition costumes frequently higher)  
- **Competition family all-in** education (tuition + choreo + costumes + entries + conventions + travel) often discussed **~$5k–$10k+/season** per dancer; elite tracks higher — *parent sticker shock is the sales conversation*  
- Mature Arthur Murray–class franchise Item 19 education (2025 FDD / FY2024 secondary): ~**216** eligible studios averaging roughly **~$716k** gross (median ~**~$634k**); wide quartile spread — *not* your forecast  
- Mature Fred Astaire–class franchise Item 19 education (2026 FDD / CY2025 secondary): ~**242** mature studios averaging roughly **~$820k** gross (median ~**~$625k**) — adult ballroom package factory; different ICP than kids rec/comp  
- Twinkle Star–class kids dance franchise secondary education: Item 7 often **~$124k–$196k**; small-sample Item 19 conversations near **~$445k** avg on continuing locations — verify current FDD; tiny n  
- Tippi Toes–class / Kinderdance–class mobile / partner-site kids dance often **lighter** CapEx (Tippi Toes Item 7 education often **~$69k–$164k**; Kinderdance–class often **sub-$30k**) — different plant than a sprung-floor competition studio

What actually drives the dollars:

1. **Active paying students** — drafts clearing *this month*, not “signed at the September open house”  
2. **Net tuition after sibling / multi-class discounts you invented under pressure**  
3. **Fee attach honesty** — recital + costume + competition entries collected *and* retained as trust, not surprise invoices in March  
4. **Program mix** — rec-only vs rec+comp vs adult packages vs camps  
5. **Class fill / room utilization** — empty Tuesday 4:30 hip-hop still burns rent and HVAC  
6. **Churn / length of stay** — sports seasons, instructor changes, competition burnout, and “we’ll restart after recital” are structural  
7. **Instructor productivity** — who converts trials, who owns the parent relationship when you’re not on site  
8. **Retail / dancewear turns** — real attach *or* dead inventory next to the lobby mirror  
9. **Summer bridge** — camps / intensives / adult fill, or July–August against full rent

Gross margin intuition (operator / FDD education shape — **not** a forecast):

- Tuition: high *gross* after variable — then **instructor pay + rent + marketing + royalties** eat the dream  
- Instructional labor is often the monster: secondary operator guides commonly discuss instructional costs in a **~40–50%+ of revenue** conversation depending on density and owner teaching hours (owner-on-the-floor masks true labor)  
- Occupancy / rent on sprung-floor strip studios is fixed whether August is empty  
- Costume / retail can show **~30–50%** gross on paper — until returns, wrong sizes, late orders, and storage tax show up  
- Competition entry / travel is often **pass-through heavy** — cash flow complexity without fat margin; the win is retention and team culture, not “entry fee profit”  
- Royalty + brand + tech stacks on branded systems can stack into **high-single to low-teens %** of gross on some models (e.g. Arthur Murray–class education often cites **~8% royalty + ~2% marketing**; Fred Astaire–class often **~7% royalty** plus tech/other fees — verify current Item 6)  
- Pack / prepaid liability and costume deposits: cash today, undelivered class or costume debt tomorrow  
- Net on a single unit is often a **tighter** band than rhinestone lobby myths — volume rises; churn, rent, instructor wages, and competition ops rise with it

You’re not a kids influencer with a lease.  
You’re a **tuition-fill + instructor-roster + fee-season + fixed-cost facility** company wearing mirrors and a Saturday lobby.

## Macro: why this industry keeps working

Parents still outsource enrichment. Screens still lose the “learn to dance / perform” argument for a large slice of families who can pay. Dual-income households still need after-school structure that isn’t only homework and devices. Competition culture (in markets that have it) still turns dance into identity — which is retention fuel *and* burnout fuel. Adult social / fitness dance still fills evening rooms when kids dayparts soften.

Good times → second styles, competition upgrades, costume upgrades, sibling enrollments, retail, intensives.  
Tight times → enrichment is often among the **first discretionary cancels** after the adult gym dies — *unless* the family is on a competition team, in which case they cut travel elsewhere and keep the season (or explode when the all-in bill hits). Underwrite which parent you actually have.

Add the sister-model contrast lightly:

[Kids-music](/models/kids-music) rides **recurring tuition + teacher roster + recital marketing**.  
[Kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness) rides **class packs + birthday ops + instructor dependency**.  
[Kids-art](/models/kids-art) rides **class fill + materials COGS + school partners (or studio mess)**.  
[Fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) rides **membership utilization + churn** on adult wallets.  
Dance rides **tuition + competition fees + instructor retention + retail** — on a facility that peaks after school and detonates around recital / costume season.

Same “parents pay for kids’ outcomes” emotion. Different plant, different lawsuit story, different March invoice.

That’s the engine. Not one viral competition solo Reel.

## Micro: what actually drives one studio

A single dance studio lives or dies on a few boring things:

**Instructor recruiting & retention (the career-defining line)**  
Parents enroll in *people*, not floor plans. The Tuesday 5 p.m. jazz teacher who remembers every combination converts trials and holds renewals. When that instructor leaves for a competitor, a touring gig, a school job, or their own garage brand — and takes “their” choreography and half the team’s loyalty — the book walks. Curriculum binders help; they do not replace chemistry. Owner-as-hero teacher looks great on Instagram and terrible on a succession plan. Gigging dancers want flexible hours you don’t have at 4–8 p.m. peaks; college students churn with finals; career changers want daytime hours your after-school density doesn’t offer.

**Competition circuit costs & fee honesty**  
Entries, conventions, choreography fees, travel, extra costumes — the all-in season can 3–5× base tuition. Studios that bury the stack until March create churn *and* review bombs. Studios that publish a full fee calendar at enrollment win trust *and* filter for families who can actually pay. Competition is culture and marketing — and a logistics company you accidentally started.

**Tuition churn & structural age-out**  
Kids don’t stay nine forever. Sports seasons, moves, divorce schedules, “goals met,” competition burnout, and instructor switches punch holes in LTV. A healthy studio is a **continuously refilled bucket**: trials → commitments → renewals → replacement. Vanity “lifetime dancers” stories ignore the refill rate.

**Facility rent & sprung-floor reality**  
Biggest fixed cost on most strip / plaza studios. Sprung floor TI (education often **~$15–$22+/psf** installed for quality systems), HVAC for closed rooms of jumping bodies, parking at 4:15 pickup, landlords who hate bass, and neighbors who hate tap. Escalators, CAM, remaining term, and assignment clauses matter as much as PSF. Overbuilding a lobby for Instagram is how working capital dies.

**Recital / costume season ops**  
The retention engine *and* the ops landmine: venue booking 9–12 months out, run-of-show, costume wholesale → parent invoices, fittings, late orders, photo/video packages, volunteer parents, “can we go last,” and review bombing when the mic cut out. Recitals print re-enrollment when SOPs are real (operator education often cites **~25–30 point** re-enrollment lifts for participants — treat as *shape*). They destroy weekends and culture when they’re improvisation — same honesty as showcase ops on [kids-music](/models/kids-music), different rhinestones.

**Retail / dancewear discipline**  
Shoes, tights, leotards, team jackets, flowers at the door. Real attach when SKUs are tight and turns are watched. Dead inventory, returns, and “can you special-order my brand” is how the front desk becomes a boutique with worse margins than tuition. Costume *program* fees ≠ open retail — underwrite them separately.

**Parent politics (especially competition)**  
Placement fights, solo vs group drama, costume comparisons, carpool feuds, social media parenting, custody schedules. Written codes of conduct and dismissal policies are product — not “mean.” Soft-heartedness without rules becomes a second job.

**Curriculum quality & leveling honesty**  
Branded systems live or die on doing the method as designed. Indie studios live or die on a coherent progression parents can explain at dinner. Drift (“every teacher freestyles”) destroys outcomes storytelling and referral engines. Online dance apps raise the bar: your counter isn’t a prettier logo — it’s **supervised progress, belonging, and a local habit**.

**Competition density**  
Indie teachers in living rooms, church basements, school dance teams, parks-and-rec, YouTube, the next studio three miles over, and enrichment adjacency ([kids-music](/models/kids-music), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), [kids-art](/models/kids-art), swim, martial arts). Differentiating on “we have mirrors too” is not a moat.

**Trial conversion & CAC**  
Intro offers and Meta will take the money. Race-to-the-bottom “first month free” fills the book with people who cancel when the promo ends — or when the costume invoice arrives. Measure retained month-3 / month-4 students *and* fee-collection completion, not tour count.

**Owner involvement**  
If you’re the only closer *and* the only beloved jazz teacher *and* the only recital producer *and* the only costume orderer when the studio director quits, you own a stressful kids job with a lease. Scale starts when a director + instructor bench can run Tuesday without you on every trial.

## Biggest challenges (say them out loud)

This section is the point. Soft marketing skips it. Operators live it.

**1. Instructor recruiting & retention**  
The #1 silent killer in lesson/class-led dance studios. Members follow teachers. When the star of the Tuesday jazz book (or the competition choreographer) leaves, renewals wobble, trials stop converting, and teams fragment. Schedule instability and teacher drama are product defects — not HR footnotes. Gig calendars, college finals, and “I got a daytime job” fight your after-school peaks. Annual turnover conversations in dance-education operator guides often land ugly (commonly discussed in the **~25%+** neighborhood for instructional staff in secondary writeups — treat as *shape*, not your metro). Choreography walk-off without work-for-hire language is a second knife.

**2. Competition circuit costs (parent sticker shock + ops tax)**  
Entries, conventions, travel, extra costumes, choreography fees — the all-in season can crush families who only budgeted monthly tuition. Pass-through cash is operationally heavy and margin-thin. Missed communication = churn + public reviews. Over-competing (too many weekends) burns instructors *and* parents. Under-competing in a competition market loses the identity buyers. This is a logistics and trust business wearing sequins.

**3. Tuition churn**  
Sports conflict, moves, sibling budget compression, “we’ll pause until after recital,” instructor switches, competition burnout, and age-out of the “phase.” Your marketing engine must refill a leaking bucket *while* you deliver. Vanity “lifetime enrollments” don’t pay March rent — or the costume wholesale bill you already floated.

**4. Facility rent & sprung-floor TI**  
The lease doesn’t care about soccer season or a choreographer who quit. Escalators, CAM spikes, and floor/HVAC TI turn “cute plaza bay” into a fixed-cost trap. Overbuilding for the Instagram lobby is how working capital dies. Noise and vibration complaints are a landlord relationship problem with a P&L.

**5. Recital / costume season complexity**  
Venue, run-of-show, fittings, late sizes, photo rights, volunteers, and parent politics. Recitals print referrals and re-enrollment when SOPs and staffing are real. They destroy weekends and culture when they’re improvisation — and a bad recital (or a costume disaster) can accelerate churn instead of stopping it. Ordering costumes without deposit discipline is how you fund parents’ regret.

**6. Retail distraction**  
Dancewear attach looks like free margin. Dead inventory, returns, special orders, and lobby clutter look like a second business you didn’t staff. Costume *program* billing ≠ open retail. If retail needs a buyer and a replenishment cadence you don’t have, shrink it.

**7. Parent politics**  
Placement, solos, costume comparisons, carpool drama, social media, custody schedules, “why isn’t she on company.” Written policies and consistent enforcement are survival tools. Competing for the loudest parent’s approval fills the studio with politics and empties it of operators who want a life.

**8. Curriculum quality & leveling honesty**  
Franchise: deviate and outcomes (and audits) suffer. Indie: no method = every teacher is a different product. Parents buy a progress story; freelancing instructors sell chaos. Online apps raise the “why pay you?” bar every year. Fake “everyone makes company” leveling destroys trust when the competition scores arrive.

**9. Seasonality (academic calendar as operating system)**  
Back-to-school and pre-recital windows fill. Late spring softens as sports and “we’re almost done.” Summer can crater without camps / intensives / adult evening fill. Fixed rent and a studio director don’t care that it’s July.

**10. Labor cost & classification**  
Instructor wages, payroll taxes, employee vs contractor games, background checks, minors as aides. “We’re a dance family” doesn’t impress the Department of Labor — especially in ABC-test states. Owner teaching hours can mask a labor problem until you get sick.

**11. Key-person / artistic-director risk**  
Lose the person who knows every parent, every instructor’s quirks, the competition weekend SOP, and the costume vendor login and the “semi-absentee” story collapses into your evenings.

**12. Trial / intro-offer addiction**  
Cheap first months train parents to cancel and rejoin on promo. Paid social will happily burn cash while your LTV assumes a 14-month stay you don’t have once costume invoices hit. Measure retained students *and* fee completion, not tour selfies.

**13. Make-ups, no-shows, and policy soft-heartedness**  
Kids get sick; travel teams travel; you reschedule into already-full peaks. Liability for undelivered prepaid hours sits on your books if policy is mush. Competition absences create fairness fights that soft policies cannot solve.

**14. Style mix & room matching**  
A waitlist for hip-hop and an empty ballet barre at 4:30 still doesn’t fill the lease. Competition programs live or die on roster balance across ages/styles — not just headcount. Adult evenings can save summer *or* steal the only rooms your kids program needs.

**15. Injury, safeguarding & music-license exposure**  
Sprung floors exist for a reason. Abuse/molestation riders, photo consent for minors, and ASCAP/BMI/SESAC/GMR licensing are not optional vibes. One allegation or one viral “unlicensed music” fight can erase years of lobby goodwill.

**16. Royalty / brand fee drag (if any)**  
Royalties, brand funds, required vendors, tech fees, and territory rules help only if they fill the roster and shorten instructor ramp. A logo does not recruit your 5 p.m. contemporary teacher or calm a costume revolt.

**17. Owner-as-hero teacher / recital producer / costume buyer**  
If only you convert trials *and* only you teach the beloved book *and* only you run June recital *and* only you chase Capezio orders, you didn’t buy freedom — you bought a high-stress kids job with a personal guarantee on the lease.

**18. Confusing “parents love dance” headlines with “your filled, collectible student-months after rent, churn, and fee-season trust”**  
Same trap as every demand-story industry. Macro vanity ≠ your Monday show-up rate and your CAM invoice.

**19. Multi-unit fantasy before one-unit mastery**  
Roll-up stories exist in mature dance systems. They’re also how people buy a second lease while the first still needs them every competition weekend and every costume crisis.

**20. CapEx creep toward retail / venue / second competition room**  
You picked dance studio to *own enrichment* — then over-improve the lobby, over-buy retail, over-book the circuit, and under-reserve for summer. Stay honest about the plant and the fee stack you can staff.

None of that means the model is bad.  
It means dance studio is a **recurring-tuition + competition-fee + instructor-roster + facility fixed-cost** business where empty rooms, open cancels, costume trust failures, and rising instructor dependency decide whether the mirrors survive payroll.

## What it takes (fit / anti-fit)

**This fits you if:**

- You can recruit and retain dance instructors *and* respect that people *are* the product  
- Churn dashboards, trial conversion, fee-calendar honesty, and recital / competition utilization energize you  
- You’re capitalized for sprung-floor TI + months of tuition ramp + costume wholesale float — and sober about personal guarantees  
- You can enforce cancel / freeze / makeup / code-of-conduct policies without becoming the villain in a parent Facebook group  
- You’ve compared this door to [kids-music](/models/kids-music), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), [kids-art](/models/kids-art), and [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) — and still want the dance facility stack  
- You’re honest about semi-absentee: director + instructor bench cost hits EBITDA hard at one unit; math improves at multi-unit *after* systems exist

**This is a bad fit if:**

- You wanted a passive “kids love dance so it prints money” story from month one  
- You’re allergic to parent conflict, costume politics, competition weekends, or instructor drama  
- You’re undercapitalized for buildout + working capital through a soft summer and a floated costume season  
- You actually want **kids music**, **kids fitness**, **kids art**, **adult gym memberships**, or **solo living-room lessons only** — different underwriting  
- You thought “we’ll add a cafe / big retail / second competition room next quarter” was a margin patch, not new complexity  
- You need the trendiest modality more than a timeless class product in *your* trade area  
- You wanted adult ballroom package sales only (or kids mobile only) and are pretending it’s the same diligence as a multi-room competition studio

Franchise vs independent is a separate call.  
Same physics either way: paying enrollments, rent, instructor bench, fee-season ops, CAC. A brand (Fred Astaire / Arthur Murray / Tippi Toes / Twinkle Star / peers) is only worth the fee if it shortens hiring, curriculum training, and ramp — not because the walls are branded.

Many operators will correctly choose a **lighter** kids-adjacent door (mobile dance, art packs, tutoring) or a non-facility vehicle first. That’s a valid read, not a lack of ambition.

## Risks (the short list)

- Instructor walk-off with the relationship (and choreography) → enrollment cliff  
- Soft class fill + full rent → structural losses  
- Tuition churn outruns trials → marketing treadmill  
- Competition fee surprise → trust collapse + mid-season exits  
- Seasonality without camps / adult fill → summer cash hole  
- Recital / costume chaos → retention and culture damage  
- Retail dead stock / costume float → working-capital trap  
- Parent politics unmanaged → culture death spiral  
- Curriculum drift → weak outcomes → dead referrals  
- Rent / CAM / sprung-floor TI too heavy for the trade area  
- CAC spike + promo addiction → busy tours, thin retained cash  
- Online / living-room substitutes undercutting price/convenience  
- Pack liability and freeze games mask true recurring revenue  
- Owner forever on the close, the class book, and the costume crisis  
- Royalty / brand drag without demand or hiring leverage  
- Buying a “book” that was one teacher + one Meta account + one soft lease  
- Confusing enrichment demand headlines with utilized student-months after rent  
- Labor misclassification / wage-hour exposure on split shifts  
- Injury / safeguarding / music-license event → existential reputation risk

## The ownership point

You’re not buying “parents want kids in dance.”

You’re buying the right to run a **dance enrichment machine** — if you can hold instructor quality, fill recurring tuition, publish and collect a fee calendar without destroying trust, run recital / competition ops without burning the weekend team, carry rent without lying to yourself, keep retail from becoming a second job, acquire families at a CAC your length-of-stay can repay, and not confuse September open-house joins for free money.

Some people should buy a tired studio and fix instructor bench, fee honesty, and cancel policy.  
Some people should open lean indie and prove class fill before a second competition room.  
Some people should choose [kids-music](/models/kids-music), [kids-fitness](/models/kids-fitness), [kids-art](/models/kids-art), or [fitness-gym](/models/fitness-gym) and never take on costume season.  
Some people should walk — and put the capital into a different vehicle entirely.

The question isn’t “do kids need dance classes?”

It’s: **does this give you control of the outcome — or a stressful kids job with Meta bills, instructor drama, March costume invoices, competition weekends, parent politics, and a personal guarantee when the only choreographer who can hold the company team texts “I’m done”?**

If you want help comparing doors (this model vs other education / enrichment vehicles vs something outside child services), that’s a conversation.  
Not a pitch deck.
