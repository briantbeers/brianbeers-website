## Start with leadership (before you drown in PDFs)

Especially with newer brands, leadership quality outweighs a clever offer.

Look for:

- **Experience** in both the industry *and* franchising  
- **Personality** — responsive, respectful, actually invested in franchisee success  
- **Team size vs unit sales** — is support hiring keeping up with (or ahead of) franchise sales?  
- **Skin in the game** — corporate or leadership-owned units used to pilot ideas  
- **Reputation** — search beyond the brochure: news, trade press, owner forums, public records  

If leadership fails this sniff test, Item 19 gymnastics will not save the deal. Related: [red flags](/franchises/franchise-red-flags-risks).

## FDD reading plan (high-signal items)

The FDD is disclosure, not a love letter. It is written to protect the franchisor and brand. Read with a franchise attorney when you get serious. This overview is **not** legal advice.

Suggested focus order (themes commonly associated with these items):

| Item themes | What to understand |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **1** | Who the franchisor is, parents/affiliates, business history |
| **3** (and often **4**) | Litigation and bankruptcy disclosures — patterns matter |
| **5 & 7** | Initial fees and estimated initial investment (build your own budget on top) |
| **6** | Ongoing royalties, ad fund, and other recurring fees |
| **8** | Supply restrictions and affiliate purchase requirements |
| **12** | Territory definition and protection |
| **15** | Whether you must personally operate day-to-day |
| **17** | Renewal, transfer, termination, dispute resolution, liquidated damages themes |
| **19** | Financial performance representations — starting point only; see misuse risks |
| **20** | Unit counts, openings, closures, transfers — ask for the *story* behind the tables |

Also note Item 1 context for affiliates that collect fees or sell you goods.

### Sample diligence prompts (tease — not the full pack)

1. “Walk me through how support headcount has grown versus franchise awards in the last 24 months.”  
2. “Which Item 7 line items do new owners most often underestimate in *this* market?”  
3. “For Item 20 closures and transfers, what are the top three reasons — in your words?”  
4. “Where does Item 19 come from (corporate, franchisee subset, averages vs medians), and what does it *not* include?”

## Financial analysis frame

Build a simple multi-year pro forma ([guide](/franchises/franchise-pro-forma)) using:

- Low / medium / high sales cases  
- Gross margin after direct cost of goods and labor  
- Variable fees (royalties, advertising, credit card, brand tech, etc.)  
- Operating expenses (rent, insurance, vehicles, sales/management payroll, admin)  
- Profit **before** debt service and owner benefits  

Sources: Item 19 (carefully), franchisee validation, competitor FDDs, and industry research — never a single sales call.

## Operations frame

You need a day-in-the-life picture for frontline, manager, and owner — new single-unit vs tenured multi-unit.

Probe:

- Initial and ongoing training quality  
- Business coach cadence and substance  
- Franchisee community (Slack / chat / peer help)  
- Biggest recurring challenges owners face  
- Culture fit (including values-based brands)

## Growth frame (if you care about scale)

- Is the model actually scalable to your target size — with proof of owners already there?  
- Does the franchisor encourage multi-unit, or prefer single-unit operators?  
- Real estate / territory inventory as a bottleneck  
- Market research help: competitors, demand, quality checks (visit and request quotes)  
- Your exit sketch: sell in X years, pass to family, or hold cash-flow with a team
