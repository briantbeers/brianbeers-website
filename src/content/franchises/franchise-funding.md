Most buyers blend sources. The goal is a stack that covers startup **and** runway — then add roughly **10–20%** working-capital cushion on top of what you think you need ([pro forma](/franchises/franchise-pro-forma)).

This is education, not a loan offer or vendor endorsement. Talk to your CPA, lender, and counsel before you move money. Hub: [complete guide](/franchises).

## Self-funded and equity-backed ideas

### Cash

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| No loan payment; no interest | Takes time to accumulate; may limit you to lower-cost concepts |
| Simpler stress profile | Opportunity cost; you may still need more WC later |

### HELOC (home equity line)

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Often flexible draws; comparatively lower rates; interest-only structures common | Limited by equity; lender can restrict or term out; home is at risk if you cannot repay |

### Loan against a taxable stock portfolio

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Avoids selling (and some tax/timing issues); interest-only structures exist | Higher rates than many HELOCs; **margin calls** if portfolio value drops; size limited by portfolio |

### Life insurance cash-value loan (whole life)

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Can borrow against cash value; structures vary | Only relevant if you have the policy; cash value takes years; may be small |

None of these are “best.” They are tools with different failure modes.

## Retirement-capital approaches (handle with care)

### 401(k) / retirement rollover structures

Sometimes discussed when someone is leaving W-2 work and exploring funding a C-Corp operating company. **Pros people cite:** using retirement capital to bet on an operating business without a classic early-withdrawal penalty *if* structured correctly. **Cons / caveats:** complexity, setup and ongoing costs, C-Corp requirements in many designs, concentration of retirement risk, and the need for experienced specialists — **plus CPA and ERISA-aware counsel**. This is not DIY weekend reading. Vendor names in old checklists are not endorsements here.

## Other people’s money and credit

### Franchisor in-house financing

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Fast; fewer banks; sometimes covers fee and/or equipment | One-size terms; possible rate/term premium; default can threaten the whole franchise relationship; personal guarantees common |

### Equipment financing

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Useful for truck-/equipment-heavy models; lenders who already know the brand can move faster | Usually equipment only — not fee, WC, or soft costs; collateral risk if you default |

### Unsecured personal loans / lines

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Flexible use of proceeds if you qualify | High bar (credit, income, clean history); higher cost; personal guarantee |

### SBA-guaranteed loans

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Can cover broader startup needs (fee, equipment, WC) for eligible deals; larger ceilings and longer terms in some structures | Paperwork; collateral expectations (often including home); restrictions and prepayment rules; personal guarantee; use lenders who understand franchise deals |

### Friends and family

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Flexible debt or equity; you can design terms | Relationship risk; unwanted “board members”; pressure to repay early can starve the business |

## How to choose a stack

1. Price the **real** all-in open + 10–20% WC buffer.  
2. Decide max personal guarantee and home-collateral comfort.  
3. Prefer simplicity where rates are close — complexity has a cost.  
4. Run debt service through your [pro forma](/franchises/franchise-pro-forma) at conservative sales.  
5. Revisit after [validation](/franchises/franchisee-validation-questions) changes your ramp assumptions.
