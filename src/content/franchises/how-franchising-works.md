A franchise is a licensed way to operate someone else’s brand and system. You pay for the right to use the brand, follow the playbook, and receive defined support. In return, you accept rules — on products, marketing, territory, and how (and when) you can sell or leave.

This page is orientation only. For tradeoffs versus building or buying independent, see [Is buying a franchise worth it?](/franchises/is-buying-a-franchise-worth-it). For the full process map, return to the [complete guide](/franchises).

Watch: [How Franchising Made Me a Millionaire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsYBbpC5UA4)

## What you are actually buying

You are not buying a guaranteed customer list. You are buying:

- Brand and trademarks (within rules)  
- Operating system, training, and (ideally) ongoing support  
- A territory definition — which may be protected, shared, or limited  
- Access to approved vendors, tech, and marketing programs  

You remain the owner of *your* local business entity. Cash flow, hiring, local execution, and debt service still sit with you.

## Common fee buckets

Exact amounts live in each brand’s FDD (especially Items 5–7). Conceptually:

| Bucket | What it usually covers |
|--------|------------------------|
| Initial franchise fee | Right to join the system; often paid at signing |
| Build-out / equipment / vehicles | Capex to open (varies wildly by model) |
| Working capital | Cash to cover ramp before the business supports itself |
| Royalties | Ongoing % of sales or flat fees for brand/system |
| Brand / ad fund | Shared marketing contributions |
| Other | Tech fees, call center, training refreshers, etc. |

Treat “all-in to open” and “cash to survive year one” as two different questions. The second is where many buyers under-reserve. See [pro forma](/franchises/franchise-pro-forma) and [funding](/franchises/franchise-funding).

## Territory basics

Territory (often FDD Item 12) answers: Where can you sell? Who else can sell near you? How is the map drawn?

Ask early:

- Exclusive, protected, or open?  
- Population, zip, radius, or account-based?  
- Can the franchisor put corporate or another franchisee next door?  
- What happens with national accounts and online leads?

A “territory” that looks big on a map can still be weak if demand, labor, or competition is thin. Pair territory talk with market research and [validation calls](/franchises/franchisee-validation-questions).

## Agreement basics (not legal advice)

The Franchise Agreement is the long-term rulebook. It typically covers term length, renewal, transfer (selling), termination, dispute resolution, and non-competes. FDDs summarize key points (Item 17 is a common place to start) — counsel still needs to read the full agreement.

Mindset that helps:

- The document is written to protect the brand. That is normal. Your job is to understand what you are agreeing to.  
- Liquidated damages, personal guarantees, and transfer fees matter as much as the royalty rate.  
- “Can I hire a GM?” vs “Must I work in the business?” often lives in active-participation rules (commonly Item 15 themes).

When you are close to a decision, use [attorney review & go/no-go](/franchises/franchise-attorney-decision).
