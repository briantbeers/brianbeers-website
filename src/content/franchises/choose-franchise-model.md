Almost no brand will check every box. The job is to find one that checks **enough** of *your* boxes — and to know which misses are deal killers.

Use three columns on a single page: **Requirements**, **Nice-to-haves**, **Deal killers**. Update it as you learn. Then compare brands against the sheet, not against each other’s sales decks.

Part of the [complete buying guide](/franchises). Next diligence: [due diligence](/franchises/franchise-due-diligence).

Watch: [How To Choose a Franchise](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DpXzbrsxFLk)

## 1) Goals and lifestyle

Write long-term and short-term outcomes: financial targets, time with family, relationship goals, how hard you want the first 24 months to be.

Lifestyle reality check:

- Weekday daytime operations vs nights/weekends vs 24/7 on-call  
- Physical / field work vs office / dispatch  
- Owner-operator → manage techs → manage managers → more board-like oversight  

Be honest about the stage you will *actually* operate in year one — not the Instagram version of year five.

## 2) Investment and cash needs

Separate:

- Liquid cash you can invest  
- Total project cost including debt  
- Cash flow needed to service debt **and** pay you  

Financing options (SBA, HELOC, stock-backed loans, friends & family, etc.) belong in [funding](/franchises/franchise-funding). Do not pick a model that only works if every financing fantasy clears.

## 3) Industry posture

Sketch preferences without worshiping trends:

- Timeless vs emerging  
- Growing, stable, or shrinking demand  
- How the category tends to behave in recessions (some trades and needs-based services hold up differently than premium discretionary work)

Pair this with [models](/models) you already like outside franchising.

## 4) Financial shape (education, not promises)

When you later read Item 19 and talk to owners, you will care about unit volume ranges, gross margin, variable vs fixed costs, and net before debt. For now, decide what *shape* you need: high-ticket few jobs vs high-volume low ticket; recurring vs on-demand vs project-based.

Cash timing matters as much as margin:

- Paid in advance (memberships)  
- Paid at service  
- 30–60 day receivables  

Seasonality and weather dependence belong in your deal-killer column if you cannot stomach them.

## 5) Complexity and customers

Simple menus often scale faster to learn; complex menus can create a moat (and a training burden). Licensing requirements can be either barrier or advantage.

Customer type:

- **B2B** — smaller market, relationship selling, often less price sensitivity  
- **B2C** — larger market, more competition, heavier marketing  

Neither is “better.” Match your personality and network.

## 6) Environment and scale

Home-based, mobile / warehouse, or brick-and-mortar each change capital, landlords, and hiring.

If you want multi-unit:

- Are there franchisees already at your target size?  
- Does the franchisor encourage scale or prefer single-unit operators?  
- Is real estate the bottleneck?  

Growth questions deepen in [due diligence](/franchises/franchise-due-diligence) and [Discovery Day](/franchises/franchise-discovery-day).

## 7) People, competition, franchisor, community

Decide your comfort with unskilled vs skilled labor, team size, and retention reality.

Competition: other franchisees expanding near you can be a feature or a fight.

Franchisor track record themes (emerging vs legacy, leadership, litigation, unit growth, control philosophy) belong in diligence — but you can already mark “heavy litigation” or “hyper-restrictive supply rules” as deal killers.

Community signals: conventions, regional meetings, franchisee advisory groups, and a real business coach — not just a sales rep.

## How to use the checklist

1. Fill the three columns in one sitting.  
2. Share with a spouse / partner if they share the risk.  
3. Only then open brand conversations ([franchisor questions](/franchises/questions-for-franchisor)).  
4. Let the scorecard break ties — feelings second.
