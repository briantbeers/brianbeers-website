A pro forma is not a prediction of what *you* will make. It is a decision tool: under boring assumptions, when does cash stop bleeding, and how much runway do you need before then?

Watch: [How to Build A Franchise Financial Model](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-YjB0SEILwk) · [When will my franchise start making money?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NkLZnLJ3-oU)

Pair with [funding](/franchises/franchise-funding) and [due diligence](/franchises/franchise-due-diligence). Hub: [complete guide](/franchises).

## Inputs that matter

Pull from Item 19 (carefully), owner validation, competitor disclosures, and your own market research:

| Input | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Sales cases | Low / medium / high — not a single hopeful number |
| Gross margin | Sales minus direct COGS and direct labor |
| Variable fees | Royalties, ad fund, credit card, tech, call center, etc. |
| Operating expenses | Rent, insurance, vehicles, sales/management payroll, admin |
| Startup capex | Fee, build-out, equipment, deposits, professional fees |
| Working capital | Cash to cover the ramp — then add **10–20%** buffer |

Profit before debt service and owner benefits is a useful checkpoint. Debt service belongs in a second layer so you see operating reality first.

## Break-even, simply

1. List fixed monthly costs you must pay even on a quiet month.  
2. Estimate contribution margin after variable costs and franchise fees that scale with sales.  
3. Ask: what monthly sales cover fixed costs? How many weeks/months of ramp feel realistic from validation — not from the sales deck?  

If break-even only works in the “high” case, treat that as a warning, not a stretch goal to brag about.

## Working capital discipline

Buyers often fund the open and underfund the wait. Practical habit:

- Estimate months to stable operations from owners who look like you  
- Multiply average monthly cash burn in that window  
- Add **10–20%** on top for surprises (hire misses, marketing lag, equipment delays)  

Then build the [capital stack](/franchises/franchise-funding) to that number — not to the brochure’s “suggested” WC alone.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Item 19 averages as *your* year-one forecast  
- Ignoring owner pay and debt service until after you “feel good” about the deal  
- Skipping low-case stress tests  
- Forgetting one-time soft costs (legal, travel, deposits, first inventory)  

For misuse of performance claims, see [red flags](/franchises/franchise-red-flags-risks).

## After the spreadsheet

- Revisit assumptions after [validation calls](/franchises/franchisee-validation-questions)  
- Carry open number gaps into [Discovery Day](/franchises/franchise-discovery-day)  
- Have your CPA pressure-test tax and entity implications before you sign
