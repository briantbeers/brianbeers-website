Franchisor calls are easy to turn into sales theater. Your job is to collect **evidence**, names, and promised follow-ups — then verify with the FDD and owners.

Use this with your [model checklist](/franchises/choose-franchise-model) and [due diligence](/franchises/franchise-due-diligence) plan. Full list of 25 stays gated.

Related watches: [Franchise FDD Deep Dive](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hcgVWaPVYwc) · [Top 3 Franchise Due Diligence Tips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GwAWr7cfqs4)

## How to run the conversation

- Capture contact name, title, date, and commitments in writing  
- Ask open questions first (“What should I know about joining?”) before fee laundry lists  
- Separate brand story from your market’s constraints  
- When they cite performance, ask what is in Item 19 vs verbal color — verbal is not a substitute for disclosure  
- Schedule validation in parallel so you are not learning only from HQ  

## Sample questions (tease — not all 25)

1. **What should a serious candidate know about joining your system that is easy to miss on the website?**  
2. **Where do you see the system in five years — and what concrete steps are underway?**  
3. **Who are the main competitors, and what truly differentiates you in a noisy market?**  
4. **Walk me through all-in startup costs and how much additional capital owners typically need after launch.** (Treat answers as inputs to *your* budget.)  
5. **How is my territory defined and protected, and what is the development plan in my state?**  

Other themes in the full list include: contract term and renewals, training and profitability support, mandatory purchases, royalties and brand fund, failed units and why, financing/real-estate help, litigation and dispute history, site selection, transfer/retirement rules, non-competes, and the biggest challenges owners face.
